What is Pakistan really seeking from its new defence alliances?

Published on October 25, 2026

Role Overview

Pakistan has established strategic defence partnerships with several Arab and Muslim states, including Saudi Arabia, Turkiye, Kuwait, Qatar, and Indonesia. Key pacts signed since September 2025 include the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement, the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement, and the Badr agreement. Each accord explicitly emphasizes joint defence production and local industrial investment. This shift aims to move Pakistan from a buyer-seller model to a co-production framework, upgrading domestic public-sector defence infrastructure while diversifying supply chains.

These joint ventures focus on direct equity participation rather than simple technology licensing. Over the coming years, new manufacturing facilities, testing ranges, and human capital initiatives are projected to create thousands of high-skilled jobs. This growth is expected to stimulate interconnected technical industries such as software engineering, electronics, and metallurgy, providing valuable domestic career opportunities for skilled individuals.

Key Details

  • Industry Focus: Defence production, manufacturing facilities, testing ranges, software engineering, electronics, and metallurgy.
  • Anticipated Investment: Potential direct foreign investment inflows between $1.2 billion and $1.5 billion over the next five years.
  • Export Targets: National defence export goals are projected to grow from $280 million in 2024 to $650–700 million by 2027, and cross $1 billion by 2030, targeting markets in the Gulf, Africa, and Central Asia.
