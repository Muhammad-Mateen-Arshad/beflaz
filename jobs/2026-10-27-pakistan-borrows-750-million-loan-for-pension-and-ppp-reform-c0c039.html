Pakistan Borrows $750 Million Loan for Pension and PPP Reforms

Published on October 27, 2026

The Government of Pakistan has approved borrowing $750 million, equivalent to Rs. 209 billion, from the Asian Development Bank (ADB) to execute comprehensive pension reforms and promote public-private partnerships (PPP). The initiative is designed to strengthen fiscal sustainability and support long-term economic growth, which is expected to assist overall job creation in the country.

Role Overview

This update details government policy and financial reform measures rather than direct job openings. However, leadership from the Planning Commission noted that fiscal stabilization resulting from these budget support programs aims to increase allocations for the Public Sector Development Program, ultimately helping to drive economic growth and generate sustainable employment opportunities.

Key Details

The total ADB funding framework consists of the following key elements:

  • Pension Reform Program: Allocated $500 million (Rs. 139 billion) to operationalize a defined contribution pension scheme, improve service delivery, and enhance federal pension governance and administration.
  • Public-Private Partnership Framework: Allocated $250 million (Rs. 70 billion) to enhance the legal and institutional infrastructure for public investment and financial management at the federal level.
  • Loan Structure: The PPP portion consists of $200 million in standard financing and $50 million provided under concessional terms.
  • Approval Authority: Approvals were granted by a concept clearance committee headed by Deputy Chairman Planning Commission Ahsan Iqbal.
