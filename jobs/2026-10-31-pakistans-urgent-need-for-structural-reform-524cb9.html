Pakistan’s urgent need for structural reform

Published on October 31, 2026

Pakistan faces severe socio-economic challenges that directly impact the domestic labor market and employment opportunities. Recent data from the Pakistan Economic Survey reveals that the national unemployment figure rose from 4.51 million to 5.9 million. At the same time, the NGO sector has stopped hiring university graduates, reflecting a major gap between higher education institutions and current market demands.

Role Overview

This overview outlines the general employment and economic situation affecting skilled workers across Pakistan. Over the last three years, more than 1.871 million educated professionals—including IT specialists, engineers, and doctors—have moved abroad due to brain drain and the search for improved livelihoods. Despite having 284 degree-awarding institutions across the country, job seekers face a contracting local employment market.
