Pakistan Auto Policy 2026-31 Overview and Key Impact

Published on November 08, 2026

This article summarizes the Pakistan Auto Policy 2026-31 Overview and Key Impact, detailing proposed industrial reforms, tariff updates, public transport developments, and electric vehicle provisions across the country. This update serves workers, commuters, and sector observers evaluating transportation access and commuting costs. The application deadline is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Role Overview

The draft Auto Policy 2026-31 focuses on adjusting vehicle tariffs, setting taxation frameworks for buses and two-wheelers, establishing a formal route for commercial used-car imports, and expanding electric vehicle incentives. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has approved the draft policy. The Ministry of Industries and Production plans to send the document to the Ministry of Finance, which intends to share it with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) for review. The draft also outlines a proposed automobile levy and determines how generated proceeds are directed.

Key Details

Key data points and figures regarding transportation, household ownership, and vehicle sales in Pakistan include:

  • Vehicle Sales and Capacity: Local assemblers sold 155,631 passenger cars in 2025-26, or approximately 206,000 four-wheelers including jeeps and pick-ups. Indus Motors estimates the industry can assemble about 600,000 vehicles annually, currently utilizing roughly 30 percent of that capacity. Motorcycle and rickshaw sales totaled around 1.97 million units.
  • Household Vehicle Ownership: Based on the 2024-25 Household Integrated Economic Survey, 6.6 percent of households own a car, 57 percent own a motorcycle, and 41 percent own neither vehicle.
  • Pricing and Incomes: A Suzuki Alto VXR lists at approximately Rs2.99 million against an average monthly household income of Rs82,179. A reported estimated 9 percent price drop over five years under the draft policy would adjust the cost to roughly 33 months of average household income.
  • Public Commuting Relief: During petrol price increases of over Rs137 a litre, temporary relief measures included 30 days of free public transport in Islamabad and 69 days of free travel in Punjab.
