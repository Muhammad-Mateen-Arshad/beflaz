Pakistan Labour Force Protection and Rights Overview

Published on November 05, 2026

This Pakistan Labour Force Protection and Rights Overview details the condition of informal workers and legislative protection across the nation. Based on the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) Labour Force Survey 2024–25, it outlines statistical data, working conditions, and legal frameworks for home-based, agricultural, and domestic workers. Role Overview

Findings from the PBS Labour Force Survey 2024–25 reveal that 80.8 percent of employed individuals in Pakistan work in informal roles under the 21st International Conference of Labour Statisticians recommendations, compared to 19.2 percent in formal employment. In addition, 72.1 percent of non-agricultural work is located within the informal sector. These roles span domestic staff, agricultural labourers, small business employees, and home-based workers performing tasks such as garment stitching, embroidery, and product packaging.

Around 4.6 million individuals work at their own dwelling, representing 6 percent of all employed workers. Female representation is notably higher in home-based roles, with 19.8 percent of working women operating from home compared to 1.8 percent of working men.

Key Details

  • Informal Employment Share: 80.8 percent overall; 72.1 percent in non-agricultural sectors.
  • Home-Based Workforce: Approximately 4.6 million people (19.8 percent of employed women and 1.8 percent of employed men).
  • Relevant Legislation: Sindh Home-Based Workers Act 2018, Punjab Domestic Workers Act 2019, Sindh Women Agricultural Workers Act 2019 (Sindh Act No. V of 2020), Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Home-Based Workers (Welfare and Protection) Act 2021, Balochistan Home-Based Workers Act 2022, and Punjab Home-Based Workers Act 2023.
  • Location: Pakistan (including Sindh, Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan).
