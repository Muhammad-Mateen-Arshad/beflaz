Pakistan Auto Industry Policy: EV and Job Creation

Published on November 08, 2026

The government has unveiled the final draft of the Pakistan Auto Industry Policy: EV and Job Creation initiative to strengthen domestic manufacturing, lower vehicle prices, and expand electric vehicle adoption. This framework seeks to enhance safety standards, expand export production, and foster economic growth across the automotive sector. Application deadlines are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Role Overview

The newly announced Automotive Industry Policy focuses on reducing reliance on imported vehicles by developing a strong domestic manufacturing base. The initiative sets a five-year target to boost automotive exports from $200 million to $6 billion, while encouraging technological advancement, local auto-parts vendor expansion, and broader employment opportunities in the manufacturing sector.

Eligibility Criteria

Not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Specific candidate qualifications, individual job roles, or personal eligibility guidelines have not been detailed in the draft policy document.

Key Details

The policy outlines major structural updates, consumer protections, and tax incentives for electric and hybrid vehicles:

  • EV Financing Limit: The financing limit for electric vehicles has been increased from Rs2 million to Rs10 million.
  • Tax Rates: Electric vehicles will incur a 1% sales tax, range-extended vehicles 6%, and plug-in hybrids 9%.
  • Charging Infrastructure: Equipment imported for charging stations will carry a 1% sales tax and zero customs duty.
  • Consumer Protection: Vehicle booking advance payments are capped at a maximum of 20%, and third-party audits will be introduced.
  • Safety Enhancements: 45 additional safety standards will be implemented to improve vehicle quality and passenger safety.
  • Export Targets: Manufacturers meeting assigned export goals will receive a 5% reward on export value in the first year, while non-compliant companies will face penalties.
