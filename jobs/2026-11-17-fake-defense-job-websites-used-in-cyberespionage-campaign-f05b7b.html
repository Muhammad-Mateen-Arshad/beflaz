Fake Defense Job Websites Used in Cyberespionage Campaign

Published on November 17, 2026

Security research has revealed that fake defense job websites used in cyberespionage campaign operations are targeting defense contractors and military personnel. Mobile security firm Lookout discovered that cyberespionage actors are operating fake job recruitment platforms to install spyware on victim devices. Role Overview

These sites do not host legitimate job openings. Instead, hackers associated with a Pakistani-aligned threat group known as SpaceCobra or Cosmic Leopard use fake recruiter personas to target defense workers. Observed activity over the past 12 months shows that attackers create convincing career portals to trick targets into downloading GravityRAT spyware.

Key Details

Security researchers at Lookout, including senior manager Parth Bhatt, highlighted several key findings regarding the malicious websites:

  • The fraudulent websites display fake "our clients" sections containing logos of prominent defense companies such as Airbus, BAE Systems, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, Rheinmetall, Rolls-Royce, Saab, Safran, and Thales.
  • Site visitors who click an application download link receive a message claiming the app is under review at the Google Play Store, prompting them to download the file directly from the site.
  • The deployed GravityRAT malware, active since 2015, is designed to steal contact lists, call history, text messages, and WhatsApp communications.
  • While some social engineering tactics specifically targeted individuals in India, other tactics lacked country-specific references, indicating potential targeting of defense personnel globally.
