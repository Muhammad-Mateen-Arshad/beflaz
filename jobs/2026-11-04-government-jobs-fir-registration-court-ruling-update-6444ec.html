Government Jobs FIR Registration Court Ruling Update

Published on November 04, 2026

Candidates seeking public sector positions should be aware of the recent Government Jobs FIR Registration Court Ruling issued by the Federal Constitutional Court. The court established that the simple registration of a First Information Report (FIR) cannot serve as grounds to disqualify an applicant from public employment. Role Overview

A division bench led by Chief Justice Amin Khan instructed federal, provincial, and law enforcement authorities to establish uniform, transparent, and constitutionally sound guidelines for background verification during recruitments. The court stressed that recruitment decisions must align with established legal standards, merit, and fundamental justice.

Key Details

  • Background Checks: Character verification must rely on authentic and independent facts rather than suspicion or assumptions.
  • Post-Acquittal Status: If a candidate is acquitted, the case must not be presented negatively on their police character certificate.
  • Ongoing Cases: For pending legal proceedings, character records may only state the fact that the matter is pending, without pre-judging guilt.
  • Job Rights: No appointee can be removed from their position without being granted a fair hearing and a decision based on legal arguments.

Eligibility Criteria

The court clarified that an FIR is merely an initial allegation and does not serve as proof or evidence of a crime. Consequently, an FIR registration alone does not alter candidate eligibility.
