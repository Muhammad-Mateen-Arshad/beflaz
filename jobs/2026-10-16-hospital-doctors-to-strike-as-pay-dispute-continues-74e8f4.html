Hospital doctors to strike as pay dispute continues

Published on October 16, 2026

Healthcare professionals and job seekers in Northern Ireland should be aware of upcoming operational updates across public health services, as resident doctors, consultants, and specialist, associate specialist, and speciality grade (SAS) doctors prepare for industrial action in November. Organized through the British Medical Association (BMA), the strike action stems from an ongoing pay dispute with the government.

Role Overview

The industrial action affects various medical posts within Northern Ireland's healthcare sector. Representatives from the BMA report that doctors' real-terms earnings have dropped by more than 26% over the past 18 years due to below-inflation pay awards. BMA representatives emphasize that competitive pay is critical for retaining medical staff, recruiting new practitioners, and ensuring sustainable healthcare delivery across the region.

Key Details

Key information regarding the scheduled industrial action includes:

  • Location: Northern Ireland.
  • Resident Doctors Strike Action: A full 24-hour strike commencing at 07:00 BST on 20 November and running until 07:00 on 21 November.
  • Consultants and SAS Doctors Strike Action: A 24-hour strike commencing at 07:00 on 23 November and running until 07:00 on 24 November.
  • Service Impact: Doctors will maintain Christmas Day cover during the strike periods. Full emergency care will remain active, while elective and routine medical services will be cancelled.
  • Pay Proposals & Position: The BMA seeks a credible pay restoration offer. The Department of Health noted that Health Minister Robbie Butler supports implementing the 2026/27 pay award and restoring parity with NHS England. Earlier this year, the NHS pay review body recommended a 3.3% pay increase.

Eligibility Criteria

The medical roles described in the announcement maintain the following definitions:

  • Resident Doctors: Must be fully qualified medical practitioners currently undergoing postgraduate clinical training in general practice or hospital settings under senior doctor supervision.
  • SAS Doctors: Specialist doctors who are employed directly by health trusts.
