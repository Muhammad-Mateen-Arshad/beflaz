Probiotics may help protect against early signs of Alzheimer’s

Published on October 26, 2026

Key Details

The provided text discusses medical research on cognitive health rather than a standard employment listing. The facts outlined in the study include:

  • A 24-week clinical trial was conducted in South Korea involving 87 older adults experiencing early cognitive impairment.
  • Researchers evaluated a daily probiotic supplement called KL-P301, which contains 3 bacterial strains, against a placebo.
  • The trial was double-blinded, meaning neither the participants nor the researchers knew who received the probiotic or the placebo.
  • Participants took the supplement daily for 6 months, either as a powder or mixed into water.
  • Findings published in the Journal of Alzheimer’s Disease indicated that the probiotic group had reduced blood levels of pTau181, a marker linked to Alzheimer’s-related brain changes.
  • The probiotic group showed improved scores on cognitive evaluations, specifically tasks measuring spatial relationship comprehension and visual memory.
  • Levels of an inflammatory marker called IL-1β decreased more in the probiotic group than in the placebo group.
  • Researchers emphasized that these outcomes are preliminary and do not prove a reduction in harmful brain tau protein buildup, noting that larger and longer studies are necessary.
