UNHCR Terminated Teachers: 291 Staff Seek Government Aid

Published on November 04, 2026

In a recent development, UNHCR terminated teachers, comprising 291 Pakistani educators, are seeking government aid after being dismissed without gratuity or benefits. These instructors spent four decades educating Afghan children in refugee camps across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa before receiving sudden phone dismissals last month. Specific application deadlines or replacement job openings are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Role Overview

The affected educators served under formal agreements with the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), teaching displaced Afghan children in refugee camps. Their long-standing service covered forty years across multiple districts in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, including Peshawar, Nowshera, Swabi, Haripur, Mardan, Timergara, Kohat, Chakdara, Dera Ismail Khan, Bannu, Lakki Marwat, Hangu, and Tall. According to representatives Tufail Ahmed and Farhana Durrani, staff members were let go over phone calls without prior warning, official written explanations, or their earned gratuity.

Key Details

  • Affected Workforce: 291 teachers working across various Khyber Pakhtunkhwa districts.
  • Former Employer: United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR).
  • Current Issues: Service termination via telephone, withholding of gratuity and end-of-service benefits, and severe financial distress affecting families and teachers with medical conditions.
  • Payment Discrepancies: Teachers reported that while funding was linked to dollar-based amounts, their compensation was delivered in small rupee sums over several years.
  • Protest Status: A three-day protest camp was set up outside the UNHCR office in Peshawar, where teachers encountered police intimidation and arrest threats.
