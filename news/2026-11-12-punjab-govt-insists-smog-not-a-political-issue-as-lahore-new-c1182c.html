Punjab govt insists smog ‘not a political issue’ as Lahore, New Delhi gasp for air

Published on November 12, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, Punjab Senior Minister Marriyum Aurangzeb stated that the severe smog blanketing the province is a scientific, environmental, and public health challenge that should not be treated as a political issue. Her remarks came as major cities across Punjab, including Lahore and Multan, experienced hazardous air quality that disrupted road travel and placed Lahore at the top of global pollution rankings.

The Punjab Environment Protection Agency (EPA) reported that pollution from Diwali fireworks in India moved westward into Pakistan, affecting roughly 50 percent of Punjab over a 24-hour period. Additional details regarding joint cross-border solutions are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What happened

Toxic smog significantly degraded air quality across both eastern Pakistan and northern India. On Wednesday night, Lahore recorded an Air Quality Index (AQI) reading of 358, making it the most polluted major city globally, while New Delhi recorded an AQI of 248. The poor visibility forced local authorities to restrict traffic and close key motorway stretches in Punjab.

Key facts

  • Cross-Border Air Movement: According to the EPA, PM2.5 levels at Lahore's Wagah Border station surged by 275 percent due to easterly winds, whereas Safari Park in western Lahore saw only a 15 percent increase.
  • Pollution Trajectory: Computer modeling indicated that Diwali emissions from India could travel to Lahore in approximately two hours, reaching Faisalabad in 12 hours and Multan in 11 hours.
  • Transport Closures: Motorway police shut down the route between Shamkot and Abdul Hakeem and halted heavy traffic on the Sher Shah-Multan section toward Khanewal.
  • Provincial Mitigation Systems: Minister Aurangzeb highlighted that Punjab has introduced automated 24/7 monitoring and five-to-seven-day forecasting systems, claiming local air pollution indicators have improved by 23 to 64 percent.
  • Instrument Overloads in New Delhi: Indian monitoring equipment failed to capture total pollution levels after concentrations exceeded the devices' upper threshold of 1,000 micrograms per cubic meter, leading opposition figures to question official data management.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, while trans-boundary emissions heavily impact regional air quality, local emissions from transport, industry, agriculture, and brick kilns remain significant contributing factors. The Punjab government maintained that continuous surveillance, technological forecasting, and systematic enforcement across key sectors are required to protect public health and manage ongoing environmental risks.
