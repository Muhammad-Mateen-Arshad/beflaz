At SCO moot in India, Musadik Malik declares climate change ‘enemy’ of civilisations

Published on November 21, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan's Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik declared climate change to be an "enemy" of civilisations during a speech at the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Civilisational Dialogue Forum in New Delhi, India. Attending the three-day forum, Malik became the first minister from Pakistan to visit India since May 2023. During his address, he warned that environmental destruction poses a direct threat to regional heritage and human civilisations across South Asia.

Key facts

Several details and recommendations were presented during the conference, according to Dawn:

  • Impact of climate disasters: Malik pointed to severe weather events, including the August 26 floods in Nepal that left over 1,400 dead, as well as the 2022 floods in Pakistan that claimed 1,700 lives and damaged the 4,500-year-old walls of Mohenjo-Daro.
  • Address in Urdu: Although initially intending to speak in English, the minister delivered his speech in Urdu, citing proximity to the tombs of poet Mirza Ghalib and Sufi saint Nizamuddin.
  • Three proposals: Malik put forward three initiatives for the SCO, recommending a digital repository for historical items, a network of regional cultural institutions, and a framework to protect historical sites against climate impacts.
  • Bilateral discussions: On the sidelines of the meeting, Malik held separate discussions with Tajikistan's Minister of Culture Sattoriyon Amonzoda and Iran's culture representative Mohammad Mahdi Imanipour to reaffirm historical and cultural ties.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Malik's trip represents a notable diplomatic visit to India following former Foreign Minister Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari's attendance at an SCO event in May 2023, as well as Indian External Affairs Minister Subrahmanyam Jaishankar's trip to Islamabad two years prior. Malik described the gathering as a vital opportunity for cross-border cultural dialogue. Looking ahead, Pakistan holds the rotating chairmanship of the SCO Council of Heads of State for the 2026–27 term and is scheduled to host the SCO summit in Islamabad in August 2027.
