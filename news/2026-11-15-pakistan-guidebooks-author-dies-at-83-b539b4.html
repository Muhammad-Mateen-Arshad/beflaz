Pakistan guidebooks author dies at 83

Published on November 15, 2026

Isobel Shaw, an Irish-born author known for writing pioneering travel guides about Pakistan, has passed away at the age of 83, according to Dawn. She died on Wednesday, November 11, at her residence in St-Cergue, Switzerland.

What happened

According to Dawn, Shaw moved to Pakistan in 1978 when her husband, Robert Shaw, took a position in Islamabad as a representative for the Ford Foundation. During her three-year stay in the capital, she studied Urdu and co-wrote A Traveller’s Guide to Pakistan alongside Hilary Adamson. Released in Islamabad in 1981, the book stood as one of the first modern guidebooks to the nation.

Shaw authored several subsequent works on the country. Her 1989 publication, Pakistan Handbook, was nominated for the Thomas Cook Travel and Guide Book Awards, while her illustrated guide to Pakistan reached five editions. Partnering with her son, Ben Shaw, she authored the Pakistan Trekking Guide in 1993 and later published Trekking Guide to the Karakoram and Hindu Kush of Northern Pakistan through Vanguard Books in Lahore in 2013. During that same year, at age 70, she revisited Chitral.

Key facts

  • Background and early life: Born Isobel O’Beirne in Meath, Ireland, in 1943, she attended Newnham College, Cambridge, and maintained a home near Geneva starting in 1981.
  • Government consultancy: In the 1990s, she acted as a tourism consultant for the Pakistani government and delivered the primary address at the 1993 Pakistan Tourism Convention in Karachi.
  • Charity and recognition: Following the 2005 earthquake, she conducted talks to raise funds for orthopaedic care for affected survivors. That same year, the Pakistan Society in London recognised her contributions to enhancing public understanding of Pakistan in Great Britain.
  • Survivors and services: She is survived by her three children, nine grandchildren, and one great-granddaughter. Information regarding memorial services was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, fellow travel authors considered Shaw to be the top guidebook writer on Pakistan. Her publications introduced thousands of foreign tourists to the region and played a significant role in promoting international awareness of the country's landscape and culture over several decades.
