Pakistan continuing efforts for peace in Middle East as before: PM Shehbaz

Published on November 04, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif announced during a federal cabinet meeting on Wednesday that Pakistan is continuing its diplomatic initiatives to bring peace to the Middle East. The premier noted that instability across the region has caused global oil prices to rise sharply. In addition to international diplomatic efforts, the prime minister outlined measures regarding domestic energy management, fuel subsidies, and security support for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

Key facts

  • Regional Diplomacy: Pakistan is actively mediating between the United States and Iran, alongside working through the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Turkiye. Recent actions include a trip to Iran by Chief of Defence Forces Field Marshal Asim Munir and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi, with military officials expressing cautious optimism about the peace talks.
  • Fuel Price Controls and Subsidies: To avoid a planned fuel price increase of Rs15, Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik asked oil refineries to absorb the cost using windfall profits. Meanwhile, a Rs28 billion fuel relief initiative launched in September remains active through late December under the supervision of the Intelligence Bureau.
  • Energy Supply Operations: Closure of Iranian border checkpoints has limited liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) imports, driving open-market cylinder prices to double the official rate. In response, the federal government plans to auction imported LPG in lots to encourage competition and subsidize public prices. Officials are also coordinating to meet winter requirements for petrol, gas, LPG, and LNG despite maritime route closures.
  • Financial Package for KP: A proposed Rs160 billion development and support package has been finalised for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, where counterterrorism and security remain top priorities for law enforcement and government authorities.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Pakistan's diplomatic intervention aims to address regional instability that drives up global fuel costs. At home, the federal government is attempting to lessen the impact of high international energy prices on ordinary citizens while addressing border-related supply shortages and security concerns in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
