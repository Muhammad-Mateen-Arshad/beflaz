Pakistan warns against escalation in Iran crisis, urges diplomacy

Published on October 28, 2026

According to a report published by Dawn, Pakistan has warned that the diplomatic and security situation concerning Iran’s nuclear file has sharply deteriorated. Speaking at a United Nations Security Council session on Iran and nuclear non-proliferation, Ambassador Usman Jadoon, Pakistan’s Acting Permanent Representative, appealed to all sides to refrain from escalating actions and to seek a peaceful resolution through dialogue amid ongoing hostilities and Security Council divisions.

What happened

During the Security Council briefing, Pakistan voiced deep concern over mounting international tensions surrounding Iran's nuclear activities. Envoy Usman Jadoon stated that a lack of consensus among world powers has made diplomatic engagement increasingly urgent. Pakistan advised all participating nations to avoid provocative statements, postures, and destabilizing actions that could raise the danger of military confrontation.

Key facts

  • The conflict involves global concerns regarding Iran’s nuclear program and verification under non-proliferation standards.
  • The original 2015 accord offered Tehran relief from economic sanctions in exchange for limits on its nuclear activities, but the United States withdrew from the agreement in 2018 during Donald Trump’s first presidency. Subsequent revival efforts were unsuccessful.
  • Pakistan urged that technical non-compliance issues be managed by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) within its mandate, free from political pressure.
  • Pakistani leadership pointed to a memorandum of understanding signed in Islamabad in June as evidence that diplomatic negotiations can achieve results. Specific terms of this memorandum were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Pakistan acknowledged assistance from international partners, including Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Turkiye, Egypt, and China, in supporting de-escalation efforts.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, Pakistan stressed that reverting to diplomatic mechanisms and enabling technical organizations like the IAEA to conduct their work without hindrance remains essential to preventing wider conflict and restoring regional stability.
