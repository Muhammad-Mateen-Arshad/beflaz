Dar says Pakistan to host next Makkah alliance summit later this month

Published on November 16, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan’s Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar announced at a news briefing in Islamabad that Pakistan is expected to host the next summit of the Makkah Defence Alliance’s primary leadership committee later this month. The briefing took place alongside an Economic Cooperation Organisation (ECO) ministerial meeting, which was also attended by ECO Secretary General Asad Majeed Khan and Information Minister Attaullah Tarar. During the session, Dar shared developments concerning the security alliance and discussed Pakistan's ongoing diplomatic engagements regarding US-Iran tensions.

Key facts

  • The Makkah Joint Defence Agreement was formed on August 7 by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye, stipulating that an assault on any member country is regarded as an assault on all three.
  • The alliance's governing body, the Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC), is composed of foreign ministers, defence ministers, and senior military personnel from each participating state. Exact dates for the upcoming gathering: Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The alliance is establishing its infrastructure, including a permanent secretariat and secretary general's office in Riyadh, along with a tri-nation working group finalizing operational procedures.
  • Dar noted that the accord adheres to Article 51 of the United Nations Charter on collective self-defence and indicated that additional nations may be allowed to join after approximately one year.
  • Regarding US-Iran relations, Dar met with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, stating Iran expedited a plan to open the Strait of Hormuz from a seven-day framework to a two-day timetable.
  • The Strait of Hormuz initiative is derived from the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding signed by Iran and the United States on June 18.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Dar remarked that regions benefit most from establishing their own security frameworks, describing the Makkah pact as a potential starting point for a localized security architecture. Additionally, Pakistan reaffirmed its stance of following international law and United Nations Security Council resolutions while continuing diplomatic initiatives aimed at resolving the confrontation between the United States and Iran peacefully.
