Turkiye, Qatar sign defence agreement: Turkish presidency

Published on November 13, 2026

What happened

Turkiye and Qatar signed a mutual defence agreement on Friday following a meeting in Istanbul between Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, according to Dawn. The Qatari ruler travelled to Istanbul after attending the United Nations COP31 climate summit located in southern Turkiye.

Key facts

Statements from the Turkish presidency and Qatar's foreign ministry outlined the primary aspects of the meeting and agreement:

  • President Erdogan noted that the defence agreement creates a new dimension in the brotherly ties between both nations and affirmed that cooperation within the defence industry would be expanded.
  • Specific provisions and structural details of the agreement were not disclosed in the official statement. Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • A joint communique issued after a session of the Qatar-Turkiye Supreme Strategic Committee stated that the deal aims to reinforce collective defence, support regional stability, and establish a unified posture against any external armed attack on either party.
  • Beyond defence matters, the leaders discussed Israel's attacks on Gaza, the humanitarian situation in the Palestinian territory, Syria normalisation efforts, and recent developments in Libya.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the agreement reinforces ongoing efforts between Turkiye and Qatar to prevent renewed conflict in the Gulf region while encouraging dialogue and peace. The development comes alongside wider regional security efforts. In August, Turkiye, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan entered into the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement, which dictates that an attack on one signatory represents an attack on all. Turkiye's parliament ratified the Makkah pact last week, allowing Ankara to send military assistance to Saudi Arabia as it deals with attacks from Yemen's Houthis.
