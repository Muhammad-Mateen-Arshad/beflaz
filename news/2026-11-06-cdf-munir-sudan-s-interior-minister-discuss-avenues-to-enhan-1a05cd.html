CDF Munir, Sudan's interior minister discuss avenues to enhance bilateral cooperation: ISPR

Published on November 06, 2026

Sudan’s Interior Minister, Lieutenant General Babiker Samra Mustafa Ali, held high-level discussions with Pakistani civil and military leadership during an official visit to Pakistan, according to reports by Dawn. The visit included separate engagements with top defense officials, diplomatic representatives, and the prime minister aimed at strengthening bilateral ties.

What happened

According to Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR), Lt Gen Ali met Chief of Defence Forces and Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir on Friday at General Headquarters in Rawalpindi. The two leaders reviewed regional security dynamics, counterterrorism efforts, and institutional cooperation. On the same day, the Sudanese minister conferred with Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in a meeting attended by Minister of State for Interior Tallal Chaudhry. These talks followed a Thursday meeting with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif at the PM House.

Key facts

  • Pakistan reaffirmed its steadfast commitment to Sudan's sovereignty, unity, and territorial integrity during discussions with DPM Dar.
  • Prime Minister Sharif highlighted potential opportunities to expand bilateral trade and investment across areas such as defence, agriculture, and education.
  • Lt Gen Ali expressed gratitude to Pakistan for providing medical and humanitarian assistance, as well as its support on international forums.
  • Specific agreements or formal deadlines resulting from the meetings were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

The diplomatic meetings occurred against the backdrop of severe instability in Sudan following hostilities that began in 2023 between the Sudanese military and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces over force integration plans, Dawn reported. The crisis has created the world's largest displacement situation, with around 8.6 million people displaced internally and over 4.6 million fleeing across borders. Furthermore, while more than 5.2 million individuals have returned to their home areas, many find essential infrastructure and basic services severely destroyed.
