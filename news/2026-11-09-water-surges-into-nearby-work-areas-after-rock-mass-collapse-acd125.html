Water surges into nearby work areas after rock mass collapse at Tarbela Dam

Published on November 09, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, a weak rock mass collapsed on Monday during excavation at the 2nd Bench Connecting Tunnel of the Tarbela Dam 5th Extension Project (T-5) in Swabi. The collapse allowed water from an intake pit to surge into a connecting tunnel and an under-construction powerhouse area. Officials from the Water and Power Development Authority (Wapda) declared an emergency, suspended construction, and safely evacuated all workers to designated secure zones. No injuries or deaths were reported.

Key facts

  • Wapda stated that water from the intake pit has been fully drained and flow to downstream work areas has ceased. Dewatering efforts at the powerhouse site are currently underway.
  • Senior Wapda officials, project engineers, and the primary contractor are evaluating the site to address operational challenges.
  • The authority refuted online rumours regarding a collapse of the T-5 tunnel, clarifying that no damage occurred to the powerhouse, main tunnel, tailrace culvert, or switchyard.
  • Swabi Deputy Commissioner Nasarullah Khan announced that unavoidable circumstances required additional water releases from the dam, raising water levels in the Indus River. He urged residents to avoid river activities such as swimming and boating, and to check official channels for verified updates.
  • A senior district administration official confirmed there is no immediate flood threat to areas downstream of Tarbela.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the T-5 project has already faced significant operational and financial disruptions. In July, a government inquiry found Wapda, the contractor, and the consultant responsible for an earlier cofferdam failure caused by unauthorized design changes. That previous event caused a cost escalation exceeding 285 percent—increasing the budget from Rs82 billion to Rs317 billion—and delayed completion by at least two years, moving the expected end date from 2026 to late June 2028. The initiative is backed by $700 million in loans provided by the World Bank and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank.
