Trump admin warns US air travellers of another messy day

Published on November 15, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, widespread United States air travel disruptions caused by a nationwide no-takeoff order on Friday are expected to extend into Monday. Although Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) leadership initially expected operations to recover by Sunday evening, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy indicated that full normalization would take longer as airlines work to re-position displaced aircraft and flight crews.

Key facts

Dawn reported several critical details regarding the air travel failure and its aftermath:

  • The FAA implemented a nationwide ground stop on Friday lasting nearly five hours, making it the longest nationwide halt to US air travel since the September 11, 2001 attacks.
  • According to flight tracking site FlightAware, Friday saw approximately 9,200 flight cancellations and 7,500 delays, followed by over 6,400 cancellations and 8,000 delays on Saturday, and 1,460 cancellations by Sunday morning.
  • The White House convened an emergency Situation Room meeting on Friday to evaluate potential causes, including assessing if the issue was a cyberattack.
  • Officials traced the technical failure to an installation issue involving contractor L3Harris while upgrading old copper lines to high-speed fiber cables, which flooded routers with communication errors.
  • The union representing FAA safety employees stated its technicians were not involved in the upgrade, while L3Harris indicated it is assisting in the official investigation.

Why it matters

The incident has renewed political debate over the reliability of aging air traffic control infrastructure in the US. President Donald Trump blamed the previous administration for FAA issues and called on Congress to approve funds for modernizing equipment. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy rejected calls for his resignation, pointing out that he inherited legacy infrastructure, including radar systems dating back to the 1960s.
