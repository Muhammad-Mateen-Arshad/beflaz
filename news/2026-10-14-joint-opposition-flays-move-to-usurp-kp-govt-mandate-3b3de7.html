Joint opposition flays move to ‘usurp KP govt mandate’

Published on October 14, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, opposition parliamentary parties convened a joint session at Parliament House in Islamabad on Tuesday to condemn the recent imposition of emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). The opposition strongly denounced the removal of the province's elected government and the subsequent arrests of political figures and activists. In a joint declaration released after the session, participants demanded an immediate end to governor's rule and urged authorities to respect the democratic mandate of the people of KP, characterizing October 11 as a black day in the country's democratic history.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the meeting statement outlined several core points:

  • Leadership present: The session was chaired by National Assembly Opposition Leader Mehmood Khan Achakzai and attended by Senate Opposition Leader Allama Raja Nasir Abbas, PTI interim chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan, JUI-F chief Maulana Fazlur Rehman, and PTI secretary general Salman Akram Raja.
  • Detentions: Detainees include PTI leaders such as KP Chief Minister Sohail Afridi along with numerous political workers.
  • Constitutional demands: Meeting participants demanded that the basic rights of all arrested political figures be protected and constitutional supremacy be sustained.
  • Status of talks: Maulana Fazlur Rehman informed leaders that the steps in KP were executed despite the completion of prior negotiations with the government.
  • Official reasons: Further background regarding the federal government's specific grounds for declaring emergency rule was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the opposition highlighted what it characterized as double standards in the government's approach toward KP and Balochistan. The attendees asserted that law and order or constitutional problems across all provinces must be addressed through consistent, uniform principles rather than dismantling elected governments due to political disputes. Furthermore, the opposition urged the federal government to replace unilateral actions with a comprehensive, national strategy against terrorism formulated alongside key stakeholders, while providing KP with necessary resources.
