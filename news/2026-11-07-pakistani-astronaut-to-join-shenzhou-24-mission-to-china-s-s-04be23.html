Pakistani astronaut to join Shenzhou-24 mission to China's space station

Published on November 07, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn citing Chinese space authorities, a Pakistani astronaut will join China's Shenzhou-24 mission to the Tiangong space station. This event will mark the first time a foreign national travels to the orbiting station. The Pakistani crew member will serve as a payload specialist for a short orbital flight before returning to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, which docked at the space station in May.

The China National Space Administration did not confirm an exact departure date, stating only that the launch is expected by the end of the year. However, Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal previously announced in October that the mission would take place on November 25. Which of the trained candidates will take the final slot on the crew is not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Earlier in April, astronauts Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud were selected for training in China, receiving praise from Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif.

Key facts

  • In February 2025, Pakistan's space agency Suparco signed a cooperation agreement with Chinese space officials to train two Pakistani candidates.
  • The agreement stipulated that one trained Pakistani astronaut would participate in a mission aboard Beijing's Tiangong space station.
  • China has made significant investments in its space sector to compete with the United States, targeting a crewed Moon landing by 2030.
  • China serves as Pakistan's largest trading partner and a primary source of foreign investment.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, this mission reflects the strategic, long-standing relationship between Pakistan and China. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif noted that the initiative is a national milestone that will deepen bilateral ties. Additionally, Beijing has actively pursued international partnerships for its expanding space programme after being excluded from the International Space Station in 2011, when US legislation banned NASA from working alongside Chinese space organisations.
