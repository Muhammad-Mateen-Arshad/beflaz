Trump intends to secure ‘permanent deal’ with Iran

Published on November 22, 2026

United States President Donald Trump has stated his intention to establish a permanent agreement with Iran to ensure Tehran is permanently barred from acquiring nuclear weapons, according to a report published by Dawn.

What happened

Speaking in an interview with Fox News, President Trump explained that his approach aims to prevent any future American administration from allowing Iran to obtain nuclear weapons, citing what he described as repeated failures by Tehran to honor prior agreements. In response, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated through state media that Tehran remains open to diplomatic channels via mediators. He emphasized that Iran seeks a dignified agreement that safeguards national interests and prevents further military conflict.

Key facts

  • US Stance: President Trump stated that his administration is seeking a lasting deal and noted that while current Iranian leaders remain challenging to negotiate with, they are relatively more reasonable than previous leadership groups.
  • Iranian Conditions: Foreign Minister Araghchi stated Tehran needs guarantees of US seriousness before advancing talks, adding that keeping highly enriched uranium within Iran remains a firm red line.
  • International Doctrine: Araghchi highlighted that both Russia and China oppose any changes to Iran's nuclear doctrine.
  • Diplomatic Discussions: US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held talks with senior Qatari officials in Berlin to evaluate mediation efforts between Washington and Tehran.
  • Maritime Escalation: Security in the Strait of Hormuz deteriorated as two tankers caught fire, with one vessel hit by an unidentified projectile resulting in no reported casualties. Iranian state media also reported that Iran shot down a US drone over Qeshm Island.

Why it matters

The ongoing regional friction has disrupted international shipping, increased global energy prices, and heightened instability across the Middle East. Although Qatar continues to mediate discussions, resolving maritime restrictions and security risks in the Strait of Hormuz depends on achieving mutual political assurances. Detailed timelines for formal treaty negotiations were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
