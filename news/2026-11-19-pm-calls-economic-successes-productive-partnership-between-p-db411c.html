PM calls economic successes ‘productive partnership’ between political, military hierarchy

Published on November 19, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif stated on Thursday that Pakistan's recent economic progress is the result of an effective collaboration between the political leadership and the military hierarchy. Addressing an event organized by the American Business Council of Pakistan, the premier emphasized that the recent economic stability relied heavily on this shared effort.

During his speech, Sharif declined to take sole credit for the achievements, attributing the success to his political team and key military partners, including Chief of Defence Forces Field Marshal Asim Munir and his team. He stated that a productive partnership of this kind between civilian leadership and the armed forces had never occurred before in the nation's history, adding that Pakistan would have transformed much sooner had such unity existed in the past.

Key facts

  • Civil-military partnership: Prime Minister Sharif described the current coordination between political figures and the military as unprecedented in Pakistan.
  • Historical context: The prime minister noted that the nation confronted a severe risk of default in 2023 before moving toward macroeconomic stability. Specific figures cited during his speech were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Energy challenges: Pakistan imports over 90 percent of its energy requirements, leaving its energy position fragile, yet it avoided fuel station queues and public panic.
  • Public support: To assist citizens affected by economic pressures and the global Gulf crisis, the government established a subsidy program called the Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the prime minister highlighted that Pakistan has sustained stability despite broader international disruption caused by the Gulf crisis, which affected major global economies. While acknowledging ongoing vulnerabilities regarding energy imports, Sharif pointed out that national stability was maintained through coordinated governance, flexibility in the national interest, and the sacrifices of the general public.
