Microsoft offers voluntary exit packages to 60,000 staff

Published on October 26, 2026

What happened

Microsoft has offered voluntary exit packages to roughly 60,000 employees, according to a report by Dawn. The voluntary buyout offer affects about 27 percent—or roughly a quarter—of the company's workforce as the firm seeks to reduce expenses while expanding its investments in artificial intelligence. The package is available to eligible staff members at the vice president level and below across selected teams in the United States, Canada, India, and Britain. However, employees working in sales, marketing, and data centres are excluded from the offer.

Key facts

According to details published by Dawn, key aspects of the buyout and Microsoft's current operations include:

  • This initiative marks Microsoft's second voluntary exit offer this year, following an April offer where around seven percent of its US staff were given early retirement options.
  • Company regulatory filings show that Microsoft's global headcount stood at 223,000 at the end of June, down from 228,000 at the end of 2025.
  • The buyout offer follows earlier cost-cutting measures, including July job cuts of nearly 5,000 positions that focused heavily on the Xbox gaming unit.
  • Amy Coleman, an executive vice president and chief people officer at Microsoft, communicated to staff via memo that the offer aims to provide flexibility and options for employees looking to transition.
  • Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta are collectively on track to spend approximately $700 billion this year on AI computing infrastructure, chips, and data centres.
  • Microsoft reported spending $41 billion between April and June, with anticipated spending of $50 billion between July and September, while lowering its total 2026 forecast to $175 billion.

Why it matters

As detailed by Dawn, Microsoft's workforce restructuring comes amid widespread job cuts across the technology sector as companies pivot heavily toward AI spending. Other major tech firms have announced significant staff reductions, including Amazon cutting 16,000 jobs in January, Meta reducing 10 percent of its workforce in May, Uber laying off around 10 percent of its staff in September, alongside smaller cuts at Netflix and eBay. These industry-wide workforce shifts occur even as major players pour hundreds of billions of dollars into specialized AI hardware and computing assets.
