Pezeshkian urges 'regional unity' in meeting with Makkah pact countries in Tehran

Published on October 26, 2026

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian called for regional cohesion during a meeting in Tehran with representatives from Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye, according to a report by Dawn. The quadrilateral discussions focused on regional stability, security cooperation, and addressing the consequences of recent US and Israeli military actions.

What happened

According to Dawn and Iranian state media, President Pezeshkian urged Islamic nations to adopt a unified stance. Attendees included Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei, Pakistani Chief of Defence Forces Field Marshal Asim Munir, and Pakistani Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi. Representatives from Saudi Arabia and Turkiye—both members of the Makkah defence pact alongside Pakistan—also participated in the security talks.

Key facts

  • Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei stated that top agenda items included US-Israeli aggression against Iran, Israeli policies in Palestine and Lebanon, and promoting self-reliant regional security in West Asia.
  • The meeting proposal originated during Field Marshal Munir’s visit to Tehran the previous week. Specific details regarding Iran's seven-point negotiation proposal mentioned during earlier discussions were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Saudi Deputy Minister for Public Diplomacy Rayed Krimly confirmed the gathering, describing it as an effort by Makkah Defence Alliance members to promote de-escalation, dialogue, and support mediation efforts led by Qatar and Pakistan.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that this gathering marks the first high-level contact between Iran and all signatories of the August Makkah defence pact—under which an armed attack on one member is treated as an attack on all—since conflict deepened in the region. Hostilities expanded following US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February during nuclear negotiations. Although an April ceasefire ended heavy initial bombardment, tensions persist over the Strait of Hormuz, alongside spiralling Houthi attacks and fighting involving Saudi Arabia in Yemen.
