US, China, Russia sign ‘peace declaration’ after historic Trump, Xi, Putin meeting

Published on November 19, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Chinese President Xi Jinping, US President Donald Trump, and Russian President Vladimir Putin signed a joint "Declaration toward Peace" following an unprecedented summit in Shenzhen, China. Held at Kylin Villa on the sidelines of an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, the closed-door meeting lasted one hour and 20 minutes after brief opening statements by the leaders.

Key facts

  • Nuclear security: The three leaders reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and pledged to conduct future dialogues focused on lowering nuclear risks and halting weapon proliferation.
  • Global institutions: The joint statement stressed respect for the immunity of state officials, stating that the International Criminal Court must not abuse its jurisdiction. The countries also committed to reforming the United Nations.
  • Energy cooperation: The leaders agreed to boost the production and supply of refined petroleum products to maintain stable global energy flows.
  • Bilateral talks: Prior to the three-way summit, Putin and Xi held their 50th meeting. Trump met separately with Putin—their first meeting since August 2025—and later held discussions with Xi.

Specific timelines and concrete steps for reforming the UN were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the trilateral summit took place amid complex international conditions, including the ongoing war in Ukraine, conflict in the Middle East, and trade friction surrounding exports of advanced microchips. The unprecedented joint gathering created anxiety among smaller nations, which fear their security interests could be ignored if the three powers attempt to split the globe into spheres of influence. Despite signing the declaration, deep geopolitical and economic differences persist among the three nations.
