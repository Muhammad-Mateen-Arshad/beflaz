KP plans to set up faceless ATCs, spend Rs70bn on CTD and police in a year as part of counterterrorism initiatives

Published on November 08, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, the administrative setup in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) has outlined plans to set up faceless anti-terrorism courts and spend Rs70 billion over a year to reinforce the province's police force and Counter-Terrorism Department (CTD). The initiatives were announced during a televised briefing attended by KP Governor Faisal Karim Kundi and presented by KP Home Secretary Amjad Ali Khan.

What happened

To address security concerns and witness intimidation in terrorism cases, the KP administration submitted a legal amendment to establish faceless anti-terrorism courts. The measure is intended to protect witnesses, prosecutors, and judicial officers. Specific implementation deadlines for the new courts were not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Alongside judicial changes, the province plans to allocate significant annual funding to boost law enforcement equipment, surveillance, and infrastructure.

Key facts

  • Funding Allocation: Out of the Rs70 billion annual budget, Rs59.8 billion is designated for the police (Rs33.4 billion for infrastructure and Rs26.4 billion for procurement), while Rs10.3 billion is assigned to the CTD (Rs5.4 billion for infrastructure and Rs4.9 billion for procurement).
  • Personnel and Casualties: The police force currently faces a shortage of over 10,000 personnel. Over the past two to three years, conflict in the region resulted in 1,003 armed forces personnel martyred, 627 police deaths, and 808 civilian casualties.
  • Infrastructure Upgrades: Plans include fortifying sensitive police posts, expanding the Safe City project, deploying anti-drone systems in Dera Ismail Khan, Lakki Marwat, and Bannu, and making District Nerve Centres functional in ten districts.
  • Capacity Targets: The administration plans to raise police surveillance capability from 15 percent to 60 percent, increase counter-aerial threat capacity from 1 percent to 50 percent, and boost air strike capability from 18 percent to 50 percent.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the initiatives aim to resolve long-standing operational gaps and political disconnects in KP. Khan noted a past lack of political ownership in counterterrorism efforts, highlighting that the federal government imposed governor's rule and an emergency in KP on Oct 11 over provincial disengagement. The province also faced severe resource deficits, including merged districts receiving only 3.2 percent of their budget for security. The updated strategy combines modern technology training, satellite communication deployment, public outreach through jirgas and liaison committees, and scaled-up intelligence-based operations to counter terrorism.
