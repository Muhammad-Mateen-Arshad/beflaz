Govt reduces petrol price by Re1 per litre, diesel by Rs2 per litre

Published on November 26, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, the government has lowered the price of petrol by Re1 per litre and high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs2 per litre, based on an official notification from the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra).

Following this price adjustment, petrol will retail at Rs313 per litre and HSD at Rs346 per litre starting Friday, Nov 27. The recent reductions place prices for both fuels below their July 18 levels, when petrol was priced at Rs316.15 per litre and HSD stood at Rs354.35 per litre.

Key facts

  • The government continues to collect Rs114 per litre in duties and taxes on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel.
  • Under a 30-day agreement announced on Nov 2, oil refineries are absorbing a combined financial impact of Rs15 billion for the petroleum levy, providing a Rs14.71 per litre price reduction for petrol consumers.
  • Fuel rates previously reached peak levels on April 3, with petrol touching Rs458.41 per litre and HSD reaching Rs520.35 per litre amid regional conflicts in the Middle East.
  • Ogra now revises fuel prices on a near-daily basis based on international market trends, following a cabinet and prime minister decision that replaced the weekly pricing system used earlier in the year.
  • The Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme, introduced on Sept 17, offers targeted subsidies for owners of motorcycles, rickshaws, and vehicles under 800cc capacity.
  • Austerity measures in response to international energy fluctuations require markets to close by 9pm and cut fuel allocations for official vehicles by 50 percent for three months.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, changes in petrol prices primarily affect middle- and lower-middle-class populations who use two-wheelers, rickshaws, and smaller personal vehicles. Diesel price adjustments have a broad economic impact across the general public, as the fuel is heavily utilized in commercial transport, large generators, and power plants.

Petrol and HSD represent the primary source of petroleum revenue in the country, with combined monthly sales ranging between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes, compared to a monthly demand of 10,000 tonnes for kerosene.
