Man buried under rubble rescued alive 2 days after building collapse in Karachi’s Baloch Colony

Published on November 27, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, a 28-year-old man named Muhammad Adil was rescued alive on Friday after spending 54 hours trapped under the rubble of a collapsed building near Baloch Colony in Karachi. The under-construction six-storey structure, reportedly built illegally on a residential plot, collapsed on Wednesday. Rescue 1122 personnel described the rescue as a major breakthrough and confirmed that search operations remain ongoing for additional individuals feared trapped beneath the debris.

Key facts

Details reported by Dawn regarding the disaster and official responses include:

  • The death toll has reached eight following the recovery of four more bodies on Friday, while 14 people were injured during the initial collapse.
  • According to reports submitted by relatives at the site, 11 individuals remain missing.
  • Rescue teams are utilizing heavy machinery alongside careful manual excavation when bodies are spotted, though congested streets have complicated the movement of equipment.
  • Sindh Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah ordered a full inquiry and directed police to arrest the building's owner, Salman Tahir, and builder, Zeeshan.
  • A First Information Report was registered at Tipu Sultan police station against the owners, builder, architect, structural engineer, and contractors over alleged use of sub-standard materials and collusion.
  • Administrative actions led to the removal of the Sindh Local Government Secretary, the East Deputy Commissioner, and the SSP East, as well as the suspension of 16 Sindh Building Control Authority officials.
  • Authorities have sealed 14 illegal buildings in KAEHS, which are scheduled to be demolished in a subsequent phase.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the building failure has exposed severe regulatory concerns over unauthorized construction on residential land in Karachi. The incident has prompted immediate government intervention, resulting in high-level suspensions, criminal charges against developers, and citywide enforcement measures against unsafe and illegal developments.
