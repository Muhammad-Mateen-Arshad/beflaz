Pakistan slams Afghan Taliban for 'shifting blame', 'persistent refusal' to address terrorism issues

Published on October 31, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistan's Ministry of Information has issued a sharp rebuke against the Afghan Taliban, accusing the regime of failing to tackle terrorism originating from Afghan territory. The official response followed statements by Afghan Taliban spokesperson Zabihullah Mujahid, who told Hurriyat Radio that Islamabad's policies were obstructing conflict resolution and that Pakistan lacked the readiness for peaceful dialogue.

In its statement, the Pakistani ministry rejected the spokesperson's remarks as a misguided effort to shift blame. Islamabad stressed that the main obstacle to normal bilateral relations is Kabul's persistent refusal to eliminate terrorist sanctuaries. Pakistani officials emphasized that national security is non-negotiable and asserted that the country reserves the right under international law to take all necessary measures to protect its citizens from external threats.

Key facts

  • Pakistan has repeatedly requested the Taliban leadership to dismantle militant hideouts, particularly those associated with the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP).
  • Prior diplomatic initiatives, including talks facilitated by Qatar and Turkiye last year, as well as efforts involving China this year, ended without achieving a breakthrough.
  • Specific details regarding scheduled future talks were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The Pakistani government declared that terrorism is a red line and that inaction by the Afghan administration will not compromise Pakistan's security.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, the diplomatic friction coincides with escalating military confrontations along the border. In September, Pakistan conducted calibrated airstrikes in border areas after suffering terrorist attacks in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. This was followed by cross-border drone incursions, which Pakistan shot down, alongside additional rounds of retaliatory strikes.

Clashes continued into October along the border in Kurram and Angoor Adda in Lower South Waziristan due to unprovoked firing from the Afghan side. Most recently, on Tuesday, Afghan security forces shelled border posts and residential areas in Angoor Adda, killing one resident and wounding five members of a single family.
