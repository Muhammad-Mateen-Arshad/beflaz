CDA finalises revised PC-I for Islamabad cricket stadium at Rs16.7bn

Published on November 16, 2026

The Capital Development Authority (CDA) has finalized a revised PC-I proposal for the Islamabad Cricket Stadium, raising the project's estimated budget from Rs11.9 billion to Rs16.7 billion, according to a report by Dawn. The updated plan covers two construction phases, encompassing structural work, electrical installations, and finishing details. Groundbreaking for the first phase is expected shortly, with officials aiming to complete the venue within one year.

What happened

According to CDA officials speaking to Dawn, the budget increased after incorporating critical features missing from the previous unapproved plan. The modified proposal will soon be presented to the CDA Development Working Party for formal approval. Bidding for the initial structural phase and associated road network has already concluded, allowing initial work to commence prior to the formal approval of the revised PC-I.

Key facts

  • Cost and scope expansion: The budget increased to Rs16.7 billion to cover grey structure construction, finishing, lighting, security cameras, ground and pitch preparation, as well as heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning systems.
  • Increased seating: Planned seating capacity was expanded from 25,000 to 35,000 spectators, making it the largest cricket venue in Pakistan. It is modeled as a replica of the Dubai Cricket Stadium.
  • Phased execution: Phase-I covers the grey structure and road infrastructure, targeted for completion within six months. Phase-II tendering will begin after PC-I approval. Exact dates for the Phase-II tender are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Bidding details: A joint venture of Habib-ZKB-EA earlier submitted a bid of Rs8.9 billion, which was later lowered to Rs8.5 billion following a Rs400 million rebate.
  • Site zoning: Located near Sector D-12 in Zone III at the foothills of the Margalla Hills. While local landowners face building bans in Zone III, the CDA maintains that sports and recreational facilities are permitted under regulations.

Why it matters

A Pakistan Cricket Board official indicated that completing the venue on schedule could allow Islamabad to host matches during the ICC Women's T20 World Cup in 2028. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi also recently stated that the project's groundbreaking would take place within 10 days. Dawn notes that a previous attempt by the PCB to build a stadium at Shakarparian was halted by Supreme Court directions, leading the CDA to execute this project directly.
