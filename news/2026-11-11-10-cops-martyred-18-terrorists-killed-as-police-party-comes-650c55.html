10 cops martyred, 18 terrorists killed as police party comes under attack in KP's Kohat

Published on November 11, 2026

What happened

According to reports published by Dawn, ten police officers were killed and 18 terrorists died during a security incident in the Darabukach locality near Kohat city, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. The clash took place on Wednesday when a joint team of police and civil administration personnel was targeted following an enforcement operation against illegal placer gold mining. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Police stated that an armed group, designated by state authorities as Fitna al Khawarij, carried out the attack in coordination with criminal elements. While the police public relations director stated the team was ambushed on its return journey, a separate statement from Governor Faisal Karim Kundi indicated the assault occurred during the operation itself.

Key facts

  • Ten members of the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Police, including Deputy Superintendent of Police Haidar Zaman, lost their lives in the fighting. Additional individual victim details are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Eighteen attackers were killed during the response by police forces.
  • A spokesperson for Rescue 1122 confirmed that 12 injured officers were transported to a hospital following the shooting.
  • The incident occurred shortly after the Kohat deputy commissioner issued administrative orders placing a 60-day ban on parking excavators near specific placer gold mining blocks and restricting the open sale of petroleum products used in unauthorized extraction.
  • Governor Kundi and Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry publicly condemned the violent act, offered condolences, and requested comprehensive reports on the ambush.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, police representatives asserted that the joint attack reveals an organized alliance linking local criminal networks, external backing, and militant groups. The violence reflects wider security challenges in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, where several police personnel have been targeted in recent attacks across districts such as Mohmand, Bajaur, and North Waziristan. Additionally, the region has experienced administrative changes, remaining under governor's rule and emergency measures since October 11 following federal actions regarding counterterrorism engagement in the province.
