Peshawar to host opening ceremony, inaugural match for 12th edition of PSL: PCB chief

Published on November 23, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) Chairman Mohsin Naqvi announced that Peshawar will host the opening ceremony and inaugural match for the 12th edition of the Pakistan Super League (PSL). Speaking at a press conference alongside Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi, Naqvi stated that multiple fixtures will take place in the provincial capital, with the full tournament schedule to be decided later. During the event, Governor Kundi also announced that his administration will restore the original name of Arbab Niaz Stadium, reversing a decision made in February 2025 by the former PTI provincial government to rename it after Imran Khan.

What happened

The PCB confirmed that Peshawar will welcome PSL matches for the first time since the tournament's inception in 2016. Naqvi noted that bringing the league to the city fulfilled a long-standing request from fans and Peshawar Zalmi owner Javed Afridi. Alongside the PSL announcement, Naqvi outlined broader sports initiatives, including a planned week-long tournament featuring the national team, Pakistan Shaheens, and U-19 players, as well as plans to establish a cricket academy serving tribal districts.

Key facts

  • Tournament dates: The 12th edition of the PSL is scheduled to run from March 18 to May 2, 2027.
  • Venue expansion: Peshawar becomes the sixth official venue added to the PSL circuit.
  • Stadium history: Arbab Niaz Stadium was constructed in the 1980s by former federal sports minister Arbab Niaz. Its original name will be officially restored within days.
  • Security and development funding: Governor Kundi stated that 70 percent of a Rs160 billion federal allocation will be directed toward 12 districts dealing with security challenges, with the remaining 30 percent distributed across the rest of the province.

Why it matters

According to statements from PCB leadership, bringing the PSL to Peshawar fulfills a long-held ambition for local supporters who previously had to travel outside their home city to watch league matches. Naqvi, who also serves as interior minister, noted that law and order conditions in the province are improving, enabling international-level sports to return despite earlier security and administrative delays. Governor Kundi emphasized that restoring the stadium's historic name respects local heritage while his administration continues to focus on regional security, anti-encroachment actions, and environmental regulations.
