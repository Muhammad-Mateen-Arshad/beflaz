PHC removes pleas against emergency, detentions from cause lists

Published on November 08, 2026

According to Dawn, the Peshawar High Court (PHC) has removed around 105 petitions from its cause lists scheduled for Nov 10. The decision follows compliance by the high court's administrative committee with a restraining order issued by the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC). Under the ruling, petitions filed under Article 199 of the Constitution on or after Oct 12 will not proceed, and affected petitioners have been instructed to approach the appropriate forum.

What happened

The deletion of the petitions has halted proceedings on multiple high-profile legal challenges as well as routine matters. Among the primary cases removed were petitions challenging the declaration of emergency and governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), filed by political figures including PTI chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan, the KP Bar Council, and leaders of other political groups. Further petitions regarding the alleged unlawful detentions of dysfunctional Chief Minister Sohail Afridi, KP Assembly Speaker Babar Saleem Swati, and various lawmakers and party workers were also delisted.

Key facts

  • The delisted items included challenges to detentions under public order regulations, anti-encroachment actions, petitions concerning missing persons, and pleas by Afghan nationals regarding official repatriation measures.
  • A petition filed by KP Assembly Deputy Speaker Suriya Bibi concerning the sealing of her official residence was also removed from the cause list.
  • The FCC stated on Nov 5 that Article 199 proceedings filed on or after Oct 12 are suspended under Article 245 Clause 4, following an Oct 11 federal order deploying armed forces to assist civil power in KP.
  • The FCC directed KP Advocate General Sanaullah Khan to submit a report within seven days detailing regions where military aid is no longer required.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, legal organizations have expressed concern over the impact of the court order on constitutional protections. Masooma Mir, speaking for the Insaf Lawyers Forum (ILF), stated that court orders should not restrict citizens' access to justice or weaken constitutional guarantees. The ILF described the limitation of constitutional remedies for the 45 million inhabitants of KP as a significant blow to judicial independence and called upon major lawyer associations to address the situation.
