Maliha Ali takes bronze at MMA worlds as Pakistan appeals semi-final decision

Published on November 06, 2026

Pakistani fighter Maliha Ali secured a bronze medal at the IMMAF World Championships in Tbilisi, Georgia, while her national federation filed a formal appeal over her semi-final defeat, according to a report by Dawn. Competing through severe physical injuries—including a fractured toe, a partially dislocated jaw, and a severely bruised eyelid—Maliha reached the featherweight (65.8kg) semi-finals before losing via a split decision to Russia's Polina Baldina.

What happened

Maliha stood with her arms raised after the final bell, believing she had won the closely contested bout. However, the three judging scorecards varied significantly: one judge scored the match 30-27 for Baldina, another 29-28 for Maliha, and the final judge gave a 29-28 score in favor of Baldina. The decision granted the victory to Baldina, leaving Maliha with a bronze medal while the Pakistan MMA Federation formally challenged the outcome.

Key facts

  • Maliha advanced to the semi-finals by defeating European champion Veronika Drozdova of Russia by split decision and Mexico's Andrea Gabriela Guzman Salazar by unanimous decision.
  • Pakistan MMA Federation President Omar Ahmed described the decision to Dawn as a clear victory for Maliha, stating that they were "robbed."
  • The federation submitted an official letter to the Head of the IMMAF Refereeing and Judging Commission requesting a round-by-round score breakdown, an explanation of the conflicting scorecards, and a face-to-face technical review.
  • Maliha's twin sister, Manesha Ali, a former IMMAF World Cup gold medalist, also lost via a narrow split decision in the lightweight (70.3kg) quarter-finals to Poland's Martyna Caruk.
  • The formal appeal regarding Maliha's semi-final match remains pending.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the appeal brings attention to concerns surrounding judging consistency and panel representation. Omar Ahmed noted that Pakistan suffered a similar split-decision semi-final loss at the previous year's event, creating uncertainty regarding officiating standards. Ahmed also alleged that former Soviet states dominate the refereeing panel and show mutual favoritism. He highlighted that while Russia brought a multi-million dollar budget and multiple competitors per division, Pakistan had zero officials on the judging panel.
