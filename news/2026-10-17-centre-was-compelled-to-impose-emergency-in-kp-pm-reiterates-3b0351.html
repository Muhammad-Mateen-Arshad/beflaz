Centre was compelled to impose emergency in KP, PM reiterates

Published on October 17, 2026

According to Dawn, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has reaffirmed that the central government felt forced to implement governor’s rule and emergency measures in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). The premier made the statements while visiting Gujranwala to express condolences over the passing of former lawmaker Mahmood Bashir Virk. He attributed the central intervention to a lack of support from the provincial leadership in tackling terrorism in the region.

What happened

According to Dawn, the federal administration declared an emergency and governor’s rule in KP on Oct 11, citing the provincial leadership's deliberate refusal to join efforts against terrorism. Parliament subsequently endorsed the measures following opposition demonstrations. PM Shehbaz explained that central officials made several efforts to reach a political settlement, including extensive talks involving government representatives, the PPP, and the opposition. However, an extended negotiation session on Oct 3 ended without a breakthrough.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the main elements surrounding the emergency declaration include:

  • The prime minister engaged in direct discussions with KP Chief Minister Sohail Afridi, whose official role is currently non-functional, as well as former Chief Minister Ali Amin Gandapur, offering central support for regional security and development.
  • Federal ministers stated that the central takeover was necessary due to ongoing casualties among law enforcement and military personnel, alleging connections between the KP government and militant factions.
  • Political tensions flared over PTI's plans for a long march on Islamabad to demand the release of its jailed founder, Imran Khan, alongside disputes regarding counterterrorism policy and dialogue with the Afghan Taliban.
  • PTI alleged that it was excluded from security decisions regarding KP, a claim the federal government denied.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the step underscores severe political and security friction between federal authorities and the PTI. The central government cited past disruptions, including protests in 2014 and November 2024, alleging that planned demonstrations threatened further unrest. The implementation of emergency rule shifts authority over KP's counterterrorism and administration toward central management during a period of heightened security challenges.
