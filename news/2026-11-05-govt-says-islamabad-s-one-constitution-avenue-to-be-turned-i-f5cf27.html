Govt says Islamabad's One Constitution Avenue to be turned into a five-star hotel

Published on November 05, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, the federal government has announced plans to convert the One Constitution Avenue skyscraper in Islamabad into a five-star hotel. The decision follows a report presented to the federal cabinet by a joint investigation team (JIT), which concluded that the building was constructed through fraudulent practices, rigging, and manipulated bidding procedures.

What happened

The site was originally sanctioned as a hotel project two decades ago but was illegally converted into residential apartments and commercial areas. Following the cancellation of the project's lease by the Capital Development Authority (CDA) due to payment defaults—a decision upheld by the Islamabad High Court in April—mass evictions took place in May. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif subsequently established the JIT to probe the project. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi confirmed that the government has legally regained control of the site and intends to select a major hotel chain to operate the building.

Key facts

  • The JIT report incorporated input from the National Accountability Bureau, Federal Investigation Agency, Federal Board of Revenue, Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan, Intelligence Bureau, and Inter-Services Intelligence.
  • Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal stated that the report identified Abdul Hafeez Sheikh as the mastermind in collusion with former CDA officials, including former chairman Kamran Lashari, Dr Faisal Awan, Azhar Ali Chaudhry, Brigadier (retired) Nasrullah, and Marhoom Rai Nawaz Kharal.
  • The JIT recommended blacklisting Sheikh and his company, initiating legal actions against suspects, and re-auctioning the renamed project through a transparent process.
  • Defrauded buyers will receive refunds of their principal investments adjusted against Kibor interest rates.

Why it matters

The investigation revealed institutional complicity within the CDA, resulting in financial losses for the state and private investors. The exact financial loss figure was not specified in the source; please check the official notice. To prevent future fraudulent schemes, the JIT advised overhauling the CDA ordinance, creating an independent master plan commission, digitising records, and mandating officer rotations every two years across planning, enforcement, and building control departments. The National Accountability Bureau has been directed to pursue formal action against all individuals involved in facilitating the scheme.
