Pakistan links regional cooperation with Afghanistan to action against terrorism at ECO moot

Published on November 16, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan’s Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar stated that meaningful progress toward regional economic cooperation requires Afghan authorities to address the security concerns of neighboring countries, particularly the threat of terrorism. Speaking at the opening of the 30th Economic Cooperation Organisation (ECO) Council of Ministers meeting in Islamabad, Dar emphasized that a stable, peaceful Afghanistan integrated into the ECO framework would bring economic advantages to the entire region, provided a secure environment is maintained.

Key facts

According to details published by Dawn, several key points were highlighted during the summit:

  • Delegation Absence: The ECO consists of ten member states—Pakistan, Afghanistan, Azerbaijan, Tajikistan, Iran, Turkiye, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyz Republic, and Uzbekistan. However, official lists showed that no representative from Afghanistan attended the Islamabad meeting.
  • Theme and Leadership: Pakistan is hosting the gathering under the theme "Reviving Regionalism for a Resilient ECO" as it holds the organization's chairmanship for 2026-27.
  • Five Core Priorities: Dar outlined five focus areas for Pakistan's chairmanship: increasing trade and investment, improving transport connectivity, advancing the ECO Trade Agreement based on a November 2025 roadmap, strengthening disaster resilience, and advancing human development through education and training.
  • Diplomatic Involvement: The report noted that Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Chief of Defence Forces and Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir have been engaged in diplomatic efforts regarding the US-Israeli war on Iran to encourage regional stability and dialogue. Further details on the finalized agenda were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, regional development relies heavily on mutual trust, compliance with international commitments, and peaceful resolution of conflicts. Unresolved disputes and regional instability threaten vital trade routes, energy supplies, and investment projects. Pakistan reiterated its goal to work alongside ECO member nations to modernize trade frameworks, streamline border management, and link trade routes from Central Asia and South Asia directly to Pakistani ports on the Arabian Sea.
