Petrol price dropped by Rs7.06 per litre, high-speed diesel by Rs3.71

Published on November 04, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the government has announced a price cut of Rs7.06 per litre for petrol and Rs3.71 per litre for high-speed diesel (HSD). Based on a notification issued by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra), the new prices stand at Rs362.00 per litre for petrol and Rs387.81 per litre for HSD, taking effect on Nov 5 (Thursday). This price adjustment represents the third consecutive reduction in domestic fuel prices.

Key facts

According to Dawn, key details surrounding the fuel price revision include:

  • The drop coincided with global oil prices falling below $100 per barrel, as Brent crude fell 4.3 per cent to $99.07 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate decreased 4.3 per cent to $92.02.
  • The government maintains taxes and duties of Rs114 per litre on petrol and Rs100 per litre on HSD.
  • A 30-day agreement enables oil refineries to bear a collective Rs15 billion burden on the petroleum levy, allowing a Rs14.71 price decrease for petrol at the retail level.
  • Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik indicated that officials are working to establish a similar relief framework for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG).
  • The government and Jamaat-i-Islami are set to hold another round of negotiations on Thursday regarding further petroleum price relief.
  • Fuel rates previously reached peak levels on April 3, hitting Rs458.41 for petrol and Rs520.35 for HSD following the start of the US-Iran conflict.
  • Government austerity measures remain active, including a 9pm closure mandate for commercial markets and a 50 per cent reduction in fuel allocations for official vehicles lasting three months.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, petrol price adjustments mainly affect lower-middle and middle-class households, as petrol is predominantly used in private vehicles, rickshaws, small cars, and two-wheelers. Fluctuations in HSD prices influence the broader population because diesel powers heavy transport vehicles, power plants, and large generators. Petrol and HSD are the primary fuel revenue sources in the country, recording combined monthly sales between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes, compared to 10,000 tonnes for kerosene.
