Diesel price dips below Rs350 after 4 months; petrol rate down by Rs4

Published on November 25, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, the government has lowered fuel prices across the country, cutting petrol by Rs4 per litre and high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs5 per litre. The new rates were announced in a notification issued by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra). Following the revision, petrol retailed at Rs314 per litre and HSD at Rs348 per litre on Thursday, Nov 26. These updates bring the prices of both fuels below the rates recorded on July 18.

Key facts

Dawn outlined key background details and policy measures related to the fuel price adjustment:

  • Taxation and Refinery Contributions: The government levies Rs114 per litre in duties and taxes on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel. Based on a Nov 2 agreement lasting 30 days, refineries are bearing a collective Rs15 billion burden, which allowed a Rs14.71 per litre drop in consumer petrol prices.
  • Price Trajectory: HSD previously reached a high of Rs520.35 per litre on April 3 after rising from Rs281 following the outbreak of the US-Iran war on Feb 28. Petrol peaked at Rs458.41 on April 3 after starting its rise from Rs266 in early March.
  • Pricing System: Ogra was tasked by the prime minister and cabinet to revise fuel prices on a near-daily basis according to global market trends. This replaced a weekly pricing mechanism in place since early March.
  • Government Schemes and Conservation: The Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme, launched on Sept 17, provides subsidies for motorcycles, rickshaws, and cars up to 800cc. To conserve energy amid Middle East tensions, the government mandated that markets close by 9pm and slashed fuel allowances for official vehicles by 50 percent for three months.

Why it matters

Price shifts in petrol affect lower-middle and middle-class citizens who depend on small vehicles, two-wheelers, and rickshaws. Diesel price adjustments affect the wider public because HSD powers heavy freight transport, industrial generators, and power plants. Petrol and HSD serve as the primary petroleum revenue generators, with combined monthly sales of 700,000 to 800,000 tonnes, compared to 10,000 tonnes for kerosene.
