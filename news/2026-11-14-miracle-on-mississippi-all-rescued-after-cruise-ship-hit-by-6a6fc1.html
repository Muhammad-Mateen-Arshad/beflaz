'Miracle on Mississippi' - All rescued after cruise ship hit by tanker in New Orleans

Published on November 14, 2026

What happened

According to BBC News, approximately 130 people were safely evacuated in New Orleans after a cargo vessel collided with a docked riverboat cruise ship on the Mississippi River. The cargo vessel Atlantic Asphalt struck the American Heritage, a riverboat operated by American Cruise Lines, around 13:30 EST (18:30 GMT) on Friday. Following the collision, the vessel took on water and tipped onto its side near the dock.

Key facts

  • All occupants were accounted for following emergency rescue efforts, with 32 individuals transported to hospitals for non-critical injuries.
  • The ship had approximately 129 passengers on board, in addition to its crew, at the time of the crash.
  • Emergency personnel, tugboat operators, local police, and the US Coast Guard participated in the rescue, leading officials to temporarily close the river to traffic.
  • The vessel was finishing an eight-day trip visiting locations in Louisiana and Mississippi.
  • Built in 2015, the four-deck paddlewheeler holds up to 150 guests and contains three lounges, a gym, and a glass-enclosed dining space.
  • Full financial estimates of the damage were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

BBC News reported that public officials commended the emergency crews whose quick response prevented fatalities. New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno and Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry both referred to the successful rescue as a "miracle on the Mississippi." US Coast Guard Captain Melanie Burnham indicated that preliminary findings suggest a potential loss of steering contributed to the crash, though a formal investigation into the exact cause is ongoing. Governor Landry emphasized that vessels have navigated the river safely for years and committed to examining how the incident occurred.
