PM stresses IT potential for economic growth

Published on November 26, 2026

What happened

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has emphasized the government’s commitment to expanding Pakistan's information technology exports to boost economic growth and generate jobs for youth, according to Dawn. During a sector performance review, the prime minister ordered the creation of a committee to conduct a third-party audit of all operational IT parks managed by the Special Technology Zones Authority (STZA). He also requested a legal review of STZA regulations to prevent the misuse of corporate establishment provisions, while separately directing officials to speed up the privatization of state-owned entities.

Key facts

According to Dawn, officials presented several key figures and project updates during the meetings:

  • Digital services platform: Alibaba is developing a centralized "Super App" for the Pakistani government under the Strategic Transformative Framework to combine all public digital services into one portal.
  • Export performance: IT exports reached $4.6 billion in FY26 ending June 30, with an additional $1.24 billion recorded in the first quarter (July–September) of FY27. Freelance exports accounted for $1.165 billion in FY26.
  • Tech zone expansion: Across seven clusters nationwide, 32 technology zones are being established, with 21 currently operational and 11 under construction. Around 300 tech firms operate within these zones, bringing Rs100 billion in realized investment alongside Rs105 billion in pipeline projects.
  • State-owned enterprise divestment: Relevant authorities were directed to identify additional loss-making state enterprises for privatization and overcome legal obstacles to stay on schedule. The specific list of entities and deadlines was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, attracting foreign and local investment into technology zones is intended to keep them functioning strictly as hubs for technological innovation and export expansion. Meanwhile, privatizing non-functional state-owned assets is expected to ease financial pressure on the national exchequer, maintain economic stability, and redirect state resources toward development projects and public welfare.
