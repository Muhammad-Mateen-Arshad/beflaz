PPP-PML-N spat continues as Asif's jibe at Bilawal draws 'red line' warning

Published on November 22, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, tensions between Pakistan's ruling PML-N and its political ally, the PPP, have intensified following critical public comments by Defence Minister Khawaja Asif directed at PPP Chairperson Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari. PPP representatives responded strongly, stating that the minister's remarks crossed a "red line" for the party. The friction escalated after Bilawal gave a speech at the Karachi Press Club on November 17, in which he addressed opposition demands, questioned coalition decisions, described Imran Khan as a political prisoner, and warned of potential conflict over creating new provinces.

Key facts

  • According to Dawn, Khawaja Asif posted video clips comparing Bilawal's statements on Imran Khan and accused him of dishonestly changing positions and being opportunistic.
  • Asif publicly raised questions on social media regarding the financial details, ownership, and legal status of Bilawal House in Lahore.
  • PPP Senator Sherry Rehman and MNA Sharmila Faruqui rejected Asif's comments as personal attacks that lacked political seriousness and violated party decorum.
  • PPP leader Nadeem Afzal Chan responded to the property remarks by sharing footage of party workers at Bilawal House, describing it as open to all party members.
  • The PPP stated that Bilawal directed party members to refrain from personal attacks and focus on policy issues, while noting past legal controversies involving PML-N leadership, such as the Panama Papers and Avenfield cases.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the exchange reflects growing strain between key coalition partners. To manage the political dispute, President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif held discussions in Islamabad on Thursday and Saturday, committing to resolve their differences through dialogue. PPP leaders subsequently called on the government to focus on addressing public concerns rather than creating new political divisions.
