11 injured as Riyadh skyscraper hit second time in less than a week

Published on November 24, 2026

What happened

Riyadh's tallest building was hit by a strike on Tuesday, leaving 11 people injured, according to Dawn. The 385-metre Public Investment Fund (PIF) tower, situated in the King Abdullah Financial District, had previously sustained damage from an attack on November 19. Saudi Arabia's Civil Defence confirmed that the casualties comprised two Saudis, four Egyptians, three Nepalese, and two Indians. Authorities acted swiftly to enforce safety measures, and local police subsequently cordoned off several entry points to the financial area.

A spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition denounced the event as a cowardly attack. While a vehicle carrying a speaker alerted nearby pedestrians to take cover during the incident, no emergency alerts were dispatched to mobile phones across the capital.

Key facts

  • The targeted landmark houses the main headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, which leads efforts to diversify the nation's economy.
  • This incident follows earlier strikes, including a hit on an Aramco tower in the same financial district and multiple attacks on Riyadh's international airport that killed 14 people in October.
  • Attacks by Houthi forces have intensified since hostilities resumed in July as a spillover from the conflict between the United States and Iran.
  • Separately on Tuesday, an attack involving buses and trucks in Yemen's Lahj province killed 26 individuals, with local officials pointing to a coalition airstrike while government forces blamed Houthi fighters.

Why it matters

The ongoing strikes have led to the cancellation of most international flights arriving in Riyadh and forced the postponement or cancellation of major gatherings, including the AFC Asian Cup. International reactions followed swiftly, according to Dawn. Pakistan’s Foreign Ministry strongly condemned the strike on Saudi infrastructure, reiterating its military commitment to defend the kingdom under mutual security agreements. Concurrently, the British government placed sanctions on the Houthis and four key leaders, implementing travel bans and asset freezes.
