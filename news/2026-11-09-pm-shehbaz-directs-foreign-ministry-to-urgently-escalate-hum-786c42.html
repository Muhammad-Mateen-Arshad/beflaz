PM Shehbaz directs foreign ministry to urgently ‘escalate humanitarian assistance’ for rain-hit Gaza

Published on November 09, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has instructed the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to urgently utilize all available channels to step up humanitarian aid for Gaza following severe weather conditions in the region.

What happened

Heavy rainfall severely impacted Gaza, flooding tens of thousands of temporary shelters housing displaced Palestinians. In a statement on social media platform X, PM Shehbaz described the situation as heartbreaking, noting that children are trapped in flooded tents while facing severe cold and hunger. He condemned ongoing Israeli actions that restrict basic aid access, characterizing them as violations of international law and ceasefire terms.

The specific timeline and procedures for dispatching the expanded aid were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

Based on official Gaza statements cited by Dawn, the emergency situation includes several critical developments:

  • At least 70,000 tents were submerged within a 12-hour period, resulting in injuries and shelter loss, though no fatalities were reported.
  • Gaza’s civil defence service answered approximately 1,500 distress calls from affected residents.
  • Local municipal authorities declared an inability to handle the storm due to fuel shortages and equipment loss, asserting that Israeli attacks destroyed hundreds of essential vehicles, including water pumps and bulldozers.
  • Residents reported severe shortages of tarpaulins and dry bedding, leaving families to sleep directly on flooded ground.

Why it matters

Dawn noted that the winter flooding has worsened the humanitarian crisis for more than two million Palestinians living in temporary settlements. Gaza has experienced widespread devastation following the Hamas attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, with Palestinian authorities reporting over 74,000 deaths since Israel began its military offensive.

Although a US-brokered ceasefire agreement called for the eventual reconstruction of the territory, implementation has stalled. Israeli forces continue to hold more than 60 percent of Gaza and execute frequent airstrikes, while severe restrictions on incoming shipments leave most residents dependent on scarce aid supplies.
