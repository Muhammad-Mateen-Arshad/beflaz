Gadi Eisenkot tipped to seek Israel government with backing of Arab lists

Published on November 05, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, Arab-majority political factions in Israel have announced their backing for Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government following the October 27 election. The endorsements strengthen Eisenkot's position as President Isaac Herzog conducts consultations to determine which candidate will receive the mandate to assemble a ruling coalition.

What happened

Dawn reported that neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor Eisenkot achieved a governing majority in the 120-member parliament. The Joint List, which secured 11 seats, and Raam, which won eight seats, both decided to endorse Eisenkot. Combined, the Arab-majority lists control 19 seats. Conversely, United Torah Judaism, an ultra-Orthodox party with seven seats that supported Netanyahu in the previous five elections, notified the president's office that it would not recommend any lawmaker to lead the next administration.

Key facts

  • Eisenkot received official recommendations from both the Joint List and Raam, giving him support from 19 Arab-majority seat holders.
  • Netanyahu's political bloc controls 53 seats, while Eisenkot's bloc holds 48 seats in the 120-seat legislature.
  • United Torah Judaism opted not to endorse Netanyahu or Eisenkot, declining to back any candidate during presidential talks.
  • The lawmaker selected by President Herzog will be granted 28 days to form a government, with a possible 14-day extension; failure leads to another candidate being tasked.
  • Specific coalition agreements and official appointment timing were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

The ongoing negotiations reflect deep policy divides, particularly over military service exemptions for religious students. Parties such as United Torah Judaism and Shas favor preserving these exemptions, which Netanyahu maintained without passing formal legislation. In contrast, Eisenkot supports ending exemptions. According to Dawn, observers consider United Torah Judaism's stance a tactic to show their votes cannot be taken for granted, though they might still join a Netanyahu-led cabinet.

While relying on Arab-majority parties could help Eisenkot build a majority, Dawn noted it risks alienating hawkish opposition figures like Avigdor Liberman. Meanwhile, Netanyahu released a statement alleging foreign financial influence in the election and questioning the loyalty of Arab citizens.
