Any talks with India on terrorism must be 'evidence-based', says Musadik Malik

Published on November 22, 2026

What happened

Pakistani Federal Minister Musadik Malik stated that any future dialogue between Pakistan and India on terrorism must be grounded in evidence, according to Dawn. Malik made the remarks during a three-day trip to New Delhi to participate in the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Civilisational Dialogue Forum. His trip marks the first visit by a Pakistani minister to India since former foreign minister Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari attended an SCO gathering in May 2023.

Key facts

  • Malik emphasized that evidence regarding terrorism can be presented through bilateral or multilateral mechanisms by both nations.
  • He clarified that his trip was strictly for the SCO multilateral event, adding that he would discuss bilateral subjects, such as the Indus Waters Treaty (IWT), when formally invited for bilateral talks.
  • At the forum, Malik identified climate change as a shared regional threat, noting that environmental destruction impacts both countries regardless of borders.
  • Tensions between the neighbours escalated following a May 2025 attack on tourists in Pahalgam, which resulted in a four-day military conflict. India attributed the attack to Pakistan without supplying proof, while Islamabad denied involvement and requested a neutral investigation.
  • Following the Pahalgam incident, India unilaterally placed the 1960 World Bank-brokered IWT in abeyance in April last year. Pakistan labeled the action an "act of war," and on August 31, the Permanent Court of Arbitration ruled that the treaty remains valid and fully in force.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Pakistan has repeatedly expressed willingness to negotiate unresolved issues with India, including disputes over shared water resources. While India's foreign minister noted in June 2024 an intention to resolve cross-border terrorism, recent military conflicts have deepened bilateral friction. Malik's participation at the New Delhi event highlights continued multilateral interaction despite stalled bilateral dialogue.
