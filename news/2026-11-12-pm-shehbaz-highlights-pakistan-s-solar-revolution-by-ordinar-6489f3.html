PM Shehbaz highlights Pakistan's solar 'revolution by ordinary citizens' at COP31

Published on November 12, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif presented Pakistan's rapid adoption of solar energy at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkiye. Speaking during a high-level session titled "35 by 35: Powering the Future," Sharif outlined national progress in renewable energy adoption and expressed Pakistan's support for international electrification targets.

Key facts

  • Solar and Nuclear Progress: Pakistan added approximately 22 gigawatts of rooftop solar capacity over the past two years. Clean sources now make up nearly 55 percent of the national energy mix, boosted further by progress on the Chashma-5 nuclear power plant, which is set to add 1,200 megawatts of clean power.
  • Electric Vehicles: A newly drafted national auto policy targets making 30 percent of new vehicle sales electric by 2030 through lower tariffs. All future government buses and motorcycles will be electric. This year, electric models accounted for more than one out of every 10 motorcycles sold in Pakistan.
  • Global Pledges: Pakistan endorsed the "35-by-35 Global Electrification Pledge," which seeks to raise electricity's share of total final global energy consumption to roughly 35 percent by 2035.
  • Summit Details: Hosted by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, the conference gathered representatives from nearly 200 countries. Turkiye chose not to invite Israel to the event. The United States skipped the summit for a second consecutive year following President Donald Trump's 2025 decision to exit international climate agreements.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, Prime Minister Sharif emphasized that climate targets cannot be achieved without dedicated international funding. Pakistan estimates it will need $138 billion by 2035 to build low-carbon power generation and another $50 billion to replace coal facilities with solar energy. Sharif cautioned that heavy debt burdens prevent developing nations from funding these initiatives independently, calling for accessible technology transfers and concessional financing to support global goals.
