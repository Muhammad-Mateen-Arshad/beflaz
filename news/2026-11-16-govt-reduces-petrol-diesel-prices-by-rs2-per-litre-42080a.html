Govt reduces petrol, diesel prices by Rs2 per litre

Published on November 16, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the government lowered the prices of both petrol and high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs2 per litre on Monday, marking the tenth straight reduction in fuel rates. Following a notification issued by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA), petrol will be sold at Rs328 per litre and HSD at Rs358 per litre on Nov 17 (Tuesday).

Key facts

Dawn reported several details surrounding the recent fuel price adjustment and related energy policies:

  • The government continues to impose duties and taxes amounting to Rs114 per litre on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel.
  • A 30-day agreement established on Nov 2 requires oil refineries to absorb a cumulative Rs15 billion cost for the petroleum levy, enabling a consumer price decrease of Rs14.71 per litre on petrol.
  • Fuel rates previously reached record highs on April 3, when petrol hit Rs458.41 per litre and HSD reached Rs520.35 per litre following international market surges linked to the US-Iran conflict.
  • Current government austerity measures mandate that commercial markets close by 9pm and official vehicle fuel allocations be reduced by 50 percent for three months.
  • Since July 17, OGRA has been authorized to adjust prices on a near-daily basis to reflect international trends, moving away from the weekly adjustment framework introduced in March.
  • Assistance measures include the Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme launched on Sept 17 for owners of motorcycles, rickshaws, and vehicles up to 800cc.

Why it matters

Fuel pricing directly influences the broader economy and household budgets, according to Dawn. Petrol is largely used for private transportation, smaller passenger vehicles, rickshaws, and two-wheelers, making price changes particularly impactful for middle and lower-middle-class consumers. HSD pricing affects the general public because it is heavily utilized in freight transport, power plants, and large industrial generators. Together, petrol and HSD represent the majority of fuel sales in the country, with combined monthly sales between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes, compared to kerosene's monthly volume of 10,000 tonnes.
