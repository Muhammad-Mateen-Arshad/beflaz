PM Shehbaz joins world leaders at COP31, will outline Pakistan’s climate priorities

Published on November 11, 2026

According to Dawn, Pakistan Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has arrived in the Mediterranean resort city of Antalya, Turkiye, to attend the COP31 Antalya Leaders’ Summit. Upon his arrival at the venue, he was received by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres before joining other global delegates for an official group photograph.

What happened

According to Dawn, Prime Minister Shehbaz is participating in the gathering alongside representatives from nearly 200 nations to outline Pakistan’s climate priorities. He is scheduled to address a high-level clean energy transition dialogue titled "35 by 35: Powering the Future" convened by the COP31 presidency. On the sidelines of the summit, the prime minister will hold bilateral talks with President Erdogan and other international leaders, while a dedicated Pakistan Pavilion at the venue is set to host 54 thematic events.

Key facts

Based on official statements reported by Dawn, Pakistan's agenda at the conference includes:

  • Climate finance and cooperation: Calling for predictable, adequate, and accessible climate funding for developing countries, alongside stronger international action to mitigate climate impacts.
  • Clean energy and resilience: Highlighting Pakistan's progress in rapid solarisation, expanding renewable energy capacity, and upgrading disaster preparedness and early-warning systems.
  • Electrification proposal: Host nation Turkiye is pushing for a global target to raise electricity's share of total energy consumption to 35 percent by 2035, though critics note the proposal lacks explicit requirements for clean power generation.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the summit occurs against a backdrop of rising global emissions and severe weather events, including 2026 heatwaves in Western Europe and a glacier collapse in Nepal that caused floods killing more than 1,450 people. UN Secretary-General Guterres warned leaders that slow international action has placed the planet on a path to destruction.

The conference also highlights key absences among major polluters and economies. The United States skipped the summit for a second straight year after President Donald Trump withdrew the country from climate cooperation in 2025. Additionally, top leaders from France, Germany, Japan, Italy, and Canada are not attending this year's talks.
