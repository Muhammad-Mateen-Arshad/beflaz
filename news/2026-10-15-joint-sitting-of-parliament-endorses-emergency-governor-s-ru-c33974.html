Joint sitting of Parliament endorses emergency, governor's rule in KP amid opposition outcry

Published on October 15, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, a joint sitting of Pakistan's Parliament approved the federal government's decision to enforce emergency rule and governor's rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). The resolutions passed following hours of debate despite vocal protests from opposition members belonging to the Pakistan Tehreek-i-Insaf (PTI), the party holding power in the province. Opposition members displayed placards opposing the action and chanted during the voting process.

Key facts

The joint session involved key parliamentary procedures and decisions, as detailed by Dawn:

  • Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry introduced a motion to suspend specific procedural rules, which was adopted after a standing vote of 214 to 56.
  • Chaudhry tabled two separate resolutions: one approving the Proclamation of Emergency under Article 232 and another approving governor's rule under Article 234.
  • National Assembly Speaker Ayaz Sadiq conducted the vote that ultimately passed both resolutions.
  • The joint session occurred a day after both individual houses of Parliament separately endorsed the emergency in KP.
  • The central government initially imposed these measures on October 11, attributing the step to the provincial government's intentional disengagement from counter-terrorism efforts.
  • Law Minister Azam Nazeer Tarar explained that approving the resolutions required a total majority vote of 51 percent.
  • Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif attended part of the joint session and met with opposition leaders from both houses.
  • Members of Parliament offered prayers for victims, primarily children, who died in a Peshawar roof collapse.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the imposition of emergency and governor's rule came after escalating political friction and talks between the Centre and PTI. Tensions grew after PTI announced a long march to Islamabad to press for the release of its jailed founder, Imran Khan. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi maintained that the federal government had no other options left and had tried until the last minute to avoid applying these measures across the province, including offering a proposal to limit the emergency to 12 districts. Naqvi denied that the government walked out of negotiations and acknowledged that key opposition figures maintained a clear stance against terrorism.
