Iran says forces ‘stronger’ than at beginning of war

Published on November 07, 2026

What happened

According to a report published by Dawn, Iranian military officials stated that their forces have grown stronger and more capable since fighting broke out with the United States and Israel. Iranian army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia noted that direct battle experience has increased the military's alert status, motivation, and self-confidence, adding that any retaliatory actions ordered by Iran's Supreme Leader would be immediate and without geographical limits. Meanwhile, the United States military offered a $20 million reward and potential relocation for information regarding Iran's nuclear programme.

Key facts

  • Conflict background: The war began in late February following US and Israeli attacks on Iran, resulting in a Middle East deadlock that has severely impacted Iran's military and economy over eight months of fighting.
  • Strait of Hormuz restriction: Iranian forces claim control over the strategic waterway, which typically carries around one-fifth of global oil consumption. Tehran stated the passage will stay restricted until its demands are satisfied by the US.
  • US nuclear reward program: Created under a 1984 State Department program, the reward was announced after the International Atomic Energy Agency lost oversight continuity regarding Iranian nuclear components such as centrifuges. The exact qualifications for the payout were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Recent military incidents: Iranian air defences reportedly brought down an American drone over Qeshm Island, where state media also reported two explosions and missile impacts.

Why it matters

As mediators attempt to prevent renewed hostilities, both sides have indicated involvement in diplomatic efforts according to Dawn. US President Donald Trump stated that American strikes effectively dismantled Iran's nuclear capabilities and mentioned discussing conflict resolution options with Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani. Simultaneously, Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian stated that his government remains committed to seeking a resolution to the crisis through dialogue.
