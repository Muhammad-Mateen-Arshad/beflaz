Makkah Defence Alliance meeting in Islamabad concludes

Published on November 27, 2026

What happened

The third session of the Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC), the principal decision-making body of the tripartite Makkah Defence Alliance, wrapped up in Islamabad on Friday after lasting approximately two-and-a-half hours, according to Dawn. In preparation for the high-level event, local authorities declared a public holiday and enforced special movement restrictions in Islamabad, alongside a local holiday in Rawalpindi. Following the conclusion of the discussions, no official statement was released. Specific decisions made during the session are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

According to reports from Dawn, the key aspects of the alliance and its latest meeting include:

  • Membership and Scope: The SPDC is composed of the foreign ministers, defence ministers, and chiefs of defence staff from Saudi Arabia, Turkiye, and Pakistan.
  • Mutual Defence Pact: Established under the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement signed on August 7, the accord dictates that an attack on any member state is viewed as an attack on all, requiring mutual aid including military support.
  • Governance: The alliance maintains a permanent secretariat in Riyadh, led by its first secretary general, retired Lt Gen Nauman Mahmood, who was appointed for a three-year term.
  • Agenda Items: Discussions were anticipated to focus on establishing standard operating procedures, building implementation mechanisms, and evaluating conflict conditions south of Saudi Arabia involving Houthi forces.
  • Integration of Military Assets: Previous bilateral Pakistani military deployments—comprising JF-17 aircraft, drones, and HQ-9 air defence systems—are set to be incorporated into the trilateral framework.

Why it matters

The alliance's development takes place against a backdrop of broader regional friction involving Iran, Israel, and the United States, according to Dawn. Although all three member states share a commitment to collective regional deterrence, their individual strategic goals differ. Pakistani Defence Minister Khawaja Asif highlighted the need for regional nations to align closely against potential external threats, even as the alliance's operational doctrine and command structures remain under development. Meanwhile, Pakistan continues diplomatic efforts to manage relations with Iran, and Turkiye maintains a cautious approach regarding direct combat involvement in Yemen while stressing the defensive nature of the pact.
