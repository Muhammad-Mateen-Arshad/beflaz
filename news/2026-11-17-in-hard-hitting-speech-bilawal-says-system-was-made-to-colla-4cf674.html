In hard-hitting speech, Bilawal says system was ‘made to collapse’

Published on November 17, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari stated during an event at the Karachi Press Club that the country's governance system did not simply collapse, but was rather "made to collapse." His remarks came in response to statements by Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi, who recently called for a governance "reset" and the establishment of new administrative units.

What happened

Bilawal asserted that any necessary reset of the system must be political, carried out by public representatives rather than non-political forces. Acknowledging his own role and past errors, he stated that state institutions have lost their legitimacy and urged steps to restore it. He also called on the federal government and PML-N to clarify their official stance on the proposed administrative reset.

Key facts

Dawn highlighted several major points from Bilawal's address:

  • Bilawal opposed creating new provinces through unconstitutional means or referendums, revealing that PPP lawmakers are facing pressure over the issue.
  • He called for an end to political revenge and advocated for releasing political prisoners, explicitly classifying former Prime Minister Imran Khan as a political prisoner.
  • He criticized the withholding of reserved seats and party symbols from the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI), calling for reserved seats to be restored to the opposition without preconditions as a confidence-building measure.
  • He noted that the ruling coalition used contested reserved seats to pass constitutional amendments twice, adding that the 27th Amendment lacked overall consensus.
  • He defended JUI-F chief Fazlur Rehman's right to criticize the government.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, Bilawal's comments represent a departure from the ruling coalition's stance on key issues involving the opposition. By demanding an end to political victimisation, advocating for the opposition's reserved seats, and insisting on political dialogue, the PPP chairman highlighted broader concerns regarding institutional legitimacy and parliamentary functionality in Pakistan.
