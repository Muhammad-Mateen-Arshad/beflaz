US says airspace now ‘opened up’ after grounding all flights for hours, sparking travel chaos

Published on November 14, 2026

What happened

The United States experienced severe aviation disruptions on Saturday after federal officials imposed a nationwide flight freeze lasting approximately four hours, according to Dawn. The Federal Aviation Authority instituted the groundstop due to a major communications failure tied to outdated technical infrastructure. The administration of President Donald Trump confirmed that the halt was lifted after system functionality was restored.

Key facts

  • According to tracking data from FlightAware, nearly 6,000 flights were canceled, affecting both domestic and international travel routes.
  • Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy stated the outage resulted from an overloaded system relying on 1980s copper lines, older switches, and legacy routers.
  • Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin emphasized that investigators found no evidence of a cyberattack, artificial intelligence threat, or link to a recent Microsoft outage.
  • Duffy urged Congress to provide $30.1 billion in funding to upgrade the national Air Traffic Control System, repeating a request he officially submitted on Thursday.
  • The stoppage left hundreds of thousands of passengers delayed at crowded airports, with many facing conflicting flight status information and long lines for ground transport.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, nationwide groundstops remain extremely rare in American airspace. Aside from a January 2023 incident caused by a pilot notification failure, the only other full national freeze occurred following the 9/11 attacks. The event underscored infrastructure challenges within one of the world's busiest airspace networks. Specific rebooking guidelines and flight status updates were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
