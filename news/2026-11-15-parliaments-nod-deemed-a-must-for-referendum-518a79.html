Parliament’s nod deemed a must for referendum

Published on November 15, 2026

According to Dawn, President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif have agreed that any initiative to create new provinces or administrative units must strictly follow constitutional provisions. Meeting at the prime minister’s Model Town residence in Lahore, both leaders agreed that holding any public referendum on the issue requires parliamentary consensus and approval.

What happened

The meeting occurred amid renewed political debate triggered by Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi’s proposal to hold a referendum regarding new administrative divisions. According to official sources cited by Dawn, President Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz affirmed that parliament cannot be bypassed. A parliamentary majority must determine whether a referendum takes place and what specific questions are presented to the public.

Key facts

  • Constitutional Adherence: Both leaders confirmed that any division of provinces must strictly adhere to the procedure prescribed by the Constitution.
  • PPP Stance: President Zardari reiterated the PPP’s opposition to dividing Sindh, aligning with a resolution previously passed by the Sindh Assembly.
  • Coalition Perspectives: Interior Minister Naqvi advocated consulting the public through a referendum, while Defence Minister Khawaja Asif noted that administrative needs could justify up to 12 provinces. PML-N stated it will present its formal policy when the matter comes before parliament.
  • Imran Khan Situation: The leaders discussed incarcerated PTI founder Imran Khan, with sources stating that his recent legal meetings and his sisters' release from prison were not tied to any behind-the-scenes developments.
  • Official Summary: The Prime Minister's Office highlighted discussions on security, political coordination, and PM Shehbaz's visit to Turkiye for the COP31 conference, though the official release did not explicitly mention the discussions on new provinces.

Why it matters

The consensus between the president and prime minister reinforces parliamentary authority over major structural changes to the country's governance. According to Dawn, the decision ensures that individual proposals for public referendums or administrative reorganization cannot move forward without broader political consensus and adherence to constitutional guidelines.
