Govt announces auto policy with focus on cheaper vehicles, EVs

Published on November 07, 2026

According to Dawn, the federal government has introduced a new auto policy focused on promoting electric vehicles (EVs) and lowering vehicle costs for the public. Information Minister Attaullah Tarar and Haroon Akhtar Khan, Adviser to the Prime Minister for Industries and Production, unveiled the strategy during a press conference in Islamabad. The official approval date was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What happened

The policy rests on six key pillars: delivering cheaper vehicles for middle and lower-middle classes, driving exports, mandating localisation, enhancing safety standards, fostering EV usage, and maintaining transparency. Under the framework, duties on assembly components and completely built units will be reduced annually over a five-year period to bring down car prices, excluding luxury models. To remain revenue-neutral under an ongoing International Monetary Fund (IMF) program, the government will collect extra revenue from conventional engine vehicles above 1,000cc, while exempting cars under 1,000cc.

Key facts

  • EV Incentives: Pending IMF approval, sales tax rates will be set at 1 percent for pure EVs, 6 percent for range-extended EVs, and 9 percent for hybrids. EVs priced up to $50,000 are exempt from capital value tax, federal excise duty, and withholding tax.
  • Financing and Charging: State Bank car financing for EVs will be increased to Rs10 million with terms extended to five years. Charging station equipment will incur zero customs duty and a 1 percent sales tax, supported by government viability gap funding.
  • Exports and Safety: Exporters can earn up to a 15 percent total Duty Drawback on Local Taxes and Levies, but face penalties if local manufacturing and export targets are missed. Vehicles must also comply with 45 UN WP.29 safety codes.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, government spokespersons noted that past protectionist measures discouraged innovation in the domestic auto industry. The new policy seeks to cut Pakistan's costly foreign oil import bill, reduce carbon emissions, boost job creation through localized assembly, and make high-quality, eco-friendly transport affordable for regular citizens.
