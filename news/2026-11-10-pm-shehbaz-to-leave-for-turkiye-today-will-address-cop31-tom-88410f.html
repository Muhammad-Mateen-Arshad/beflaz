PM Shehbaz to leave for Turkiye today, will address COP31 tomorrow

Published on November 10, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif is travelling to Antalya, Turkiye, from November 10 to 12 to attend the Leaders’ Summit of the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31) under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. The Foreign Office stated that the Prime Minister will deliver a speech on November 11, calling on the global community to take decisive actions against the climate crisis.

During the summit, the Prime Minister plans to highlight Pakistan's initiatives in accelerating solar energy adoption, expanding renewable capacity, and improving disaster preparedness alongside early-warning systems. He will also call for dependable and accessible climate finance for developing countries. Additionally, he will participate in a high-level roundtable hosted by the COP31 presidency and meet with Turkish leadership and other global leaders. Specific schedules for these bilateral talks were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • The opening of COP31 in Antalya was delayed by an hour and a half following late-night disagreements among negotiators over including climate finance on the agenda.
  • Developing countries pushed for agenda items focused on tripling adaptation finance and implementing funding framework discussions established at COP30 in Brazil.
  • Jasper Inventor, leading the Greenpeace COP31 delegation, noted that developed nations were maintaining a firm stance against adding these funding items to the agenda.
  • A senior European Union official stated last week that political will for new funding pledges at the conference is lacking.
  • The conference follows recent severe weather events, including heatwaves across Europe, wildfires in Indonesia, and catastrophic flooding in Nepal, exacerbated by El Nino.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the summit comes amid alarming environmental indicators. A recent report from the World Meteorological Organisation highlighted ongoing record-high temperatures, melting ice, and rising sea levels in 2026, while European Union climate monitors project 2026 to likely be the second-hottest year ever recorded.

However, analysts expect limited major breakthroughs during the negotiations. Progress is hindered by global economic strain from an oil price shock and broader geopolitical conflicts, including the US war on Iran and Russia’s conflict in Ukraine.
