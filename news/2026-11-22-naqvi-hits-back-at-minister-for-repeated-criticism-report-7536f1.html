Naqvi hits back at minister for repeated criticism: report

Published on November 22, 2026

What happened

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has responded to ongoing criticism from a federal minister, claiming the individual has been making frequent television appearances targeting him to gain attention, according to a report by Dawn citing Geo News. Speaking in an informal conversation, Naqvi stated that he has patiently endured these statements for two and a half years during the incumbent government's tenure following the 2024 elections. He warned that if he chose to respond, the minister would be unable to show his face. The specific identity of the minister targeted by Naqvi was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Naqvi defended business leaders who attended the Pakistan Economic Summit in Islamabad in July, describing them as respectable citizens, former chamber of commerce leaders, and top taxpayers.
  • Naqvi questioned why political parties accept billions of rupees in party funds from businessmen during elections if they view them unfavorably.
  • The interior minister's remarks followed comments by Inter-Provincial Coordination Minister Rana Sanaullah, who criticized the background and intentions of certain attendees at the July forum.
  • Sanaullah referred to both Naqvi and PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari as being under the discipline of President Asif Ali Zardari, claiming a recent meeting at President House aimed to reduce political tensions.
  • Sindh Local Government Minister Nasir Hussain Shah countered Sanaullah's remarks, clarifying that Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari is President Zardari's only son and rejecting any comparison with Naqvi.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the exchange reflects broader disagreements among leaders associated with the ruling coalition regarding public debates on governance. Sanaullah noted that while discussions on creating new administrative units are not inherently wrong, such significant policy matters should be addressed within Parliament rather than raised at seminars, business forums, or on social media.
