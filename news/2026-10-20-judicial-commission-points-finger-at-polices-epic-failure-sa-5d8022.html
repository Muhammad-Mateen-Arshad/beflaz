Judicial commission points finger at police’s ‘epic failure’, says Mir Raza was murdered

Published on October 20, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, a judicial commission appointed to examine the death of businessman Mir Raza Ali concluded that his death was a murder, while highlighting major investigative failures by law enforcement. The 62-page report, prepared by Sindh High Court Justice Omar Sial, was released to the public by the Sindh cabinet in a meeting chaired by Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah. The panel began its proceedings on August 28 to review statements from individuals connected to the case.

Key facts

  • Ruling out suicide: The commission determined the case was a homicide based on medical jurisprudence, bullet trajectory, wound placement, acid burns, lack of blood, body state, and the detection of anesthesia in the blood.
  • Procedural failures: The report found that police violated required protocols, made premature public claims, and failed in their primary responsibility to find the truth.
  • Lack of documentation: Dozens of authorized and unauthorized officers collected evidence and conducted questioning without keeping formal records, leaving no one accountable for errors.
  • Potential cover-up: The panel stated that senior officers ignored lapses instead of correcting them, noting that an active cover-up during early investigative stages could not be ruled out.
  • Controversial meeting cleared: An August 9 meeting at CM House involving senior officials and Medico-Legal Officer Dr. Osama Sheikh was found to have no underlying ulterior motives despite public speculation.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the commission stressed that public confidence in the police has collapsed and much of the physical evidence has been compromised. To rebuild trust and resolve the case through intelligence-led methods, the commission recommended several corrective steps:

  • Creating a Joint Investigation Team led by Deputy Inspector General Irfan Baloch, incorporating members from the Inter-Services Intelligence, Intelligence Bureau, Pakistan Rangers (Sindh), and the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency.
  • Forming a high-level committee, preferably headed by the Federal Investigation Agency, to pursue criminal charges against responsible police personnel.
  • Requiring the police force to publicly apologize for operational errors and immediately launch official inquiries against culpable officers.
