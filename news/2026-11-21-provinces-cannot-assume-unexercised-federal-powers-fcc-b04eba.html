Provinces cannot assume ‘unexercised’ federal powers: FCC

Published on November 21, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) has ruled that a province cannot claim a fiscal authority simply because parliament has not exercised it. A two-judge bench comprising Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan and Justice Aamer Farooq issued a 59-page detailed judgment addressing 568 legal challenges by taxpayers against infrastructure development cesses imposed by Sindh and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. The court set aside previous decisions by the Sindh High Court and Peshawar High Court, declaring the provincial levies and related enforcement actions unconstitutional.

What happened

The FCC determined that the challenged provincial charges unlawfully encroached upon parliament's exclusive authority over imports, exports, and interprovincial trade. Consequently, the court ruled that no surcharges, penalties, or demands based on these invalidated charges may be enforced, and any securities provided for them must be discharged after verification.

Key facts

  • Under Article 142 of the Constitution, federal law takes precedence over provincial law whenever a conflict arises, rendering conflicting provincial legislation void to the extent of that conflict.
  • Taxpayers argued that the provincial levies interfered with federal authority over trade and could not be justified merely as infrastructure fees.
  • Provinces defended the cesses using residual legislative powers, citing infrastructure services provided to commercial entities.
  • The court clarified that while the 18th Amendment expanded provincial autonomy by removing the Concurrent Legislative List, subjects reserved for parliament were not transferred to the provinces.
  • Provinces retain the right to impose legal charges on goods that are both manufactured and consumed within their own borders.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the judgment emphasizes that financial necessity cannot grant legislative authority that is absent from the constitution. The court noted that under Article 160 and National Finance Commission arrangements, receiving a share of federal tax revenues does not authorize a province to independently levy those federal taxes.
