Info minister alleges 'nexus' between PTI-led KP govt, terrorists

Published on October 11, 2026

What happened

The federal government has declared an emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) under Article 232 of the Constitution, according to Dawn. In a press conference in Islamabad, federal ministers stated that the intervention was required because the provincial administration, led by Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI), failed to combat terrorism and maintain security.

Key facts

According to reports from Dawn, the federal ministers outlined several accusations and administrative adjustments:

  • Accusations against PTI: Information Minister Attaullah Tarar accused the KP government of maintaining a power-sharing alliance with terrorist groups, alleging that several districts and police stations were handed over to militants and that a recent police bill attempted to hire individuals from terrorist groups.
  • Direct Federal Management: An Area Development Secretariat (ADS) overseen by the governor was established to directly manage 12 districts described as being in near-war conditions. The federal Parliament has taken over legislative authority as the provincial assembly is currently non-functional.
  • KP Advisory Council: An 11-member technocratic council was instituted to help the governor manage the province. Appointees include Sanaullah (Advocate General), Kifayatullah Khan (Home Affairs), Tariq Mahmood Pasha (Finance), Samar Haroon Bilour (Information), retired Lt Gen Nigar Johar (Health), Amir Javed (Law), Osman Saifullah Khan (Energy and Power), Asif Rafiq Afridi (Communication and Works), Maulana Tayyab Qureshi (Religious Affairs), Dr. Muhammad Irshad (Industries and Commerce), Dr. Sarfraz Ali Shah (Mines and Minerals), and Dr. Syed Amir Abdullah (Education).

Why it matters

According to Dawn, disagreements over counterterrorism policies between the federal government and PTI previously caused the breakdown of talks on October 3. The Cabinet Division formally noted that KP could no longer be governed according to constitutional provisions. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi warned gathering crowds in KP to disperse, stating that federal authorities will use preventive detention and strict legal measures to restore order across the province.
