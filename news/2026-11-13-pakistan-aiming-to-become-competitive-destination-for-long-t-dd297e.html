Pakistan aiming to become competitive destination for long-term investment, CDF Munir tells minerals moot

Published on November 13, 2026

What happened

Pakistani officials presented a strategy to position the country as an attractive destination for long-term mining ventures during the Pakistan Minerals Investment Forum 2026 in Islamabad, according to Dawn. Speaking to international attendees, exhibitors, and delegates at the two-day gathering, Chief of Defence Forces Field Marshal Asim Munir and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif emphasized a national shift away from exporting raw minerals toward domestic refining and industrial processing.

Key facts

  • Security Commitments: As reported by Radio Pakistan via Dawn, Field Marshal Munir affirmed that security measures are actively being implemented to protect mining projects, assets, workers, and supply networks across the country.
  • Reko Diq Progress: Prime Minister Sharif noted that copper and gold extraction at the Reko Diq site along the Tethyan copper belt is scheduled to begin in 2028, following a delay of nearly two decades.
  • Economic Outlook: The Prime Minister expressed confidence that Pakistan could become the sixth-largest economy globally before 2050, adjusting a previous Goldman Sachs projection that targeted 2075.
  • Administrative Reform: Government initiatives include establishing a National Minerals Data Centre and digitizing resource records so investors can review underground reserves beforehand. The Prime Minister pledged direct intervention to clear bureaucratic delays. Specific licensing procedures were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, both military and civilian leadership stressed that Pakistan seeks long-term commercial partnerships that develop full value chains, such as local smelters, refineries, and manufacturing plants. The government stated that local communities near mineral deposits must directly benefit from this economic activity through job creation, clean drinking water, healthcare, schools, and targeted vocational training for the nation's youth, while maintaining strict environmental standards.
