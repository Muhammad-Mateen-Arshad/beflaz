Naqvi complicated provinces debate by 'irrelevant speech at irrelevant forum', says Rana Sanaullah

Published on November 23, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, PML-N Senator and prime minister's political adviser Rana Sanaullah has reiterated his criticism of Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi over his handling of the debate on creating new administrative units. Speaking on a television news program, Sanaullah maintained that Naqvi complicated the discussion by delivering an inappropriate speech at a business forum in Islamabad, turning the policy debate into a controversial topic before reaching Parliament.

Key facts

  • Criticism of speech venue: According to Dawn, Sanaullah stated that Naqvi chose the wrong forum and method during his address at the Pakistan Economic Summit in July, which distracted from constructive discussions about establishing new provinces.
  • Views on governance system: Sanaullah rejected the notion that the governance system collapsed on its own, asserting instead that politicians, political parties, the establishment, the judiciary, and bureaucratic circles collectively caused it to fail. He noted that PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari shared a similar view.
  • Reaction to warnings: In response to recent veiled warnings from Naqvi, Sanaullah questioned the minister's short temper and insistence on issuing threats, asserting his right as a colleague to evaluate Naqvi's public actions.
  • Lack of consultation: Sanaullah criticized Naqvi for failing to consult key political figures, including President Asif Ali Zardari, over the four months following the initial speech.
  • Position of other officials: While acknowledging that federal figures such as Khawaja Asif, Tariq Fazal Chaudhry, and Tallal Chaudhry have expressed support for new administrative units, Sanaullah noted that discussing the matter outside official legislative settings has only fueled controversy.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Sanaullah stressed that the issue of establishing new provinces must eventually be presented to Parliament. He emphasized the necessity of building consensus among political parties when the matter is formally introduced and debated in the Senate and National Assembly.
