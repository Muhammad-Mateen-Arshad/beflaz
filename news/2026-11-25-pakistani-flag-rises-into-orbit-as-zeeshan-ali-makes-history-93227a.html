'Pakistani flag rises into orbit' as Zeeshan Ali makes history aboard China's Shenzhou-24 spacecraft

Published on November 25, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, 40-year-old Zeeshan Ali has become the first astronaut from Pakistan to reach orbit. He launched aboard China's Shenzhou-24 spacecraft, which was propelled by a Long March 2-F rocket from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre at 8:18 a.m. Beijing Time (5:18 a.m. Pakistan Time) on Wednesday. The China Manned Space Agency confirmed that the spacecraft separated from its carrier rocket approximately 10 minutes after liftoff and successfully entered its intended orbit.

Key facts

Dawn reported several key details regarding the spaceflight:

  • operations are commanded by Chinese astronaut Tang Hongbo, with Wu Yi serving as pilot and Ali participating as payload specialist.
  • Once docked with China's Tiangong space station, Ali will be the first foreign national to board the orbital laboratory.
  • Ali is expected to remain in orbit for about a week to conduct scientific studies. According to Pakistan's space agency Suparco, his experiments include growing microalgae as a potential oxygen and food source, as well as evaluating spaceflight impacts on quinoa seeds.
  • The China Manned Space Agency stated that the crew will execute roughly 90 science experiments, manage routine space station maintenance, and complete a crew rotation with the Shenzhou-23 mission.
  • The initiative stems from a February 2025 agreement between Suparco and CMSA. Ali and Khurram Daud began training in China in April, with Ali ultimately assigned to this flight.

Why it matters

Dawn noted that Pakistan's Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal witnessed the launch in China, describing the mission as the start of a new phase for Pakistan's space endeavors and highlighting cooperation under the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor. Ali expressed gratitude to the Chinese government and stated that he views the flight not as a final goal, but as a foundation for future joint space ventures to the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

The mission also aligns with Beijing's broader space goals, which include landing astronauts on the Moon before 2030. As part of the crew transition, Shenzhou-23 commander Zhu Yangzhu will stay on Tiangong for a full year to monitor human body adaptation in space, while his remaining crewmates will return to Earth alongside Ali.
