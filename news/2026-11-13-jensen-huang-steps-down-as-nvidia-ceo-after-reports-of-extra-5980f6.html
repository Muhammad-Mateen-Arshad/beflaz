Jensen Huang steps down as Nvidia CEO after reports of extramarital affairs

Published on November 13, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Jensen Huang has resigned as chief executive, president, and board member of Nvidia, concluding his 33-year tenure at the artificial intelligence chip maker he co-founded. His immediate departure follows reports revealing two extramarital affairs. One involved a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed whom United States authorities suspect of spying for China, and another involved Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, who reported directly to him.

Key facts

  • Leadership transition: Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress has stepped in as interim chief executive while keeping her current financial duties. The specific timeline for appointing a permanent replacement is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Board changes: Former Synopsys chief executive and current board member Harvey Jones was appointed as Nvidia's first board chairman. Huang, who owns approximately 3.61 percent of the firm, will also leave the board.
  • Inquiries and reactions: Dawn reported that Nvidia's board held a meeting on Wednesday regarding Huang's position. Independent directors had previously started an internal probe into whether Huang had been targeted by foreign intelligence. Beijing denied the espionage allegations, and US President Donald Trump stated that Huang claimed to be unaware of any espionage connection. Additionally, President Trump publicly suggested that former Apple chief executive Tim Cook should take over the role.

Why it matters

Dawn highlighted that Nvidia's stock declined by over 10 percent in premarket trading following the news, putting pressure on the broader market. As a company valued at $4 trillion, Nvidia's hardware is central to the global boom in artificial intelligence and serves as a key asset in US-China technological competition. Market analysts cited in the report noted that while Nvidia's technology strategy remains established for the coming years, Huang's exit introduces execution risks and could influence US policies regarding chip exports to China.
