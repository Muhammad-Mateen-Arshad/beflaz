Govt snaps petrol price reduction spree as rate raised by Re1 per litre; diesel remains unchanged

Published on November 19, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, the government has raised the price of petrol by Re1 per litre while leaving high-speed diesel rates unchanged. This decision ended a 12-day sequence of consecutive petrol price reductions. Based on a notification issued by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority, the revised rates take effect on Friday, November 20, bringing petrol to Rs323 per litre and diesel to Rs355 per litre.

Key facts

  • The government maintains taxes and duties of Rs114 per litre on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel. Under a 30-day plan announced on November 2, refineries are absorbing a cumulative cost of Rs15 billion, enabling a consumer price reduction of Rs14.71 per litre for petrol.
  • The Prime Minister's Fuel Relief Scheme, launched on September 17, provides assistance to owners of motorcycles, rickshaws, and vehicles with engine capacities up to 800cc.
  • Fuel prices previously surged to historical peaks on April 3, when petrol hit Rs458.41 per litre and diesel reached Rs520.35 per litre following conflict between the US and Iran.
  • Since July 17, fuel prices have been adjusted on a near-daily basis by the regulator to match global market trends. The government has also imposed austerity rules requiring markets to close by 9pm and cutting fuel allowances for official vehicles by 50 percent for three months.

Why it matters

Fuel costs directly impact the daily financial situation of the general public. Petrol is predominantly used in two-wheelers, rickshaws, and small cars, meaning rate adjustments heavily affect middle and lower-middle-class households. Diesel is crucial for heavy commercial transport, power plants, and large generators, influencing wider supply chain costs. Combined monthly consumption for both fuels stands between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes.
