'Please take the job': Trump asks UFC chief Dana White to be White House press secretary

Published on November 07, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, U.S. President Donald Trump revealed on Saturday that he invited Dana White, the chief executive of the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC), to serve as the White House press secretary. Trump also asked White to bring along several professional fighters to act as assistants in the administration, noting that some of these individuals are from outside the country.

In a post on Truth Social, Trump referred to White as a warrior and requested that six specific current or former UFC champions join him. Neither White nor UFC's parent organization, TKO Group Holdings, offered an immediate response to media inquiries. Whether an official offer has been formally accepted was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Proposed team members: Trump listed six athletes to assist White: Russian fighters Islam Makhachev and Khabib Nurmagomedov from Dagestan; American fighters Daniel Cormier, Justin Gaethje, and "Kayla Harris" (likely referencing Kayla Harrison); and Amanda Nunes, a Brazilian native who became a U.S. citizen last year.
  • Stated motive: Trump stated on social media that he wanted these individuals to help defend against political opponents on the left.
  • Previous press secretary departure: The position opened up after former press secretary Katie Zacharia was dismissed last month, remaining in the role for less than a week.
  • Qualifications controversy: Zacharia was let go following public scrutiny after admitting she had failed the bar exam, which is required to practice law in the U.S. She had previously referred to herself as a lawyer on television and social media, while also listing herself as general counsel for a California-based conservative organization.

Why it matters

The invitation comes shortly after rapid turnover in the White House press office. Dawn reported that Trump had advised the previous press secretary to leave the position following intense public focus on her legal qualifications. Asking a prominent sports executive and a group of combat sports athletes to handle press duties marks an unconventional proposal for managing official White House communications.
