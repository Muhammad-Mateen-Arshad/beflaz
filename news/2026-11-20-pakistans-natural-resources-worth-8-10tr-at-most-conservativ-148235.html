Pakistan’s natural resources worth $8-10tr at 'most conservative estimate', says Dar

Published on November 20, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar stated that the country's natural resources are worth between $8 trillion and $10 trillion by conservative estimates. Speaking at a ceremony in Karachi to mark the arrival of the Turkish seismic research vessel Oruc Reis, Dar rejected claims that Pakistan is trapped in debt, asserting that its natural wealth—including hydrocarbons, minerals, gemstones, and rare earth materials—far outweighs its financial obligations. The Turkish vessel arrived on Thursday to begin offshore petroleum exploration.

Key facts

  • The Oruc Reis will conduct a seismic survey expected to take five to six months to collect geological data beneath the seabed.
  • Three memorandums of understanding were signed between Pakistani and Turkish agencies to establish a long-term energy partnership covering offshore exploration, minerals, and renewable energy.
  • Turkish Petroleum Offshore Company (TPOC) is collaborating with local state-owned firms, including OGDCL, Mari Energies, and PPL. TPOC holds operatorship and a 25 percent stake in the Eastern Offshore Indus-C block in the Arabian Sea.
  • Pakistan's offshore zone covers roughly 300,000 square kilometres, but only 18 wells have been drilled there since 1947. A study by US firm DeGolyer and MacNaughton previously confirmed significant untapped hydrocarbon potential in these basins.
  • The project follows the launch of the Offshore Bid Round 2025 in January of last year, which offered 40 offshore blocks across the Indus and Makran basins.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, the mission reactivates Pakistan’s offshore exploration efforts after nearly twenty years. While acknowledging that seismic surveys do not guarantee commercial discoveries, Dar described the data collection as a vital step for future energy development. He called for direct collaboration between Turkish experts and Pakistani engineers, geologists, and universities, while emphasizing that all exploration activities must maintain high standards of maritime safety and environmental protection.
