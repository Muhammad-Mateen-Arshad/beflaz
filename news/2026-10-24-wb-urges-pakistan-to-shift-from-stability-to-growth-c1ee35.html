WB urges Pakistan to shift from stability to growth

Published on October 24, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, representatives from the World Bank held discussions with Pakistan's Finance Minister, Muhammad Aurangzeb, on Friday regarding the country's economic trajectory. The international financial institution urged Pakistan to move from economic stabilisation toward long-term, sustainable growth driven by increased investment and private sector activity.

World Bank Regional Practice Director Sandeep Mahajan commended the Pakistani government for restoring macroeconomic stability and handling economic pressures resulting from regional conflict. Mahajan attended the meeting alongside Country Director Bolormaa Amgaabazar and Simon Andrews, Division Director at the International Finance Corporation.

Key facts

  • The World Bank and the IMF have both encouraged Pakistan to transition toward sustainable growth to generate employment opportunities for youth.
  • World Bank representatives highlighted that earlier stabilisation efforts created financial buffers that helped Pakistan withstand external shocks.
  • Finance Minister Aurangzeb noted that Pakistan recently re-entered international capital markets through a $3 billion Eurobond sale featuring five-year and ten-year maturity terms.
  • Aurangzeb stated that the government prioritizes durable stability, higher investor confidence, improved credit ratings, and a stronger sovereign credit profile.
  • Discussions covered privatisation, regulatory clarity, policy predictability, and infrastructure financing through collaboration with development partners.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, transitioning from basic stabilisation to sustainable economic expansion is considered essential for job creation and private sector development in Pakistan. Both sides reaffirmed the importance of maintaining reform momentum, ensuring policy continuity, and maintaining fiscal discipline to protect economic progress against external risks.
