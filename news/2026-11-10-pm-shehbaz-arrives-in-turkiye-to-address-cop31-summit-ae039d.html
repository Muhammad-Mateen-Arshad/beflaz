PM Shehbaz arrives in Turkiye to address COP31 summit

Published on November 10, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif arrived in Antalya, Turkiye, on Tuesday for a three-day visit to participate in the Leaders’ Summit of the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31) to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change.

What happened

Upon his arrival, the prime minister was received by Turkish Deputy Culture and Tourism Minister Gökhan Yazgı, Pakistan’s Ambassador-designate Amna Baloch, and senior Turkish officials. PM Shehbaz is visiting to present Pakistan’s climate priorities, advocate for global climate action, and deliver a speech at a high-level clean energy dialogue titled “35 by 35: Powering the Future.” He is also scheduled to conduct bilateral discussions with Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and other international leaders on the sidelines of the event.

Key facts

  • The prime minister's delegation includes Senator Sherry Rehman, Information Minister Attaullah Tarar, and adviser Tariq Fatemi.
  • A "Pakistan Pavilion" has been set up at the COP31 site, where 54 themed events will take place.
  • According to the Foreign Office, PM Shehbaz will highlight Pakistan's measures regarding solarisation, renewable energy expansion, disaster preparedness, and early-warning systems.
  • He will call for predictable, adequate, and accessible climate financing to assist developing nations with sustainable development.
  • The COP31 conference opened with procedural disputes regarding climate finance demands put forward by developing countries.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that the summit comes amid ongoing debates over the global transition away from fossil fuels. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres urged nations to phase out fossil fuels, citing severe climate and economic risks. Meanwhile, summit host Turkiye is emphasizing electrification and recently announced new crude oil discoveries. Developing countries continue to press wealthier nations for increased financial support to build climate resilience, though delegates note that negotiations face challenges due to geopolitical conflicts and energy market pressures.
