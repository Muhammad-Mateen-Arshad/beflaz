Govt reduces diesel price by Rs4 per litre, petrol by Re1 per litre

Published on November 24, 2026

Dawn reported that the Pakistani government has lowered the retail price of diesel by Rs4 per litre and petrol by Re1 per litre, following an official notification from the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra). Effective Wednesday, November 25, petrol retails at Rs318 per litre, while diesel costs Rs353 per litre.

What happened

The price revision comes alongside ongoing taxation and refinery contributions. The government continues to collect Rs114 per litre in duties on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel. However, under an agreement established on November 2, domestic refineries are absorbing a cumulative cost impact of Rs15 billion over 30 days, enabling a Rs14.71 per litre price reduction for petrol consumers.

Key facts

  • Prior to this adjustment, fuel prices peaked on April 3 at Rs458.41 per litre for petrol and Rs520.35 per litre for diesel, after rising from earlier baselines of Rs266 and Rs281 respectively due to the US-Iran conflict.
  • Since July 17, Ogra has been tasked by the federal cabinet and the prime minister with determining near-daily fuel price revisions based on global market movements.
  • To assist low-income citizens, the Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme was launched nationwide on September 17, offering relief to owners of rickshaws, motorcycles, and vehicles under 800cc.
  • Active austerity measures require commercial markets to close by 9pm and reduce fuel allowances for official vehicles by 50 percent for a three-month period.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, petrol and diesel serve as the nation's primary fuel revenue sources, generating monthly sales between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes, compared to just 10,000 tonnes for kerosene. Shifts in petrol prices directly affect lower-middle and middle-class households relying on small transport and two-wheelers. Similarly, diesel price adjustments impact the wider public by directly altering costs in the heavy transport sector, power plants, and industrial generators.
