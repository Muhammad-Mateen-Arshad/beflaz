COP31 must deliver climate justice, not empty pledges, says Sherry

Published on November 14, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Senator Sherry Rehman issued a statement calling on the COP31 conference in Antalya to move beyond unfulfilled pledges and establish a dependable, accessible, and fair climate financing framework. Rehman commended Turkiye for taking on the presidency and hosting the summit, noting that its leadership provides a key opportunity to bridge divisions between developed and developing nations during a time of escalating global tensions and environmental emergencies.

Rehman cautioned that Pakistan and other climate-vulnerable countries face a destructive cycle of climate-driven disasters, growing national debt, and declining economic growth. She urged the international community to convert climate solidarity into concrete financial support and measurable results for impacted countries.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the statement detailed several critical figures and assessments regarding global climate efforts and local impacts:

  • Global subsidies for fossil fuel industries stand at an estimated $7 trillion, which Rehman described as a severe misallocation of funds given the financial deficits faced by front-line countries for adaptation and recovery.
  • Data from Climate Action Tracker indicates current global policies put the world on course for roughly 2.7 degrees Celsius of warming by the end of the century, up from an earlier estimate of 2.6 degrees Celsius.
  • The UN Secretary-General warned at the COP31 Leaders’ Summit that the world must completely phase out fossil fuels.
  • Flooding in Pakistan in 2022 impacted 33 million residents and generated approximately $30 billion in financial losses and damages.
  • Agricultural damage from floods in 2025 alone resulted in an estimated $1 billion in losses for Pakistan.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Rehman stressed that vulnerable nations are seeking climate justice, financial accountability, and a fair international structure rather than charity. She stated that the global community possesses sufficient financial capital to address these issues, but lacks the political determination to redirect funding away from polluting industries toward nations facing severe threats to food security, water availability, infrastructure, and human survival.
