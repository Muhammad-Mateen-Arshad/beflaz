'Will petrol be available in December?': Senate echoes with shortage concerns even as state minister says no supply issues

Published on October 23, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, members of Pakistan's Senate raised concerns over national petroleum availability for December amid heightened conflict in the Middle East. PPP Senator Sherry Rehman and Opposition Leader Raja Nasir Abbas questioned the government's contingency and rationing plans, citing potential risks to energy security. In response, Minister of State for Finance and Revenue Bilal Azhar Kayani stated that there are currently no fuel supply issues and that government relief schemes remain active at fuel stations.

Key facts

  • Kayani informed the Senate that more than 10 million people are receiving targeted fuel subsidies, and Rs1,676 billion has been allocated under the petroleum levy for the current year.
  • Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb previously stated that energy supplies are sufficient to last through November, but Rehman requested a formal parliamentary briefing regarding contingency plans for December.
  • Pakistan imports nearly 70 percent of its LPG requirement, while domestic production provides 30 percent. Rehman pointed out that Pakistan received five LPG cargoes last year, compared to only two expected this year.
  • Rehman noted that a finance ministry official confirmed the carbon levy collected on petroleum products is placed into the Federal Consolidated Fund rather than being earmarked for climate action or resilience projects.
  • Abbas warned that middle eastern conflicts threatening key maritime chokepoints, such as the Straits of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab, could worsen energy access, adding that parts of the country already face LPG and flour shortages.

Why it matters

The debate highlights growing concerns within Parliament regarding Pakistan's vulnerability to global energy price spikes and maritime route disruptions. With winter approaching and domestic heating demands expected to rise, lawmakers are seeking clear assurances and transparent revenue management from the federal government to maintain economic stability and protect citizens from potential energy shortages.
