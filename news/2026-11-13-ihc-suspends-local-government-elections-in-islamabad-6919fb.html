IHC suspends local government elections in Islamabad

Published on November 13, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the Islamabad High Court has issued an interim order suspending the upcoming local government elections in the federal capital. Justice Umair Majeed Malik placed the Election Commission of Pakistan's Oct 21, 2026 election schedule and order on hold following a writ petition filed by Chaudhry Amjad Ali.

Key facts

  • The local polls were originally scheduled for Dec 20, but all related activities are halted until the next court hearing on Nov 26, 2026, Dawn reported.
  • The petitioner was represented in court by Barrister Qazi Adil Aziz, Malik Talha, and Muhammad Yaseen Iqbal.
  • The court instructed the respondents to submit their reports and detailed comments before the next scheduled date. The specific list of respondents was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The legal dispute arises from legislative shifts between the Islamabad Capital Territory Local Government Act of 2015 and the Local Government Ordinance of 2026.
  • The 2015 framework provided for one Metropolitan Corporation and 125 Union Councils divided into nine wards each.
  • The 2026 Ordinance modified the structure to three Town Corporations and 130 Union Councils based on census blocks, removing the nine-ward system.
  • Following a 120-day extension, the 2026 Ordinance lapsed under Article 89 of the Constitution, reviving the 2015 Act. However, the Election Commission issued an order to hold polls under the restored 2015 Act using constituency boundaries drawn under the lapsed ordinance.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, the petitioner argued that conducting elections based on boundary delimitations created under an expired ordinance violates constitutional mandates under Article 218(3) as well as existing local government laws. Legal counsel maintained that new delimitation procedures and updated electoral rolls under the 2015 Act and the Elections Act of 2017 must be completed prior to holding polls to prevent the misuse of public funds. The court determined that the legal issues raised warranted further review, leading to the temporary suspension of the election schedule.
