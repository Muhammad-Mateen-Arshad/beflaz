Flour gets costlier as imports stay insufficient

Published on November 17, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, wheat and flour prices across Pakistan continue to climb despite the arrival of imported grain intended to ease shortfalls. Retailers have raised the price of flour No. 2.5 to Rs180 per kilogram and fine flour to Rs200 per kilogram, representing a Rs10 per kilogram increase linked to higher milling charges. Although over 100,000 tonnes of imported wheat have arrived since late October, open market wheat prices quickly rebounded to Rs140 per kilogram after briefly dropping to Rs130 following the arrival of the first shipment.

Key facts

  • Import Shipments: Over 100,000 tonnes of imported wheat have reached the country since the final week of October, starting with an initial cargo of 51,000 tonnes on October 28.
  • Production Deficit: Official figures from the Economic Survey FY26 show national production increased by 4.3 percent to 29.61 million tonnes from 28.40 million tonnes in the previous year. However, annual demand ranges between 31 million and 32 million tonnes due to population growth exceeding 3 percent annually.
  • Industry Warnings: Cereal Association of Pakistan Chairman Muzammil Chappal reported warning the government in May about an anticipated 4 million tonne deficit and requesting permission for the private sector to import 2 million tonnes, which went unheeded.
  • Price Adjustments: Millers raised 50kg bag prices for flour No. 2.5, Maida, and Fine Flour to Rs8,100, Rs9,000, and Rs9,150, respectively. High-quality Chakki flour has reached Rs200 per kilogram in wholesale and Rs215 in retail.
  • Harvest Timeline: New wheat crops from Sindh and Punjab will not enter the market until between March and May next year.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, market observers and traders highlight severe concerns over government oversight, noting an absence of campaigns against hoarding, market speculation, and stock pilfering. In Karachi, where flour mills require 80,000 bags of 100kg daily, millers face a severe shortage as grain movements from Punjab have stopped while the region awaits imported supplies. Rising costs are also affecting Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Additionally, tandoor operators face dual cost pressures from expensive flour and rising LPG prices, though traditional bread rates currently remain unchanged.
