Silicon Valley elites 'bankrolled' news platform Visegrad24 publishing pro-Israel content: report

Published on November 19, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn citing an investigation by Wired magazine, Polish right-wing media outlet Visegrad24 intentionally engineered a pro-Israel editorial stance to raise money from influential Silicon Valley figures and wealthy donors. The platform, founded in 2020 by Stefan Tompson, experienced a massive surge in views on the social media platform X following the October 7, 2023 attack by Hamas.

Dawn stated that it has not independently verified the internal communications or documents mentioned in the report. However, internal records reviewed by Wired indicated that the outlet's editorial stance was chosen specifically to monetize high-profile coverage of the Gaza conflict.

Key facts

  • Social media impact: Within three days following October 7, 2023, Visegrad24 published nearly 400 posts that accumulated 370 million views on X.
  • Editorial decision: In internal text messages, Tompson weighed four options for covering the conflict—saying nothing, remaining objective, taking a pro-Muslim stance, or taking a pro-Israel stance. He chose the pro-Israel stance to raise funds from influential and wealthy supporters.
  • High-level networking: Tompson was recommended by Sequoia Capital partner Shaun Maguire and added to group chats containing prominent figures from Silicon Valley, Wall Street, and Hollywood focused on building support for Israel.
  • Financial backing: Visegrad24 raised over $1 million in total donations. This included $175,000 from DonorsTrust, a conservative donor-advised fund, as well as contributions linked to executives at various tech and investment firms. Individual donation breakdowns for all listed parties were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Dual strategy: Messages showed Tompson also instructed a colleague to solicit potential funding from Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates to cover both sides.
  • Denials: Tompson provided a blanket denial via a lawyer's email and declined an interview with Wired. Named individuals either declined to comment, reported having no donation records, or denied that the outlet was a far-right group.

Why it matters

The report illustrates how online influence can be strategically aligned with specific editorial positions to secure significant financial backing and visibility from powerful global elites and dark-money philanthropic funds.
