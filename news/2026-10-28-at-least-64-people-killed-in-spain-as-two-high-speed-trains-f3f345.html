At least 64 people killed in Spain as two high-speed trains collide

Published on October 28, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, at least 64 people have been killed after two high-speed trains collided in Toledo, Spain. The rear-end crash took place shortly after 9:00 a.m. on the rail route linking Madrid to Seville, resulting in two carriages of an Iryo train derailing and sliding down a steep embankment. Emergency responders, including hundreds of ambulances, funeral vans, and helicopters, gathered at the scene, while local volunteers supplied water to injured passengers who were evacuated along a nearby road. Castilla-La Mancha regional leader Emiliano Garcia-Page reported that the fatality count is expected to rise further.

Key facts

  • The crash involved services operated by rail companies Renfe and Iryo.
  • Network operator Adif suspended all train traffic along the Madrid-Andalusia high-speed line.
  • The municipal council of nearby Urda was asked to set up emergency facilities, such as a sports hall, to aid victims and travelers.
  • The specific cause of the derailment and the total count of injured individuals: Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

This event represents the second fatal high-speed train disaster in Spain this year, according to Dawn. A prior derailment and collision in Cordoba province during January resulted in 46 deaths and raised questions regarding national investments in railway track maintenance. Following Wednesday's collision, Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez cleared his official agenda, while the opposition People's Party suspended all political campaign events. International political leaders, including Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and President Asif Ali Zardari, also issued messages of sympathy and support to the Spanish government and affected families.
