President, PM agree on resolving 'all matters' through dialogue amid rising political temperatures

Published on November 19, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif met in Islamabad on Thursday and agreed to address all national issues through mutual consultation and dialogue. The high-level meeting took place amid rising political tension inside the ruling coalition following sharp criticism from Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari regarding previous government decisions.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the meeting and surrounding political context involve several key elements:

  • Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi, who was recently criticized by Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari, was present at the Islamabad meeting.
  • The President and Prime Minister reviewed the current domestic political situation, evaluated past constitutional amendments, and discussed the ongoing Middle East conflict.
  • The Presidency emphasized in a statement that prior constitutional amendments were enacted through mutual agreement among all coalition allies.
  • Friction within the PML-N-led government increased after Naqvi proposed a governance reset in July, triggering debates over creating new administrative units.
  • The PPP opposes dividing the province of Sindh, citing a unanimous resolution passed by the Sindh Assembly, though President Zardari has not made public statements on the matter.
  • Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari voiced opposition to a proposed referendum on new provinces and warned that forced division of Sindh could lead to bloodshed.
  • The PPP chairman advocated for a political reset to help address growing economic challenges and rebuild opposition confidence in Parliament.
  • Further details regarding exact scheduling or official government notifications were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the decision to rely on dialogue comes as public disagreement over legislative processes and administrative reforms spread among key allies, including the PML-N, PPP, and Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P). Recent disputes have touched upon significant legislative milestones like the 27th Amendment, making joint consultations essential for maintaining coalition unity and managing constitutional procedures.
