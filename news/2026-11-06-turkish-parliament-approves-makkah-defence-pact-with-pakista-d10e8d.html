Turkish parliament approves Makkah defence pact with Pakistan, Saudi Arabia

Published on November 06, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the Turkish parliament voted on Thursday to approve the Makkah Agreement on Joint Defence alongside Pakistan and Saudi Arabia. Out of 592 parliamentary members, 398 participated in the ballot, which passed with 279 votes in favor and 119 opposed. This vote enables the trilateral security pact to officially come into force, following the prior completion of ratification procedures by both Pakistan and Saudi Arabia.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the agreement includes the following key provisions and legislative details:

  • The pact was initially signed on Aug 7 at a summit in Makkah to enhance collective security and deepen defense ties.
  • It establishes a framework for mutual assistance if any of the three participating countries faces external armed aggression, with the specifics of assistance to be determined jointly.
  • Ruling Justice and Development Party lawmaker Ziya Altunyaldiz stated during parliamentary debate that the accord aims to prevent conflicts and expand security without being directed at any third country.
  • Opposition lawmaker Mehmet Guzelmansur of the Republican People's Party expressed reservations over the agreement's scope and oversight, calling for clear rules to avoid conflicts with Turkiye's existing international obligations.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the ratification brings the alliance closer to full implementation. An Oct 5 meeting of the Strategic, Political and Defence Committee in Riyadh previously urged the immediate execution of collective defense commitments and the rapid deployment of agreed military forces to Saudi Arabia, subject to national laws. This development comes amid rising regional tensions stemming from attacks on Saudi Arabia by Yemen's Ansar Allah movement. Further specifics regarding troop levels or exact implementation dates are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
