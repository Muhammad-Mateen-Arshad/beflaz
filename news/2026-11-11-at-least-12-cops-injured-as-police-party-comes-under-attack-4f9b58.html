At least 12 cops injured as police party comes under attack in KP's Kohat

Published on November 11, 2026

According to Dawn, a police party was targeted in an armed attack on Wednesday in the Gumbat tehsil of Kohat, located in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. At least 12 police personnel sustained injuries and were moved to a hospital following the incident, as confirmed by Rescue 1122 spokesperson Jawad Afridi. The contingent came under fire in the Darbukach area while carrying out an operation against illegal gold mining linked to criminal and terrorist networks.

What happened

The attack occurred within the jurisdiction of the Gumbat police station during an official crackdown on unlawful gold extraction. Statements from local police identified Fitna al Khawarij—a designation used by authorities for the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan—as the perpetrators of the assault. Additional police forces were dispatched to the site, leading to an ongoing exchange of fire in the area.

Key facts

  • Rescue officials confirmed shifting 12 injured officers to the hospital, with reports from injured personnel indicating that multiple bodies remained at the scene. The total number of casualties is Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The attack took place a day after the Kohat deputy commissioner issued a 60-day ban on parking excavators near designated placer gold areas and banned the open sale of petroleum products intended for unauthorized mining.
  • Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi requested a detailed report on the event, condemned the attack, and expressed sorrow over the fallen personnel.
  • Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry expressed grief over the loss of life and called on institutions to hold the perpetrators accountable.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the ambush aligns with a recent surge in militant violence targeting law enforcement officers across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, including prior attacks in Mohmand, Bajaur, North Waziristan, and Kohat. Data from the Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies indicates ongoing security instability in the region, which has been operating under governor's rule and emergency measures following federal actions regarding counterterrorism oversight.
