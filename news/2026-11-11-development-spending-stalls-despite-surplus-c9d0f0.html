Development spending stalls despite surplus

Published on November 11, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn based on data from the Ministry of Planning and Development, expenditure under Pakistan's Public Sector Development Programme (PSDP) reached Rs103.87 billion during the first four months (July–October) of fiscal year 2026–27. This accounts for 10.4 percent of the annual Rs1 trillion budget allocation. The actual usage of funds remained far below the Rs412.3 billion authorised by the Planning Commission, despite the country posting a 0.8 percent budget surplus in the first quarter.

Key facts

Figures released by the ministry outline spending across various sectors and state entities:

  • Federal Ministries: Approximately 33 ministries and divisions spent Rs74.65 billion, or about 11 percent of their total Rs682 billion allocation. Six federal ministries recorded zero development spending, including commerce, communication, petroleum, privatisation, religious affairs and interfaith harmony, and Kashmir affairs, Gilgit-Baltistan and states and frontier regions.
  • State Corporations: Corporations collectively spent Rs29 billion, representing 9.3 percent of their Rs313 billion annual budget. The National Highways Authority spent Rs13.3 billion (5.8 percent of its Rs225 billion allocation), while the power sector spent Rs16 billion (18 percent of its Rs98 billion allocation).
  • Department Breakdown: The Water Resources Division recorded the largest outlay among ministries at Rs18.7 billion (18 percent of Rs103 billion). The National Health Services Division registered a 41 percent utilization rate by spending Rs6.6 billion of its Rs16 billion allocation. Federal Education spent Rs6.88 billion (19 percent), the Higher Education Commission spent Rs4.7 billion (10 percent), Railways spent Rs4.65 billion (11 percent), and Climate Change spent Rs259 million (10.4 percent). National Food Security spent Rs65 million, representing under 1.5 percent of its allocation.
  • Provinces and Special Regions: Combined spending for provinces and special areas (Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan) reached Rs23.8 billion, which equals 10 percent of the annual Rs233 billion target.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that slow progress on public-interest projects adversely impacts living standards and national economic growth. The Ministry of Finance operates a quarterly fund release mechanism (15 percent in the first quarter, 20 percent in the second, 25 percent in the third, and the remainder in the fourth) designed to manage potential tax revenue shortfalls by curbing development spending to align with fiscal targets set with the International Monetary Fund. Specific project-level reasons for spending delays are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
