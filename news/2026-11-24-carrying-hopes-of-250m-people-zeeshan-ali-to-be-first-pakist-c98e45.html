'Carrying hopes of 250m people': Zeeshan Ali to be first Pakistani in space aboard China's Shenzhou-24

Published on November 24, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Zeeshan Ali has been selected as a crew member for China's upcoming Shenzhou-24 space mission. The announcement by the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) marks Ali as the first Pakistani national to travel into space, as well as the first foreign astronaut to board China's Tiangong space station. During the mission, Ali will fulfill the role of a payload specialist.

Key facts

  • The three-person crew consists of Zeeshan Ali alongside Chinese astronauts Tang Hongbo and Wu Yi.
  • The spacecraft is scheduled to launch at 8:18 am Beijing Time (5:18 am Pakistan Time) on Wednesday, November 25, from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China.
  • Ali is expected to remain in space for around a week to conduct various experiments with the crew.
  • The mission includes completing around 90 scientific experiments, performing regular station maintenance, and executing an orbital rotation with the Shenzhou-23 crew. The exact list of scientific payloads was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Ali will return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, which docked with the space station in May.
  • Ali and Khurram Daud were selected for training in China in April, following a February 2025 agreement between CMSA and Pakistan's space agency, Suparco.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that the joint initiative underscores the growing space cooperation between Pakistan and China, arriving ahead of the 75th anniversary of their space cooperation and diplomatic relations in 2026. Speaking at a press conference in Jiayuguan, Ali expressed appreciation to the Chinese government and instructors, emphasizing that the mission reflects the close friendship between the two countries. Pakistani leadership, including Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal, and Information Minister Attaullah Tarar, commended the milestone as a historical achievement for Pakistan. The joint effort also forms part of China's expanding space program, which aims to land astronauts on the Moon by 2030.
