DG ISPR says Pakistan 'cautiously optimistic' on US-Iran mediation

Published on October 30, 2026

What happened

Pakistan is cautiously optimistic regarding its mediation efforts between Iran and the United States, according to statements made by Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR) Director General Lieutenant General Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry reported by Dawn. Speaking in Rawalpindi, Chaudhry referenced recent high-level diplomatic visits to Tehran led by Pakistan's Chief of Defence Forces and Chief of Army Staff, Field Marshal Asim Munir.

Field Marshal Munir visited Tehran twice during the month, with the most recent trip taking place on Monday alongside Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin. Chaudhry noted that these visits indicate positive progress, though he refrained from detailing what was discussed or confirming if the United States backed the mission. Not specified in the source; please check the official notice for further details on the specific talk topics.

Key facts

  • Diplomatic Engagement: The Monday meeting was the first high-level interaction between Iranian officials and members of the Makkah Defence Alliance since Saudi Arabia became more deeply involved in the regional conflict.
  • Makkah Defence Alliance: Formed on August 7, the mutual defense treaty includes Saudi Arabia and Turkiye, stipulating that an attack on one member is viewed as an attack on all.
  • Potential Membership: Chaudhry stated that the alliance pact does not exclude new members, noting that any nation sharing a vision for collective deterrence and peace could join if the political leadership of the three founding countries agrees.
  • Iranian Stance: Following Monday's discussions, Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, stated that regional security must be managed by countries in the region without external intervention.
  • Military Commitments: Pakistan reaffirmed its commitment to defending Saudi Arabia and the holy sites of Makkah and Madina. Chaudhry clarified that Pakistan is not participating in the intra-Yemen conflict, and any decision regarding additional troop deployments to Saudi Arabia depends on political decisions and situational needs.
  • Counter-Terrorism Operations: Regarding Khyber Pakhtunkhwa following the imposition of emergency and governor's rules, Chaudhry stated that the army will not launch broader campaigns but will instead increase the pace and operational freedom of intelligence-based operations (IBOs).

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the conflict involving Iran has reshaped regional alliances and heightened concerns over a broader Middle East escalation, making the Makkah Defence Alliance increasingly significant. While Chaudhry acknowledged that unidentified detractors and spoilers who benefit from conflict still exist, Pakistan continues to engage with regional partners to foster stability and collective deterrence.
