Pakistan among 15 nations welcoming UK, Australia sanctions on Israeli settlements, hoping others follow suit

Published on November 20, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, foreign ministers from 15 nations, including Pakistan, issued a joint statement welcoming sanctions recently imposed by the United Kingdom and Australia against Israeli settlement activities in the occupied Palestinian territory. The nations expressed hope that the actions taken by the UK and Australia would encourage other countries to implement similar measures.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the key details of the measures and statement include:

  • The United Kingdom announced asset freezes and director disqualifications targeting eight Israeli construction companies involved in West Bank settlement projects, as well as an organization blocking humanitarian aid deliveries to Gaza.
  • Australia placed sanctions on three individuals—including settler group leader Daniella Weiss and construction business owner Eliav Libi—and four organizations for involvement in illegal settlement expansion, recruitment, fundraising, and settler violence.
  • Australian measures prohibit citizens and domestic businesses from trading with or providing assistance to the sanctioned persons and entities.
  • The joint statement was released by foreign ministers representing Pakistan, Jordan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, Indonesia, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Turkiye, Kuwait, Algeria, Niger, Palestine, Libya, and the Maldives.
  • The ministers strongly condemned Israeli settlement policies, particularly the E1 settlement project, which would divide the West Bank and separate it from East Jerusalem.
  • The diplomatic group also called for an end to military escalation in Gaza and urged the full and immediate implementation of President Trump's Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the 15 foreign ministers stated that the British and Australian sanctions accord with international law and relevant United Nations resolutions regarding non-recognition of illegal occupations. Australian Foreign Minister Penny Wong noted that settlement activities and surrounding violence undermine the viability of a two-state solution. The joint statement reaffirmed that a peaceful resolution requires a sovereign, contiguous, and independent Palestinian state based on the June 4, 1967 borders, with East Jerusalem serving as its capital.
