Eisenkot says Israel in 'devastating diplomatic crisis' as he seeks to form govt

Published on November 08, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, former Israeli military commander Gadi Eisenkot has begun efforts to form a new government after receiving an official mandate from President Isaac Herzog. Eisenkot warned that Israel faces a profound diplomatic crisis and has reached a critical moment under Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. He called for an emergency national government to address deep internal polarization and growing international isolation.

What happened

Eisenkot was granted 28 days to build a ruling coalition despite his political bloc lacking a clear majority. During a ceremony at the presidential office, he appealed to rival factions, including Netanyahu's Likud Party and Bezalel Smotrich's far-right party, to join a coalition led by him as prime minister. Netanyahu called for a unity government among Zionist parties while ruling out alliances with Arab parties, though he gave no indication of whether he would serve under Eisenkot. Specific details regarding the exact dates of the preceding elections were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Eisenkot has 28 days to assemble a government, with the possibility of a 14-day extension. Failure could result in Netanyahu receiving the mandate or new elections being held.
  • Potential paths to power include forming a minority government relying on outside votes from Arab-Israeli parties, aligning with ultra-Orthodox parties, or forging a unity government with Netanyahu.
  • Political expert Assaf Shapira noted that a government backed by only 48 lawmakers in the 120-member parliament would face extreme instability.
  • Eisenkot's core conditions include defining Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, mandatory national service for all, and completely dismantling Hamas's military and governing capabilities.
  • Eisenkot shares Netanyahu's opposition to Palestinian statehood and supports West Bank settlements, though he has pledged to curb violence committed by extremist settlers.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that Israel is encountering severe international friction, with close strategic allies reassessing their relationships and Netanyahu facing International Criminal Court warrants over allegations of war crimes in Gaza. President Herzog cited threats from Iran, along with ongoing diplomatic consultations involving Gaza, Lebanon, and regional neighbors. Additionally, recent US midterm election results showed Democratic gains accompanied by calls to limit Israel following conflicts in Gaza and Iran. If Eisenkot succeeds, he will become Israel's first Sephardic prime minister.
