Govt moves to overhaul ‘weak’ audit system

Published on November 23, 2026

What happened

The Pakistani government has moved to restructure its national audit system following warnings that the current regulatory structure is weak, according to Dawn. The federal cabinet has approved the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) (Amendment) Bill, 2026. This approval comes after reviews by three ministerial-level committees and feedback from relevant stakeholders. The proposed legislation stems from recommendations by the Prime Minister’s Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) Task Force. A similar bill was previously passed by the National Assembly in 2021, but it was subsequently withdrawn from the Senate in 2022 to undergo resubmission.

Why it matters

According to documents cited by Dawn, weak audit practices have hurt local and international investment while restricting access to capital markets. The existing framework governing the Audit Oversight Board (AOB) was found to be non-compliant with international standards. Consequently, the International Organisation of Securities Commissions declared Pakistan non-compliant regarding independent audit oversight, and the European Union withdrew Pakistan’s equivalence status. Weak audit frameworks and disclosures have previously impacted entities such as Unity Foods, SE Foods, and Hascol. The proposed reforms aim to shift Pakistan from self-regulatory accountancy oversight toward an independent oversight model, aligning the country with standard international regulatory practices.

Key facts

Key details of the proposed audit reforms include:

  • The FBR chairman will replace the president of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Pakistan on the nominating committee for AOB board appointments.
  • AOB board members will be strictly prohibited from maintaining any affiliations with audit firms.
  • The AOB board will concentrate on policy, leaving management, operational, and administrative duties to separate governance units.
  • The AOB will perform risk-based inspections of auditors overseeing Public Interest Companies (PICs), while traditional accountancy bodies will continue supervising non-PIC audits under AOB oversight.
  • The AOB will be granted authority to directly investigate complaints originating from the SECP, State Bank of Pakistan, and FBR, as well as file formal complaints over criminal falsification.
  • The specific timetable for parliamentary submission and final implementation is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
