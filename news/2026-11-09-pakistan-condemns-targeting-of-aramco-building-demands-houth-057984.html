Pakistan condemns targeting of Aramco building, demands Houthis cease all attacks against Saudi Arabia

Published on November 09, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan's Foreign Office issued a statement condemning a Houthi rebel attack that targeted a Saudi Aramco corporate facility in Riyadh. The Pakistani government demanded that the group immediately and unconditionally cease all strikes against Saudi Arabia.

The Saudi energy ministry reported that an attempted attack occurred on Sunday, resulting in damage to a skyscraper in the King Abdullah Financial District. Houthi rebels claimed responsibility for the strike. Coalition spokesperson Turki al-Malki stated that Saudi air defenses intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched toward the capital. Pakistan's Foreign Office confirmed that no casualties were reported and praised the prompt response of Saudi authorities.

Key facts

  • The damaged building is situated in the King Abdullah Financial District, a hub that houses major domestic and international entities such as Goldman Sachs, the Saudi stock exchange, and the Public Investment Fund.
  • Witnesses in the area reported hearing loud explosions and observing physical damage to the Aramco tower. The full extent of structural damage was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Houthi rebels based in Yemen have repeatedly targeted Aramco's assets in recent months, including storage areas, refineries, oil tankers, and corporate offices.
  • The strike occurred hours after the Saudi education ministry resumed in-person classes in Riyadh following weeks of remote instruction prompted by earlier attacks.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Pakistan reiterated its firm solidarity with Saudi Arabia, describing the kingdom's security and sovereignty as being of paramount importance. Both nations are bound by defense pacts, including the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement and the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement, which also involves Turkiye.

Under the Makkah alliance, representatives from Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye recently met in Riyadh to arrange the rapid deployment of military assets to defend the kingdom. Military spokesperson Lieutenant General Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry clarified that Pakistan is providing necessary defense support, emphasizing that the coalition is strictly defensive in nature with no offensive intent.
