Dar, Singapore FM discuss possible resumption of direct Pakistan-Singapore flights

Published on October 30, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar held talks and addressed a joint press conference with Singaporean Foreign Minister Vivian Balakrishnan during his official visit to Pakistan. The meeting marks the first visit to Pakistan by a Singaporean foreign minister in more than 20 years. During their talks, both sides held delegation-level and restricted discussions focused on expanding diplomatic, economic, and technological cooperation, including the potential resumption of direct flights between the two countries.

Key facts

  • Diplomatic History: The meeting commemorates 60 years of diplomatic relations. Pakistan was one of the first countries to recognise Singapore following its independence in 1965.
  • Direct Flights: The foreign ministers discussed restarting direct flights to help facilitate business trips and direct contact between citizens. Specific timelines or flight arrangements were not provided (Not specified in the source; please check the official notice).
  • Trade and Commerce: The two sides discussed reopening the Trade and Investment Office at Pakistan's High Commission in Singapore and encouraging exchanges between business delegations and chambers of commerce.
  • Broad Cooperation: Discussions covered expanded partnership in artificial intelligence, digital technologies, higher education, research, climate domains, agriculture, and human resource development.
  • ASEAN and Regional Issues: Dar noted Pakistan's position as ASEAN's oldest sectoral dialogue partner and backed Singapore's upcoming ASEAN chairmanship in 2027.
  • Global and Regional Peace: Dar voiced concern over maritime supply chain security, the humanitarian situation in Gaza and West Bank settlement expansions, and highlighted the importance of resolving the Jammu and Kashmir dispute under UN Security Council resolutions.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the talks signal a shared desire to unlock untapped potential in trade, technology, and regional ties after two decades without a Singaporean foreign minister visiting Pakistan. Re-establishing direct air links and reopening trade facilities are intended to bolster commercial interaction and strengthen broader bilateral relations.
