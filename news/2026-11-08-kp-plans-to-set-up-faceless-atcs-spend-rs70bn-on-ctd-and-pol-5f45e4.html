KP plans to set up faceless ATCs, spend Rs70bn on CTD and police in a year as part of initiatives against terrorism

Published on November 08, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the administrative setup in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) has announced plans to establish faceless anti-terrorism courts and allocate Rs70 billion to the police and Counter-Terrorism Department over the course of one year. Speaking at a televised briefing alongside KP Governor Faisal Karim Kundi, KP Home Secretary Amjad Ali Khan stated that a legal amendment has been submitted to set up the anonymous court system. The measure is intended to safeguard witnesses, prosecutors, and judges who face intimidation during terrorism trials.

Key facts

  • Planned funding: The provincial setup intends to spend Rs70 billion within a year to build operational capacity for police and counterterrorism forces.
  • Force shortages: Khan noted a deficit exceeding 10,000 police personnel, inadequate security infrastructure, and a shortage of SWAT units. In merged districts, security accounts for just 3.2 percent of the budget, leaving high reliance on former Fata Khasadaars and Levies who lack adequate training.
  • Equipment gaps: Defense capabilities against militant drone usage are currently running at only 12 percent capacity.
  • Human toll: Over the previous two to three years, terrorism-related incidents caused 1,003 casualties among armed forces personnel, 627 among police personnel, and 808 among civilians.
  • Political background: The central government declared governor’s rule and an emergency in KP on October 11, pointing to disengagement by the PTI-led provincial government. Khan cited past political disownership, provincial actions that conflicted with federal Afghan policy, and anti-security propaganda that undermined counterterrorism work.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, these proposed initiatives are designed to address critical resource deficits and protect judicial participants following years of heavy casualties among civilians and security personnel. The plan seeks to improve coordination between the public, political figures, and security agencies. Exact dates for when the new courts will become operational were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
