Makkah pact nations review regional security, condemn Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia

Published on November 27, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, the Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC) of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement held a two-and-a-half-hour meeting in Pakistan's federal capital. High-level officials from Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye convened to review regional security developments and advance trilateral defence cooperation. The exact date of the meeting was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What happened

The decision-making body evaluated the evolving security situation across the region and formally condemned persistent Houthi attacks targeting civilians and civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia. According to statements reported by Dawn, the participating states reaffirmed their resolve to support Saudi Arabia's security, sovereignty, and territorial integrity under the collective defence provisions of the agreement.

Authorities declared a public holiday in the federal capital and a local holiday in Rawalpindi due to movement restrictions implemented for the high-level gathering. Outside the main session, Pakistani Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar held bilateral discussions with Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan regarding cooperation in trade, investment, defence, and energy.

Key facts

  • Delegations: The meeting brought together foreign ministers, defence ministers, and military chiefs from all three member countries.
  • Pakistani Officials: Attended by Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, Defence Minister Khawaja Asif, and Chief of Army Staff General Asim Munir.
  • Saudi Officials: Attended by Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan, and Chief of General Staff Gen Fayyadh bin Hamed Al-Ruwaili.
  • Turkish Officials: Attended by Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Defence Minister Yasar Guler, and Chief of General Staff Gen Selcuk Bayraktaroglu.
  • Alliance Secretariat: The SPDC reviewed past decisions and expressed satisfaction with progress toward establishing an alliance secretariat in Saudi Arabia.

Why it matters

According to statements cited by Dawn, the pact formalises collective defence, mutual solidarity, and respect for sovereignty among the three partner nations. The member states pledged to enhance joint technological development, training, and defence industry cooperation to improve military cohesion, strengthen self-reliance, and encourage peaceful resolution of regional conflicts.
