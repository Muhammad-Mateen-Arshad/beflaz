At least 28 killed, including 27 students, in Peshawar school roof collapse

Published on October 15, 2026

What happened

At least 28 people were killed when the roof of Al-Khair School collapsed in the Afridi Abad area of Peshawar, according to Dawn. The two-storey private school building caved in while students were inside their classrooms, trapping occupants beneath the fallen rubble. Police officials noted that the building had a weak structure and that recent construction on a upper floor made the roof overweight, causing it to collapse onto the lower storey.

Emergency medical services were activated at Lady Reading Hospital following the incident, and local police established a dedicated "green corridor" to allow ambulances to transport the injured without traffic delays. Local authorities confirmed that search and rescue teams cleared the debris and concluded operations at the site.

Key facts

  • The casualties include 27 students and one female teacher, while 19 other people sustained injuries, as reported by Dawn.
  • Peshawar Capital City Police Officer Dr Mian Saeed Ahmed attributed the structural failure to heavy construction built on an unstable structure.
  • Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi, President Asif Ali Zardari, and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed grief and directed authorities to conduct a probe and offer full medical treatment to victims.
  • The specific deadline for the official inquiry report was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, roof and building collapses are a persistent issue in Pakistan, frequently caused by weak safety protocols and substandard building materials. The Peshawar tragedy adds to a series of recent structural failures involving educational facilities and private residences across regions such as Lahore, Dera Ghazi Khan, and Umerkot. Government officials stressed that safety guidelines across educational facilities must be enforced to prevent similar incidents.
