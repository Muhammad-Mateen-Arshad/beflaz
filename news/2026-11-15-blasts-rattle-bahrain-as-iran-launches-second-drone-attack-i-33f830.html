Blasts rattle Bahrain as Iran launches second drone attack in 3 days

Published on November 15, 2026

Bahrain was shaken by explosions on Sunday following an Iranian drone strike, marking the second such attack within three days and ending a multi-month pause in strikes, according to Dawn. Authorities sounded sirens and posted social media warnings advising residents to seek cover, with blasts reported around 4:00 a.m. and 6:30 a.m.

What happened

According to Dawn, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards claimed that six drones belonging to its Aerospace Force targeted an American air defence radar system at the Al-Riffa helicopter base in Bahrain. In response, Bahrain’s military and foreign ministry announced that their air defence forces intercepted the drones and stopped them from hitting their intended targets. The interior ministry urged citizens to remain calm, locate safe shelter, and monitor official news channels.

Key facts

  • Iran also launched a drone assault against Bahrain on Friday, which followed US strikes on Qeshm island earlier in the month.
  • US Central Command reported shooting down two Iranian drones during Friday's incident, with no casualties or damage recorded.
  • Whether any casualties or physical damage occurred during Sunday's attack is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards stated that the Strait of Hormuz is closed and threatened stronger counteractions against further US actions.
  • Bahrain's foreign ministry stated that the nation refuses to let its territory be used to settle regional disputes and holds Iran entirely responsible for the strikes.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that the latest strikes break a lull in attacks on Gulf nations that host American military infrastructure, such as Bahrain's major naval facilities. Over the course of the eight-month war—which started on February 28 after US-Israeli strikes killed Iranian Ayatollah Ali Khamenei—Iran has disrupted oil shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and hit regional energy, port, and military assets. While months of diplomacy have failed to stop the hostilities, US President Donald Trump plans to address the war in a trilateral meeting with Russian and Chinese leadership this week.
