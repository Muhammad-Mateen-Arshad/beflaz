In hard-hitting speech, Bilawal says system was ‘made to collapse’ amid debate on governance ‘reset’

Published on November 17, 2026

According to Dawn, Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari stated on Tuesday that the country's governance system did not collapse on its own, but was rather intentionally "made to collapse." Speaking at the Karachi Press Club, Bilawal addressed ongoing national discussions regarding a potential system reset while acknowledging his own shared responsibility in past political mistakes.

What happened

According to Dawn, Bilawal responded to recent remarks by Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi, who called for creating new administrative units after claiming the current governance framework had collapsed. Bilawal noted that while there is widespread agreement regarding the system's failure, there has been little meaningful debate about why it occurred. He stressed that proposed remedies do not address the underlying causes of the breakdown and insisted that any necessary reset must be political.

Key facts

  • According to Dawn, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi previously stirred political discussion by advocating for a governance reset and new administrative units.
  • Bilawal asserted that politicians must lead any reset because they serve as the direct representatives of the public.
  • He emphasized that determining why the system broke down is essential to tackling major public issues, including poverty, unemployment, and inflation.
  • Bilawal stated that state institutions have lost their legitimacy and called for steps to restore public trust in them.
  • He admitted that everyone makes mistakes and called for action to correct past governance errors.
  • Further details regarding specific reform proposals or official schedules were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the statements highlight a growing national debate over institutional reforms and the role of elected officials. Bilawal argued that public grievances regarding economic hardship cannot be resolved unless political leaders actively address the root causes of systemic failure and restore legitimacy to state institutions.
