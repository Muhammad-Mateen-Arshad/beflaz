Banks, utilities among services put on alert against AI-enabled cyberthreats

Published on November 06, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, the National Cyber Emergency Response Team (NCERT) issued an advisory on Nov 5 warning organizations about an escalation in cyberthreats leveraging artificial intelligence (AI) and automated bots. Malicious actors are increasingly deploying botnets, agentic tools, and automated scripts to scout for system vulnerabilities, harvest credentials, and execute synchronized cyberattacks.

While NCERT emphasized that all sectors should remain vigilant, it highlighted that financial institutions and operational technology managing critical infrastructure face the highest exposure to these rapidly evolving automated threats.

Key facts

The advisory outlined several key risk areas, indicators of potential compromise, and sector-specific warnings:

  • Threats to banking: NCERT warned that financial entities risk attacks targeting digital platforms, payment gateways, customer applications, and core banking databases. These attacks could cause service outages, credential theft, or unauthorized alteration of displayed account balances.
  • Risks to critical utilities: Facilities across power generation, water utilities, oil and gas, manufacturing, and transport networks face potential disruptions to physical operations due to exposed industrial control systems, connected IoT devices, third-party vendor access, or insecure remote connections.
  • Warning indicators: Organizations were alerted to monitor suspicious signals such as frequent automated login failures, abnormal API traffic, transaction irregularities, unexpected remote access sessions, and unauthorized modifications to operational settings like temperature, pressure, or grid parameters.
  • Real-world examples: The alert referenced recent cyberattacks in Europe targeting supermarket refrigeration systems to illustrate the operational and financial impact of compromises, though NCERT noted that AI involvement in those specific cases has not been confirmed.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the integration of AI tools allows cybercriminals to increase the speed, volume, and complexity of their attacks. A successful breach of utility networks or banking services could result in physical disruptions and significant economic damage.

To defend against these threats, NCERT recommended that financial providers implement multi-factor authentication, restrict bot activity, protect APIs, and verify data consistency between public apps and core records. For utility operators, the agency urged limiting internet exposure, segmenting operational networks from IT infrastructure, updating security patches, and maintaining offline emergency procedures. All organizations were advised to retain incident logs and strengthen response strategies. For further actionable technical guidance, details are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
