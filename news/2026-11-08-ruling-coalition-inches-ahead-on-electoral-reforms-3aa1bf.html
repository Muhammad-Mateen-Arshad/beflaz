Ruling coalition inches ahead on electoral reforms

Published on November 08, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan's ruling coalition parties have submitted their nominees for a proposed parliamentary panel focused on electoral reforms, while opposition parties have yet to provide their lists. Parliamentary Affairs Minister Dr Tariq Fazal Chaudhry confirmed receiving proposed names from coalition partners, including the Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP). However, the specific names of the submitted nominees were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

The government initiated the effort to bring all parliamentary groups to the negotiating table through the proposed Parliamentary Committee on Electoral Reforms (PCER). While the ruling Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N) has shortlisted its representatives, the minister noted that these names will be sent to the National Assembly Secretariat only after receiving final approval from top party leaders.

Key facts

  • According to Dawn, the parliamentary committee will comprise 30 total members from both the Senate and the National Assembly, allocated in proportion to party strength.
  • Under the general allocation formula, the PML-N receives 10 seats, though it may yield spots to smaller parties, while the PPP is assigned seven seats.
  • For Senate representation, PML-N, PPP, and PTI are allocated two seats each, while the Awami National Party (ANP), Balochistan Awami Party (BAP), National Party, and JUI-F receive one seat each.
  • The final selection of committee members will be determined by the prime minister and deputy prime minister following consultations with party leaders and the opposition.
  • Main opposition party PTI, through chief whip Aamir Dogar, requested additional time to respond, while JUI-F spokesperson Aslam Ghouri stated his party's executive committee has yet to deliberate on the matter.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, opposition involvement remains uncertain due to doubts over representation and intent. PTI Information Secretary Sheikh Waqas Akram stated that five opposition members on the panel would not be enough to implement their proposals. He reaffirmed PTI's support for genuine reforms—including electronic voting machines and measures against election rigging—but explained that recent internal meetings were restricted to discussing Imran Khan's health issues. PTI leadership plans to hold core and parliamentary committee meetings within two to three days to decide whether to participate in the panel.
