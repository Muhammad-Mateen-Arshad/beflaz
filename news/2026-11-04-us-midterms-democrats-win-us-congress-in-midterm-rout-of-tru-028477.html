US midterms: Democrats win US Congress in midterm rout of Trump

Published on November 04, 2026

According to Dawn, the Democratic Party gained control of both chambers of the US Congress following major victories in the midterm elections. The results give Democrats the authority to block legislation, halt judicial and government appointments, and launch investigations into Donald Trump's administration during the final two years of his term.

What happened

Democratic candidates flipped key seats in both the Senate and the House of Representatives. Democrats overturned a previous 53-47 Republican Senate majority by picking up six seats, securing victories in states such as Kansas, Iowa, Texas, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Ohio. In the House, Democrats gained control after taking Republican-held districts in states including Indiana, Pennsylvania, Arizona, Kentucky, and New Jersey.

Key facts

  • In Kansas, Adam Hamilton defeated incumbent Roger Marshall to become the state's first Democratic senator since 1932.
  • Democrat James Talarico won a Texas Senate seat over Ken Paxton, while Gina Hinojosa defeated incumbent Greg Abbott in the Texas gubernatorial race.
  • Xavier Becerra won the California governor's race against Steve Hilton, making him the state's first elected Latino governor.
  • Keisha Lance Bottoms was elected governor of Georgia, becoming the first Black woman elected governor in US history.
  • Republicans secured wins in Florida, where Byron Donalds became the state's first Black governor and Ashley Moody retained her Senate seat.
  • Trump told The New York Times he was open to accepting the outcomes and working with Democrats, but later posted an image of a hat displaying the word "VETO" on social media.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that the voting outcomes were heavily influenced by public anger regarding tariffs, general living costs, and high fuel prices associated with the conflict against Iran. Todd Belt, a professor at George Washington University, characterized the results as a major repudiation of Trump's agenda. Controlling Congress enables Democrats to initiate broad inquiries into the administration's handling of immigration, corruption allegations, and the Iran war, while effectively stopping Republican legislative initiatives.
