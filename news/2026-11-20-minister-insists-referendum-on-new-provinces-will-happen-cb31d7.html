Minister insists referendum on new provinces 'will happen'

Published on November 20, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry has insisted that a referendum regarding the creation of new provinces will take place, despite objections from coalition partner Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP). PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari recently voiced strong opposition to the proposal, describing a referendum as unconstitutional and warning that any forced attempt to divide provinces, particularly Sindh, could lead to bloodshed. In response, Chaudhry stated that gathering public sentiment is a lawful democratic process and emphasized that the government will work to build a broader national consensus.

Key facts

According to Dawn, key elements of the debate include:

  • Government Position: Parliamentary Affairs Minister Chaudhry and Information Minister Attaullah Tarar defended the proposed referendum as a legal mechanism to measure public opinion. Chaudhry confirmed that PML-N leader Nawaz Sharif fully supports the initiative.
  • Political Division: While the PPP and JUI-F oppose the vote—with JUI-F cautioning against potential misuse—the PTI-led opposition supports creating new provinces. Additionally, MQM-Pakistan proposed a nationwide referendum before 2027.
  • Coalition Leadership Talks: President Asif Ali Zardari, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi met in Islamabad to address political tensions, agreeing to resolve their differences through dialogue.
  • Referendum Timeline: Exact dates and formal schedules for the proposed vote are not yet finalized. Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the debate highlights ongoing friction within the ruling coalition regarding federal administrative structure. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi previously called for new administrative units, stating that the current governance system had collapsed and needed a reset. While Defence Minister Khawaja Asif cautioned politicians against treating provinces as personal fiefdoms, Chaudhry expressed confidence that dialogue would keep the ruling coalition intact.
