Netanyahu scrambles for votes as Israelis turn out in record numbers

Published on October 27, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Israelis turned out in record numbers to vote in the nation's first parliamentary election since the attacks of October 7, 2023. As voting concluded, initial exit projections showed a close contest, with the opposition bloc holding a minor lead over Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s coalition. Mainstream pollsters estimated the opposition would take 52 or 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, compared to 50 seats for Netanyahu's camp, though his Likud Party was projected to remain the largest individual party.

Faced with high voter participation among Israeli Arabs, Netanyahu sought to mobilize his core supporters while making controversial statements alleging that minority leaders were trying to subvert the vote. His party unsuccessfully requested to extend voting hours past 10:00 p.m., drawing accusations from political opponents that he was establishing grounds to dispute the election outcome. Additionally, election authorities reported an active police investigation into localized voter bribery allegations.

Key facts

  • Nationwide turnout reached 66.7 percent by 6:00 p.m., marking the highest participation rate recorded at that time of day since 1973.
  • The opposition coalition is led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot and includes prominent figures such as Yair Lapid, Naftali Bennett, Avigdor Lieberman, and Yair Golan.
  • Police in Tel Aviv arrested an individual carrying a knife who threatened opposition figure Yair Golan during the campaign.
  • Netanyahu highlighted backing from US President Donald Trump during the final days leading up to the election.
  • The official final vote tally and confirmed seat allocations were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, the vote serves as a major test of leadership following the trauma of the October 7 attacks and the subsequent regional conflicts across Gaza, southern Lebanon, and Iran. The election highlights deep political divisions regarding security, accountability, the role of Arab lawmakers, and the country's international standing amidst growing global isolation.
