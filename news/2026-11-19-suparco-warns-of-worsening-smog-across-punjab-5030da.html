Suparco warns of worsening smog across Punjab

Published on November 19, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, the Pakistan Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (Suparco) has alerted authorities to worsening smog conditions across Punjab over a three-day period. Satellite-based assessments recorded 690 active fire incidents in a 24-hour timeframe across Punjab and nearby transboundary areas, contributing to heavy air pollution across the region.

What happened

Dawn reported that satellite monitoring across 36 districts identified severe smog buildup, driven by localized fires and poor atmospheric dispersion that traps pollutants near the ground. Lahore temporarily topped a global ranking of major polluted cities after recording an Air Quality Index (AQI) of 339. Other cities in Punjab, such as Faisalabad, Gujranwala, and Multan, registered AQI levels reaching into hazardous territory above 300.

Key facts

  • Fire Detections: Satellite data detected 386 fires within Punjab and 304 in adjacent transboundary areas over 24 hours.
  • Severe Districts: Suparco highlighted Jhang, Narowal, Sheikhupura, Sialkot, Hafizabad, Gujranwala, and Dera Ghazi Khan as areas facing high or severe smog conditions.
  • Air Quality Readings: Faisalabad recorded a national high AQI of 686. Globally, Lahore was followed by Dhaka, Baghdad, Tashkent, Kolkata, and Karachi in city pollution rankings.
  • Atmospheric Indicators: Tropospheric nitrogen dioxide levels were 21 percent below the 2019–2025 average, but atmospheric dispersion remained very poor. The Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service model projected PM2.5 concentrations between 51 and 71 micrograms per cubic metre.
  • Weather Forecast: The Pakistan Meteorological Department forecast dry weather with morning and night fog or smog in isolated places over 48 hours.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, government agencies have initiated field operations to curb smoke sources. The Punjab Environment Protection Agency dispatched five senior headquarters directors across divisions to supervise anti-stubble burning efforts and coordinate with district administrators. In Multan, local officials deployed two specialized anti-smog gun vehicles to spray water continuously across key areas and launched enforcement against illegal burning. Additional details regarding long-term pollution policy measures were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
