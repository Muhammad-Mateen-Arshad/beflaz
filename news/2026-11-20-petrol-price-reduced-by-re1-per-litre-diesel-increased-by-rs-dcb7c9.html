Petrol price reduced by Re1 per litre; diesel increased by Rs2 per litre

Published on November 20, 2026

What happened

The government has revised domestic fuel prices, lowering the rate of petrol by Re1 per litre while increasing high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs2 per litre, according to Dawn. The decision ends a 12-day streak of consecutive reductions in petrol prices. Following an official notification from the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra), petrol will cost Rs322 per litre and diesel will cost Rs357 per litre beginning Saturday, Nov 21.

Key facts

  • The government maintains fixed levies of Rs114 per litre in duties and taxes on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel.
  • Under a 30-day agreement initiated on Nov 2, oil refineries are bearing a Rs15 billion burden, allowing a Rs14.71 per litre price reduction for retail petrol buyers.
  • The Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme, launched on Sept 17, offers subsidies to owners of motorbikes, rickshaws, and cars with engine capacities up to 800cc.
  • Ogra now adjusts fuel prices on a near-daily basis based on international market trends, replacing the weekly adjustments previously used.
  • Energy conservation rules require commercial markets to close by 9pm and cut fuel allocations for official state vehicles by 50 percent for three months.
  • Both fuels previously peaked on April 3, with petrol hitting Rs458.41 per litre and HSD reaching Rs520.35 per litre amid Middle East tensions.
  • Combined monthly demand for petrol and diesel reaches between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes, compared to just 10,000 tonnes for kerosene.

Why it matters

Fuel price revisions significantly influence daily expenses across the country. Lower- and middle-income households are particularly sensitive to petrol costs, as the fuel powers private vehicles, two-wheelers, and rickshaws. Meanwhile, diesel price fluctuations ripple through the general economy because HSD is essential for heavy freight transport, industrial generators, and power plants.

According to Dawn, Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar stated that petroleum policy changes and targeted relief measures reflect the administration's commitment to easing public financial strain caused by international market challenges.
