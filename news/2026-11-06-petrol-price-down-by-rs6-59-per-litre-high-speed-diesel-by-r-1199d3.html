Petrol price down by Rs6.59 per litre, high-speed diesel by Rs4.25 in fifth straight reduction

Published on November 06, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, the government has lowered fuel prices across the country for the fifth consecutive time. Petrol has been reduced by Rs6.59 per litre, while high-speed diesel (HSD) has fallen by Rs4.25 per litre. Following an official notification from the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra), the new retail prices are Rs346 per litre for petrol and Rs378 per litre for HSD. These updated rates remain in effect from Saturday, Nov 7, through Tuesday, Nov 10, taking into account a public holiday scheduled for Nov 9.

Key facts

  • Dawn reported that the government continues to collect Rs114 per litre in duties and taxes on petrol, alongside Rs100 per litre on diesel.
  • Under a 30-day arrangement, domestic oil refineries will absorb a cumulative financial impact of Rs15 billion, allowing a price reduction of Rs14.71 per litre for petrol at the consumer level.
  • Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik indicated that efforts are being made to establish a similar pricing structure for liquefied petroleum gas (LPG).
  • Prices previously reached high levels on April 3, when HSD hit Rs520.35 per litre (rising from Rs281 following the outbreak of conflict on Feb 28) and petrol reached Rs458.41 per litre (rising from Rs266 in early March).
  • To manage fuel usage amid regional turmoil, austerity measures were reinstated, requiring commercial markets to close by 9pm and reducing official vehicle fuel quotas by 50 percent for three months.
  • Prime Minister Shehbaz announced a targeted relief initiative on Sept 13 aimed at users of motorcycles, rickshaws, and motor vehicles up to 800cc.
  • Daily price adjustments were officially delegated to Ogra in July to reflect international market movements, replacing previous weekly updates.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, petrol price adjustments directly influence middle- and lower-middle-class consumers, as the fuel is predominantly used in private transport, small cars, rickshaws, and motorbikes. Diesel price changes carry a wider economic impact because HSD is critical to heavy commercial transport, power generation, and large generators. Both products remain the main source of fuel revenue for the state, registering monthly sales between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes, compared to a monthly consumption of 10,000 tonnes for kerosene.
