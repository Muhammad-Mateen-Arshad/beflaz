Copper wire upgrade caused nationwide flight suspension, US transport secretary says

Published on November 14, 2026

According to BBC News, a nationwide ground stop suspended all domestic flight departures in the continental United States for over four hours on Friday, 13 November. The operational halt began at 15:54 EST and represents the longest nationwide ground stop since the attacks on 11 September 2001.

What happened

The ground stop led to widespread aviation disruption, with ongoing effects expected to linger for days. On Saturday, flight tracking data recorded more than 3,500 flight cancellations and over 2,000 delays across the country. Some passengers were stranded inside aircraft on airport tarmacs, while select international flights were allowed to depart to help free up space. Specific airports affected were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Technical cause: Maintenance was underway to upgrade an outdated telecommunications system from copper wiring to fibre optic lines. A router being serviced flooded air traffic control networks, creating aircraft communication issues that prompted an airspace shutdown for safety.
  • Contractor involved: Technology provider L3Harris was performing the maintenance. The company stated it had completed more than 70 percent of the infrastructure modernization before the incident and is assisting in the ongoing investigation.
  • Government inquiry: US Transport Secretary Sean Duffy stated that investigators are determining whether the disruption was caused by equipment faults or contractor incompetence.
  • No foul play: Transport Secretary Duffy and Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) head Bryan Bedford confirmed there is no evidence of a cyber-attack, criminal activity, or artificial intelligence involvement.

Why it matters

The failure highlighted significant vulnerabilities in aging US telecommunications infrastructure. Transport Secretary Duffy warned that similar disruptions could happen again unless Congress approves funds to overhaul air traffic control systems. US President Donald Trump has requested $30.1 billion for these infrastructure upgrades, which require approval from a Republican-controlled Congress.

Former TSA Administrator John Pistole observed that restoring operations in four and a half hours demonstrated system resilience, though he raised concerns regarding whether a similar shutdown could occur in the future.
