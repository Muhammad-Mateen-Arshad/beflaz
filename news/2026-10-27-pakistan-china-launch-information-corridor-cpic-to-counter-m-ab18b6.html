Pakistan, China launch information corridor 'CPIC' to counter misleading narratives, promote cultural exchange

Published on October 27, 2026

What happened

Pakistan and China have officially launched the China-Pakistan Information Corridor (CPIC) during a televised ceremony in Islamabad, according to Dawn. The joint initiative aims to foster cultural exchange, strengthen cooperation between research institutions, and combat misleading narratives and misinformation.

The ceremony was attended by Pakistan's Information Minister Ataullah Tarar, Chinese Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Mao Ning, and Chinese Ambassador Jiang Zaidong. Describing CPIC as a digital corridor, Tarar stated that the initiative will build upon the economic foundations of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) to foster deeper mutual understanding between the populations of both nations.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the new information corridor operates across four primary pillars:

  • Media: Includes a news exchange working group formed by 18 Pakistani and Chinese media entities, supported by a specialized fact-checking mechanism to correct false reports.
  • Business and enterprise: Brings together major commercial groups, creates a trade portal to monitor foreign direct investment and trade, launches a podcast highlighting entrepreneurs, and opens a regional office in Karachi.
  • Think tanks: Spearheaded by the Pakistan-China Institute and leading research bodies to publish joint studies and conduct shared dialogues.
  • Culture and tourism: Focuses on expanding travel ties, including progress on the Xinjiang-Gilgit-Baltistan tourism corridor.

Additionally, the first cohort of CPEC Youth Ambassadors has been chosen from 13 universities across Pakistan. During the event, Mao Ning stated that both countries face ongoing fake news campaigns, emphasizing the role of the CPIC in highlighting genuine bilateral cooperation.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, CPIC is intended to complement CPEC by acting as a digital voice that enhances public trust and communication, whereas CPEC previously focused on physical connectivity, energy, and infrastructure. The launch follows high-level diplomatic engagements; a day prior, Pakistan's Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar met Ambassador Zaidong in Islamabad to evaluate progress on CPEC and discuss modernizing trade, agriculture, and regional links.

Specific timelines and complete policy documents were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
