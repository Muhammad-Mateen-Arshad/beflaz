PTA says submarine cable fault may cause internet connectivity issues

Published on November 18, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) announced on Wednesday that a technical fault in an international undersea cable system may cause intermittent connectivity issues and reduced service quality for internet users in Pakistan. NetBlocks, an internet monitoring group, later confirmed a drop in connectivity speeds, noting that operators are working to establish alternative data paths to address the issue on the impacted cable line.

Key facts

  • The fault occurred on the SEA-ME-WE 5 (SMW5) international subsea cable system, located approximately 11 kilometers from Pakistan's coast.
  • Transworld Associates is working alongside the SMW5 consortium to identify the underlying cause of the failure and determine how long repairs will take. The estimated restoration time is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • To lessen the impact on users, internet traffic is being directed through alternative international links to maintain continuous service.
  • PTA stated it is collaborating with relevant stakeholders and monitoring the situation to restore normal internet operations nationwide.
  • This follows a prior incident on July 1, when an SMW5 cable fault triggered widespread connection problems in Pakistan before being fixed on July 3.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, damage to international subsea infrastructure directly impacts internet performance across the country. Although traffic rerouting measures are being used to maintain service continuity, users may encounter temporary connection slowdowns while technical teams work to resolve the cable fault.
