Amid talk of new provinces, Sindh CM announces consultations on local govt empowerment

Published on November 27, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Sindh Chief Minister Syed Murad Ali Shah has announced a comprehensive consultation process aimed at crafting a new local government law. Speaking at a press conference at the CM House, the Chief Minister announced the formation of a two-member ministerial committee tasked with consulting opposition figures, civil society, political parties, and other relevant stakeholders.

Key facts

The core elements of the announcement reported by Dawn include:

  • Committee Composition: The two-member panel consists of provincial ministers Syed Nasir Hussain Shah and Ziaul Hassan Lanjar.
  • Timeline: The committee is scheduled to begin consultations next week and present its final recommendations within three months.
  • Legislative Approach: The changes will be introduced as amendments to the current local government law, and the next local body elections will proceed on schedule.
  • Governance Goals: The plan intends to increase autonomy for local councils, upgrade administrative capabilities, and boost funding mechanisms through the Provincial Finance Commission.
  • Attendees: Provincial ministers Sharjeel Memon, Nasir Shah, Saeed Ghani, Ziaul Hassan Lanjar, and Karachi Mayor Murtaza Wahab were present alongside the Chief Minister.
  • Additional Details: Specific clauses of the proposed legal amendments were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the move comes amidst nationwide discussions about establishing new administrative units and provinces, a concept strongly opposed by the Pakistan Peoples Party. Addressing questions about potential referendums on dividing the province, Chief Minister Shah stated that no one had contacted him on the issue. He underscored that the Sindh Assembly has previously rejected proposals to create new provinces inside Sindh's boundaries by a two-thirds majority, maintaining that strengthening local governments remains the provincial administration's primary strategy.
