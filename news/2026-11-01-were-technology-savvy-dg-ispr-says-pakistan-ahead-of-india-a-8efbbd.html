‘We’re technology-savvy’: DG ISPR says Pakistan ahead of India, Afghan Taliban in operational readiness

Published on November 01, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR) Director General Lieutenant General Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry stated that Pakistan's armed forces are significantly ahead in operational readiness compared to India and the Afghan Taliban. Speaking during an international media interview, the spokesperson emphasized that Pakistan is actively incorporating emerging technologies into its defence strategy amid persistent tensions between Islamabad and Kabul.

Key facts

The military spokesperson outlined several statements regarding technology, regional security, and diplomatic relations:

  • Lt Gen Chaudhry highlighted Pakistan's integration of advanced capabilities, including artificial intelligence, quantum technology, cyber systems, long-range missions, and drone warfare. He noted that during a May 2025 conflict with India, Pakistan shot down dozens of sophisticated drones launched from across the eastern border.
  • The spokesperson claimed the Afghan Taliban lacks self-contained military strength and acts as a proxy for India. He accused India of sponsoring groups operating on Afghan soil, specifically naming the Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) and the Balochistan Liberation Army (BLA), while referencing United Nations reports and international intelligence assessments.
  • Lt Gen Chaudhry rejected allegations from Kabul that Pakistan coordinates anti-Taliban elements, describing the current Afghan leadership as a regime run by a non-state actor shaped by decades of foreign interventions.
  • He stated that Afghanistan currently produces only terrorism and drugs, but opposed isolating the country. Instead, he called for international engagement to pressure Kabul to stop harboring militant leadership.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the comments point to growing international concerns over the proliferation of non-contact warfare technologies among non-state actors, including the Taliban, Khawarij, and Houthis. Pakistan maintains that neighboring states must re-evaluate their security frameworks and that Kabul must dismantle terrorist sanctuaries to establish regional stability.
