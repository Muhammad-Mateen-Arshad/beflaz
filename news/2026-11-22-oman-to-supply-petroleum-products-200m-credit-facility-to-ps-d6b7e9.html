Oman to supply petroleum products, $200m credit facility to PSO under new agreement

Published on November 22, 2026

Pakistan State Oil (PSO) and Oman's state-owned OQ Trading (OQT) have signed a Sale and Purchase Agreement for petroleum products, according to a report by Dawn. Under the deal, OQT will supply petroleum products to PSO and extend a $200 million credit line to facilitate energy purchases.

What happened

The agreement was finalized on Sunday during a visit to Muscat by Pakistan's Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik, who held talks with Oman's Minister of Energy and Minerals Eng. Salim Al Aufi. The Petroleum Division stated that both ministers witnessed the signing of the agreement between the two state-owned companies. Specific terms regarding the duration or implementation timeline of the credit facility were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

During their meeting, the ministers discussed opportunities to expand cooperation in the oil, gas, and minerals sectors. Both officials supported utilizing the newly established Pakistan-Oman Joint Working Group on Oil and Gas to advance bilateral engagements in energy and mining.

Key facts

  • OQT is supplying petroleum products to PSO along with extending a $200 million credit facility.
  • OQT supplied over 1 million tonnes of gasoline to PSO during 2026.
  • The Omani company supplies liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) to public and private sector entities in Pakistan, with total LPG shipments expected to surpass 120,000 metric tonnes by the end of 2026.
  • The agreement follows last month's two-day visit to Pakistan by Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi for the eighth session of the Pakistan-Oman Joint Ministerial Commission.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, citing statement details from the Petroleum Division, the commercial agreement provides a structured arrangement to support supply reliability amid regional energy market volatility. It offers Pakistan a mechanism to strategically diversify its sourcing of petroleum products while the Strait of Hormuz remains disrupted into the ninth month of the US-Iran conflict.

Both ministers stated that the implementation of the agreement will enhance bilateral energy ties, advance commercial partnerships, and assist Pakistan's efforts to reinforce energy security through broader import diversification.
