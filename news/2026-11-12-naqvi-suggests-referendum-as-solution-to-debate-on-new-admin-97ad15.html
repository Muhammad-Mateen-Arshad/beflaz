Naqvi suggests referendum as 'solution' to debate on new administrative units

Published on November 12, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi suggested on Thursday that political parties should hold a referendum to determine public opinion on establishing new administrative units or provinces. Speaking to reporters at the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency in Lahore, Naqvi described a public vote as the solution to the ongoing debate. He stated he would fully abide by the public's decision regardless of the outcome, emphasizing that no changes would be made without consensus among allies in the ruling coalition. Specific dates or legal frameworks for the proposed referendum were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the ongoing discussion involves several key viewpoints and related political developments:

  • Governance reset: Naqvi has argued over recent months that the current governance system has collapsed and requires a reset, a position he previously raised in July and September.
  • Political reactions: Naqvi claimed his stance aligns with PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari's call for a political reset. However, the PPP opposes creating a new province in Sindh and contends that new administrative units will not solve issues such as inflation and unemployment. Bilawal also stressed that proposals should be debated in Parliament.
  • Provinces proposal: Defence Minister Khawaja Asif recently added to the debate by mentioning the creation of 12 additional provinces.
  • Comments on corruption: Asked about federal minister Chaudhry Salik's remarks regarding corruption in Punjab, Naqvi stated that corruption exists across the entire country and must be eradicated. Salik's earlier statements prompted Punjab Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz to issue a Rs5 billion defamation notice against him.
  • CDA notice: Naqvi defended a Capital Development Authority notice regarding the residence of the JUI-F chief, stating the authority's campaign has been ongoing for months and is not aimed at any specific individual.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the proposal to form new administrative units has stirred significant political debate. While Naqvi views a referendum as a democratic approach to conclude the matter, major political allies assert that addressing national economic challenges and following parliamentary procedures must take priority.
