Pakistan’s Zeeshan Ali among crew of China’s Shenzhou-24 mission; liftoff scheduled for Nov 25

Published on November 24, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) announced that Pakistani astronaut Zeeshan Ali has been selected as part of the crew for China’s Shenzhou-24 space mission. Serving as a payload specialist, Ali will become the first foreign national to board China's Tiangong space station. He will join Chinese astronauts Tang Hongbo and Wu Yi on the three-member expedition.

Key facts

The upcoming mission includes several primary targets and background developments reported by the CMSA and Pakistani officials:

  • Launch details: The spaceflight is scheduled to lift off at 8:18 am Beijing Time on Wednesday, November 25, taking off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center located in northwest China.
  • Mission scope: The team is set to manage regular station operations and maintenance, rotate crew duties with the earlier Shenzhou-23 mission, and execute around 90 scientific experiments. Specific experiment details were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Duration and return: Ali is slated to spend about one week in space before returning aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, which originally docked with Tiangong in May.
  • Selection agreement: Following a February 2025 cooperation agreement between Suparco and the CMSA, Ali and astronaut Khurram Daud began training in China in April. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal, and Information Minister Attaullah Tarar expressed national pride and praised the mission as a demonstration of strong bilateral relations.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the mission highlights growing space collaboration between Beijing and Islamabad. Since 2011, when US restrictions prevented NASA from working with Chinese space organizations and barred China from the International Space Station, Beijing has built independent space capabilities and sought foreign partnerships. As China advances its space program with plans to land astronauts on the Moon by 2030, Pakistan stands as a key partner building on decades of close diplomatic and economic ties.
