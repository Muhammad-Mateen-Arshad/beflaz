FCC bars PHC from hearing cases related to PTI workers' preventive detention

Published on November 05, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) has restrained the Peshawar High Court (PHC) from continuing hearings on cases concerning the preventive detention of Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) workers. The detentions were enforced under the Maintenance of Public Order Ordinance (MPO) following the declaration of emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). A two-judge FCC bench led by Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan and Justice Aamer Farooq issued the direction while reviewing more than 60 petitions challenging interim bail previously granted to detainees by the PHC.

Key facts

  • The appeals before the FCC were submitted by the additional chief secretary of the KP Home and Tribal Affairs Department against PHC orders that granted interim bail on surety bonds of Rs100,000 each.
  • KP Advocate General Sanaullah Khan argued that under clause 3 of Article 245 of the Constitution, a high court cannot exercise jurisdiction under Article 199 in regions where armed forces are acting in aid of civil power.
  • The advocate general also cited Article 10, maintaining that MPO provisions are based on executive evaluations to preserve public safety and order.
  • In response to legal arguments, Justice Farooq questioned whether high courts should be prevented from hearing daily constitutional petitions, to which the advocate general noted specific civil matters could still proceed.
  • Former additional advocate general Shah Faisal Ilyas objected to issuing an order without hearing all parties first; Chief Justice Khan instructed him to submit formal written objections after reviewing the court's order.
  • The FCC paused PHC proceedings, directed notices to be issued to the respondents, and allowed them to file pleas before the higher court. The PHC had been scheduled to hear the matter on Nov 12.

Why it matters

The court's decision occurs in the backdrop of heightened political tension following the federal government's Oct 11 imposition of emergency and governor's rule in KP, which was justified by citing the provincial administration's detachment from counter-terrorism efforts, according to Dawn. The move prompted opposition criticism from political figures including Maulana Fazlur Rehman, Mehmood Khan Achakzai, and Raja Nasir Abbas. Multiple PTI leaders and activists have been detained since the proclamation, including Sohail Afridi, who was rendered non-functional as chief minister. Full details regarding future court schedules are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
