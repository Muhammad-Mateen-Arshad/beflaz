Water demand outstrips supply in Punjab: UN agency

Published on November 16, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, an assessment conducted by the Food and Agriculture Organisation (FAO) indicates that water demand in Punjab has exceeded total supply across every sector. The study, titled "Water and Land Accounting in Punjab," analysed five water years spanning from 2012 to 2017 and warned of critical pressures facing both groundwater and surface water reserves.

Key facts

  • The FAO report found that groundwater in the province is depleting at a rate of around 5.5 billion cubic metres per year.
  • An additional 10 billion cubic metres of usable water is required annually to bridge the supply gap and sustain the groundwater aquifer.
  • River flows passing through the province have dropped by approximately 30 billion cubic metres following the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty with India, leaving wetlands covering just 1.7 per cent of Punjab's area.
  • Water specialists, including former Wapda chairman Shamsul Mulk and former Irsa chairman Rao Irshad Ali Khan, provided expert guidance for the assessment.
  • Shortages have led to an increased use of recycled low-quality water, compounded by pollution from city wastewater and agricultural runoff.
  • The analysis highlighted shortcomings in data collection and recommended extending the land-use classification system across the entire province.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the growing gap between water supply and demand poses severe risks to urban centers and agricultural output, both of which are critical to Pakistan's overall economic stability. To tackle these issues, the report advised forming a commission to conduct a complete review of provincial water policies, strategies, and resource projections through 2050. The proposal also calls for creating a specialized coordination cell within the Planning and Development Department to manage routine operations and clarify overlapping institutional responsibilities.
