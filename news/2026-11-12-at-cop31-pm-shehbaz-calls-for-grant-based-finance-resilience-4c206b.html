At COP31, PM Shehbaz calls for grant-based finance, resilience funding for climate-vulnerable countries

Published on November 12, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif addressed world leaders at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkiye, calling for grant-based financing and resilience funding for nations vulnerable to climate change. Highlighting that domestic efforts cannot substitute for a just global response, the prime minister emphasized the need for financial assistance that does not add to the debt burdens of struggling economies. On the sidelines of the summit, PM Shehbaz also held bilateral discussions with leaders from Sudan and Finland.

Key facts

  • Financial and Emission Proposals: PM Shehbaz advocated for grant-based finance, public-private partnerships, and increased adaptation funds. He noted that clean energy accounts for nearly 55% of Pakistan's energy needs and highlighted national goals to advance electric mobility and cut projected emissions by approximately 50% by 2035.
  • Climate Disasters and Water Security: The premier recalled the 2022 Pakistan floods—which claimed over 1,700 lives and caused economic damage exceeding $30 billion—as well as flash floods in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa’s Buner district in August 2025. He warned against the weaponisation of water and demanded that international water-sharing agreements be fully honored.
  • International Acknowledgments: PM Shehbaz expressed gratitude to Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan for Turkiye's support during Pakistan's 2010 and 2022 floods.
  • Meetings on the Sidelines: The Prime Minister's Office reported that PM Shehbaz met General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, President of Sudan’s Transitional Sovereignty Council, reaffirming support for Sudan's sovereignty and discussing joint climate concerns. He also met Finnish President Alexander Stubb to discuss bilateral trade, conflict resolution efforts in Ukraine and West Asia, and technical cooperation in water management, glacier monitoring, and flood forecasting.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, climate-vulnerable nations continue to suffer severe economic losses and loss of life despite contributing the least to global greenhouse gas emissions. The appeal at COP31 underscores the urgent demand for global solidarity, non-debt financial aid, and shared technical expertise to protect developing nations from worsening environmental catastrophes while navigating ongoing economic and political crises.
