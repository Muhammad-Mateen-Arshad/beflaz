Israelis to decide Netanyahu’s fate in pivotal election today

Published on October 27, 2026

According to Dawn, Israelis are heading to the polls on Tuesday in a pivotal election to decide the political fate of long-serving Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. This marks the country's first national vote since the October 7, 2023 attack, taking place against the backdrop of three years of multi-front regional conflict and growing international isolation.

What happened

Voters across Israel are casting their ballots in a close election that pits 77-year-old Netanyahu against his primary centrist rival, 66-year-old Gadi Eisenkot. Netanyahu, who has served as prime minister on and off for over 19 years, has campaigned heavily on his security credentials and his relationship with US President Donald Trump. Meanwhile, Eisenkot, a political novice and former military chief leading the Yashar party and an informal opposition bloc, has promised to establish an independent state commission of inquiry into the security failures surrounding October 7.

Key facts

  • Around 7.3 million citizens are eligible to vote, including more than half a million young first-time voters who surveys show overwhelmingly identify as centre-right or right-wing.
  • The exact calendar date of the election is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The parliament contains 120 seats, requiring 61 for a majority. Because no single party is expected to win an outright majority, forming a coalition could take weeks or months.
  • Eisenkot opposes the creation of a Palestinian state and supports settlements, but has promised to crack down on violent settlers.
  • Netanyahu faces criticism from political opponents for failing to accept responsibility for October 7 and for supporting military draft exemptions for ultra-Orthodox religious students.
  • Predominantly Arab lists represent roughly 21 percent of Israel's population and could potentially serve as kingmakers, although neither main candidate has publicly agreed to partner with them.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the vote highlights deep internal divisions over governance, security, and the country's rightward political trajectory. The outcome comes after years of conflict involving Gaza, southern Lebanon, and direct strikes on Iran, as well as domestic bombardment from Hamas, Hezbollah, Iran, and Houthi rebels, shaping the nation's future character and international standing.
