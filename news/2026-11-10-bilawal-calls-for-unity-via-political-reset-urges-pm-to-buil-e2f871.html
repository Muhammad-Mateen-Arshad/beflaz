Bilawal calls for unity via ‘political reset’, urges PM to build trust with allies and opposition

Published on November 10, 2026

PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari has called for a political reset in Pakistan, urging Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif to cultivate trust with political allies and opposition parties, according to Dawn. Speaking at an event marking the construction of one million homes under the Sindh Flood Resilience Project (SFRP), Bilawal emphasized that national political parties must set aside rivalries to collectively address severe national and global economic challenges.

What happened

According to Dawn, Bilawal stated that Pakistan faces a major economic crisis, which has been exacerbated by a global energy and oil emergency linked to the US-Iran war. He stressed that overcoming these financial difficulties requires unified action from all political sides rather than continued political fighting. During his speech, Bilawal urged Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif to focus on rebuilding trust with coalition partners before extending similar efforts toward opposition parties.

Key facts

  • The SFRP has completed one million houses to date, with a target of reaching two million upon the project's completion.
  • The overall initiative costs nearly $2 billion and seeks to restore roughly 2.1 million rural homes destroyed during the 2022 floods, serving 40 percent of rural households in Sindh.
  • Financial backing includes a €100 million loan from EIB Global, while the provincial government has requested $350 million in assistance from the World Bank for fiscal year 2026-2027.
  • Bilawal expressed gratitude to Prime Minister Sharif for federal assistance, contrasting this support with Sharif's predecessor, who provided no funds to aid flood victims in Sindh.
  • Dismissing claims of financial misconduct during relief efforts, Bilawal proposed that the Sindh chief minister invite international corruption oversight bodies to investigate the housing initiative.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Bilawal maintained that government policies should focus on supporting the poor and homeless rather than serving the interests of the wealthy business sector. As citizens face persistent poverty, inflation, and unemployment, he argued that joint political cooperation is vital to alleviate financial pressures on the public.
