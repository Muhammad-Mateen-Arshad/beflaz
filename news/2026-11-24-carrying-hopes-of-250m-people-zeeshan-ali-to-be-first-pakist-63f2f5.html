'Carrying hopes of 250m people': Zeeshan Ali to be 'first' Pakistani astronaut in space aboard China’s Shenzhou-24

Published on November 24, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistani astronaut Mohammad Zeeshan Ali has been selected as a crew member for China’s Shenzhou-24 space mission. The launch will make Ali the first Pakistani to travel on an orbital space mission and the first foreign national to board China’s Tiangong space station. Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal confirmed Ali's role, referring to him as the first astronaut from Pakistan.

Key facts

According to official statements from the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) cited by Dawn, the primary facts of the mission include:

  • Launch Schedule: The mission is scheduled to launch aboard a Long March-2F rocket at 8:18 am Beijing Time (5:18 am Pakistan Time) on Wednesday from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center.
  • Crew Structure: The flight is commanded by Chinese astronaut Tang Hongbo, with Wu Yi acting as pilot and Ali serving as payload specialist.
  • Mission Tasks: The crew is tasked with executing around 90 scientific experiments, performing station maintenance, and carrying out an orbital rotation.
  • Duration and Return: Ali is set to spend approximately one week aboard the space station. He will return to Earth on the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft alongside astronauts Lai Ka-ying and Zhang Zhiyuan, while Zhu Yangzhu stays behind to complete a full-year test of endurance.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, the event marks a milestone for Pakistan's space ambitions and its partnership with China. Speaking at a press briefing, Ali expressed gratitude to the Chinese government, Pakistan’s space agency Suparco, the CMSA, and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. Ali detailed his training at the Astronaut Centre of China, where he acquired spaceflight skills and learned the Chinese language to communicate with his crewmates. He noted that the year 2026 marks the 75th anniversary of diplomatic ties and space cooperation between the two nations, expressing hope that this mission will serve as a launchpad for future joint space endeavors.
