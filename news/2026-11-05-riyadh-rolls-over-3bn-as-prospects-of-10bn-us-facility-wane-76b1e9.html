Riyadh rolls over $3bn as prospects of $10bn US facility wane

Published on November 05, 2026

Pakistan has secured a $3 billion debt rollover from Saudi Arabia as prospects for a requested $10 billion backstop facility from the United States have diminished, according to a report by Dawn.

What happened

Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb confirmed that Saudi Arabia extended the $3 billion financing initially provided in April to help cover debt repayments to the United Arab Emirates. The facility had reached maturity on October 31. Addressing queries regarding a $10 billion liquidity support facility requested from the United States in July, Aurangzeb indicated that securing market access was the primary objective, which Pakistan achieved through a $3 billion bond placement in September. He stated that while the US facility would offer additional liquidity if approved, Pakistan is prepared to move forward with or without it.

Key facts

  • Saudi Arabia rolled over the $3 billion facility, adding to its previous agreement extending a $5 billion facility until 2028.
  • Pakistan intends to issue Eurobonds, Panda bonds, and rupee-linked dollar-settled bonds to replace expensive short-term borrowing with longer-term financing.
  • Specific launch dates for the planned bond issuances were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The government aims to reduce external debt by $4 billion during the current fiscal year and by $3.5 billion in the following fiscal year.
  • Foreign exchange reserves have surpassed $21.5 billion, providing over three months of import cover, with plans to reach four months of cover by the end of the year.
  • An IMF Executive Board meeting to approve a $1.2 billion disbursement to Pakistan is scheduled for December.
  • Finance Secretary Imdadullah Bosal stated public debt fell to 68.6% of GDP by the end of June 2026, with remaining net financing needs at $6 billion over the next seven months.

Why it matters

The rollover and upcoming bond offerings align with Pakistan's goal to lower refinancing risks by shifting to longer-maturity obligations. According to Aurangzeb, fiscal discipline has helped control the current account deficit, which is projected to remain under 1% this year. Additionally, the Senate Standing Committee on Finance instructed the State Bank of Pakistan to review clearance rules after expressing dissatisfaction over foreign exchange access procedures for parliamentarians.
