Trump’s threat to strike Iran raises public concerns, congressional questions

Published on October 24, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, US President Donald Trump has renewed threats of military action against Iran during a rally in Florida, referencing a potential repeat of operation "Epic Fury" without providing a specific timeline. The statement follows an ABC News report stating that US and Israeli military forces have drafted options for joint strikes, which sources indicate would likely take place after the November 3 midterm elections. White House spokesperson Anna Kelly clarified that the US will not conduct strikes against Iran prior to the midterms.

Key facts

According to Dawn, public opinion polls and expert analyses outline the following details regarding the conflict:

  • Public disapproval: A Pew Research Centre survey showed that 65 percent of US adults disapprove of Trump's handling of the military action, compared to 33 percent who approve. High fuel prices concerned 73 percent of respondents, while 71 percent expressed concern over American troop casualties.
  • Election priorities: An AP-NORC poll revealed that about seven in 10 registered voters want the next Congress to prioritize ending the conflict, while about eight in 10 prioritize reducing living costs.
  • Strategic choices: Analysis from the Council on Foreign Relations outlined options for the administration to escalate, negotiate, or maintain its current posture, noting each carries significant downsides.
  • Role of Congress: Lawmakers can use spending controls, oversight hearings, and the War Powers Resolution to challenge military operations, though these measures do not automatically prevent a presidential strike order.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, public dissatisfaction over the war's economic impact and troop safety is creating significant political pressure ahead of the midterm elections. Although joint military planning and recent statements keep the prospect of future military action open, it remains uncertain whether these election pressures will push the administration toward diplomatic negotiations or renewed strikes.
