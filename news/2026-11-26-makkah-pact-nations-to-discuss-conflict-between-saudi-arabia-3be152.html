Makkah pact nations to discuss conflict between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthis in Islamabad meeting, Asif says

Published on November 26, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, member nations of the Makkah pact—Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye—are gathering in Islamabad on Thursday. Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Asif informed AFP that defence and foreign ministers from all three countries will address the ongoing turmoil between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthi fighters as a key agenda item. Specific meeting schedules and complete delegation lists were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Mutual defence agreement: Signed in August, the security pact commits Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye to come to one another's defence in the event of an attack. Asif stressed that any threat facing Saudi Arabia is viewed as a threat to Pakistan and Turkiye.
  • Conflict in Yemen: Houthi fighters, who control Sanaa and other parts of Yemen, have aimed attacks at Saudi cities, shipping routes, and oil infrastructure since civil war hostilities intensified in July.
  • Alliance development: Established following US-Israeli strikes on Iran in February, the pact remains at an early stage. Asif noted that frequent meetings over the next few months will help finalize its operational structure.
  • Security perspectives: Asif declined to discuss how the alliance would react to a direct attack on Saudi Arabia by Iran, but he identified Israel as a potential regional threat.

Why it matters

The meeting reflects ongoing attempts by regional powers to align their security strategies, according to Dawn. Beyond the Makkah agreement, Pakistan has served as a mediator between Washington and Tehran. Pakistan helped arrange a June agreement that briefly halted hostilities and established a roadmap regarding Iran's nuclear programme and the Strait of Hormuz. Although that truce collapsed amid mutual accusations, diplomatic efforts led by Pakistan and Qatar continue, with Asif describing Pakistan's role as constructive despite the fragile state of negotiations.
