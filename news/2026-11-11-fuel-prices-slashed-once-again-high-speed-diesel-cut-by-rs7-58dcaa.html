Fuel prices slashed once again; high-speed diesel cut by Rs7 per litre, petrol by Rs3

Published on November 11, 2026

What happened

The government has announced another reduction in domestic fuel prices, cutting high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs7 per litre and petrol by Rs3 per litre, according to a report by Dawn. Based on a notification issued by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA), the new retail price for petrol is Rs335 per litre, while HSD will cost Rs364 per litre effective Thursday, Nov 12. Since July 17, OGRA has been tasked with revising petroleum prices on a near-daily basis to align with international market shifts caused by escalating tension between the US and Iran.

Key facts

  • Under a 30-day agreement announced on Nov 2, oil refineries are absorbing a collective financial burden of Rs15 billion, enabling a consumer price reduction of Rs14.71 per litre for petrol.
  • The government continues to charge Rs114 per litre in duties and taxes on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel.
  • Fuel rates previously reached historic peaks on April 3, with HSD reaching Rs520.35 per litre and petrol rising to Rs458.41 per litre following conflict in the Middle East.
  • To support citizens, the government introduced the Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme on Sept 17 for owners of motorcycles, rickshaws, and vehicles up to 800cc.
  • Official austerity measures currently mandate commercial markets to close by 9pm and reduce fuel allocations for government vehicles by 50 percent for three months.
  • Government representatives held discussions today with Jamaat-i-Islami to address further options for petroleum price relief.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, adjustments in petroleum prices heavily influence various sectors of the economy. Petrol is primarily consumed by two-wheelers, rickshaws, and small private vehicles, directly affecting the daily expenses of lower-middle and middle-class households. High-speed diesel is utilized in heavy transport, power generation, and large generators, meaning price changes ripple across broader public services and industry. Petrol and HSD remain the primary drivers of petroleum revenue, with combined monthly sales reaching 700,000 to 800,000 tonnes, compared to 10,000 tonnes for kerosene.
