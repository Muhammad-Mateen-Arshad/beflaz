Islamabad set to host Makkah Defence Alliance meeting today; Yemen conflict also on agenda

Published on November 27, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan is hosting a two-day meeting in Islamabad for the Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC), the top decision-making body of the Makkah Defence Alliance. The summit brings together the foreign ministers, defence ministers, and military chiefs of Saudi Arabia, Turkiye, and Pakistan. To facilitate the high-level gathering, authorities declared a public holiday in Islamabad and a local holiday in Rawalpindi, alongside special movement restrictions.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the key aspects of the alliance and its current gathering include:

  • Core Objectives: The Islamabad meeting focuses on finalizing standard operating procedures, defining institutional coordination, and creating implementation mechanisms among the three nations.
  • Yemen Focus: Discussions will feature the ongoing conflict involving Saudi Arabia and Houthi fighters (Ansar Allah) from Yemen, who have targeted Saudi cities, shipping, and oil infrastructure.
  • Mutual Commitment: Signed on Aug 7, the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement states that an armed assault on any member country will be treated as an attack on all three, requiring mutual aid.
  • Governance: The alliance features a permanent secretariat in Riyadh, headed by Pakistan's retired Lt Gen Nauman Mahmood, who serves as secretary general for a three-year term.
  • Current Status: Turkiye's ratification finalized the accession process for all members, though Pakistani Defence Minister Khawaja Asif described the operational setup as being in an early stage.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the gathering occurs amid broader regional friction involving the United States, Iran, and Israel. Defence Minister Khawaja Asif emphasized that any threat to Saudi Arabia constitutes a threat to Pakistan and Turkiye, while also identifying Israel as a potential security risk in the region. Pakistan continues to preserve diplomatic engagement with Iran while advancing defense cooperation with Riyadh and Ankara. Turkiye has stressed the alliance's defensive nature and remains cautious about participating in combat operations in Yemen. Specific operational doctrines and joint force command details are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
