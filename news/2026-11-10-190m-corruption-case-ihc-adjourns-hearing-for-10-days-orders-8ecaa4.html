£190m corruption case: IHC adjourns hearing for 10 days, orders meeting between Imran and legal team

Published on November 10, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the Islamabad High Court (IHC) has adjourned the hearing of appeals filed by Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) founder Imran Khan and his wife, Bushra Bibi, for 10 days. The couple is appealing their convictions in the £190 million Al-Qadir Trust corruption case, in which an accountability court previously sentenced Imran to 14 years in prison and Bushra to seven years. Heading a division bench, Chief Justice Sardar Muhammad Sarfraz Dogar directed government authorities to facilitate meetings between Imran and his legal team. The exact calendar date for the upcoming session was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Legal Access Orders: Defense counsel Barrister Salman Safdar stated he was denied access to Imran for seven months and to Bushra for 11 months, noting a recent 40-minute meeting was insufficient for preparation. The court ordered Islamabad Advocate General Naveed Hayat Malik to arrange a meeting of at least one hour between lawyer Sardar Latif Khan Khosa and Imran, while also allowing Barrister Safdar to meet both clients.
  • Case Records: The defense highlighted that the appeals involve roughly 3,500 pages of documentation and testimony from 35 witnesses. The IHC directed that official paper books be completed and delivered prior to the next court date.
  • Medical Claims and Bail Request: Barrister Safdar raised concerns over Bushra's severe eye infection and requested a two-week bail for specialist care, while also mentioning Imran's fluctuating blood pressure. The Advocate General maintained that proper medical care had been provided, and the bench noted Bushra's medical petition is pending before the Federal Constitutional Court.
  • Public Response: Following the hearing, PTI Chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan called for non-discriminatory justice, alleging that court orders favoring the party were not being given equal implementation.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the court's latest directives address long-standing defense claims regarding restricted lawyer access and delayed case documentation. The decisions aim to establish necessary pre-trial preparation and client consultation before formal arguments on the £190 million corruption convictions resume.
