US attorney general dodges questions about Nvidia investigation

Published on November 25, 2026

What happened

US Attorney General Todd Blanche has described Nvidia as a "good corporate citizen" while refusing to clarify whether the artificial intelligence chip maker is under federal investigation, according to BBC News. Blanche also stated he lacked information regarding the security clearance status of newly reinstated chief executive Jensen Huang, who returned to his post less than two weeks after resigning over extramarital affairs, including one involving an alleged Chinese spy.

Key facts

  • President Donald Trump acknowledged speaking directly with Nvidia's board members to persuade them to bring Huang back as chief executive.
  • Nvidia board chairman Harvey Jones stated that Huang's reinstatement was in the best interests of shareholders, staff, partners, and customers, adding that government input indicated no national security risk.
  • Huang is scheduled to appear before Congress next week to answer national security questions concerning his relationship with an individual tied to Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer.
  • The US Department of Commerce is preparing to blacklist Megaspeed due to suspicions that advanced microchips were diverted to China, following a joint investigation launched with the Department of Justice in 2024. Specific details on the exact timeline for the blacklisting were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Bipartisan lawmakers expressed sharp criticism over Trump's intervention, questioning national security oversight, potential ethics violations, and pre-announcement stock movements.
  • Nvidia's share price surged over 31% on Monday after Huang's return was made public, before dropping nearly 6% on Tuesday.

Why it matters

The developments underscore heightened political and regulatory scrutiny surrounding advanced technology supply chains and international security risks. According to BBC News, while Blanche asserted that Nvidia has not been identified as uncooperative or targeted in criminal proceedings, lawmakers are continuing to push for full transparency regarding federal export investigations and corporate oversight.
