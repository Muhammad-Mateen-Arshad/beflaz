Cabinet approves deregulation of sugar sector to meet vital IMF benchmark

Published on November 24, 2026

What happened

Pakistan's federal cabinet has approved the National Policy for Deregulation of Sugar Sector via circulation to fulfill a postponed structural benchmark set by the International Monetary Fund (IMF), according to Dawn. The decision completely removes government intervention in sugar operations, ending export subsidies and lifting restrictions on the movement of sugar between provinces.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the deregulation policy introduces several structural changes across the sugar sector:

  • Sugarcane crop pricing and refined sugar prices will be governed by market forces instead of government price controls.
  • Restrictions on planting zones, cane varieties (excluding a negative list of banned varieties), raw material selection, and mill capacity expansion have been lifted.
  • To protect farmers, the policy includes safeguards such as third-party verification of weighing scales, a sucrose testing framework, and required interest payments by millers on overdue sugarcane payments.
  • The government retains the right to hold buffer sugar stocks, collect crop and market data, and promote ethanol blending.
  • The policy was finalized by a ministerial committee chaired by Power Minister Awais Leghari, following approval from the Sindh government on Nov 21 after backdoor negotiations.
  • The cabinet's approval follows recent permission to export more than 400,000 tonnes of sugar, adding to 200,000 tonnes shipped earlier.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, fulfilling this benchmark was mandatory to clear the way for an IMF executive board meeting scheduled for Dec 11. Pakistan had been in breach of the benchmark, which carried an original completion target of June 30, 2026. The IMF actively participated in shaping the policy to ensure the state completely exits pricing, production, and trade operations. Specific terms of the transitional arrangements for the ongoing crushing season were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
