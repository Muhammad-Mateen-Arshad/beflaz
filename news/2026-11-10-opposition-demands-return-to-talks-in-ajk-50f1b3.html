Opposition demands ‘return to talks’ in AJK

Published on November 10, 2026

According to a report by Dawn published on November 10th, 2026, the main opposition alliance in Pakistan, Tehreek-i-Tahafuz Ayin-i-Pakistan (TTAP), claimed that its delegation was blocked from travelling to Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK).

What happened

TTAP spokesperson Hussein Ahmed Yousafzai stated that the political delegation was travelling toward AJK to meet with demonstrators, display solidarity, and evaluate the local situation. However, the group was halted at Kahuta and refused permission to proceed. Yousafzai argued that there was no justification for preventing a peaceful political delegation from reaching protesters, stating that the government should have offered an official explanation for the action.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the opposition alliance presented a series of demands regarding the events in AJK:

  • The alliance called for prompt and constructive negotiations with the Joint Awami Action Committee alongside measurable progress on their demands.
  • It demanded an independent investigation into claims regarding the unnecessary use of force against demonstrators.
  • It stated that injured persons must be granted immediate and high-quality medical treatment.
  • It insisted that arrested individuals be given proper access to legal justice according to law.
  • It urged that political and social representatives, including the TTAP delegation, be permitted unhindered entry into AJK.

Why it matters

Reassuring Kashmiri citizens that they were not alone in their efforts, the opposition alliance pledged to advocate for their fundamental human, democratic, and constitutional rights across every legal and constitutional platform. Additional details concerning the specific demands of the demonstrators or the government's official response were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
