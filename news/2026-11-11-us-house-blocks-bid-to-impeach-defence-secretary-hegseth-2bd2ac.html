US House blocks bid to impeach Defence Secretary Hegseth

Published on November 11, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Republicans in the US House of Representatives blocked a motion to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth. Members voted 211 to 204 to send eight articles of impeachment to the Republican-led House Judiciary Committee, a procedural step that effectively ends the current effort.

The push was introduced by Representative Thomas Massie, a Republican from Kentucky, who sought to gather votes from colleagues dissatisfied with Hegseth's policies and the conflict in Iran. However, no other Republicans voted with Massie. Meanwhile, 11 Democrats did not participate in the vote, including former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi. Dawn reported that if eight of those absent Democrats had voted against the procedural motion, it would have failed, opening the door for a full impeachment vote. The specific reason for Pelosi's absence is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • US President Donald Trump commended the outcome on social media, describing Hegseth's actions regarding Iran and Venezuela as an exoneration. Vice President JD Vance also supported Hegseth, stating the dispute was over Hegseth confronting Pentagon bureaucracy.
  • House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries accused Hegseth of abusing power and managing a failed war in Iran, noting that Democrats would discuss whether to attempt impeachment again in the future.
  • Hegseth defended his record as lawful during a Pentagon briefing, dismissing the impeachment push as a farce and defending actions such as a January military operation against Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro.
  • Hegseth has actively opposed internal diversity programs and previously dismissed several senior military officials, including the Black chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the female top admiral of the Navy.
  • The last time a US civilian military leader was impeached was in 1876, when William Belknap served as secretary of war.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that the vote grants Hegseth a temporary reprieve before Democrats take control of Congress in January following recent midterm elections. Democratic momentum was largely driven by public opposition to the ongoing war in Iran, which remains focused around the Strait of Hormuz.

Once in the majority, Democrats could initiate broader proceedings, potentially calling dismissed military commanders to testify. Future inquiries could also review policies regarding Ukraine and military strikes on suspected drug trafficking boats in Pacific and Caribbean waters. Furthermore, Hegseth's tense relationship with lawmakers comes as the administration seeks to raise the defense budget from $950 billion to $1.5 trillion.
