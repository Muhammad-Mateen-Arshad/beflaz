Germans join Europe's wave of 'Gen Z' protests; police fire water cannon on demonstrators

Published on October 31, 2026

What happened

Youth-led demonstrations took place across Germany on Saturday, organized by the group "GenZ regelt!" (Gen Z has got this). According to Dawn, thousands of police officers were deployed across the country as rallies were held in locations including Berlin, Frankfurt, Cologne, and Hamburg. While the gatherings were largely peaceful during the day, evening tensions escalated into confrontations in several areas.

In Cologne, law enforcement used a water cannon to disperse crowds after police stated officers were targeted with fireworks. Processions in both Cologne and Frankfurt were temporarily halted due to pyrotechnics. In Berlin, demonstrators threw bottles and eggs, while barricades were set on fire in Hamburg, prompting police to use pepper spray in some locations, as reported by German news outlet Bild.

Key facts

  • Police turnout estimates included approximately 8,000 demonstrators in Frankfurt, 5,000 in Berlin, and several thousand in Cologne, falling below the 170,000 nationwide turnout anticipated by organizers.
  • Protesters displayed signs targeted at conservative Chancellor Friedrich Merz, calling for resistance.
  • Movement spokesperson Veronika Dinter stated that participants are demanding a life that everyone can afford, citing widespread youth pessimism about the future.
  • Demonstrators voiced opposition to high living expenses, poorly maintained schools, government austerity measures, elevated military spending, and a new voluntary service framework they fear could lead to mandatory conscription.
  • Exact numbers of arrests and injured officers or protesters were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

The rallies in Germany form part of a broader wave of youth-led political unrest inspired by similar recent movements in France and Italy. German authorities established contact with officials in France and Italy due to concerns that radical activists from abroad might try to join the German demonstrations, though police noted no concrete evidence of planned violence prior to the events.

While "GenZ regelt!" explicitly distances itself from violence, extremism, and hate speech, protest expert Christian Volk from Humboldt University noted that the movement holds potential for significant growth in Germany, despite key differences from France's more confrontational protest history.
