NCCIA claims uncovering network allegedly involved in up to Rs1tr betting, money laundering

Published on November 20, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan's National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) has uncovered a widespread financial network involved in illegal online gambling, betting, fraudulent business operations, and suspected money laundering. Karachi NCCIA Additional Director Shahzad Akbar revealed at a press conference that the total transaction exposure linked to the network could reach up to roughly Rs1 trillion.

The inquiry began after the State Bank of Pakistan flagged suspicious activities operating across commercial banks, microfinance institutions, and digital wallets. Investigators determined that illegal payments were gathered through digital channels, including two major mobile wallets, before being funneled through registered firms, dummy business establishments, and rented accounts to hide the money's true source and beneficiaries.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the agency's investigation established several key operational and financial details:

  • Targeted platforms: The gambling network operated using platforms such as 1XBet, Stake, X777, X555, 99c444, X111, and MelBet.
  • Transaction volumes: Financial reviews uncovered Rs3.72 billion credited to one settlement account within around 22 months, Rs2.43 billion deposited into a business account, and Rs1.97 billion received through suspected mule accounts.
  • Asset freeze: Karachi NCCIA seized approximately Rs1 billion in suspected illicit funds across several bank accounts within a single day to prevent transfers.
  • Legal proceedings: A First Information Report (FIR) filed on November 18 charged 40 individuals and business entities under electronic crime laws and the Pakistan Penal Code. The document lists 32 company chief executive officers and five arrested suspects.
  • App development: The agency claimed that the majority of individuals who developed the various gambling applications were Chinese nationals.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the case exposes critical vulnerabilities in financial compliance and corporate oversight. NCCIA Islamabad/Rawalpindi Additional Director Muhammad Shoaib Khan previously noted that weak verification systems allow fake companies to open bank accounts that are subsequently linked to betting operations and illicit transactions.

Additionally, investigators are scrutinizing bank officials who allegedly allowed suspicious accounts to continue operating despite receiving anti-money laundering alerts. Specific identities for all charged entities and individuals were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
