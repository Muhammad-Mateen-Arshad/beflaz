Makkah alliance binds partners to decade-long pact

Published on October 21, 2026

What happened

Turkiye has submitted the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement to its parliament for ratification, according to Dawn. Signed in August by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye, the pact binds the three nations to a mutual defence commitment where an armed attack on one member is treated as an attack on all.

Key facts

According to Dawn, key elements of the alliance and related developments include:

  • Duration and activation: The pact is valid indefinitely, and members cannot withdraw during the first 10 years. It will enter into force after ratification documents are deposited in Riyadh. Specific deployment numbers are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Ratification process: Pakistan has already ratified the treaty through federal executive authority under Article 90 of its constitution. Turkiye requires explicit parliamentary approval to send forces abroad.
  • Nature of assistance: An official from Turkiye noted that Ankara’s involvement is expected to center on intelligence sharing, weapons systems, and military equipment rather than direct troop deployment.
  • Scope of agreement: The pact commits members to use all necessary means to protect territorial sovereignty and includes joint efforts in logistics, counter-terrorism, and defence manufacturing.
  • Additional defence deals: Egypt's parliament is scheduled to vote on authorizing military support for Saudi Arabia on October 26. Meanwhile, Pakistan, Turkiye, Qatar, and Indonesia are slated to sign a separate defence industry cooperation memorandum in Doha.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Turkish officials framed the trilateral treaty as a critical step toward formalizing regional security and deterrence. Burhanettin Duran, communications director for President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, stated that the partnership aims to build joint capabilities and safeguard stability. Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan clarified that the pact is purely defensive rather than offensive, confirming Turkiye will not dispatch military forces to launch attacks inside foreign territories.
