Barrick Mining expresses interest in mineral processing industries in Karachi, Gwadar

Published on November 13, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, a delegation from the Barrick Mining Corporation, led by Chairman John Thornton, met with Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in Islamabad. The Prime Minister’s Office announced that the delegation expressed a strong interest in setting up mineral processing facilities in Karachi and Gwadar. Discussions during the meeting covered regulatory, policy, and infrastructure frameworks intended to advance the nation's mining industry. In a separate meeting, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif also hosted Robert Friedland, the founder and executive co-chairman of Ivanhoe Mines, to discuss potential foreign partnerships in local mineral extraction.

Key facts

  • Barrick Mining voiced strong interest in processing plants in Karachi and Gwadar, praising the government's decision to hold a minerals conference. Specific financial amounts for these proposed processing projects were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The Reko Diq mine, a massive copper and gold deposit, is jointly owned by Barrick Mining alongside the federal and Balochistan governments. Dawn reported that the site is expected to generate $70 billion in free cash flow and $90 billion in operating cash flow.
  • Per industry insiders cited by Dawn, production at Reko Diq could face a two-year delay until 2030, pending improved security conditions in Balochistan and de-escalation in the Middle East.
  • Ivanhoe Mines expressed interest in cooperating with local partners, with the Prime Minister promising support through the Special Investment Facilitation Council following an exchange of MoUs.
  • Attendees at the meeting included Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar, federal Ministers Muhammad Aurangzeb and Ali Pervaiz Malik, and Special Assistants Tariq Fatemi and Lieutenant General (Retd.) Muhammad Zafar Iqbal.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, the government considers projects like Reko Diq to be critical milestones for Pakistan's economic future. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif highlighted that harnessing natural resources in Balochistan is a priority to generate local employment, foster skills development, and boost regional prosperity. The country aims to leverage international expertise and modern technology to establish a stable environment for ongoing global mining investments.
