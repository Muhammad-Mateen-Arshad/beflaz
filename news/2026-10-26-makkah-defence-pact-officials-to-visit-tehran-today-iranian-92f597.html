Makkah defence pact officials to visit Tehran today: Iranian state media

Published on October 26, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, high-ranking representatives from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkiye are traveling to Tehran on Monday for talks with Iranian leaders. The visiting delegation features Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin, and Pakistan’s Chief of Defence Forces Field Marshal Asim Munir. During the visit, officials will hold discussions with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and security chief Mohsen Rezaei.

Key facts

According to Dawn, key elements of the talks and diplomatic efforts include:

  • Main agenda: Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei indicated the discussions will center on pressing regional security concerns, including developments in Palestine and Lebanon, as well as the fallout from US and Israeli aggression against Iran.
  • Alliance stance: Saudi official Rayed Krimly stated that the three visiting nations are members of the Makkah Defence Alliance. The trip aims to foster de-escalation, advance diplomatic solutions, and reinforce regional security alongside ongoing mediation efforts by Qatar and Pakistan.
  • Alliance terms: Under the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement signed in August, an armed strike against any single member state is treated as an attack on all members.
  • Prior discussions: The meeting follows a proposal made during Field Marshal Munir's visit to Tehran the previous week. Further specific details regarding his itinerary were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, this delegation represents the initial high-level contact between Iranian leadership and the full Makkah Defence Alliance since regional hostilities widened. Conflict escalated in late February when US and Israeli strikes hit Iran during nuclear talks. Despite an April ceasefire ending major bombardments, friction remains high over the Strait of Hormuz, where Iran targets unauthorized ships while the US enforces a naval counter-blockade. With formal diplomatic efforts stalled since a June agreement collapsed, conflict has broadened to involve combat between Saudi Arabia and Houthi forces in Yemen.
