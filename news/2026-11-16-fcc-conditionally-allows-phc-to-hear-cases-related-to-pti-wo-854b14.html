FCC conditionally allows PHC to hear cases related to PTI workers' preventive detention

Published on November 16, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the Federal Constitutional Court (FCC) has modified its prior directives to conditionally allow the Peshawar High Court (PHC) to hear cases regarding the preventive detention of Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) workers under the Maintenance of Public Order Ordinance (MPO). The decision follows the imposition of emergency and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). The two-member FCC bench, comprising Chief Justice Aminuddin Khan and Justice Aamer Farooq, stipulated that the PHC remains barred from entertaining petitions that challenge the October 11 proclamation of emergency and governor’s rule in the province.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the primary details surrounding the legal proceedings and provincial security stance include:

  • The FCC previously halted PHC hearings on November 5, after the high court granted interim bail to detainees against surety bonds of Rs100,000 each.
  • The KP Home and Tribal Affairs Department's additional chief secretary filed over 60 appeals challenging the PHC's interim orders.
  • Under constitutional provisions cited by the court, high courts are restricted from exercising jurisdiction under Article 199 in regions where the armed forces are assisting civil power.
  • KP Advocate General Sanaullah Khan submitted a report stating that security threats across the province remain volatile, rendering the ongoing deployment of armed forces indispensable.
  • The provincial government urged the FCC to maintain the suspension of high court proceedings under Article 199 for petitions filed on or after October 12.
  • The court accepted applications from PTI Chairman Barrister Gohar Ali Khan and the Peshawar Bar Council to become parties to the proceedings.
  • Full details regarding future hearing schedules were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the ruling touches on key constitutional questions regarding high court jurisdiction while armed forces assist civil administration. The federal government enacted emergency measures and governor's rule on October 11, citing the provincial government's deliberate disengagement from counter-terrorism efforts. During the hearing, Justice Farooq noted that while constitutional provisions limit high court jurisdiction during military assistance, a strictly literal interpretation could bring the entire judicial system to a halt.
