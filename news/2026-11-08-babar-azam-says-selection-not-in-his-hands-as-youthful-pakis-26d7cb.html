Babar Azam says selection not in his hands as youthful Pakistan face Sri Lanka

Published on November 08, 2026

According to Dawn, Pakistan Test captain Babar Azam clarified during a pre-series press conference on Sunday that squad selection remains the duty of the selectors and head coach. He stated that his primary role is to lead and maximize the performance of the 15 players assigned to him as a revamped Pakistan team prepares to face Sri Lanka in a two-match Test series.

What happened

According to Dawn, Babar addressed questions regarding squad changes following Pakistan's recent 3-0 Test series loss in England. He explained that team management decided to shift focus toward preparing for the next World Test Championship (WTC) cycle by integrating several domestic players alongside senior members to build a balanced squad for the future.

Key facts

According to Dawn, the main details of the upcoming series and team standings include:

  • Squad composition: The team includes relatively inexperienced players such as Sameer Minhas, Mohammad Taha, Mohammad Suleman, Azan Awais, Arafat Minhas, Aamir Jamal, Ali Shabbir, Saqib Khan, and Mohammad Tayyab Arif, supported by seniors Saud Shakeel, Salman Ali Agha, and Mohammad Abbas.
  • WTC standings: Pakistan is ninth and last in the 2025-27 WTC standings with a 4.63 points percentage after seven losses in nine games, heavily affected by a 19-point deduction for slow over-rates. Sri Lanka ranks seventh with 33.33 percent.
  • Head-to-head record: Out of 59 previous Test meetings between the teams, Pakistan has won 23, Sri Lanka has won 17, and 19 ended in draws.
  • Match schedule: The first Test will be played at the Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium from November 9 to 13, followed by the second Test at Lahore's Gaddafi Stadium from November 17 to 21.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the upcoming series provides Pakistan a chance to rebuild following a challenging WTC campaign. Even a 2-0 series victory will do little to restore Pakistan's chances of reaching the WTC final, but it allows the management to test young talent. The series also offers Babar Azam an opportunity to end a prolonged century drought in Test cricket, with his last hundred coming against New Zealand in Karachi in December 2022. Pakistan also enters the series backed by recent confidence after beating Sri Lanka in an ODI tri-series final in late October.
