PIA passengers in UK left distressed as 2 flights cancelled due to abrupt Norse Atlantic suspension

Published on November 25, 2026

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistan International Airlines (PIA) was forced to cancel two UK flights after its wet-lease partner, Norse Atlantic Airways, abruptly suspended operations without prior warning. The sudden halt affected passengers preparing to board flight PK786 from London Heathrow to Islamabad and flight PK710 from Manchester to Lahore. In response, PIA announced it will move its UK flight operations to its own Boeing 777 aircraft starting November 26 to protect schedule integrity.

What happened

PIA expressed regret over the inconvenience caused to travelers and stated its support teams are actively contacting impacted passengers to arrange alternative flights. Specific refund procedures were not specified in the source; please check the official notice. The airline confirmed it is pursuing the matter with Norse Atlantic Airways at the highest contractual levels while transitioning directly to its own dedicated aircraft to maintain reliable service.

Key facts

  • Norse Atlantic Airways stated its financial situation is critical and that it requires new financing for short-term liquidity needs to stay operational.
  • The long-haul budget carrier saw its stock plunge by 76 percent following its financial announcement.
  • Norse Atlantic faced severe cash flow pressures due to soaring fuel costs linked to conflict disruptions involving the US, Israel, and Iran, alongside a lack of fuel hedging.
  • Norway's Civil Aviation Authority imposed a daily fine of 8,000 Norwegian crowns on Norse Atlantic for failing to pay financial compensation owed to passengers.
  • PIA had signed an agreement with Norse Atlantic in October to lease two Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners to meet rising passenger demand.
  • PIA was privatized in December of last year after a consortium led by Arif Habib Corporation Limited purchased 75 percent of its shares for Rs135 billion.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the disruption reflects broader financial strains across smaller, low-cost European carriers exposed to elevated jet fuel prices. By shifting its UK network to its own Boeing 777 fleet, PIA intends to shield its schedule from third-party risks and ensure continuous operation across all flight routes.
