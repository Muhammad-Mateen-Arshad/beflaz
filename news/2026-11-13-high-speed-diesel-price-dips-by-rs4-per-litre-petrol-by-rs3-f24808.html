High-speed diesel price dips by Rs4 per litre, petrol by Rs3

Published on November 13, 2026

What happened

The government has lowered domestic fuel prices, reducing petrol by Rs3 per litre and high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs4 per litre, according to a report by Dawn. This adjustment marks the eighth consecutive drop in fuel costs. Following a notification issued by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (Ogra), petrol will retail at Rs332 per litre and HSD at Rs360 per litre on Friday, Nov 13.

Key facts

According to Dawn, several factors and regulations surround the current pricing structure:

  • Taxation remains at Rs114 per litre for petrol and Rs100 per litre for diesel.
  • Under a 30-day agreement starting Nov 2, oil refineries are absorbing a collective Rs15 billion impact on the petroleum levy, providing a Rs14.71 reduction for petrol consumers.
  • Pricing authority was shifted to Ogra to allow near-daily revisions based on international trends following hostilities between Iran and the US.
  • Prices previously reached record peaks on April 3, with petrol hitting Rs458.41 per litre and HSD reaching Rs520.35 per litre.
  • Global crude markets saw Brent futures rise to $93.40 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate move to $86.54 a barrel amid geopolitical tensions.

Why it matters

Changes in fuel rates significantly affect everyday consumers and key industries. Petrol is predominantly used in private vehicles, two-wheelers, rickshaws, and small cars, directly impacting middle and lower-middle-class households. High-speed diesel powers heavy transport, power generation, and large commercial generators, influencing broad economic sectors. Combined monthly sales for petrol and HSD range between 700,000 and 800,000 tonnes, compared to 10,000 tonnes for kerosene.

In response to international price instability, government measures include the Prime Minister’s Fuel Relief Scheme for small-engine vehicles, motorcycles, and rickshaws, as well as energy conservation rules such as closing markets by 9pm and reducing official vehicle fuel quotas by 50 percent for three months.
