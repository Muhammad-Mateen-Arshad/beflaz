This Diet May Help Build Cognitive Resilience As You Age, Study Shows

Published on November 24, 2026

Key Takeaways

Research suggests that following the MIND diet may help older adults build cognitive resilience. A study published in Frontiers in Nutrition indicated that this eating pattern helped participants maintain cognitive performance and memory even when structural brain changes were present. This protective buffering effect was not observed with general healthy eating guidelines.

What the Source Says

As people age, structural changes can occur in the brain, including shrinking cortical volume and accumulating white matter hyperintensities. To see how diet affects these changes, researchers studied 66 older adults aged 60 to 82 who were at elevated risk for dementia based on family history or personal cognitive concerns. Roughly 65% of participants identified as Black and 73% identified as female.

Participants completed food questionnaires to determine their MIND diet and Healthy Eating Index scores, alongside brain MRI scans and tests for memory, executive function, and overall cognition. The study authors found that higher MIND diet scores were associated with better memory test results overall. For individuals with low adherence to the MIND diet, markers of brain damage predicted worse cognitive performance. However, high adherence to the MIND diet significantly blunted this negative impact, demonstrating cognitive resilience. Research suggests antioxidants and anti-inflammatory compounds in these foods may support the blood-brain barrier and enhance synaptic function.

Practical Notes

The MIND diet combines aspects of the Mediterranean and DASH diets, emphasizing specific nutrient-dense foods:

  • Leafy greens like kale, spinach, or arugula (aim for at least six servings a week)
  • Berries such as blueberries and strawberries (aim for at least two servings a week)
  • Nuts such as almonds, walnuts, and pistachios
  • Beans (aim to include them in at least three meals per week)
  • Whole grains (target three or more servings daily)
  • Fatty fish like salmon and sardines (aim for at least two servings a week)
  • Olive oil used for cooking or salad dressings

Before making any major changes to your diet or nutrition routine, be sure to speak with your doctor.
