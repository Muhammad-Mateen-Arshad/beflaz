Scientists discover a surprising paradox in how our cells age

Published on October 15, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests cellular recycling, known as autophagy, does not universally decline with age as long believed.
  • How cellular cleanup changes over time depends significantly on the specific type of cell and an individual's sex.
  • In older adults, higher rates of cellular recycling were linked to reduced physical performance markers.

What the Source Says

Autophagy is the natural process cells use to clear out damaged materials and turn cellular waste into basic components that can be reused. While researchers traditionally assumed that this cleanup system slows down as people age, a study from the Sanford Burnham Prebys Medical Discovery Institute indicates a far more nuanced process.

Scientists gathered blood and skin samples from healthy participants between 23 and 72 years old, converting some skin samples into specialized neurons to evaluate three cell types. The findings revealed that cellular recycling activity changes differently based on the cell type and sex. For instance, skin cells from male participants showed declining recycling with age, whereas female skin cells remained stable, and female neurons demonstrated increased recycling as age progressed.

The study also highlighted that measuring gene expression alone does not reliably predict the actual speed of cellular recycling. Furthermore, for participants aged 70 and older, increased autophagy was associated with lower physical health measures, such as weaker grip strength, lower aerobic fitness, higher body mass index, lower metabolism, and higher resting heart rate.

Practical Notes

Research suggests that cellular health and aging vary across different body tissues and between sexes. Because higher cellular recycling may have different health implications for older adults compared to younger individuals, experts emphasize the need to study autophagy on a cell-specific level before using it for targeted health therapies.

As scientists continue to study how cellular processes affect overall vitality, older adults should focus on maintaining general wellness habits. Please consult your doctor for personal medical guidance or before making changes to your health and exercise routines.
