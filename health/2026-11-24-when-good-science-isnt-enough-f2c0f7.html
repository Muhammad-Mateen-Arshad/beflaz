When good science isn’t enough

Published on November 24, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • The National Institutes of Health awarded a $3.67 million grant renewal to the Washington University Center for Diabetes Translation Research to support its work through 2031.
  • Estimates from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention show that approximately 40 million Americans have diabetes and 115 million adults have prediabetes.
  • Research suggests that connecting clinical research with real-world community programs helps deliver vital health interventions to more people.

What the Source Says

Despite progress in diabetes care—including continuous glucose monitors, lifestyle interventions, and newer medications—the public health impact of diabetes remains severe. Officials at the Washington University in St. Louis Center for Diabetes Translation Research (CDTR) note that high-quality clinical science must extend beyond clinic walls to effectively support populations in need.

To achieve this, the CDTR partners with the University of Missouri and the Parents as Teachers National Center. The initiative utilizes four specialized research cores: implementation science, health informatics using artificial intelligence, rural health networks, and home visiting. A landmark study in 2002 demonstrated that structured lifestyle changes could lower the risk of type 2 diabetes by 58% among high-risk adults. However, because traditional programs required 16 individual sessions across six months, bringing health education directly into home visits helps families overcome scheduling and participation barriers.

Practical Notes

Translational research focuses on taking proven scientific strategies and turning them into practical, community-based support networks. Specific local participant eligibility guidelines are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

If you have questions about blood sugar, lifestyle changes, or your overall health, please talk to your doctor.
