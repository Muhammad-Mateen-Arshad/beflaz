Oat sales soar as shoppers seek 'nature's Ozempic' products that keep you fuller for longer

Published on November 25, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Demand for nutrient-dense, high-fibre foods like porridge oats and seeds has grown significantly in supermarket baskets.
  • Interest in GLP-1 appetite-regulating medications is encouraging broader food shopping changes toward high-protein and high-satiety options.
  • While foods do not replicate the potency of medical GLP-1 treatments, items high in fibre and lean protein can provide a milder feeling of fullness over shorter periods.

What the Source Says

According to data from Worldpanel by Numerator, approximately 2.6 million adults in Britain were using GLP-1 medications—such as Ozempic, Wegovy, or Mounjaro—in August this year. A survey of over 4,000 customers revealed that 88 percent of these users adjusted their shopping habits, with more than half eating more protein and a third reducing alcohol consumption.

This shift has influenced broader consumer behaviour, driving popularity for foods that encourage satiety. Waitrose reported in its annual food and drink report that sales of Scottish Oats surged by 124% this year, while sales of a savoury seed and nut topper grew by 236%. Foods linked to this trend include items rich in fibre, lean protein, and monounsaturated fats, such as oats, eggs, Greek yoghurt, seeds, and legumes like lentils and chickpeas.

Dr. Joanne Lunn, head of health and nutrition at Waitrose, explained that GLP-1 trends and social media interest in overnight oats are reshaping food choices toward smaller, nutrient-rich portions. She also noted an emerging preference for savoury porridge served with ingredients like bone broth or roasted vegetables. While prescription GLP-1 medications regulate hunger signals for up to a week, research suggests that everyday foods like oats offer a similar but much milder effect over a shorter timeframe.
