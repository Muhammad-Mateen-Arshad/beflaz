Northwell to Convene Inaugural Food ‘Is’ Health Symposium

Published on November 10, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Northwell Health is hosting its first Food ‘Is’ Health Symposium on December 1, 2026, to examine community nutrition and food access.
  • More than 47 million Americans live in food-insecure households across the country.
  • Northwell Health has created 25 food-focused programs, including vertical gardening projects that have engaged over 250 senior citizens.
  • Initiatives also feature free refrigerated community food lockers, produce prescriptions, and upgraded patient dining options.

What the Source Says

Information from Northwell Health highlights a growing initiative connecting daily meals directly to general well-being. According to statements from the health system, its inaugural Food ‘Is’ Health Symposium brings together healthcare leaders, community organizers, and policymakers. Presenters include representatives from non-profit groups such as Harlem Grown, Sharing Excess, Rethink Food, and the West Side Campaign Against Hunger, alongside Northwell leadership including Dr. Debbie Salas-Lopez and Sven Gierlinger.

Northwell Health notes that its food services underwent a major transformation starting in 2016. By bringing in professional chefs, the health system eliminated deep fryers, introduced plant-based menu options, partnered with local farms, and built on-site gardens. These updates aimed to improve patient satisfaction while working toward a 25 percent reduction in food-related greenhouse gas emissions by 2030.

Additionally, Northwell Health highlights community-based projects like the Tower Garden initiative. This program uses soil-free vertical growing units across schools, senior centers, and community sites in Queens, Nassau, and Suffolk counties. Since 2024, over 250 older adults and 1,500 students have learned to grow their own food. Northwell Health research suggests that hands-on gardening helps foster healthier dietary habits that may lay the groundwork for better overall health outcomes. The health system is also expanding community food lockers and produce prescription options.

Direct registration links or specific virtual sign-up details were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Practical Notes

Older adults looking to enhance their nutrition can explore local community gardening programs or regional food assistance resources. Always discuss your personal dietary needs and any major nutritional changes with a qualified healthcare professional. Readers should talk to their doctor before making significant alterations to their daily diet.
