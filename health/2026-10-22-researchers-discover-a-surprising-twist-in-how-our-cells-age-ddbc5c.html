Researchers Discover a Surprising Twist in How Our Cells Age

Published on October 22, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests cellular recycling, known as autophagy, does not simply decline across the entire body as people get older.
  • How cells process internal waste varies depending on biological sex and the specific cell type involved.
  • In individuals aged 70 and older, higher cellular recycling was unexpectedly linked to lower measures of physical fitness.

What the Source Says

Cells maintain their standard operations by breaking down damaged proteins and unwanted waste through a natural recycling system called autophagy. Researchers at the Sanford Burnham Prebys Medical Discovery Institute evaluated how this process behaves across adulthood in a study published in Aging Cell.

Scientists collected blood and skin samples from disease-free volunteers aged 23 to 72, and also generated nerve cells from the skin samples. Rather than finding a uniform decrease in cellular cleanup over time, they observed distinct patterns. In male participants, skin cell recycling declined with age, whereas it stayed constant in female participants. Unexpectedly, nerve cells derived from female volunteers showed higher recycling activity with advancing age.

For participants aged 70 and older, higher rates of cellular recycling were linked to lower physical function. Elevated recycling in blood cells was associated with reduced aerobic fitness, higher activity in skin cells was linked to a higher body mass index, and increased recycling in nerve cells corresponded to weaker grip strength, lower metabolism, and a elevated resting heart rate. The team also conducted a small trial with five volunteers aged 77 to 88 participating in a gentle physical activity routine. Full details on the results of this exercise program were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Practical Notes

This research suggests that higher cellular cleanup activity is not automatically a sign of better health in later life. Because cellular processes respond differently based on age, sex, and tissue type, scientific approaches to healthy aging require careful customization. Always consult your doctor before making any changes to your exercise routine or health habits.
