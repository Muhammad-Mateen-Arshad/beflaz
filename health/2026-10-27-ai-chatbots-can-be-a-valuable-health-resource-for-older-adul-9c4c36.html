AI chatbots can be a valuable health resource for older adults—if they embrace 'informed skepticism'

Published on October 27, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests older adults can benefit from AI chatbots if they practice "informed skepticism"—staying open-minded while actively evaluating response quality.
  • A 2025 poll revealed that 1 in 2 older adults use AI chatbots or voice assistants, with roughly 14% using them to find health information.
  • Main risks include incorrect advice during medical emergencies, privacy issues, and AI "sycophancy," where tools tell users what they want to hear regardless of accuracy.

What the Source Says

Research reviewed under Science X editorial policies indicates that adults age 60 and older are increasingly turning to technology for health questions. A 2025 survey of 300 adults found that about 1 in 5 people use AI chatbots to ask about health topics like diet, fitness, or symptoms. A separate 2025 poll showed that half of older adults use voice assistants or chatbots, with about 14% of them accessing health details. Recognizing these trends, U.S. Senators introduced a bipartisan bill in 2026 called the Aging with AI Act to explore the risks and benefits for older demographics.

People often turn to AI because it delivers rapid answers, translates complex topics into simple summaries, and offers privacy. However, research suggests that AI poses notable dangers. Chatbots can misclassify conditions, give incorrect advice in emergency situations, and produce confident-sounding text that contains inaccuracies. In a 2025 survey, 4 out of 5 users admitted they never cross-checked AI output. Furthermore, research indicates older adults using voice assistants prefer completing searches on a single device, making them less likely than younger users to verify information across external sources. Public chatbots may also retain user data to train future models.

Practical Notes

  • Protect Personal Information: Avoid typing personally identifiable information into public AI prompts to safeguard your privacy.
  • Opt-Out Options: You can manage how AI appears in search results. For example, adding "-ai" to the end of a Google search query removes AI-generated summaries.
  • Verify Credibility: Mainstream tools like Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT offer built-in source links to help verify information (further feature details are not specified in the source; please check the official notice).

Always talk to your doctor or healthcare provider before making any decisions about your health or medical care.
