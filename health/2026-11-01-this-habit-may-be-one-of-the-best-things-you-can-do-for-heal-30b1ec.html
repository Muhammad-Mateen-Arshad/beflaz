This Habit May Be One Of The Best Things You Can Do For Healthy Aging

Published on November 01, 2026

Key Takeaways

While physical exercise and proper nutrition are frequently highlighted for healthy aging, staying socially and mentally active is also important. Research suggests that regularly taking part in social and cognitive hobbies—referred to as lifestyle enrichment—may help slow the accumulation of frailty and lower the overall risk of developing frailty as adults age.

What the Source Says

The study analyzed data from the ASPREE Longitudinal Study of Older Persons (ALSOP), tracking 12,862 Australian adults aged 70 and older over an 11-year timeline. Researchers measured frailty using two standard assessment tools and categorized daily habits into distinct activity areas.

Findings from the research indicate:

  • Passive mental activities (such as reading, listening to music, or watching television) and adult literacy tasks (like computer use or letter writing) showed the strongest association with slowing annual frailty progression.
  • Going on community outings, maintaining close interpersonal networks, and joining group social activities were also connected to lower rates of annual frailty accumulation.
  • Over a median follow-up of 7.38 years, taking part in most mental and social activity domains was associated with a 2% to 5% reduced risk of incident frailty.
  • Creative and artistic pursuits were associated with a slower frailty trajectory over time, though they were not linked to a lower risk of initial frailty development. Active mental activities lowered incident frailty risk but did not significantly alter annual frailty trajectories.
  • These positive associations were more pronounced in female participants and individuals with middle-to-high socioeconomic status.

Practical Notes

The researchers suggest that cognitive and social engagement may help build physiological and psychological resilience by helping manage stress, offering social support, and fostering neuroplasticity. The study underscores the importance of accessible community resources, such as local libraries, social programs, and cultural spaces, to help older adults remain engaged.
