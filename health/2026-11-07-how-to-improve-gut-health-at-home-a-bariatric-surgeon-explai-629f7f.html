How to improve gut health at home? A bariatric surgeon explains how better gut health improves and supports healthy aging

Published on November 07, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests gut microbes contribute to digestion, immune responses, and broader bodily functions.
  • Disruptions in gut microbes, referred to as gut dysbiosis, are connected to altered immunity and ongoing low-level inflammation.
  • Microbiome compositions differ between individuals and evolve over a person's lifespan.

What the Source Says

Standard guidance for healthy aging often highlights physical activity, adequate sleep, and good nutrition. However, research suggests that gut microbes are also an important factor. In the digestive tract, microorganisms help process dietary fiber and create short-chain fatty acids, while also interacting with immune cells to help distinguish harmful threats from safe substances.

Dr Sandip Banerjee, a bariatric surgeon at Pelvinic Micro-Hospital, highlights the relationship between gut health and aging. When intestinal microorganisms become unbalanced, gut dysbiosis can occur. This condition is linked to immune changes and persistent, low-grade inflammation, which is associated with conditions that frequently affect older adults. This interaction flows both ways, as diet, medication, illness, and aging can all modify the microbiome, meaning bacterial changes might be either a cause or a symptom of a health issue.

Studies focusing on older populations have observed connections between specific microbial patterns, frailty, and poorer health outcomes. Researchers are investigating whether the state of the gut microbiome offers clues regarding biological aging.
