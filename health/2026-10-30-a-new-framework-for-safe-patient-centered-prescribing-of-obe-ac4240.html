A new framework for safe, patient-centered prescribing of obesity medications in older adults

Published on October 30, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • The American Geriatrics Society has introduced a framework to guide safe, patient-centered prescribing of obesity medications for older adults.
  • Because older individuals are underrepresented in clinical studies, treatment requires extra caution regarding risks like muscle loss, frailty, and dehydration.
  • Guidelines recommend combining medical therapies with supportive care, such as protein intake and resistance exercise.

What the Source Says

According to a paper developed by the American Geriatrics Society Healthy Aging Special Interest Group and published in the Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, clinicians now have a practical, case-based framework for prescribing weight management treatments to older adults. This includes GLP-1 and incretin therapies that act through gut-derived hormonal pathways.

Research suggests these medications can provide benefits for weight management, glycemic control, and other medical conditions. However, because older adults are frequently underrepresented in clinical trials, the American Geriatrics Society stresses the need to carefully evaluate age-related health factors before starting treatment. Clinicians are advised to conduct pretreatment evaluations of cognitive impairment, functional status, and nutritional health to manage risks such as sarcopenia, frailty, dehydration, malnutrition, and lean muscle loss.

The framework illustrates how healthcare providers can apply the Geriatrics 5Ms Framework—which focuses on Mind, Mobility, Medications, Multicomplexity, and Matters Most—to incretin therapy in older populations.

Practical Notes

The source emphasizes that prescribing obesity medication should be accompanied by key co-interventions. Specifically, treatment ought to be paired with adequate dietary protein intake and regular resistance training to support overall physical health.

To help manage these lifestyle changes, the framework notes that a collaborative team approach may be optimal for some individuals. This care team might include a registered dietitian, a physical therapist, a fitness trainer, and a behavioral health professional. Specific exercise routines and medication dosages are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Always talk to your doctor to discuss whether any medication or lifestyle program is appropriate for your individual health situation.
