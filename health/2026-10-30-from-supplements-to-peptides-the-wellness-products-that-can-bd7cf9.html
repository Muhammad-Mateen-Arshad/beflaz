From supplements to peptides, the wellness products that can get athletes busted for doping

Published on October 30, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Over-the-counter dietary supplements and wellness products can contain unlisted or restricted substances.
  • Research indicates widespread labeling errors and unexpected ingredients across various health products.
  • Intravenous (IV) fluid treatments can breach anti-doping guidelines due to strict volume restrictions.

What the Source Says

Many individuals assume that because a wellness product is legally sold over the counter, it is completely safe and free from hidden ingredients. Performance physiologist Dr. Mark Kovacs explains that wellness products pose risks through inaccurate labeling, manufacturing contamination, or listing prohibited ingredients under unfamiliar names. Research suggests significant problems with product accuracy; one study evaluating 3,132 dietary supplements revealed that 28 percent contained undeclared substances, while another study found nearly 90 percent of sports supplements were inaccurately labeled.

According to the US Anti-Doping Agency, categories such as weight-loss aids, energy boosters, pre-workout formulas, muscle builders, and sexual-enhancement items carry particularly high risks of contamination or hidden stimulants. Furthermore, David Trosin from the independent testing organization NSF notes that under the principle of strict liability, individuals are fully accountable for whatever substances enter their bodies, regardless of whether exposure was accidental.

Beyond traditional pills, health aids like hair-loss remedies and specialized med-spa therapies can cause unexpected complications. Research suggests that World Anti-Doping Agency rules prohibit receiving IV therapy infusions greater than 100 mL within a 12-hour period without a specific medical exemption, as large fluid amounts can dilute blood and urine samples.

Practical Notes

Everyday items purchased from stores, ranging from energy gummies to hair treatments, may contain hidden components not clearly listed on the package. Because manufacturing practices and label accuracy can vary widely, caution is necessary when choosing any health aid or recovery service.

Always talk to your doctor before starting any new dietary supplement, over-the-counter remedy, or medical treatment.
