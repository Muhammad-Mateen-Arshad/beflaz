Dr. Frank Lipman’s 5 Healthy Aging Habits for Men

Published on November 11, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that consistent lifestyle choices strongly influence healthspan, which is the period of life spent remaining healthy and active.
  • Prioritizing strength training, nutrient-rich foods, adequate sleep, active recovery, and social connection supports healthy aging.
  • Long-term consistency matters more than short-term perfection when building wellness habits.

What the Source Says

Dr. Frank Lipman, founder of the Eleven Eleven Wellness Center, outlined five core priorities for long-term health during an interview with Men's Fitness. His recommendations focus on practical habits that adults can maintain over decades to support physical function and daily independence.

For movement, Lipman highlights strength training to preserve everyday mobility, such as climbing stairs or getting out of a chair. He recommends targeting main muscle groups at least two days a week with movements like squats, presses, rows, deadlifts, or balance exercises. To support endurance, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines suggest at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly, such as brisk walking or swimming. Research suggests that higher cardiorespiratory fitness relates to a lower mortality risk.

In terms of nutrition, Lipman encourages a routine of minimally processed foods, adequate protein for muscle maintenance, and polyphenol-rich plant foods like berries, extra-virgin olive oil, and vegetables. This aligns with American Heart Association dietary guidance promoting whole grains, varied produce, and healthy proteins. Reducing refined carbohydrates and added sugars works best when the routine remains flexible and sustainable.

For sleep, CDC recommendations suggest seven to eight hours nightly for adults aged 65 and older. Keeping consistent bedtimes, putting away screens, moving caffeine earlier, and avoiding late alcohol or heavy meals encourage better rest. For recovery, simple habits like outdoor walks help, with research suggesting three 15-minute postmeal walks improved daily glucose control in older adults at risk for glucose intolerance. Finally, maintaining regular social ties through family, volunteering, or hobbies provides vital purpose.

Practical Notes

When starting a new strength or cardiovascular routine, build your activity level gradually and focus on proper technique. Specific individual workout prescriptions are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. If you experience persistent daytime exhaustion or loud snoring, mention it to your healthcare professional to evaluate possible sleep disorders. Always talk to your doctor before making major updates to your exercise or dietary habits.
