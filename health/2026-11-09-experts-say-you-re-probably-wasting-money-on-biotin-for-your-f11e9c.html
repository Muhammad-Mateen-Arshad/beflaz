Experts say you're probably wasting money on biotin for your hair. Here's what actually helps

Published on November 09, 2026

Many adults look for simple ways to support thinning hair or improve volume, often turning to popular beauty supplements that contain biotin. However, health experts note that taking extra biotin rarely delivers the promised results for average consumers.

Key Takeaways

  • Biotin supplements offer limited benefit for hair growth in adults who do not have a true nutritional deficiency.
  • Most people obtain enough biotin naturally through everyday foods such as meat, fish, eggs, nuts, and vegetables.
  • Unnecessary supplementation can distort critical lab test results, including emergency heart health screens.

What the Source Says

Biotin, also known as vitamin B7 or vitamin H, is a water-soluble nutrient that helps activate enzymes involved in metabolic processes. These functions support keratin, a key structural protein in hair and nails. Research suggests, however, that taking biotin beyond basic body requirements does not boost hair thickness or growth in healthy individuals.

A 2024 review published in the Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology evaluated biotin studies and found sparse, low-quality evidence supporting its use for hair loss. According to the National Institutes of Health Office of Dietary Supplements, true deficiencies are rare. Higher risk is generally limited to individuals who are pregnant, breastfeeding, drink heavily, or have a rare genetic condition called biotinidase deficiency.

Medical professionals also highlight safety concerns regarding lab testing. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) warns that biotin supplements can interfere with diagnostic tests, such as troponin assays used to diagnose heart attacks, as well as thyroid panels.

Practical Notes

Rather than relying on targeted hair supplements, specialists suggest focusing on lifestyle strategies and overall nutrition:

  • Consult a medical provider to evaluate potential underlying causes of hair shedding, such as low levels of iron, zinc, vitamin D, or thyroid hormones. Specific supplement doses are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Focus on a well-rounded diet rich in protein, such as a Mediterranean-style pattern incorporating lean meats, fish, seeds, and nuts.
  • Prioritize consistent sleep and stress management to help prevent elevated stress hormones from interrupting the natural hair growth cycle.

Be sure to talk to your doctor before taking any new supplement or changing your daily routine.
