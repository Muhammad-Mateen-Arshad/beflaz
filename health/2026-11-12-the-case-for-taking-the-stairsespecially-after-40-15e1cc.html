The Case for Taking the Stairs—Especially After 40

Published on November 12, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that stair climbing supports heart health, muscle strength, and bone density.
  • Short periods of stair climbing after eating may help lessen post-meal blood sugar spikes.
  • Building a consistent daily habit is highlighted as more important than high workout intensity.

What the Source Says

According to nutritionist Marián García, author of Mujeres de Hierro, choosing the stairs over elevators provides substantial wellness advantages. Research suggests that stair climbing enhances cardiovascular fitness, boosts daily energy expenditure, and strengthens both muscles and bones. These effects are particularly useful after age 40, when changes in metabolism, muscle mass, and hormones naturally occur. The source also points out that long-lived residents in Barbagia, Sardinia—a known Blue Zone—regularly walk up steep hills and staircases.

In one study cited in the source, sedentary adults climbed about 60 steps (around three flights) at a quick pace three times a day, three days a week. After six weeks, participants showed significant gains in heart fitness. Additionally, research suggests that just one minute of stair climbing following a meal helps muscles absorb circulating glucose, reducing post-meal blood sugar spikes.

Practical Notes

García suggests aiming for three flights of stairs, three times per day, targeting an effort rating of six or seven out of ten. This level of exertion should cause noticeable breathing and leg work without causing total exhaustion.

To further challenge the body, the source suggests two adjustments based on Frost's mechanostat concept, which describes how bone adapts to physical stress:

  • Climbing two steps at a time: This expands your stride, forces leg and glute muscles to work harder, and increases the physical stimulus placed on bones.
  • Varying your pace: Alternating between faster and slower speeds raises physical demand and prevents your legs from moving automatically.

Stair climbing is presented as a simple, repeatable daily action rather than a complete substitute for cardio or strength training. Please talk to your doctor before beginning or changing any physical exercise routine.
