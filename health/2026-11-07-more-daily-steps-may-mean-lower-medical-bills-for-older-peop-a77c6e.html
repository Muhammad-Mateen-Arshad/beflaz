More daily steps may mean lower medical bills for older people

Published on November 07, 2026

Key Takeaways

Research suggests that older adults who walk more each day may experience lower subsequent healthcare expenses. A study conducted by a researcher at Osaka Metropolitan University indicates that the connection between daily step counts and medical spending is notably stronger for individuals aged 65 and older than for middle-aged adults.

What the Source Says

In a study published in SSM – Population Health, researcher Haruka Kato linked daily step counts with health insurance claims using data from 20,950 long-term users of Osaka Prefecture's A-Smile health app. The analysis evaluated information collected between 2020 and 2023 to observe healthcare spending over the subsequent six months.

The study used a flexible model to examine how spending patterns change across different activity levels, revealing several notable trends:

  • Among adults aged 65 and older, predicted medical spending was highest at low step counts and dropped more sharply as walking increased.
  • For adults aged 40 to 64, predicted healthcare costs changed relatively little across different step counts.
  • Across both age groups, the statistical curve leveled off at approximately 11,000 steps per day.

Practical Notes

The study authors emphasize that the 11,000-step benchmark is an observational finding rather than a mandatory daily goal. Because the investigation tracked spending rather than every overall physical or mental health outcome, walking beyond that level may still hold health value.

The findings do not prove that adding steps directly cuts medical bills. An alternative explanation is that individuals with poorer health or existing disabilities may walk less while simultaneously requiring more medical care. Additionally, the participants were long-term health app users within one specific region and insurance system in Japan, meaning the results may not automatically apply to populations elsewhere. Further research is required to see if walking programs can reduce costs and to identify practical approaches for people with differing physical abilities.

Please speak with your doctor before starting a new exercise program or making changes to your daily physical activity.
