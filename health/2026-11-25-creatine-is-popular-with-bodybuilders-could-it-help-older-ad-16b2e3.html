Creatine is popular with bodybuilders – could it help older adults stay independent?

Published on November 25, 2026

Key Takeaways

As people age, maintaining muscle strength becomes essential for daily independence. Research suggests that supplementing with creatine while engaging in resistance training may help older adults build slightly more strength than exercise alone.

What the Source Says

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound synthesized from amino acids and found in foods such as meat and fish. Muscles store creatine to quickly replenish energy during short bursts of exertion. Taking a supplement increases stored muscle creatine, which may help delay muscle fatigue during exertion.

  • Strength and Exercise: A 2026 review of 11 randomized trials in adults aged 60 and older revealed slight strength improvements when creatine was paired with resistance exercise. However, clear gains in muscle mass were less obvious, partly because creatine causes muscles to store additional water.
  • Everyday Function: Pooled trial data from chair-rise tests showed that middle-aged and older adults combining creatine with physical training performed better when standing up from a chair. Evidence regarding walking speed, balance, and reducing fall risks remains limited or inconsistent.
  • Cognition and Frailty: In trials involving frailer older adults, resistance training improved strength, but adding creatine provided no clear extra benefit. Research regarding brain health, memory retention, or dementia prevention is currently too limited to draw conclusions.

Practical Notes

NHS guidance recommends that adults aged 65 and over participate in activities to improve strength, flexibility, and balance on at least two days each week. Creatine monohydrate is the most thoroughly researched form, with trials using amounts ranging from 3 to 5 grams daily or higher. The exact dosage that might enhance everyday physical tasks is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Creatine is generally well tolerated, though some people report stomach discomfort or weight gain from fluid retention. Because creatine breaks down into creatinine, taking it raises blood creatinine levels, which can make routine kidney test results harder for clinicians to evaluate. Safety evidence is limited for individuals with kidney disease or those taking several prescription medicines.

Be sure to consult your doctor before starting any new dietary supplement.
