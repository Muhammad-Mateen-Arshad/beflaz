We tested 16 mushroom coffees — only 2 tasted like the real thing

Published on October 27, 2026

Key Takeaways

Mushroom coffee combines traditional coffee beans with functional mushrooms and adaptogenic ingredients. In an evaluation of 16 products across seven brands, a registered dietitian noted that while many options lack classic coffee flavor, select blends successfully mirror regular coffee while delivering about half the standard caffeine content.

What the Source Says

According to the source evaluation, mushroom coffee is frequently chosen by individuals looking to enhance focus, manage stress, or support immune health without the heavy caffeine kick of standard brews. Common formulations mix coffee with specialized mushrooms and herbs, such as lion's mane, cordyceps, reishi, chaga, and ashwagandha. Some manufacturers also fortify their blends with extra nutrients like vitamin D, vitamin B12, or gut-friendly additions like probiotics and inulin.

Research suggests that adaptogens may assist the body in responding to daily stress. Specific ingredients highlighted in the report include:

  • Cordyceps, which research suggests may assist in reducing stress and supporting physical stamina.
  • Lion's mane, which research suggests may help reduce stress while enhancing mood and mental focus.
  • Ashwagandha, an herb studied in human trials for its potential to help lower stress levels.

The author emphasizes that overall scientific research on these functional mushrooms remains limited, and there is currently no direct research evaluating mushroom coffee as a finished product.

Practical Notes

For older adults seeking a lower-caffeine option, tested top performers contained roughly 45 to 48 milligrams of caffeine per 8-ounce cup, compared to approximately 96 milligrams in traditional brewed coffee. Depending on whether the blend is instant or ground coffee, preparation may involve dissolving powder in hot water using a frother or using standard equipment like drip coffee makers, French presses, or pour-over devices.

Carefully review product labels before trying new blends. Ingredients like ashwagandha are not recommended during pregnancy and can potentially interact with medical treatments. If specific brand testing details or full ingredient breakdowns are missing, that information is Not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Always talk to your doctor before introducing new herbal supplements or mushroom products to your daily routine.
