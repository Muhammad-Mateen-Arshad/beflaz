The Surprising Nutrient That Could Lower Your Risk of Frailty by 18%, According to a New Study

Published on November 21, 2026

Key Takeaways

Research published in the Journal of Nutrition, Health & Aging suggests that higher blood levels of DHA, an omega-3 fatty acid, are associated with an 18% lower risk of developing frailty in aging adults. This relationship was primarily driven by a reduced likelihood of experiencing exhaustion and low physical activity. Notably, total omega-3 levels did not demonstrate a significant link to frailty risk.

What the Source Says

Investigators analyzed data from the UK Biobank, tracking 38,577 adults between the ages of 40 and 69 who were not frail when they entered the study. Over a median follow-up period of about 9.4 years, 1,024 participants became frail. Frailty was evaluated using five criteria: exhaustion, low physical activity, slow walking speed, low grip strength, and weight loss.

Higher blood plasma DHA levels were associated with a 24% lower risk of low physical activity and a 15% lower risk of exhaustion. In the group with the highest DHA levels, frailty affected fewer than two out of 100 participants, compared to four out of 100 in the group with the lowest levels.

Because the investigation was observational, it cannot prove that increasing DHA directly prevents frailty. Exact dietary targets or blood level standards were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Practical Notes

The report highlights several key points regarding how DHA is obtained:

  • Fatty fish such as salmon, trout, and sardines are rich dietary sources of DHA.
  • Algae-based supplements can serve as a direct DHA source for those who avoid fish.
  • Plant foods like chia, flax, and walnuts contain ALA, an omega-3 that the body only converts to DHA in small amounts.

You should always talk with your doctor before starting any new dietary supplement or making significant changes to your nutrition.
