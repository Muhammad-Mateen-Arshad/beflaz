Why Do You Wake Up With a Dry Mouth and Sore Throat in Winter? We Asked a Doctor

Published on November 28, 2026

Key Takeaways

Waking up with a dry mouth or throat discomfort during the winter months is a common experience. While many people attribute this to seasonal infections, research suggests that environmental factors like low humidity, reduced thirst, mouth breathing, and acid reflux are often the primary causes.

What the Source Says

According to Dr Tushar Tayal, Director of Internal Medicine at CK Birla Hospital in Gurugram, winter environments make the respiratory tract more susceptible to dryness. Cold outdoor air holds less moisture, and indoor heating systems further reduce humidity. Breathing dry air for extended periods while sleeping evaporates moisture from the throat and mouth.

Additionally, a study published in the journal PLOS One indicates that individuals often feel less thirsty during cold weather, even though the body continues losing water through normal functions. This reduced fluid intake can decrease saliva production. Mouth breathing—often caused by sinus congestion, allergies, colds, snoring, or sleep apnoea—also drastically increases moisture loss during sleep.

Another potential factor is gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD). Research published in BMC Gastroenterology shows that stomach acid can travel upward to the throat during sleep, irritating delicate tissues. This can result in hoarseness, throat clearing, or a bitter taste without obvious heartburn. Furthermore, certain medications like antidepressants or antihistamines can contribute to dry mouth. The source notes that a dry throat does not automatically mean a viral or bacterial infection is present unless accompanied by signs such as fever, body aches, persistent cough, or swollen lymph nodes.

Practical Notes

Simple daily habits can help maintain moisture and comfort throughout the winter:

  • Maintain steady fluid intake throughout the day, even if you do not feel thirsty.
  • Use a clean humidifier to keep indoor relative humidity between 30% and 50%.
  • Limit caffeine and alcohol consumption, especially near bedtime.
  • Use saline sprays for nasal congestion and avoid heavy meals two to three hours before sleeping if reflux is suspected.
  • Maintain good oral hygiene and avoid smoking or second-hand smoke.

If you experience persistent dry mouth, recurrent throat pain, trouble swallowing, or frequent snoring, we recommend that you talk to your doctor for proper medical evaluation.
