New health risk warning for anyone using almond, soy or oat milk

Published on November 05, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that replacing traditional dairy with plant-based alternatives like oat or almond milk may reduce the intake of essential vitamins and minerals.
  • Iodine levels dropped most significantly when dairy was replaced, alongside reductions in calcium, vitamin B2, and vitamin B12.
  • Nutrient fortification varies widely among plant-based alternatives, with only 12% of tested products containing added iodine.

What the Source Says

A study published in the Lancet Planetary Health by researchers from the University of Surrey and Queen’s University Belfast evaluated the dietary impact of swapping dairy for plant-based options. The researchers analyzed data from over 3,500 individuals in the National Diet and Nutrition Survey, reviewing 146 plant-based milks, 76 yoghurts, and 67 cheese substitutes.

The research suggests that even partial replacements of dairy led to lower levels of four key micronutrients: iodine, calcium, vitamin B2 (riboflavin), and vitamin B12. According to information cited from the NHS, calcium supports strong bones, healthy teeth, and muscle function, while vitamin B12 maintains the nervous system and blood cells. Vitamin B2 aids skin, eyes, and nervous system health, and iodine is vital for thyroid hormone production, metabolism, and brain function.

The study highlighted significant gaps in product fortification. Among the plant-based items evaluated, 61% contained added calcium, 46% contained vitamin B12, 19% had riboflavin, and just 12% included added iodine. Lead author Dr. Sarah Bath noted that dairy is a primary source of iodine in the UK. Providing additional perspective, Professor Tom Sanders noted that iodine in cow's milk comes from supplemental salt licks given to cattle, pointing out that seafood serves as the primary natural source of iodine.

Practical Notes

For adults looking to maintain balanced nutrition while using plant-based milks, checking nutrition labels for added minerals like calcium and iodine can provide clarity on nutrient content. Readers are encouraged to speak with their doctor before making major changes to their regular diet.
