Creatine Is Moving Beyond the Weight Room, Especially Among Adults 55 and Older

Published on November 26, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Data from MyFitnessPal shows a 74.9% increase in creatine logging among app users aged 55 and older.
  • MedlinePlus notes that creatine may improve strength in older adults, particularly when combined with muscle-building workouts.
  • Mayo Clinic indicates that research on creatine for memory and brain health is preliminary and requires further study.
  • Creatine serves as an addition to, not a replacement for, regular physical exercise.

What the Source Says

Creatine is a compound produced by the body and stored mostly in skeletal muscle. It is also naturally present in foods like meat and seafood. Muscles utilize stored creatine to produce energy during short, intense physical activities, such as sprinting or lifting weights. Most dietary supplements use creatine monohydrate, which is the most thoroughly researched form.

Recent tracking data from MyFitnessPal highlights a notable shift in interest, showing that creatine logging grew fastest among users age 55 and older, while users ages 45 to 54 logged the highest total volume. However, app data reflects only user entries rather than a broad scientific survey, and logging does not explain why individuals take the supplement or if they experience results.

Research supports using creatine alongside exercise to boost physical performance and strength gains. According to MedlinePlus, creatine may enhance strength in older adults, working best when paired with resistance training. Taking the supplement without engaging in regular exercise is unlikely to provide the strength benefits demonstrated in scientific studies.

Additionally, Mayo Clinic notes that early findings suggest potential benefits for memory and thinking skills, though more research is needed. Current evidence is far stronger for exercise performance than for treating health conditions or preventing cognitive decline.

Practical Notes

For healthy adults, creatine is generally considered safe when taken as directed. Possible side effects include stomach discomfort and weight gain caused by increased water retention in the muscles. Daily dosage amounts are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Mayo Clinic advises anyone with kidney disease to consult their healthcare team before using creatine. It is also wise to check with a health professional if you take medications or manage chronic conditions. Consumers looking to buy creatine should seek products that list creatine monohydrate as the main ingredient and have undergone independent quality testing. To ensure a supplement is appropriate for your specific health needs, talk to your doctor.
