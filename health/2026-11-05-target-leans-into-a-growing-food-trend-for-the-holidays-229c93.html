Target leans into a growing food trend for the holidays

Published on November 05, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Survey data shows a sustained high interest in protein intake among Americans over recent years.
  • Retailers are expanding protein-forward holiday treats, partly influenced by the growing use of GLP-1 weight-loss medications.
  • Nutrition professionals remind consumers that adding protein to processed treats does not automatically turn them into healthy foods.

What the Source Says

Data from the IFIC Food & Health Survey indicates that the percentage of Americans attempting to consume protein grew from 59% in 2022 to 67% in 2023, reaching 71% in 2024 and 70% in 2025. IFIC CEO Wendy Reinhardt Kapsak explained that many individuals are prioritizing protein for long-term health, even if they do not immediately see or feel the progress they hope for.

This interest is partly connected to GLP-1 weight-loss medications. Research highlighted by the Mayo Clinic notes that weight reduction often includes lean body mass, which covers muscle, water, and other tissues. Medical guidance for individuals taking GLP-1 drugs emphasizes obtaining adequate protein to avoid burning muscle instead of fat.

In response, major retailers like Target are adding wellness-focused holiday snacks. Target Chief Merchandising Officer Cara Sylvester described offering protein-forward holiday options alongside traditional festive foods to give shoppers options. Specific item release dates or a complete product list were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

However, health experts advise caution regarding protein-boosted snacks. Sofia Whitefields, a registered dietitian at the University of Utah's Osher Center for Integrative Health, pointed out that adding protein to items like chips, popcorn, or cereal does not make them inherently healthy. Furthermore, Cory Lomell from Cargill noted that standard animal proteins remain the primary source for main meals, with fortified snacks serving as a supplement.

Practical Notes

While high-protein festive items offer convenient snack options, nutrition experts suggest viewing the complete food profile rather than relying only on protein claims. Incorporating a balance of whole foods during regular main meals remains an established way to support overall nutrition.

Before making significant changes to your nutritional intake or starting high-protein supplements, readers should talk to their doctor.
