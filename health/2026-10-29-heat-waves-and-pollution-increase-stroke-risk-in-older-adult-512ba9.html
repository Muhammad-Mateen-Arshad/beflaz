Heat waves and pollution increase stroke risk in older adults, study finds

Published on October 29, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that consecutive days of extreme heat combined with fine particulate air pollution increase the immediate risk of acute stroke in older adults.
  • High temperatures can trigger dehydration, causing blood to thicken and placing strain on thermoregulation and blood pressure.
  • Higher cardiovascular health scores and regular physical activity are associated with reduced markers of cerebral small vessel disease.

What the Source Says

A study conducted by researchers at the University of São Paulo analyzed data from the Stroke Mortality and Morbidity Study (EMMA) focusing on adults aged 65 and older. The research defined a heat wave as at least two consecutive days with peak temperatures of 33.1 °C (92 °F) or higher. Findings indicate that each additional day spent under heat wave conditions was associated with an elevated risk of acute stroke.

This impact was heightened when high temperatures coincided with elevated fine particulate matter (PM10) air pollution. According to data presented by a professor from the USP Medical School, the continuous interaction of heat stress and PM10 pollution was associated with a 40.4% increase in acute stroke risk per additional day. Researchers explained that exposure to intense heat causes dehydration, which reduces fluid in the blood and makes it thicker. This disrupts normal thermoregulation and cardiovascular function, directly affecting blood pressure and cerebral blood flow.

In related research from the Longitudinal Study of Adult Health (ELSA-Brasil), investigators examined protective factors for brain health. Findings showed that every 10-point increase in the Life's Essential 8 cardiovascular health score was linked to a 14% reduction in the risk of severe cerebral small vessel disease lesions. Furthermore, engaging in moderate to high levels of physical activity was associated with a 21% to 22% lower risk of small vessel disease markers and a 21% to 30% reduction in risk of tissue loss in the entorhinal cortex, a brain region essential for memory processing.

Practical Notes

Specific regional environmental advisories or local emergency protocols are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Paying attention to heat advisories and maintaining daily cardiovascular health habits may help protect overall well-being. Always talk to your doctor for personalized medical guidance, exercise recommendations, and advice on managing heat exposure.
