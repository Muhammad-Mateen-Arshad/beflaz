Dropping dairy milk to go vegan is bad for you, study warns

Published on November 05, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that substituting dairy products with plant-based alternatives like almond or oat milk can reduce your intake of key vitamins and minerals.
  • Iodine levels were found to decrease most significantly, alongside drops in calcium, vitamin B12, and vitamin B2 (riboflavin).
  • The extent to which plant-based milks, cheeses, and yogurts are fortified with extra nutrients varies widely by product.

What the Source Says

A study published in The Lancet Planetary Health journal by researchers from the University of Surrey and Queen's University Belfast examined the nutritional impact of swapping standard dairy for plant-based alternatives. Using data from more than 3,500 individuals, the team analyzed 146 plant-based milks, 76 yogurts, and 67 cheese alternatives.

The study found that even partial replacement of dairy led to declines in essential micronutrients, particularly iodine. Researchers noted that standard dairy serves as a primary source of iodine, which supports thyroid function and brain health. Calcium is essential for building strong bones and teeth and regulating muscle contractions like the heartbeat. Vitamin B12 helps maintain healthy blood cells and the nervous system, while vitamin B2 (riboflavin) supports the skin, eyes, and nervous system.

Fortification levels in plant-based alternatives were shown to vary considerably. Among the products evaluated, only 12 percent contained added iodine, 61 percent had added calcium, 46 percent included vitamin B12, and 19 percent contained riboflavin. While younger demographics and women experienced the highest rates of nutrient inadequacy when eliminating dairy, nutrient reductions occurred across several age groups. Experts noted that while sustainable diet transitions are gaining interest, public health policies may need to address food fortification to ensure proper nutrition.
