Cancer Survivors Face Elevated Heart Disease Risk, But New Research Shows Healthy Diets May Help

Published on October 30, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests cancer therapies can place strain on the cardiovascular system, making heart disease a primary non-cancer cause of death for survivors.
  • A long-term study indicates that following certain nutritious eating patterns may help reduce elevated heart risks in cancer survivors.
  • Seven out of eight evaluated dietary styles were associated with cardiovascular benefits, whereas a healthy low-carbohydrate diet did not show significant heart advantages.

What the Source Says

A study published in JACC: Advances evaluated health records and dietary habits from more than 8,700 cancer survivors in the UK Biobank who had no history of heart disease at the start. Over an average follow-up period of 12 years, more than 1,100 participants developed cardiac conditions, such as stroke and ischemic heart disease.

Researchers examined eight dietary patterns to look for connections with heart health. Research suggests that seven patterns were tied to lower cardiovascular risks. Participants adhering closely to the Alternate Healthy Eating Index experienced lower risks of stroke and ischemic heart disease. The DASH diet was linked to reduced heart failure risk, and the MIND diet was tied to the lowest risk of early death from any cause. The healthy low-carbohydrate diet was the only pattern that did not display significant cardiac benefits.

Cardiologists from the University of Louisville Medical Center and City of Hope noted that treatments can accelerate heart disease risks, sometimes making cardiovascular issues the leading cause of death for older survivors within ten years of diagnosis. However, because this was an observational study, experts point out it cannot prove that dietary changes directly cause improved heart outcomes.

Practical Notes

Experts state that there is no single ideal diet, but finding a healthy approach that can be maintained over time is essential. Shared characteristics of beneficial eating styles include an abundance of fiber and plant foods—such as fruits, vegetables, beans, lentils, and whole grains—which support weight management, healthy cholesterol, and blood sugar levels.

Replacing saturated and processed fats with healthy fats, such as olive oil, nuts, seeds, and fatty fish, may help support blood vessel health and ease inflammation. Experts also advise pairing a nutritious diet with regular physical activity and proper control of health conditions like high blood pressure and diabetes. Please talk to your doctor before making any adjustments to your diet or health routine.
