NIH diagnostics accelerator enables first-ever FDA authorization for a point-of-care test for active syphilis

Published on November 24, 2026

A recent release from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) highlights a new diagnostic tool designed to rapidly identify the cause of certain skin lesions during a single medical appointment.

Key Takeaways

  • The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued an emergency use authorization for a point-of-care diagnostic panel developed by Roche.
  • The test analyzes a single lesion swab to detect four separate pathogens in approximately 15 minutes.
  • It identifies active infections by differentiating between syphilis, herpes simplex virus types 1 and 2, and monkeypox.

What the Source Says

According to information provided by the NIH, skin lesions caused by syphilis, herpes, and monkeypox often look similar, making diagnosis based on visual appearance challenging. The newly authorized molecular panel, known as the cobas® liat Lesion Panel, directly detects four target pathogens: Treponema pallidum (the bacterium that causes syphilis), herpes simplex virus types 1 and 2, and monkeypox virus. Research mentioned in the NIH release suggests that point-of-care direct detection helps clinicians distinguish between these conditions quickly to inform timely care.

Traditional diagnostic methods for syphilis rely heavily on blood tests for antibodies, which can reflect past successfully treated infections or miss early stages of an infection. Standard lab testing for herpes also typically takes longer than a single office visit. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention noted approximately 190,000 reported syphilis cases in the United States in 2024. If untreated, syphilis can lead to serious complications involving the brain, nervous system, and other body organs. Development and evaluation of this test involved a public-private collaboration, with NIH investing approximately $3.7 million toward the project.

Practical Notes

The diagnostic panel is intended for clinical use in primary care offices, health clinics, urgent care centers, emergency departments, and public health facilities. The source notes that the test is expected to become available in the United States in the coming months. Specific pricing and exact rollout schedules were not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Readers should also note that an Emergency Use Authorization allows product use during a public health emergency and is distinct from standard FDA clearance or approval. Always speak with your doctor or healthcare provider regarding any health symptoms or diagnostic concerns.
