AQI at Its Worst: Delhi, Gurugram and Mumbai Struggle to Breathe as Pollution Levels Soar Across Cities

Published on November 09, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Air pollution levels recently spiked across multiple cities in India, placing several regions in poor or very poor air quality categories.
  • Fine particulate matter, such as PM2.5, can travel deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream.
  • Older adults, children, pregnant women, and individuals with pre-existing heart or lung conditions face elevated risks from exposure.

What the Source Says

According to reporting shared by Onlymyhealth, measurements recorded on November 9, 2026, showed high pollution readings across several major cities. Delhi registered an average Air Quality Index (AQI) of approximately 370, with individual stations reporting levels above 400. Noida recorded an AQI of about 340, and Gurugram reported around 328, placing all three locations in the very poor category. Lucknow recorded an AQI of approximately 250, Pune registered around 242 (both categorized as poor), and Mumbai reported an AQI of about 171 (moderate category).

The source notes that India's Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) utilizes the AQI to convey health risks associated with air quality. Key contributors to elevated pollution include emissions from post-Diwali firecrackers, vehicular exhaust, road and construction dust, industrial output, rapid urbanization, and seasonal weather patterns. The source highlights that lingering in high pollution can trigger throat irritation, coughing, eye discomfort, and breathing difficulties, while potentially worsening asthma or elevating cardiac and respiratory risks.

Practical Notes

The source suggests several precautions to help lower personal exposure to outdoor air pollution:

  • Check live AQI monitors before planning outdoor activities.
  • Avoid exercising outdoors when the AQI enters the poor category (201–300) or higher.
  • Wear a well-fitted N95 mask if outdoor exposure cannot be avoided.
  • Keep home doors and windows closed during heavy pollution, and run an indoor air purifier if available.
  • Reduce indoor air pollution by avoiding smoking, incense sticks, and fume-producing household items.

Detailed threshold definitions for all six CPCB classifications are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

We recommend talking to your doctor for personalized advice regarding your health and managing any pre-existing respiratory or cardiovascular conditions.
