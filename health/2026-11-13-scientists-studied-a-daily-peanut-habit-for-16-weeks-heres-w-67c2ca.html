Scientists Studied a Daily Peanut Habit for 16 Weeks. Here’s What Changed in the Brain

Published on November 13, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that eating unsalted, skin-on roasted peanuts daily may alter how insulin affects memory-related brain regions in older adults.
  • The study observed shifts in brain blood flow response among participants after 16 weeks of daily peanut consumption.
  • The trial was small and did not demonstrate that eating peanuts improves memory or prevents age-related cognitive decline.

What the Source Says

In a study published in the journal Food & Function, researchers at Maastricht University Medical Center evaluated 31 healthy adults between the ages of 60 and 75 who had no diabetes or heart disease. Participants ate 60 grams—slightly over two ounces—of whole, unsalted, skin-on roasted peanuts daily for 16 weeks. This phase was followed by an eight-week break and another 16-week period without peanuts.

To assess the impact, scientists administered an insulin dose using a nasal spray and tracked changes in brain blood flow over 30 minutes with MRI scans. The researchers found that regions on the right side of the brain, including parts of the hippocampus and the lingual gyrus, responded differently to insulin after the peanut routine. Blood flow increased less in response to insulin, which senior author Peter Joris noted may reflect brain cells managing the hormone more efficiently. However, no changes were observed in liver fat, fasting blood sugar, general insulin levels, cholesterol, or markers of inflammation.

Practical Notes

The authors emphasized that these findings do not prove that eating peanuts prevents cognitive decline or sharpens memory. Because participants added the peanuts to their standard diets, researchers could not determine whether the results came from the peanuts themselves or general dietary changes involving added calories. Additionally, because the trial was limited to healthy older individuals, the potential effects on younger people or those with chronic conditions remain unknown.

The study received funding from The Peanut Institute Foundation, though the authors stated the foundation had no role in conducting or reporting the research. If you are considering changes to your diet, please talk to your doctor.
