Shanthi Johnson receives national honour for advancing healthy aging

Published on November 05, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Dr. Shanthi Johnson received the Canadian Association on Gerontology's 2026 Victor Marshall Distinguished Member Award for her leadership in healthy aging research.
  • Her work over nearly three decades focuses on fall prevention, social connection, and helping older adults maintain independence at home.
  • She developed a specialized home exercise program designed to help frail older adults maintain mobility and reduce their risk of falling.

What the Source Says

According to the Canadian Association on Gerontology (CAG), Dr. Shanthi Johnson was presented with the 2026 Victor Marshall Distinguished Member Award on October 17 during the organization's annual conference. The award, presented by CAG president Dr. Brad Meisner, represents the association's highest honor for contributions to aging practice, research, and policy.

Dr. Johnson currently serves as the vice-president of research and innovation at the University of Windsor. Her career includes establishing a falls prevention research laboratory while at the University of Regina, serving as dean of the School of Public Health at the University of Alberta, and contributing to national policy on the National Seniors Council.

Research suggests that physical activity tailored for home settings can help vulnerable seniors stay mobile. Dr. Johnson created a home-based routine targeting mobility and fall reduction, which earned an innovation award from the Regina Chamber of Commerce in 2016. At the University of Windsor, she works with Dr. Clinton Beckford on the Age-Friendly University Committee to promote lifelong engagement and age inclusivity.
