Thinking About Taking More Creatine? Safety Experts Say Your Current Dose Is Likely Fine

Published on November 06, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • The Council for Responsible Nutrition updated its safety evaluation for supplemental creatine monohydrate, establishing a higher safety benchmark for adults.
  • An increased safety limit indicates what research supports as safe, rather than a recommendation to increase your current daily routine.
  • Specific populations, including individuals with kidney issues or active cancer, should seek medical guidance before supplementation.

What the Source Says

On November 5, the Council for Responsible Nutrition (CRN) published an updated safety evaluation supporting 10 grams of daily supplemental creatine monohydrate for adults. This figure doubles the organization's benchmark from its 2006 review. CRN reports that its evaluation examined 162 clinical studies and found no serious adverse events connected to creatine use. Andrea Wong, PhD, senior vice president and chief science officer at CRN, noted that research reinforces the supplement's safety profile as interest in the ingredient expands.

The publication clarifies that 10 grams serves as a Highest Observed Intake (HOI)—a parameter evaluating safety based on available literature—rather than a targeted recommendation or proof that higher amounts offer better outcomes. For context, the National Institutes of Health's Office of Dietary Supplements describes a standard maintenance amount as 3 to 5 grams daily after an initial loading period.

Practical Notes

To help minimize potential gastrointestinal discomfort, the review suggests splitting daily creatine intake into single servings of no more than 5 grams. Furthermore, the assessment explicitly excludes individuals with impaired kidney function from its target group and advises them to consult a healthcare provider. People with active cancer are also advised to speak with their physician before taking the supplement. Readers should also note that CRN represents the supplement industry and funded this review of existing literature, meaning it is an industry safety assessment rather than a new clinical study.

Always talk to your doctor before making any changes to your supplement routine or daily dosage.
