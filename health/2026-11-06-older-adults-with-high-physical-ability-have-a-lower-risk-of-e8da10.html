Older adults with high physical ability have a lower risk of death

Published on November 06, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that higher physical fitness levels in older adults are associated with a reduced risk of all-cause mortality.
  • Objective physical tests evaluating balance, agility, leg strength, and endurance demonstrated strong links to improved survival outcomes.
  • The link between physical ability and lower mortality remained significant even after accounting for pre-existing medical conditions and activity levels.

What the Source Says

A cohort study published in JAMA Network Open by Wu, Min-Chen, and colleagues tracked 13,423 community-dwelling older adults aged 65 and older in Taiwan. Participants underwent physical fitness assessments across four main areas: cardiorespiratory fitness, muscle strength, flexibility, and balance/agility.

Over a median follow-up period of 7.0 years, researchers evaluated overall mortality rates. When comparing participants with the highest physical function to those in the lowest group, the study observed the following risk reductions:

  • 2-minute step test (cardiorespiratory endurance): 42% risk reduction
  • 30-second chair stand test (lower body strength): 45% risk reduction
  • One-leg stance test (balance): 50% risk reduction
  • 8-foot up-and-go test (balance and agility): 59% risk reduction
  • Composite physical function index: 61% risk reduction

These findings persisted after adjusting for sociodemographic factors, existing health conditions, and self-reported physical activity. The researchers noted that balance, agility, and overall composite scores showed particularly strong connections to lower mortality risk.

Practical Notes

Research suggests that objectively measuring physical fitness provides meaningful insight into an older adult's physiological reserve. However, because this was an observational cohort study rather than an intervention trial, it does not prove that actively improving physical function will directly extend lifespan. Specific individual exercise guidelines or clinical protocols are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Always talk to your doctor before beginning a new exercise program or making changes to your physical routine.
