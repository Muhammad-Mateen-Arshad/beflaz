What to eat for younger-looking skin

Published on October 20, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that a diet rich in plants, healthy fats, nuts, and oily fish supports skin structure and collagen.
  • Carotenoids found in colourful produce like tomatoes, peppers, and carrots help shield skin cells from environmental damage.
  • Portion sizes matter; small servings of fruits like mangoes show benefits, whereas higher sugar consumption may increase wrinkles.

What the Source Says

Skin naturally loses collagen as time passes, leading to visible lines. Jean Krutmann at the Leibniz Research Institute for Environmental Medicine notes that environmental factors—including air pollution, infrared radiation, visible sunlight, and rising temperatures—also speed up ageing.

Dietary choices can help reinforce the skin barrier. Volker Böhm at Friedrich Schiller University Jena explains that foods containing carotenoids, such as carrots, peppers, and tomatoes, build a continuous baseline defense against sunlight. Adding olive oil or similar healthy fats improves antioxidant absorption. Beta-carotene converts into vitamin A to aid skin-cell growth, while carotenoids assist cells in producing hyaluronic acid for firmness. Research suggests consistent consumption over eight to ten weeks is needed before environmental protection becomes measurable.

Fruit research highlights specific effects. A University of California Davis study led by Vivien Fam observed post-menopausal women eating 85g of mango four times weekly for 16 weeks. The participants experienced a reduction in new and existing wrinkles. However, research co-author Robert Hackman noted that consuming 250g of mango daily actually increased wrinkles, likely due to high natural sugar levels harming skin molecules. Specific dietary plans for other age groups are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Additionally, citrus and mangoes contain vitamin C, which aids collagen production. Snacking on almonds for 16 weeks was associated with fewer visible wrinkles in a small study. Nuts and seeds contain vitamin E and omega-6 fatty acids to bolster skin hydration and wound healing. Oily fish like salmon, sardines, and mackerel offer omega-3 fatty acids, which research suggests reduce sun sensitivity, lower inflammation, and help preserve existing collagen.

Practical Notes

Incorporate colourful produce, healthy fats, and nuts steadily over time rather than expecting instant results. Always discuss any major dietary changes or personal health concerns with your doctor.
