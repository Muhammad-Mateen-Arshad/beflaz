3 Popular Diets Led to Similar Weight Loss, but One Had a Surprising Advantage

Published on November 22, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that while low-fat, Mediterranean, and ketogenic diets yield similar overall weight loss, a ketogenic diet may provide a greater reduction in liver fat.
  • In a clinical study, participants following a ketogenic eating plan experienced a 67% average decrease in liver fat, compared to a 45% reduction for those on low-fat or Mediterranean diets.
  • A ketogenic approach also led to notable improvements in blood sugar management and liver insulin sensitivity for individuals managing prediabetes and fatty liver disease.

What the Source Says

A randomized trial conducted by researchers at Washington University School of Medicine and published in Cell Metabolism examined 55 adults living with obesity, prediabetes, and fatty liver disease, with 42 participants included in the final analysis. Over a period of four to five months, participants received pre-provided meals tailored to reduce total body weight by approximately 10%.

While every eating plan enhanced metabolic wellness, the ketogenic diet demonstrated distinct advantages. Participants in the ketogenic group received roughly 73% of their daily calories from fat. Despite the high fat content, researchers noted no significant increases in blood fat or cholesterol levels during the intervention. Furthermore, about half of the ketogenic group no longer met the criteria for prediabetes by the end of the trial, compared to 29% in the Mediterranean group and 7% in the low-fat group.

Study author Dr. Samuel Klein noted that reducing carbohydrate intake can create valuable metabolic benefits beyond weight loss alone, particularly for individuals with elevated liver fat and prediabetes.

Practical Notes

The trial was small, short, and relied on strictly monitored meal provision. Whether these metabolic advantages persist over multiple years or apply to people without these specific conditions was not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Furthermore, the findings do not establish that consuming high levels of saturated fat is harmless.

Before making major changes to your eating habits, always talk to your doctor to determine the safest approach for your health.
