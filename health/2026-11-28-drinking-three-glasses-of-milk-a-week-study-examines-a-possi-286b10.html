Drinking Three Glasses of Milk a Week? Study Examines a Possible Link With Liver Cancer Risk

Published on November 28, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • A study published in the European Medical Journal explored potential connections between weekly milk intake and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the most common primary liver cancer.
  • Participants consuming three or more glasses of milk or milk substitutes weekly had higher odds of HCC compared to those drinking fewer than three glasses.
  • Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) accounted for approximately 16.4 percent of this observed association.
  • Researchers emphasize that an association does not prove direct cause and effect, and milk remains a source of calcium and essential nutrients.

What the Source Says

Research published in the European Medical Journal examined the dietary habits of 249 adults recruited from outpatient clinics across six public hospitals in Kuwait. The study group included 53 individuals diagnosed with HCC and 196 control participants. Through face-to-face interviews, participants recorded how often they drank milk or milk substitutes.

Researchers divided consumption into two categories: fewer than three glasses per week, and three or more glasses per week. The statistical analysis suggested that individuals who drank three or more glasses a week had nearly six times higher odds of developing HCC.

The study also investigated the role of MASLD, a condition characterized by fat accumulation in the liver alongside metabolic issues such as obesity, insulin resistance, or Type 2 diabetes. The findings suggested that MASLD explained roughly 16.4 percent of the link between milk consumption and liver cancer risk, indicating metabolic health may play a role in this relationship.

However, researchers cautioned that the study used a relatively small sample size and a case-control design relying on memory, which can introduce inaccuracies. The results show an association rather than proof that milk causes liver cancer, and the findings cannot be automatically generalized to populations in other countries.

Practical Notes

Milk provides protein, calcium, and key nutrients, and the study authors note there is not enough evidence to remove it from a balanced diet. General strategies to support liver health include staying physically active, maintaining a healthy weight, managing diabetes or other metabolic conditions, limiting alcohol, and addressing conditions like viral hepatitis.

Always talk to your doctor before making major changes to your diet or health routine.
