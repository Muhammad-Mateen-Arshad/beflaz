Patients engage with epilepsy monitor in real time, improving seizure detection

Published on October 13, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research supported by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) demonstrates a new conversational system that allows patients to communicate directly with epilepsy monitoring tools in real time.
  • Patient input provided through a smartphone app helped refine monitoring algorithms, reducing false seizure alarms by roughly 80%.
  • Scientists at the University of Pennsylvania are planning broader outpatient trials and investigating similar technology for Parkinson's disease.

What the Source Says

Traditional electroencephalography (EEG) devices measure brain activity continuously to assist in diagnosing epilepsy. However, patients rarely see this information in real time because data is usually sent to clinical reviewers on a daily or multi-day schedule. Additionally, automated device algorithms frequently flag harmless brain signals as potential seizures, requiring manual correction by specialists.

To bridge this gap, NIH-funded researchers designed a conversational super intelligence platform connected to continuous EEG data. In a small study conducted at the Hospital of the University of Pennsylvania, 13 hospitalized patients used a smartphone application to view alerts and ask questions about their brain activity, such as checking for nighttime seizure activity.

Research suggests that two-way communication through the system was highly active, totaling over 1,300 messages. The platform provided accurate, relevant answers to over 92% of patient questions. Furthermore, patients responded to more than 67% of real-time seizure detections by marking them as true, false, or uncertain. By adjusting its predictions based on patient feedback, the software decreased false alarms by about 80%, with expert clinician feedback reducing false alarms by an additional 2%.

Practical Notes

This early study was limited to a small group of participants in a hospital monitoring unit. Details regarding public availability, pricing, or official release dates are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

The research team intends to evaluate this system in larger outpatient studies and plans to prepare a similar tool for individuals with Parkinson's disease who use deep brain stimulation devices. Always talk to your doctor about your personal health, medical diagnoses, and appropriate management options.
