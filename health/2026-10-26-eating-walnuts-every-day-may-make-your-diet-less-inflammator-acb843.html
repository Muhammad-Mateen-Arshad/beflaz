Eating Walnuts Every Day May Make Your Diet Less Inflammatory, Study Finds

Published on October 26, 2026

Key Takeaways

Research suggests that incorporating walnuts into your daily eating habits may lower the overall inflammatory potential of your diet without requiring complicated changes.

  • A two-year study found lower dietary inflammatory scores among older adults who ate walnuts daily.
  • Walnuts provide fiber, magnesium, plant-based omega-3 fatty acids, and polyphenols.
  • Chronic low-grade inflammation is linked to heart disease, type 2 diabetes, and cognitive decline.

What the Source Says

In a study published in the Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, researchers analyzed data from the Walnuts and Healthy Aging trial, funded by the California Walnut Commission. Led by senior author Sujatha Rajaram, PhD, from the School of Public Health at Loma Linda University, the investigation tracked 319 healthy residents aged 63 to 79.

For two years, one group consumed 1 to 2 ounces of raw walnuts daily (making up roughly 15% of their daily calorie intake), while a control group avoided walnuts entirely. Researchers measured dietary patterns using the Dietary Inflammatory Index, which evaluates 45 food components tied to inflammation markers. The walnut group scored about 0.59 points lower than the control group, indicating a statistically significant shift toward a less inflammatory diet. Registered dietitian Jamie Lee McIntyre highlighted that observing participants over two years makes these findings stronger than short-term studies.

Research suggests walnuts may produce this outcome because they contain alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), a plant-based omega-3 fat, alongside polyphenols that convert into anti-inflammatory urolithins. Walnuts have also been linked to benefits in gut health, blood pressure, and cholesterol. However, study authors noted that diet data was self-reported, and the participant group was primarily white, healthy older adults from a single location.

Practical Notes

If you want to include more walnuts in your daily routine, research suggests simple ways to add them to meals. You can blend walnuts into smoothies, stir them into cottage cheese, yogurt, or oatmeal, or sprinkle them over roasted vegetables, grain bowls, and salads for added crunch.

Be sure to talk to your doctor before making any significant changes to your diet.
