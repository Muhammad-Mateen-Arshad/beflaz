Experts Say Older Adults Should Know About This Version of the Flu Shot—It's Linked to a Lower Alzheimer's Risk

Published on November 27, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that getting a high-dose flu vaccine is linked to a lower risk of developing Alzheimer's disease in adults 65 and older.
  • High-dose flu shots contain four times the antigen of standard doses to assist aging immune systems.
  • The American Academy of Family Physicians recommends that adults 65 and older preferentially receive an enhanced flu vaccine.

What the Source Says

An analysis of health data from nearly 200,000 older adults, published in the journal Neurology by researchers at UTHealth Houston, examined how different types of flu shots relate to brain health. Senior author Dr. Paul Schulz and his team found that adults aged 65 and older who received a high-dose flu shot had a nearly 55% lower risk of developing Alzheimer's disease. In comparison, receiving a standard-dose shot was tied to a roughly 40% lower risk. Research suggests this connection was notably strong among women.

According to a 2026 report by the Alzheimer's Association, an estimated 7.4 million Americans aged 65 and older live with Alzheimer's disease, which accounts for about one in nine people in that age bracket. As bodies age, immune systems become less effective at responding to infections. To help compensate, high-dose flu vaccines are formulated with four times as much influenza antigen as standard-dose options. The study authors emphasize that these findings show an association rather than direct cause and effect, meaning further research is needed to determine if high-dose vaccines directly reduce Alzheimer's risk.

Practical Notes

Official 2026–2027 guidance from the American Academy of Family Physicians advises adults 65 and older to preferentially get an enhanced flu vaccine, which includes the high-dose version. Because high-dose vaccine supply can sometimes fall short of demand, contacting a pharmacy or medical office in advance can help confirm availability. If an individual has already received a standard-dose shot, receiving any flu vaccine still provides essential protection against illness.

Flu vaccination does not replace foundational wellness practices such as maintaining physical activity, getting quality sleep, and managing blood pressure. Before making decisions about your vaccinations or healthcare routine, please talk to your doctor.
