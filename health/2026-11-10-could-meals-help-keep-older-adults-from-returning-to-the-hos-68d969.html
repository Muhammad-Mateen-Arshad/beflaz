Could Meals Help Keep Older Adults From Returning to the Hospital?

Published on November 10, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that receiving regular meal deliveries after leaving the hospital may lower the risk of readmission for older adults who face food insecurity.
  • A study led by Duke University researchers found that post-discharge meal support was associated with lower hospital readmission rates at both 14 days and three weeks.
  • Access to proper nutrition after a hospital stay can support basic recovery needs, such as managing blood sugar and helping wounds heal.

What the Source Says

A study published in the Journal of Hospital Medicine examined a North Carolina pilot program created in 2021 to assist hospital-discharged residents aged 60 and older who were at risk of malnutrition or food insecurity. Coordinated by state aging services alongside Meals on Wheels of North Carolina and NCCARE360, the initiative provided one home-delivered meal daily for two weeks, with providers reimbursed $15 per meal.

Duke University researchers analyzed 380 qualifying referrals representing 368 patients discharged from Duke hospitals between September 2022 and April 2024. About two-thirds of these individuals received the meals. Data revealed that older adults who received the two-week meal supply were 69 percent less likely to be readmitted within 14 days compared to those who were referred but did not receive meals. At two weeks, nearly 5 percent of meal recipients were readmitted, compared to roughly 13 percent of non-recipients. The positive pattern continued past the delivery window, with meal recipients being 54 percent less likely to be readmitted after three weeks at home.

According to Dr. Susan Spratt, adequate food intake directly influences post-hospital healing. For instance, sufficient protein helps heal wounds, while appropriate food choices help prevent blood sugar spikes that could hinder recovery.

Practical Notes

Duke incorporated social needs screening directly into standard post-discharge care routines, allowing nurses to connect older adults with support during routine follow-up calls. However, researchers noted that community infrastructure to meet such nonmedical needs remains limited in many areas. Whether similar hospital-discharge meal programs are available in other regions is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Please talk to your doctor or healthcare provider regarding your post-hospital recovery plan and dietary needs.
