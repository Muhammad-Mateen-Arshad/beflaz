What happened

According to Dawn, Houthi forces launched strikes targeting Saudi Arabia on a Saturday, including attacks directed at the capital city of Riyadh. The strikes were carried out in retaliation for a recent military operation conducted by the Saudi-led coalition. Coalition forces stated that they successfully intercepted and destroyed three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh. Explosions were reported in the capital after authorities temporarily issued aerial threat alerts. Additional Houthi strikes targeted infrastructure elsewhere in the country, including an electrical power station in Al-Shuqaiq and sites near Jizan.

Key facts

  • The Saudi-led coalition launched a special operation targeting weapons-smuggling networks in the port city of Hodeida, along with military locations in Sanaa, Saada, and Taiz.
  • Coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki reported that coalition forces hit drone facilities, missile storage sites, and supply depots in Sanaa.
  • Houthi representatives warned commercial airlines that Saudi airspace was unsafe, with exceptions for Makkah and Madina, while telling maritime traffic to clear Jeddah port.
  • A Houthi strike damaged a skyscraper in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District, drawing public condemnation from Pakistani Prime Minister Shahbaz Sharif.
  • Casualty claims remain disputed and unverified; the Houthis reported 41 deaths from coalition attacks, while Saudi-backed forces claimed nearly 200 Houthi casualties.
  • Specific dates for future operational deadlines were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the latest exchange represents a significant escalation following the collapse of a four-year truce in Yemen's civil war earlier in the summer. Houthi forces have recently advanced toward Aden after Saudi-backed troops retreated from key positions near the Red Sea. The broader conflict, which drew coalition intervention in 2015, continues to endanger key economic infrastructure, disrupt commercial transportation routes, and prompt concern from the United Nations over regional instability.
