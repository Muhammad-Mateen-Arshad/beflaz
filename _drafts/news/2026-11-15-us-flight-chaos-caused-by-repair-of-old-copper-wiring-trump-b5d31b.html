What happened

A nationwide ground stop plunged air travel across the United States into widespread disruption starting on Friday, according to Dawn. The Trump administration attributed the failure to a botched attempt to replace old copper wire communication lines with high-speed fiber cables. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy stated that either a contractor error or faulty equipment caused air traffic control routers to become flooded, disrupting radar feeds and communication with planes.

The four-hour halt represented only the second nationwide ground stop declared in US airspace since the 9/11 attacks. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) officials lifted the stoppage on Friday evening after restoring full system functionality, and flight schedules are expected to normalize by Sunday.

Key facts

  • FAA chief Bryan Bedford reported that 12,000 flights were cancelled on Friday, with another 4,000 to 5,000 projected to be cancelled on Saturday out of 41,000 scheduled flights.
  • Flight-tracking site FlightAware logged over 6,200 flight cancellations by Saturday evening, according to Dawn.
  • The four-hour ground stop was ended at 8:15 pm Eastern Time on Friday.
  • Hundreds of thousands of travelers experienced delays or cancellations, with knock-on effects impacting international flights to and from the country.
  • Tim Johnson of the UK Civil Aviation Authority noted that passengers could face continued follow-on disruptions while airlines work to clear backlogs.

Why it matters

The operational breakdown has intensified scrutiny of the country's aging infrastructure, according to Dawn. Secretary Duffy appealed to Congress for $50 billion to upgrade and modernize the US air traffic control system. The incident sparked sharp political criticism, with Illinois Governor JB Pritzker arguing that the Trump administration and congressional Republicans already had the opportunity to fund system upgrades. New York Governor Kathy Hochul called for Duffy to resign or be removed from office. Meanwhile, US lawmakers have called for formal inquiries into the cause of the system failure and raised questions regarding cyber security preparedness.
