Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that substantial, sustained weight loss may help some adults put Type 2 diabetes into remission.
  • According to international consensus standards published in Diabetes Care, remission involves maintaining an HbA1c level below 6.5% for at least three months without using glucose-lowering drugs.
  • Remission is different from a permanent cure, as blood glucose levels can rise again if weight is regained.

What the Source Says

Excess body fat, particularly visceral fat stored around internal organs like the liver and pancreas, creates insulin resistance. Medical specialists from KIMS Hospitals and Max Super Speciality Hospital explain that shedding this excess fat lowers metabolic stress, improves insulin sensitivity, and assists the body in managing blood sugar.

Expert insights from Dr Sabine Kapasi indicate that losing 10% to 15% of initial body weight offers significant metabolic advantages. This is especially true for people diagnosed relatively recently who retain active insulin-producing cells. Furthermore, Dr Alok Krishna Sahay of Prayag Hospitals highlights that individuals with a normal body mass index can still carry abdominal fat, making waist measurements and lipid profiles critical evaluation tools alongside BMI, particularly among South Asian populations.

For individuals living with obesity and diabetes, clinical research—including the STAMPEDE trial in The New England Journal of Medicine and the ARMMS-T2D study in JAMA—demonstrates that metabolic surgery can lead to long-term weight reduction and better blood glucose control. Practical Notes

Weight reduction should be gradual, continuous, and professionally managed. Recommended lifestyle steps include limiting refined carbohydrates, managing portion sizes, and incorporating regular physical activity. Individuals must never reduce or discontinue their prescribed diabetes medications independently, as adjustments require close medical supervision. Routine blood sugar monitoring remains essential even after reaching remission. Always talk to your doctor before making any changes to your diet, activity level, or medication routine.
