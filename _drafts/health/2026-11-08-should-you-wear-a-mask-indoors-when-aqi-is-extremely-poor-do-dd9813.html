Key Takeaways

  • Indoor air is not guaranteed to be safe when outdoor air pollution reaches severe levels.
  • Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) can seep inside buildings via open doors, windows, and ventilation setups.
  • Respirators such as properly fitted N95 masks provide better particulate filtration than loose cloth or surgical masks.
  • Older adults and individuals with heart or respiratory conditions belong to higher-risk groups during poor air quality events.

What the Source Says

Information shared by pulmonology specialists at Aster RV Hospital and Aster CMI Hospital in Bangalore explains that staying inside reduces outdoor exposure, but fine particles measuring 2.5 micrometres or smaller (PM2.5) can still enter homes. These tiny particles can travel deep into the respiratory system, enter the bloodstream, and potentially trigger a chronic cough. Indoor sources like cooking smoke or burning incense can further increase pollution levels inside. Specific numerical AQI levels requiring mask use are not specified in the source; please check the official notice for local environmental guidance.

The source notes that wearing a mask indoors all day is not required for everyone, particularly in well-sealed spaces using air purifiers. However, if outdoor pollution infiltrates your space and indoor filtration is inadequate, a well-fitting N95 or N99 respirator can provide protection. Research suggests that under standard testing conditions, N95 respirators filter at least 95 percent of airborne particles. While HEPA air purifiers reduce particulate matter inside rooms, the source points out that they do not remove indoor gases such as nitrogen dioxide or carbon monoxide.

Practical Notes

To improve indoor air quality, the source advises keeping doors and windows closed during heavy pollution, operating appropriately sized HEPA air purifiers, and avoiding indoor smoke from candles, incense, or mosquito coils. Using kitchen exhaust systems that vent outdoors during cooking is also recommended. When using a respirator, ensure a proper facial seal, as facial hair or loose edges can allow polluted air inside. Some users may experience mild sweating, skin irritation, or discomfort.

Anyone experiencing breathing difficulty should seek immediate medical attention rather than depending only on a mask. We recommend that readers talk to their doctor about managing existing health conditions during severe air pollution events.
