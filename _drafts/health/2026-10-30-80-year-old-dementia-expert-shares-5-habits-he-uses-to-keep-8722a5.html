Key Takeaways

  • Cognitive changes do not have to be accepted as an unavoidable part of getting older.
  • Research suggests five key lifestyle areas can support brain health: physical activity, mental stimulation, balanced nutrition, consistent sleep, and strong social ties.
  • Everyday health choices that protect heart function generally benefit brain function as well.

What the Source Says

According to Dr. Ron Petersen, an 80-year-old neurologist who directs the Mayo Clinic Study of Aging, older adults can take an active role in maintaining cognitive health. Drawing from over five decades of research on dementia, Alzheimer's disease, and mild cognitive impairment, he emphasizes that lifestyle choices significantly influence how the brain ages.

The source outlines five key habits practiced and recommended by the expert:

  • Physical Activity: Aiming for roughly 150 minutes of weekly aerobic exercise, such as brisk walking or treadmill sessions, combined with strength and flexibility training. Research associated physical activity with slower cognitive decline and lower fall risks.
  • Intellectual Stimulation: Engaging in enjoyable activities like reading nonfiction, following sports, reading newspapers, and attending lectures rather than relying solely on traditional brain games.
  • Balanced Nutrition: Following Mediterranean-style or MIND diets focused on vegetables, fruits, nuts, beans, seafood, and poultry, while limiting red meat and avoiding excessive alcohol.
  • Quality Sleep: Prioritizing nightly rest from around 10:30 p.m. to 6:30 a.m. to aid memory consolidation and brain clearance, while paying attention to potential sleep disorders and avoiding night caffeine.
  • Social Connection: Maintaining active relationships with family and community. Research has linked loneliness to increased dementia risk.

Practical Notes

The Mayo Clinic researcher stresses that people do not need to become elite athletes to see benefits; simply doing slightly more physical activity than before is helpful. Additionally, while these daily habits offer practical ways to support overall cognitive wellness, research does not promise that lifestyle adjustments act as an absolute cure or guarantee against cognitive disorders.

Always talk to your doctor before making changes to your health habits, diet, or exercise routine.
