Key Takeaways

  • Data from nutrition technology company Elutra indicates that 72% of surveyed adults prioritize a shorter lifespan in good health over living longer with chronic illness.
  • Among adults aged 65 and older, 82% value health span—the portion of life spent healthy—over sheer longevity.
  • Research published by PlanAdviser suggests many individuals are open to making lifestyle adjustments, such as reducing workplace stress or increasing daily exercise, to gain extra years of good health.

What the Source Says

A survey conducted by nutrition technology company Elutra among adults aged 18 to 85 suggests that prioritizing quality of life over total age is common across generations. While 70% of younger adults aged 18 to 34 preferred a healthier, slightly shorter life, that figure rose to 82% among adults 65 and older.

Related research published on the 12th by PlanAdviser examined how people view longevity. In that study, 78% of respondents connected longevity to physical health, 59% emphasized muscle strength and mobility, 54% cited mental clarity, and 43% highlighted independence and energy. The exact month and year of these studies are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

The PlanAdviser data also revealed that 82% of participants would accept a lower-paying, less stressful job if it added 10 healthy years to their lives. A matching 82% said they would consider delaying retirement if they could maintain their health, mobility, and financial stability longer. Within that group, 71% noted they would take a $5,000 annual pay cut in exchange for flexible schedules, lower stress, and preventive healthcare benefits.

Practical Notes

If guaranteed at least 10 additional healthy years, 87% of surveyed individuals reported they would alter their habits. The most common daily modifications included increasing exercise (34%), spending more time with family and friends (34%), and reducing stress (30%). Additionally, 39% would make major life changes, such as pursuing education, starting a business, or traveling long distances.

Financial worries remain connected to health expectations. The survey noted that 46% of all respondents worry their retirement savings might run out, including 58% of households earning under $50,000 annually compared to 36% of those earning $100,000 or more.
