Hair loss is a challenging side effect of cancer treatments like chemotherapy. Scalp cooling, commonly known as cold cap therapy, is a medical technique designed to help patients reduce hair loss during their care.

Key Takeaways

  • Scalp cooling works by constricting blood vessels and slowing cellular activity in hair follicles, which limits chemotherapy drug exposure to those cells.
  • A clinical study published in the journal In Vivo suggests scalp cooling can reduce chemotherapy-induced hair loss, particularly in breast cancer patients.
  • The technique is primarily evaluated for solid tumours and is not appropriate for blood, scalp, or brain cancers.
  • Outcomes vary based on chemotherapy drug type, dosage, treatment duration, and individual hair characteristics.

What the Source Says

According to Dr. Darshana Rane from HCG Cancer Hospital, Mumbai, and Dr. Sunil Kumar Prabhu from Aster RV Hospital, Bangalore, cold cap therapy involves wearing a cap containing cooling gel or cold fluid before, during, and after a chemotherapy session. Specific cooling temperatures and exact session durations are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Research suggests that while scalp cooling cannot guarantee complete hair retention, it can help some individuals preserve a substantial portion of their hair. Temporary side effects may include headaches, a tight feeling around the head, and chills from the low temperature. Importantly, studies involving breast cancer patients have not shown an increased risk of cancer spreading to the scalp as a result of reduced scalp blood flow.

Practical Notes

Along with medical options, gentle daily habits can support hair and scalp care during treatment:

  • Wash hair with mild shampoos and avoid rough brushing or pulling.
  • Limit heat styling tools and refrain from chemical treatments like dyes or straighteners.
  • Maintain proper hydration and balanced nutrition to support overall health.
  • Explore soft head coverings or wigs, and utilize counselling or support groups for emotional well-being.

Always talk to your doctor to determine if scalp cooling is safe and appropriate for your specific health needs.
