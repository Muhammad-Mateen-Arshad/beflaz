Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that blood levels of NAD+ remain steady across adult age groups rather than declining significantly.
  • Oral supplements such as NR and NMN, as well as high-cost intravenous drips, elevate blood NAD+ primarily by converting into vitamin B3.
  • Human trials show inconsistent and limited physical benefits from raising blood NAD+ levels.

What the Source Says

According to research highlighted by Men's Fitness, NAD+ is a molecule cells rely on to convert food into energy. While popular longevity discussions suggest that NAD+ falls as people age, a study evaluating about 300 individuals between 19 and 87 years old found that blood NAD+ levels stayed stable across all ages. Neither exercise nor dietary programs altered these blood levels, questioning the premise that supplementation is necessary for aging adults.

Although oral precursors like nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) do raise NAD+ markers in the blood, research indicates that this does not necessarily translate to benefits inside muscle tissue. In a 2018 human trial, oral NR raised blood NAD+ but failed to increase NAD+ inside skeletal muscles or produce metabolic gains. A major reason is that the gut and liver break down oral doses into nicotinamide, a form of vitamin B3. Consequently, standard vitamin B3 accounts for much of the observed rise in blood NAD+.

While rodent studies yielded strong improvements in metabolism, endurance, and lifespan, human trials have shown far more modest and inconsistent results. Walking distances sometimes improved, but effort-based measures like VO2max and grip strength did not. Furthermore, expensive intravenous NAD+ drips costing $500 to $1,000 are broken down by blood enzymes into smaller components like nicotinamide before entering cells, utilizing the same cellular pathway as simple oral supplements.

Practical Notes

There are no controlled clinical trials demonstrating that intravenous NAD+ treatments offer wellness or anti-aging benefits. Since both oral capsules and intravenous drips rely on breakdown into basic vitamin B3 compounds, claims regarding direct cellular delivery remain unproven in human studies.

Always talk to your doctor before considering any new supplement or medical treatment.
