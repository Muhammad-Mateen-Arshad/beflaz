Artificial intelligence is emerging as a significant driver for workforce productivity in developing economies. Instead of replacing labor, AI offers opportunities to assist workers across key sectors including agriculture, healthcare, education, manufacturing, financial services, and public administration by streamlining repetitive tasks and improving decision-making.

Role Overview

While advanced economies face higher job replacement risks, developing nations stand to gain primarily from AI-driven productivity enhancements. In Pakistan, targeted government initiatives aim to expand AI adoption, build sovereign computing infrastructure, and upskill the national workforce to integrate these technologies into daily workflows.

Key Details

  • Job Productivity Potential: World Bank estimates indicate that 16.2% of jobs in developing economies could see productivity increases from AI, compared to 18.7% in high-income nations.
  • Automation Risk: Approximately 4.5% of jobs in low- and middle-income countries face automation risks, compared to 14.2% in high-income economies.
  • Task Efficiency: International Labour Organization findings show task-level productivity gains between 10% and 70% in select settings.
  • National Investment: Pakistan has pledged a $1 billion investment in AI by 2030 to develop research capabilities and infrastructure.
  • Training Initiatives: Plans include training 1,000,000 non-IT professionals in AI skills and offering 20,000 training opportunities across seven newly established AI hubs under the National AI Advancement Initiative.
  • Higher Education: The initiative offers 1,000 fully funded doctoral scholarships in artificial intelligence.
