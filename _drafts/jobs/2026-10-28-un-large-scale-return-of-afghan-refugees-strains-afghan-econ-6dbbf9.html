Role Overview

Reports from the United Nations highlight a growing strain on Afghanistan's job market, local infrastructure, and essential public services following a mass return of Afghan refugees from neighboring nations. Economic difficulties and policy revisions in Iran and Pakistan have triggered large-scale deportations and voluntary returns. Representatives from the United Nations Development Program and regional commerce organizations report that neither government institutions nor private sector enterprises currently have the structural capacity to absorb millions of job seekers at once.

Key Details

The ongoing economic and labor market crisis involves several key figures and observations:

  • Return Statistics: Nearly 1.3 million Afghans have returned from Iran and Pakistan so far in 2026. This follows approximately 4.9 million returns recorded between 2023 and 2025.
  • Refugee Population Abroad: About 4.4 million Afghans still reside in Iran (including 1.4 million undocumented individuals), while around 1.6 million live in Pakistan (including an estimated 159,000 undocumented individuals).
  • Labor Market Impact: Experts point to widespread poverty, a severe lack of employment opportunities, and insufficient official planning to integrate returnees into the workforce. Economic recovery will require improved international relations and increased domestic and foreign investment.
  • Humanitarian Conditions: The World Food Program reports that 19.2 million people face acute food insecurity, while 4 million women and children are expected to experience malnutrition in 2027.
  • Returnee Resources: Many displaced individuals are forced to settle in remote mountainous areas without access to healthcare, clean water, identification documents, or steady employment.
