Egypt & Switzerland Explore New Archaeological Partnerships At Cairo Forum

Published on November 09, 2026

Researchers, museum leaders, and heritage specialists from Egypt and Switzerland recently gathered at the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization in Cairo for the Swiss-Egyptian Archaeological Forum. Organized by the Swiss Embassy alongside Egypt's Supreme Council of Antiquities and the museum, the event highlighted longstanding scientific partnerships. Key field initiatives include research by the University of Basel in the Valley of the Kings and Sheikh Abdel Qurna, missions led by the University of Geneva at South Saqqara and Hiu, and investigations by the Swiss Institute for Architectural and Archaeological Research on Ancient Egypt in Aswan and Elephantine Island.

What Scientists Found

The gathering highlighted how modern technology is reshaping archaeological study. Specialists demonstrated the application of three-dimensional scanning, virtual reconstruction, and digital documentation. A major feature presented at the forum was a virtual reality model of Tutankhamun's tomb, built using high-resolution digital scans.

The forum also detailed progress regarding artifact recovery and funding support:

  • Following a bilateral agreement that entered into force in 2011, Switzerland has returned roughly 80 cultural objects to Egypt.
  • Returned artifacts include a fragment of a statue depicting Ramses II as well as a Ptolemaic funerary mask returned in September 2026.
  • Switzerland's Federal Office of Culture has provided more than CHF 500,000 over the past decade toward Egyptian heritage preservation projects.

Why It Matters

Beyond field excavations and artifact restitution, attendees examined key ethical questions surrounding ancient heritage. Discussions focused on establishing appropriate standards for analyzing ancient human remains and balancing public museum access with ongoing conservation needs. Officials at the event—including Swiss Ambassador Andreas Baum, Supreme Council Secretary-General Hisham El-Leithy, and museum CEO El-Tayeb Abbas—emphasized creating opportunities for early-career Egyptian researchers in joint ventures. An accompanying exhibition displaying these collaborative projects runs through November 15.
