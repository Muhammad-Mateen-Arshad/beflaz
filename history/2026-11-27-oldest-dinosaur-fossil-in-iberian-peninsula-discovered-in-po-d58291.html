Oldest dinosaur fossil in Iberian Peninsula discovered in Portugal

Published on November 27, 2026

Researchers have announced the discovery of a 227-million-year-old dinosaur fossil in southern Portugal, marking the oldest dinosaur remnant ever uncovered in the Iberian Peninsula. This significant find offers fresh insights into the earliest phases of dinosaur evolution.

The Discovery

The fossil consists of a single spinal bone, or vertebra, measuring just 3.7 centimeters long. A student from the NOVA School of Science and Technology spotted the specimen during fieldwork in Penina, a site within the municipality of Loule. Details regarding the recovery and analysis were published in the journal Fossil Imprint.

What Scientists Found

Octavio Mateus, an earth sciences professor at NOVA University Lisbon, noted that the bone belonged to a carnivorous dinosaur that was relatively advanced for its era. By comparing the specimen to closely related species, researchers reconstructed the animal's physical proportions:

  • Total length: Estimated between 2.5 and 3 meters, including the tail.
  • Height: Stood about 1 meter tall.

Why It Matters

This discovery provides the first evidence of land-dwelling vertebrates preserved within the Triassic rocks of the Penina site. Previously, fossil finds in these specific rock layers consisted predominantly of aquatic creatures.

The Penina area is notably rich in material dating to the Late Triassic period. Past excavations at the location unearthed a giant amphibian measuring over 2 meters long. That historic find laid the foundation for establishing the Algarvensis Geopark, which achieved status as a UNESCO Global Geopark this year.
