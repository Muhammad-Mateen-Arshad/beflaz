The Power of Archaeology to Add Meaning to Life

Published on November 11, 2026

The Discovery

Archaeological work in Jerusalem has uncovered significant historical sites and ancient artifacts over two decades of research. Volunteers and students from Herbert W. Armstrong College joined the late Dr. Eilat Mazar to excavate the site of David's palace in the City of David. This effort renewed an earlier partnership with the Mazar family and Hebrew University. Notable items and exhibit themes associated with these excavations include seals belonging to King Hezekiah, Isaiah, and Jeremiah's captors, as well as discoveries related to the Kingdom of David and Solomon.

What Scientists Found

Teams participating in 13 separate excavations across Jerusalem have methodically documented ancient architecture and historical remains. Following the death of Dr. Mazar in 2021, Hebrew University professors Uzi Leibner, Orit Peleg-Barkat, and Yosef Garfinkel took over leadership of the digs. Revealing these ancient structures required continuous routine field activities, including:

  • Shoveling soil and filling buckets to expose ancient walls and floor surfaces.
  • Washing uncovered pottery fragments at designated cleaning stations.
  • Recording data through photographs, architectural drawings, locus sheets, and elevation readings.

Not specified in the source; please check the official notice regarding exact dimensions or ages of the uncovered structures.

Why It Matters

Beyond individual historical finds, these efforts demonstrate how thousands of ordinary, routine tasks combine to reconstruct ancient civilizations. The long-term project has involved over 150 volunteers and led to educational tours in the Ophel and City of David regions. The organization also maintains an office in Jerusalem containing the complete academic libraries of Prof. Benjamin Mazar and Dr. Eilat Mazar, while distributing research findings through podcasts, magazines, and websites. Ultimately, working together in the field fosters strong personal connections among researchers while adding profound meaning to human life.
