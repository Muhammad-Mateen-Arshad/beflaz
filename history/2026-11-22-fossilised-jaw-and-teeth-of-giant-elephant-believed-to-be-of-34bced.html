Fossilised Jaw And Teeth Of Giant Elephant, Believed To Be Of Palaeoloxodon Species, Found In MP's Raisen

Published on November 22, 2026

The Discovery

Children playing on the banks of the Narmada River at Patai Ghat in Madhya Pradesh's Raisen district unexpectedly uncovered the fossilised jaw and teeth of a giant prehistoric elephant. While digging soil near the riverbank to wash their hair, the children spotted the stone-like object. Local villagers initially believed the structure was a divine miracle and began worshipping it. Upon being alerted, Raisen Collector Rohit Sisonia dispatched an official to secure the site, appointed a watchman, and contacted archaeological authorities.

Following the discovery on November 10, a team of experts from Bhopal, Lucknow, and Pune arrived to examine the site. The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) excavated the fossil under the supervision of Dr Anjali Trivedi from Lucknow and Professor Vijay Sathe of Deccan College University in Pune. The team safely extracted the remains and transported them to Bhopal for preliminary laboratory testing.

What Scientists Found

The extracted jaw bone measures approximately 75 centimetres in length and 25 centimetres in width, featuring a broad chin structure at the lower part. Key physical details of the fossil include:

  • Jaw bones arranged in seven to nine parallel rows.
  • High, prominent teeth featuring 11 tooth-like projections on one side and seven on the other.
  • Location within a yellow soil layer geologically linked to the Baneta and Sohagpur Formation.

Geological analysis indicates the soil layer dates back to the Pleistocene period, estimated between 24,000 and 500,000 years ago. Preliminary evaluations suggest the fossil itself is likely from the Middle Pleistocene era, roughly 400,000 to 500,000 years old, and may belong to the ancient Palaeoloxodon species.

Why It Matters

The Narmada Valley is a renowned region for ancient remains, having previously yielded human skulls in Hathnora, elephant tusks in Surajkund, and dinosaur fossils. Scientists note that prehistoric elephants during the Quaternary Period were significantly larger than modern elephants, growing up to 4 to 5 metres tall and weighing between 18 and 22 tons.

This discovery could provide vital insights into the region's environmental history and biological evolution spanning from 2.5 million to 12,000 years ago. ASI researchers, alongside teams from Pune and Lucknow, will conduct joint testing and chemical analysis to confirm the specimen's precise species, age, and characteristics.
