Walk Among 60 Life-Size Dinosaurs At This Connecticut Park Straight Out Of Jurassic Park

Published on November 08, 2026

The Discovery

Nestled within a natural forest in Oakdale, Connecticut, lies an outdoor trail system known as The Dinosaur Place. The attraction features roughly 60 life-sized dinosaur sculptures placed throughout authentic woodland. Paths wind organically through real trees, ferns, and foliage, giving visitors the opportunity to encounter representations of prehistoric life scattered across the landscape.

What Scientists Found

The site presents a broad selection of detailed prehistoric creatures designed to show realistic scales, muscle definitions, and distinct colorations:

  • Brachiosaurus: Painted in amber and brown shades, its long neck extends above the treetops next to a pond filled with lily pads.
  • Euoplocephalus: A low-slung, heavily armored animal protected by bony plates, sharp spikes, and bony eyelids ( pronounced YOU-OH-PLOH-SEFF-UH-LUSS).
  • Styracosaurus: Rendered in dark brown and rust tones, featuring a head frill crowned with radiating horns.
  • Tyrannosaurus Rex: Positioned in a forward posture with open jaws displaying rows of teeth.
  • Therizinosaurus: Painted green and tan with an elongated neck, situated near a gated trail segment.
  • Additional Dinosaurs: A striped yellow-green carnivore behind a warning fence, a forward-facing Triceratops with three horns, and smaller creatures hidden in the brush, including an orange-and-black striped predator resting between logs.

Why It Matters

Each exhibit includes educational signage that details the creature's appearance, diet, and name pronunciation guides to inform guests. To represent ancient environments, the landscape features a charcoal-and-red volcano sculpture crafted to look like cooling lava rising from the water. Exact historical eras and physical measurements of the animals are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
