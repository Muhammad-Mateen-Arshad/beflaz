Největší zabiják jurského světa

Published on November 15, 2026

Recent paleontological research confirms that Torvosaurus tanneri was the largest land predator of the Jurassic period. Freshly analyzed fossil evidence has reshaped our understanding of this massive theropod, revealing its global reach and impressive physical features.

The Discovery

Although first formally described in 1979 from Colorado's Morrison Formation, earlier remains of this dinosaur were uncovered as far back as 1899 by Elmer S. Riggs in Wyoming. A newly studied specimen, designated CMC VP15575, was excavated between 2012 and 2014 at Skull Creek Quarry in northwestern Colorado. Found within the Brushy Basin Member of the Morrison Formation, these fossils date back 152.1 to 148.0 million years and joined the Cincinnati Museum Center collection in 2018. Remains of Torvosaurus have been documented across North America and Europe, including Portugal, Germany, Spain, and the UK, with potential finds in Tanzania and Uruguay.

What Scientists Found

The new fossil specimen provides rare structural details of the dinosaur's skull and skeleton:

  • The skull was notably low, narrow, and elongated, measuring 36.5 cm in height and about 3.3 times longer than it was tall.
  • The individual reached an estimated body length of 10.4 meters, a skull length of 1.2 meters, and a mass of 4 to 5 metric tons (3,600 to 4,500 kg). Bone analysis indicates this animal was not yet fully adult.
  • Its teeth were exceptionally large; the third maxillary tooth measured 25 cm including the root, with crown lengths up to 14.55 cm.

Why It Matters

The updated analysis confirms that the European species previously known as Torvosaurus gurneyi is actually the same species as Torvosaurus tanneri. This taxonomic revision demonstrates that dinosaur megafauna in North America and the Iberian Peninsula were deeply connected during the Late Jurassic, a relationship also reflected in shared genera like Allosaurus and Diplodocus. Spanning nearly 20 million years from the Callovian to the Tithonian stages, Torvosaurus tanneri stood out as a dominant apex predator significantly heavier than typical contemporary carnivores.
