Archaeological treasures uncovered beneath Kabul’s crumbling ancient fortress

Published on November 09, 2026

High above the old city of Kabul, the ancient citadel of Bala Hissar is undergoing a major long-term restoration effort. Scarred by centuries of conflict, invasions, and neglect, the historic site is being painstakingly preserved to protect its rich heritage.

The Discovery

Archaeological work at the fortress has revealed evidence of human occupation dating back more than 2,000 years. Excavations conducted across five distinct trenches have unburied historical layers from multiple eras. One excavation trench extends 11 meters into the ground, revealing artifacts buried deep beneath the surface. The exact timeline for when the entire project will be finalized is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

Teams sifting through the earth at the fortress have unearthed a wide array of ancient relics and structural remains, including:

  • Ancient coins, oil lamps, glazed Islamic ceramics, and numerous pieces of pottery.
  • Fragments of Buddhist statues alongside the traces of an ancient monastery.
  • The remains of a Mughal palace containing wall murals, a throne-like feature, and a star-shaped pool carved straight into the rock.
  • Remnants of historic dwellings, royal palaces, and defensive walls, as well as abandoned military equipment from modern conflicts.

Why It Matters

Situated on a mountain ridge overlooking the Afghan capital, Bala Hissar historically held immense strategic importance. It guarded trade routes linking the Indian subcontinent with Central Asia and served as a fortress, palace, administrative center, and military garrison. Controlling the citadel effectively meant controlling Kabul and the surrounding region throughout Afghan history.

The ongoing conservation initiative led by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture seeks to transform the citadel into a public archaeological park featuring landscaped gardens, a museum, restaurants, and a training program for local archaeology students.
