With Just One Pair of Nippers, a Dinosaur Stood Up in 5 Minutes: Building Tamiya's "Fukuiraptor" and Wanting to Display It at Our Local Summer Festival

Published on November 27, 2026

Fossil discoveries provide a captivating window into the ancient world, bringing long-extinct creatures back into public awareness. Among these remarkable finds is Fukuiraptor, a prehistoric predator whose remains offer valuable insights into ancient ecosystems.

The Discovery

Fossilized remains of Fukuiraptor were uncovered in Katsuyama City, situated within Fukui Prefecture. Preserved in rock strata dating back approximately 120 million years, the animal was officially reported as a new species in the year 2000. Its name translates to "thief of Fukui." Research shows that it shared its habitat and era with other notable regional dinosaurs, including the plant-eating Fukuisaurus and the colossal Fukuititan.

What Scientists Found

Key details revealed by the fossil record include:

  • Size: The dinosaur reached a total length of roughly 4.7 meters.
  • Physical Features: It was a carnivorous species equipped with sharp teeth and hooked claws.
  • Coexistence: It lived alongside both herbivorous and massive prehistoric creatures in the same geographical region.

Modern educational materials and models reflecting scientific evidence as of 2026 illustrate the size of this 4.7-meter predator by comparing it to human parent and child scale silhouettes. Educational resources also explore questions regarding what the dinosaur ate and what color it might have been.

Why It Matters

Highlighting prehistoric finds connects local communities directly to their ancient geological heritage. Towns such as Sannan-cho, where fossils of Tambatitan were discovered, share a strong connection to regional paleontology. Presenting models of species like Fukuiraptor and Fukuisaurus at local community festivals provides an exciting opportunity to inform children that extraordinary prehistoric animals once inhabited their hometowns.
