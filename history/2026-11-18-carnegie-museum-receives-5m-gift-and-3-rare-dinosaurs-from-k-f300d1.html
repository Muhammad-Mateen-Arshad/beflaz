Carnegie museum receives $5M gift and 3 rare dinosaurs from Ken Griffin

Published on November 18, 2026

The Discovery

A set of three rare dinosaur fossils will soon arrive at the Carnegie Museum of Natural History in Pittsburgh. Philanthropist Ken Griffin—the founder and CEO of Citadel—is loaning the plant-eating sauropods to the institution for a ten-year period. The commercial paleontology lab Fossilogic excavated the adult and two juvenile specimens in Washakie County, Wyoming, between 2023 and 2025. The creatures were fossilized together in the Morrison Formation, a famous Jurassic rock layer originating 157 to 150 million years ago.

What Scientists Found

The long-necked reptiles, nicknamed Daisy, Lucky, and Lucy, belong to the same species and may represent an intact family unit. They belong to the same dinosaur family as Dippy, the famous 80-foot-long Diplodocus carnegii associated with the museum. The discovery includes impressive physical remains:

  • An adult measuring between 80 and 100 feet long, with at least 120 excavated bones.
  • Two juvenile dinosaurs, each 18 feet in length, yielding around 150 recovered bones together.
  • What may be the largest sample of fossilized sauropod skin discovered to date.

Why It Matters

Along with the loan, Griffin contributed a $5 million gift to expand paleontology research, upgrade museum spaces, and strengthen educational programs. The fossils will arrive in December or January still encased in rock. Over an estimated five years, museum staff will extract the bones inside an expanded, public-visible laboratory named after Griffin. The donation funds five full-time staff members and two interns to prepare the fossils, while paleontology educators provide live updates to visitors. Following extraction, the reassembled skeletons will be mounted for a five-year public exhibit.
