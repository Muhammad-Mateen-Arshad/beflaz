Climate: China pulls out of the 2028 COP, but greenhouse gas emissions appear to have already peaked

Published on November 14, 2026

The Discovery

Recent legal rulings and energy updates have highlighted major developments in global environmental governance. The Norwegian Supreme Court annulled three oil licences in the North Sea because impact assessments covered extraction but failed to evaluate greenhouse gas emissions from burning the oil. Meanwhile, Turkey celebrated the discovery of two oil fields while participating in climate negotiations in Antalya. What Scientists Found

Tracking of global energy data reveals notable shifts in emissions and clean power output:

  • China's carbon emissions dropped by three percent in the third quarter, partly due to the crisis in the Strait of Hormuz, marking a half-percent drop since the beginning of the year.
  • Annual growth in China's solar, wind, and nuclear power reached 110 terawatt-hours, an amount equal to Germany's total electricity production.
  • Negotiators accept that the target of limiting global temperature increases to a 1.5-degree rise will inevitably be exceeded.

Why It Matters

International negotiations remain focused on creating a framework to phase out fossil fuels, which UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres described as "dinosaurs that must disappear." However, major energy consumers and producers—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and India—oppose setting new reduction targets. China has decided not to host the 2028 COP summit, avoiding the intense global scrutiny that host nations face regarding climate actions and human rights. Despite opposition from key nations, over fifty countries have supported a proposal to increase electricity's share of global energy use to thirty-five percent.
