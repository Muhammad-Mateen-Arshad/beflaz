Scientists Find Dinosaur Blood and Cells For First Time in Fossil

Published on November 04, 2026

An international team of researchers led by Curtin University has announced the first chemical evidence of dinosaur blood and cells retrieved from a fossil, opening new avenues for understanding ancient life.

The Discovery

Researchers uncovered preserved biological structures inside the limb bone of an Aratasaurus museunacionali fossil located in northeastern Brazil. The specimen is estimated to be 115 million years old. Unlike fictional depictions of ancient amber preservation, these organic components were found embedded directly within the fossilized bone's vascular canals. The findings were documented in a paper published in the journal Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

What Scientists Found

Through chemical testing, the research team successfully confirmed the presence of ancient organic structures inside the fossilized limb. Key findings include:

  • Mineralized red blood cells preserved within the fossilized tissue.
  • Intact heme-derived molecules located inside the vascular canals.

Additional details, such as the full physical dimensions of the specimen, were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why It Matters

Study lead author Professor Kliti Grice from Curtin University described the findings as a landmark milestone. Identifying intact heme-derived molecules in 115-million-year-old red blood cells broadens the known fossil record of preserved blood. The discovery provides researchers with a direct molecular perspective on the physiology of creatures that vanished long ago, aiding in the study of how these ancient animals operated and adapted to their environments.
