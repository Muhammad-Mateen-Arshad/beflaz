Road Construction in Greece Reveals a Monumental 3,200-Year-Old Mycenaean Tomb

Published on November 28, 2026

The Discovery

Road construction on the Greek island of Euboea recently led to the uncovering of a monumental Mycenaean tholos tomb dating back more than 3,200 years. Uncovered near the town of Psachna during work on the Chalkida–Psachna bypass, the burial structure ranks among the 20 largest of its kind in Greece and marks the 126th Mycenaean tholos tomb identified across the country. The tomb's sheer scale indicates that it was built for a prominent family or individual possessing considerable wealth and influence.

What Scientists Found

While early accounts labeled the site a royal tomb, archaeologists have not confirmed the identity of the occupants or established a direct tie to a ruling dynasty. Specific details regarding the tomb's state of preservation, exact dimensions, and grave goods are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. However, the monument reflects the classic beehive design characteristic of Late Bronze Age Mycenaean burial architecture, which typically includes three primary features:

  • A long entrance pathway known as a dromos.
  • A monumental doorway called a stomion.
  • A circular chamber created through corbelling, where stones were set progressively inward to build a vaulted roof without relying on a true arch.

Why It Matters

The Mycenaean civilization flourished between roughly 1600 and 1100 BCE, building powerful networks across the Mediterranean. While mainland discoveries have traditionally guided modern understanding of Mycenaean traditions, finding such a significant monument on Euboea provides fresh insights into regional elite power and trade communication outside the major mainland hubs. To preserve the site during roadwork, parliamentarian Thanasis Zempilis suggested building an elevated bridge over the archaeological site following a visit on November 23. Additional proposals seek funding for ongoing excavation, restoration, and eventual public presentation.
