Dinosaur Dimensions gets children roaring with prehistoric puppetry

Published on November 05, 2026

The Discovery

Children in Spearfish, South Dakota, experienced an interactive presentation at the Matthew’s Opera House on Nov. 1. The event was brought to life by Dinosaur Dimensions, a Milwaukee-based educational theater team led by founder and host Curt Strutz. Framed as a real scientific laboratory that solved the secret of time travel, the production transported prehistoric creatures from the past directly onto the stage through puppetry and performance.

What Scientists Found

During the show, Strutz demonstrated several tools created by the fictional laboratory. He utilized a spinning whistling tube to attract the animals and guided them using circular pool rings. The central feature of the presentation was a hatching device called the "Dino 5000," which required the stomping feet and loud roars of the children to operate.

The interactive stage show introduced a variety of prehistoric animals to the audience:

  • A newly hatched T-rex that children were able to pet.
  • A massive Brachiosaurus that audience members helped feed.
  • Sparky the Ankylosaurus, Bluey the Velociraptor, and Mary the Pteranodon.
  • An eight-foot-tall Dilophosaurus that was released during a equipment malfunction, which the children determined to be friendly and named Ruby.

Specific geological excavation details and fossil ages are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why It Matters

The overall goal of Dinosaur Dimensions is to inspire young people to pursue interests in science, theater, education, or paleontology. For the Matthew’s Opera House, host events like this support an ongoing initiative to increase youth-focused offerings. Heath Johnson, the executive director of the opera house, noted that the facility has doubled its volume of children's plays for the year to ensure multi-generational engagement. The performance concluded with a lobby meet-and-greet, allowing children to pet the dinosaurs goodbye before leaving.
