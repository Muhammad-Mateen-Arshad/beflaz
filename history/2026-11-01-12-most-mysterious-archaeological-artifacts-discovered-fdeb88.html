12 Most Mysterious Archaeological Artifacts Discovered

Published on November 01, 2026

The Discovery

Archaeologists exploring ocean depths and ancient ruins occasionally unearth objects that challenge modern understandings of early human history. These baffling artifacts reveal that historical societies possessed engineering skills and technical knowledge far more sophisticated than previously assumed, leaving experts to debate how and why they were created.

What Scientists Found

Researchers have highlighted several notable objects discovered around the world that continue to puzzle scholars:

  • Astronomical Computing: In 1901, divers pulled a corroded bronze artifact from an ancient Greek cargo shipwreck in the Mediterranean Sea. Built with interlocking gears and detailed etchings, this hand-powered device is the oldest known analogue computer, constructed to forecast eclipse times and astronomical positions decades in advance.
  • Ancient Power Sources: Discovered near Baghdad, Iraq, in 1936 by archaeologist Wilhelm König, two-thousand-year-old clay jars featuring asphalt stoppers and iron rods can generate 0.5 to 1.5 volts of electricity when filled with acidic liquid.
  • Undeciphered Manuscript: Tested via radiocarbon dating in 2009, a 240-page parchment manuscript produced between 1404 and 1438 features drawings of unknown plants, astrological symbols, and human figures. Neither wartime codebreakers nor modern algorithms have successfully deciphered the script.
  • Advanced Maps: Drawn on gazelle skin in 1513 by Turkish admiral Piri Reis, a surviving map accurately plots the coastline of South America relative to Africa. Some scholars contend it shows an ice-free Antarctic landmass, which was officially undiscovered until 1820.
  • Roman Dodecahedra: Approximately 130 twelve-sided copper alloy objects from the Roman period in Britain have been uncovered in northwestern provinces. Each face contains circular holes of varying sizes, but no ancient artwork or writings record their existence.

Why It Matters

These discoveries demonstrate incredible technical skill, yet written records explaining their creation are virtually non-existent. For example, no surviving texts mention the electrical function of the Baghdad jars, and only one gear-based astronomical computer of its kind has ever been retrieved.
