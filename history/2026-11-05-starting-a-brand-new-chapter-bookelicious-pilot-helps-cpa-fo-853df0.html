Starting a Brand New Chapter: Bookelicious Pilot Helps CPA, FOSA Parents Rebuild Bonds at Home

Published on November 05, 2026

The Discovery

During a home visit, a three-year-old named Asher engaged with a book featuring brightly colored cartoon dinosaurs alongside his father, Thomas Jesse Rasher. The child identified several prehistoric figures, including the Pterodactyl, Brachiosaurus, Triceratops, and Stegosaurus, while mimicking a Tyrannosaurus rex roar. What Scientists Found

Scientific research concerning ancient biological origins or field discoveries is not specified in the source; please check the official notice. However, social science research cited from the Pew Charitable Trusts indicates that twenty-three percent of children with a father who served time in prison have experienced school expulsion or suspension. To assist families facing these academic and behavioral hurdles, the Washington State Department of Corrections partnered with the literacy platform Bookelicious. Through this pilot initiative, children design digital avatars called Bookmojis with corrections specialists, allowing librarians and educators to create customized reading lists tailored to each child's interests.

Why It Matters

Financed with twelve thousand dollars from the Incarcerated Individual Betterment Fund, the initiative covers six hundred initial books, ongoing material replenishment, and staff training. The program serves non-violent, low-risk parents participating in the Community Parenting Alternative and Family Offender Sentencing Alternative programs. By bringing tailored book deliveries directly into homes, the effort builds reading confidence, supports foundational literacy skills, and helps reduce children's anxieties regarding law enforcement officers.
