Dinosaur Fossil Exhibition Opens in Hong Kong, Showcasing Treasures from Port Island and the Chinese Mainland

Published on November 20, 2026

The Discovery

A historic chapter in palaeontological research has opened following the discovery of dinosaur fossils on Port Island. Excavation efforts at the location began in October 2024, marking the first time fossilized dinosaur remains have ever been uncovered in Hong Kong. The findings offer fresh insight into the region's ancient landscape and confirm that large dinosaurs once inhabited the area. The exact geological age of the Port Island specimen is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

The unearthed remains are now on view at the Hong Kong Heritage Discovery Centre as part of a showcase featuring 66 sets of rare items. Port Island's highlights include several fossilized bones that likely belonged to a single titanosauriform dinosaur. The exhibition also displays Cretaceous fossils gathered from the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area and other parts of Mainland China, including 34 National Class I key protection fossils.

Key featured specimens include:

  • An Asiatyrannus xui skeleton with an intact skull.
  • A Banji long cranium featuring a prominent bony crest and toothless jaws.
  • Jawbone fossils from Zhuchengtyrannus magnus, China's largest meat-eating dinosaur.
  • Elongated eggs laid by oviraptorosaurs alongside egg fossils containing preserved embryos.

Why It Matters

Organized through a collaboration between the Development Bureau and the Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the event highlights Hong Kong's role in the evolutionary history of dinosaurs. By integrating physical models, public lectures, and interactive workshops, the free exhibition aims to deepen public appreciation of prehistoric life and ancient ecosystems.
