‘The world we know is over’ without urgent climate action, leaders tell Cop31

Published on November 12, 2026

The Discovery

During global climate discussions held in Turkey, the recent discovery of two new oilfields containing an estimated in-situ potential of 135 million barrels was announced. At the same time, leaders from island nations documented alarming transformations in their local environments, such as vibrant aquatic coral turning bleached white within a span of just a few months.

What Scientists Found

Climate experts and global monitoring bodies highlighted several findings regarding the state of the planet:

  • Climate scientist Bill Hare reported that experts focusing on rainforests and cryosphere regions express a darkening mood, with many feeling environmental efforts are moving backward.
  • The United Nations Environment Programme established that exceeding the 1.5C heating limit is now unavoidable, warning that the resulting effects on icy regions will be calamitous.
  • Data from the UN reveals that current global policies place the earth on a path toward 2.8 degrees of warming, whereas delivering on every existing pledge would still lead to 2.6 degrees.

Why It Matters

In a speech to international leaders, UN Secretary General António Guterres stated that fossil fuels need to go the way of the dinosaurs, observing that ancient dinosaurs lacked a lobby to protect their interests. Representatives from vulnerable regions warned that missing climate targets creates a debt of heat borrowed against future generations, threatening low-lying island communities with severe flooding, shore erosion, and widespread environmental destruction.
