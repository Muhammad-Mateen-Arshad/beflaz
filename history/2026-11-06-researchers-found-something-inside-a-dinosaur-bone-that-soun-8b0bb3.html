Researchers Found Something Inside a Dinosaur Bone That Sounds Straight Out of ‘Jurassic Park’

Published on November 06, 2026

The Discovery

A research team led by Kliti Grice from Curtin University has shared remarkable findings in a study published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. The team examined the fossilized remains of a juvenile Aratasaurus museunacionali unearthed in Brazil, which measured 10 feet long and weighed 75 pounds. Inside the leg bone of this ancient specimen, scientists detected traces of dinosaur red blood cells that managed to endure for 115 million years.

The extraordinary preservation occurred because the creature was buried in low-oxygen mud shortly after death, which rapidly stopped the decay process. Over time, a manganese oxide mineral known as hausmannite enveloped the cell framework and preserved collagen amino acids like natural protective wrap.

What Scientists Found

Inside the vascular canals of the fossil's leg bone, researchers identified microscopic oval shapes filled with heme. Heme is an iron-loaded chemical ring present in hemoglobin that moves oxygen through an animal's body. This discovery marks the very first time heme has been detected inside these structures within a dinosaur fossil.

Key elements of the finding include:

  • Oval cellular structures preserved inside the leg bone's vascular channels.
  • The presence of iron-rich heme, essential for oxygen transportation.
  • Structural support provided by hausmannite, which protected amino acids over hundreds of millions of years.

Why It Matters

While companies like Colossal Biosciences explore ideas regarding extinct species, researchers note that recovering heme is quite different from finding functional, intact DNA. Grice explained that the team has no plans to attempt creating a live dinosaur. Instead, scientists will utilize these blood traces to gain direct insights into the animal's metabolic functions, activity levels, and oxygen transport systems.

Additionally, the research suggests that other dinosaur bones stored in museum archives or buried in unexcavated ground might also contain preserved biological materials waiting to be discovered.
