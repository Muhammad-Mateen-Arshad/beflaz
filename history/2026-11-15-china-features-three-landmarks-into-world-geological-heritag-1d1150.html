China features three landmarks into world geological heritage list

Published on November 15, 2026

The Discovery

China has expanded its presence on the International Union of Geological Sciences (IUGS) list of geological heritage sites by adding three new locations. Announced on Saturday, this latest addition brings the country's total to 13 sites, placing China among the nations with the largest number of recognized geological heritage locations. The IUGS has been releasing these designations in batches since 2022, with China securing seven designations in the first batch, three in the second, and three in the latest update.

What Scientists Found

The three newly recognized sites preserve remarkable geological structures and prehistoric remains:

  • Middle Jurassic Dinosaur Fossil Site: Located in Yunyang within southwest China's Chongqing Municipality, this location spans the entire Middle Jurassic period. It features fossils from 11 dinosaur species representing 19 families, 12 orders, and 6 classes. The site also contains the world's largest in-situ Jurassic fossil wall.
  • Xianshuihe Fault: Situated in southwest China's Sichuan Province on the eastern edge of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, this feature ranks among the most active intracontinental faults on Earth.
  • Dabie Ultrahigh-Pressure Metamorphic Belt: Located in east China's Anhui Province, this Triassic site is the Earth's largest, most extensive, and best-preserved ultrahigh-pressure metamorphic belt.

Why It Matters

These sites offer significant insights into Earth's history and ancient ecosystems. The dinosaur discovery at Yunyang fills an important gap in the evolutionary history of dinosaurs. The Xianshuihe Fault provides scientists with an accessible natural laboratory to study active continental deformation, fault mechanics, and seismic risks.

Additionally, the Dabie region acts as a natural laboratory for studying subduction, exhumation, and deep fluid-rock interactions, offering crucial evidence to advance plate-tectonic theory. According to Zhang Jianping, a professor at China University of Geosciences (Beijing) and vice chair of the IUGS International Commission on Geoheritage, these listed sites possess international scientific significance, preserve key geological processes, set global standards, and demonstrate high levels of protection.
