Travels to National Natural History Museums [87] Kuzuryu Fossil Lab Ga-Ono

Published on November 15, 2026

The Discovery

Fukui Prefecture stands as Japan's primary hub for dinosaur research, supported by a specialized academic department at the prefectural university. Located in Ono City near Roadside Station Kuzuryu, the Kuzuryu Fossil Lab Ga-Ono—also known as the Izumi Local History Museum—functions as a key research and exhibition facility. The center displays authentic fossil specimens collected from local rock layers, which include some of the oldest geological strata in the region dating back to the Precambrian era.

What Scientists Found

Excavations across Ono City and the Izumi district have produced a wide variety of ancient plant and animal remains:

  • Limestone near Hakubado in Hakogase has yielded honeycomb coral and crinoid fossils from the Silurian to Devonian periods.
  • Carboniferous strata contained tiny conodont fossils, which belong to aquatic creatures that lived between the Cambrian and Triassic eras.
  • Sandstone within the Permian Oguradani Formation, originating around 270 million years ago, contained abundant bryozoan fossils that formed ancient reef structures.
  • Jurassic layers preserved the hard internal guards of belemnites—marine cephalopods related to squids and octopuses—alongside rich ammonite fossils from the Kuzuryu Group, Nagano Shale Formation, and Kamihanbara Formation.
  • Cretaceous strata in the Tetori Group revealed plant and animal specimens, including finds from the Itsuki Formation dated to around 127 million years ago and the Nochino Formation dated to around 120 million years ago.
  • Researchers also recovered a tyrannosaurid tooth fossil and some of Japan's oldest bird footprint fossils in the area.

Why It Matters

The fossil deposits in Ono City allow scientists to trace massive biological and environmental shifts from the Paleozoic era through the Mesozoic era. By displaying extremely rare, real specimens alongside research from the Fukui Prefectural Dinosaur Museum, the facility helps connect local paleontology to global scientific research.
