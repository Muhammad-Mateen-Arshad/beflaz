Wyoming bone fossils reveal 156-million-year-old reptile unlike any known species

Published on November 21, 2026

The Discovery

In August 2022, field workers from Switzerland's Aathal Dinosaur Museum excavated a small vertebral column encased in sandstone at the Meilyn Quarry in Carbon County, Wyoming. Found within the Morrison Formation—a region famous for giant dinosaurs—the partial skeleton was initially impossible to identify with confidence. Researchers considered several possibilities, including an ancient snake, a relative of crocodiles, or a juvenile dinosaur.

What Scientists Found

To inspect the fossil hidden inside the rock without damaging it, an international research team jointly led by Pavel Trtik of the Paul Scherrer Institute and Torsten Scheyer of the University of Zurich turned to specialized scanning methods. Initial X-ray scans could not clearly separate the delicate bones from the rock. However, neutron imaging successfully targeted hydrogen contained in hydroxyapatite, a mineral component of bone. Combining neutron scans with extensive X-ray examinations allowed the team to distinguish bone from sandstone and conservation material.

By sifting through tens of thousands of cross-sectional images and digitally tracing individual bones, researchers reconstructed the animal's skeleton. They identified a new genus and species named Meilynodraco amonamarthi, combining the discovery site with a reference to a Swedish death metal band.

Key findings about the 156-million-year-old creature include:

  • A small, lizard-like body plan measuring about 45 centimeters long.
  • Skeletal features indicating a likely semiaquatic lifestyle.
  • A small braincase less than two centimeters across with backward-pointing, dragon-like skull projections.
  • A torso separated from the skull, raising unconfirmed possibilities of a predator attack.

Why It Matters

The reptile belongs to the choristoderes, an extinct group of freshwater reptiles whose early history and place on the reptile family tree have remained unclear due to their unusual skull shapes. Detailed anatomical information provided by this Jurassic fossil, described in Royal Society Open Science, offers crucial new evidence for understanding how these ancient creatures evolved.
