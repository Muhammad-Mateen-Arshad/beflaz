Scientists just found dinosaur blood that survived 115 million years

Published on November 06, 2026

The Discovery

Scientists have uncovered remarkably preserved traces of dinosaur blood within 115-million-year-old fossilized bones. An international research team, led by Curtin University, identified fossilized red blood cells and ancient biological molecules inside the limb bones of Aratasaurus museunacionali, a meat-eating theropod dinosaur found in northeastern Brazil.

The study, published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, was led by Professor Kliti Grice from Curtin University alongside researchers from the Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology, the Brazilian National Museum, the Regional University of Cariri, and Johns Hopkins University. The research was supported by a 2021 Australian Research Council Laureate Fellowship.

What Scientists Found

Inside the vascular canals of the dinosaur's limb bones, the team discovered numerous mineralized red blood cells. Detailed chemical analysis confirmed the presence of amino acids associated with collagen, an essential structural protein, as well as preserved heme molecules. Heme is the key component of hemoglobin, which enables red blood cells to carry oxygen throughout the body.

The extraordinary survival of these organic components was aided by a mineral called hausmannite, which acted as a protective shield for the cells and their chemical contents. Scientists also believe the dinosaur was rapidly buried beneath sediment in an environment characterized by low oxygen and high microbial activity, preventing the delicate material from breaking down.

Why It Matters

This study offers the first definitive chemical confirmation of hemoglobin-derived molecules inside fossilized dinosaur blood cells. It challenges a long-held paleontological belief that delicate biological structures cannot persist across millions of years.

The findings offer valuable insights across multiple fields:

  • They allow researchers to study the physiology, metabolism, and activity levels of extinct species at a molecular level.
  • They help astrobiologists understand how fragile chemical signatures endure over 100 million years, aiding the search for ancient life beyond Earth.
