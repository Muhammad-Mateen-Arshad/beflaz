Spain may have uncovered Europe’s biggest dinosaur as 18-tonne fossil is recovered

Published on November 24, 2026

The Discovery

Paleontologists working in Catalonia, Spain, have retrieved a massive fossil block from the Molí del Piteu site near Isona, in the province of Lleida. The site yielded the remains of a long-necked, plant-eating titanosaur that roamed the Earth during the Cretaceous period, approximately 70 million years ago. To transport the delicate find safely, researchers encased it in a seven-cubic-metre structure made of acrylic resin, iron, and polyurethane—a heavy casing known informally among specialists as a mummy. The complete structure weighed 18 tonnes and required a large excavator and four hours of work to turn and load onto a trailer.

What Scientists Found

Excavations revealed an unusually varied collection of bones from a single creature, including:

  • A neck measuring roughly seven metres long
  • Portions of the skull area, chest, and front limbs
  • Vertebrae that may still remain attached in their original positions

Palaeontological preparator Elena Nieto highlighted that several of the specimen's ribs are longer than those of Abditosaurus, a 15- to 18-metre titanosaur discovered at nearby Orcau in 2014. During the transport operation, a front fragment split off from the block, meaning researchers must evaluate whether any bones inside were damaged.

Why It Matters

Researchers suggest the dinosaur could be larger than any titanosaur previously recovered in Europe, or it may prove to be a completely unrecorded species. Experts from the Catalan Institute of Palaeontology and the Museu de la Conca Della will systematically clear away the rock and clean the bones in Isona. This work will start outdoors before moving to an enclosed area, where organizers plan to host public visits so spectators can watch the preparation unfold. The excavation adds to Spain's growing list of significant fossil discoveries, which also includes a Diplodocus in Teruel and a nearly complete Stegosaurus skull.
