Where did it go? National Park Service investigates 88-year mystery of missing snake fossil

Published on November 17, 2026

The Discovery

A remarkable prehistoric skeleton has become the subject of an 88-year mystery. Originally obtained between 1875 and 1913, the fossilized remains of an extinct snake were discovered in the Green River Formation within southwestern Wyoming's Fossil Basin. Belonging to the Cenozoic era, the artifact initially belonged to Max Idelman before passing to his son-in-law, a silk merchant named Edgar S. Weinberg, who settled in White Plains, New York.

What Scientists Found

During the 1930s, Weinberg loaned the ancient specimen to Charles W. Gilmore at the U.S. National Museum in Washington, D.C. National Park Service officials suspect Weinberg brought it in to determine its financial value. Gilmore identified the creature as a brand-new species named Boavus idelmani. In his 1938 study, Fossil Snakes of North America, Gilmore declared it to be the most complete fossil snake ever recovered in that region. Shortly after its identification, the specimen disappeared without a trace.

Several details surround the history and research of this specimen:

  • Museum staff may have created a replica cast for study, though no physical modifications were formally documented.
  • It is uncertain whether Gilmore performed any further documentation before returning the object to Weinberg.
  • In unrelated fossil news from May 14, a diver named Kristina Scott found a six-inch megalodon tooth off the coast of Venice, Florida.

Why It Matters

The National Park Service considers the Boavus idelmani skeleton to be priceless for scientific research. Representatives in Kemmerer, Wyoming, are actively asking the public to assist in locating the lost artifact. Authorities hope that raising awareness will encourage the owner to return the fossil so scientists can continue studying it. Specific instructions on who to contact regarding the fossil's location are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
