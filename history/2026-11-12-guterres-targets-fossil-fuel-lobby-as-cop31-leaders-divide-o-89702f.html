Guterres Targets Fossil Fuel Lobby as COP31 Leaders Divide Over Debt, Finance and War

Published on November 12, 2026

The Discovery

During a climate summit in Antalya, United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres invoked the concept of extinction to highlight modern environmental challenges. He asserted that fossil fuels must follow the path of extinct dinosaurs, observing that while prehistoric dinosaurs lacked advocacy groups, modern fossil fuels possess powerful lobby interests. Details regarding physical fossil sites or paleontology digs are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

Scientific updates and assessments presented at the gathering outlined several key findings regarding global environmental changes:

  • The World Meteorological Organization tracked global average temperatures from January to September at 1.47°C above pre-industrial baselines, with an El Niño expected to reach record strength by December.
  • Scientific consensus indicates that exceeding the 1.5°C warming threshold established under the Paris Agreement is now inevitable, though human action can still influence the severity and duration of the overshoot.
  • Field observations in the Marshall Islands revealed rapid environmental impacts, where half of a monitored coral reef suffered bleaching over a period of just a few months.
  • The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change is working to complete scientific evaluations for the 2028 global stocktake, despite resistance from countries including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and India.

Why It Matters

The gap between economic interests and environmental recovery presents significant global challenges:

  • The nine largest oil companies generated $95 billion in profit in a single quarter, whereas a climate disaster recovery fund created at COP28 in Dubai has gathered approximately $600 million—an amount equivalent to roughly 14 hours of those corporate earnings.
  • UNCTAD estimates that developing nations will owe $995 billion in public debt interest payments in 2025, leaving 3.7 billion people in countries that spend more on debt interest than on healthcare or education.
  • Small island developing states receive only 0.2% of climate adaptation funding, while developing economies outside China contain nearly two-thirds of humanity but receive less than one-fifth of global investments in clean energy.
