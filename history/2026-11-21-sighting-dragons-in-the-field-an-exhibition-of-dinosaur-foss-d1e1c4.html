Sighting Dragons in the Field: An Exhibition of Dinosaur Fossils from Port Island and the Chinese Mainland

Published on November 21, 2026

The Discovery

A historic milestone in palaeontology has unfolded with the revelation of Hong Kong's very first dinosaur fossils. Excavation work on Port Island began in October 2024, uncovering rare prehistoric remains that shed light on the region's ancient environment. The findings confirm the presence of giant dinosaurs in Hong Kong's deep past and establish the territory's unique position in dinosaur evolution research.

What Scientists Found

The unearthed Port Island materials include several bones that likely originate from a single titanosauriform dinosaur. Alongside these local discoveries, a comprehensive exhibition displays a total of 66 sets of invaluable fossil specimens gathered from the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area and other regions across Mainland China.

Key scientific highlights featured in the collection include:

  • The fossil skeleton of Asiatyrannus xui featuring a complete cranium.
  • A Banji long cranium fossil with a prominent bony crest and toothless upper and lower jaws.
  • Massive jawbone fossils from Zhuchengtyrannus magnus.
  • Elongated fossil eggs laid by oviraptorosaurs.
  • Rare dinosaur egg fossils preserved with fossilized embryos inside.

Of the 46 sets of fossils brought from the Mainland, 34 hold National Class I key protection status, with most being exhibited in Hong Kong for the first time. The exact age and physical measurements of these specimens are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why It Matters

Jointly presented by the Development Bureau and the Institute of Vertebrate Paleontology and Paleoanthropology of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the collection provides an in-depth view of Cretaceous dinosaur evolution and ancient ecosystems in China. Complemented by illustrative models, the exhibition helps visitors trace how prehistoric life changed over time. To broaden public knowledge of palaeontology, the Hong Kong Heritage Discovery Centre is organizing public lectures and educational workshops alongside free public admission to the display.
