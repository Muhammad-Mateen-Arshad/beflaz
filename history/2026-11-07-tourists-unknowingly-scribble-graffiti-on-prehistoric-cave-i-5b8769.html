Tourists unknowingly scribble graffiti on prehistoric cave in Spain dating back to the Ice Age

Published on November 07, 2026

For years, tourists visiting a popular destination in Spain unknowingly scrawled modern graffiti over prehistoric rock art. The ancient Ice Age etchings remained hidden on the walls until researchers discovered what was concealed beneath decades of visitor markings.

The Discovery

The cave, known as Cova de la Font Major, is located near the town of Espluga de Francolí in Tarragona, northeastern Spain. Although the site has attracted visitors since it was found in 1957, researchers first identified the prehistoric art in 2019. The findings were published in the journal Antiquity.

Co-author Míriam Salas-Altès, a doctoral student at the University of Rovira i Virgili and IPHES-CERCA, explained that dark conditions inside the cave made the faint carvings difficult to see. Additionally, modern visitor graffiti scrawled across the stone obscured the ancient line work.

What Scientists Found

Archaeologists documented 280 Paleolithic etchings on the cave walls, forming one of the largest collections found on the Iberian Peninsula. The discovery includes:

  • 55 animal depictions, including deer, horses, and a bear
  • Abstract patterns featuring loops and swirls

The Paleolithic era represents humanity's earliest prehistory, spanning from roughly 3.3 million years ago to about 11,650 years ago. However, the exact age of these specific carvings is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why It Matters

This discovery provides rare evidence of prehistoric human activity in Mediterranean Iberia. Previously, researchers had only recorded two figures in Catalonia, as most Paleolithic cave art on the peninsula was concentrated in the northern Franco-Cantabrian region.

Authorities have restricted cave access to protect the site while research continues. Scholars hope to determine whether prehistoric people used the cave for shelter, rituals, or another purpose. Experts also suspect that sediment filling the cave over time may be hiding additional ancient artwork.
