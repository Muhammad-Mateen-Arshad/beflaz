Kultepe excavation — South Korean Archaeologists Discover Hellenistic Mosaic

Published on October 28, 2026

The Discovery

South Korean researchers participating in an international excavation in central Turkey have unearthed significant Hellenistic-era remains at the ancient Kultepe site. Digging approximately two meters beneath the surface, the archaeological team uncovered four distinct, overlapping cultural layers alongside a building foundation featuring a mosaic floor. Although historical texts previously mentioned Hellenistic presence in the area, physical evidence had never been found since excavations first began at Kultepe in 1948.

What Scientists Found

The newly identified mosaic architectural feature measures about 163 centimeters east-west and 165 centimeters north-south. Excavators determined that the foundation was built using a careful layering technique:

  • Leveling the initial ground surface
  • Laying down a base layer of fine soil and crushed stone
  • Adding a layer of small river gravel
  • Finishing the upper surface with lime and pottery shards

Researchers also recovered Hellenistic-era pottery stamped with specific artisan names and quantity markings from the same layer. Because mosaic architecture in this region typically denoted sacred places used for religious rituals, the building indicates that Kultepe remained a prominent, thriving urban center during the Hellenistic period (323–30 BC).

Why It Matters

Kultepe, historically known as Kanesh, was previously recognized mainly as a Bronze Age hub for the Assyrian and Hittite civilizations, famed for yielding thousands of cuneiform trade tablets. These new findings demonstrate that active Hellenistic cultural exchanges reached deep into the Anatolian interior rather than remaining limited to coastal zones. With only about three percent of the site excavated so far, researchers anticipate further findings across stacked layers that span from the Paleolithic era to modern times. The project also represents a major milestone for South Korean archaeology, expanding its research footprint into ancient global civilizations.
