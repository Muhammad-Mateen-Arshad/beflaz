Scientists Identify Giant 66-Pound Rodent That Lived 34 Million Years Ago

Published on November 13, 2026

The Discovery

In 2004, researchers examining sediment deposits at a coal mine in Thailand's Krabi Province uncovered two fossilized teeth: a large molar and an incisor. Geologist Pannipa Tian, working with Thailand's Department of Mineral Resources, found the incisor and initially assumed it was an entire bone because of its massive size. After showing it to her supervisor, the team recognized it belonged to an extraordinarily large rodent, though they lacked enough information at the time to identify the exact species. More than two decades later, an international team of researchers from Thailand, France, and McGill University in Canada reexamined the tooth structures to classify the newly discovered ancient creature.

What Scientists Found

The newly identified species was named Yakamys sirindhornae. The name blends the Thai word for giant ("yak") with the Greek word for mouse or rat ("mys"), while the second portion honors Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn of Thailand for her long-standing support of scientific and paleontological research.

To estimate the animal's physical size, scientists applied seven separate mathematical formulas that use tooth dimensions to calculate body weight. They concluded that the rodent weighed around 66 pounds—roughly double the weight of other rodents living in the same geological era. Study co-author Virginie Millien, a biology professor at McGill University, noted that the figure remains a rough estimate because researchers only had two teeth to analyze.

Why It Matters

The team's research, published on November 4 in the journal Scientific Reports, shows that Yakamys sirindhornae lived approximately 34 million years ago during the Eocene period. Scientists already knew that giant prehistoric rodents evolved in South America, but this discovery proves that rodent gigantism was occurring in Southeast Asia over 30 million years ago—far earlier in that region than previously established.

While 66 pounds is huge compared to typical modern rats and mice, it is still surpassed by today's largest living rodent, the capybara. Native to South America, capybaras can reach over four feet in length and weigh up to 174 pounds according to the San Diego Zoo, making them over 100 pounds heavier than this prehistoric Asian giant.
