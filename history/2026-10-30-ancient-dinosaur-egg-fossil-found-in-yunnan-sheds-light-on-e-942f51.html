Ancient dinosaur egg fossil found in Yunnan sheds light on eggshell evolution

Published on October 30, 2026

The Discovery

In 2008, scientists uncovered an extraordinarily well-preserved dinosaur egg fossil in the Lufeng Formation, located in Lufeng within Yunnan province's Chuxiong Yi autonomous prefecture. Encased inside a rock slightly smaller than a chicken egg, the fossilized shell has been named Mutabilisoolithus dawaensis. It ranks among the oldest and best-preserved dinosaur eggshells known globally. A team involving researchers from Hebei University and the dinosaur fossil conservation and research center of Lufeng's natural resources bureau analyzed the specimen, publishing their findings in Nature Communications on October 26.

What Scientists Found

Using chemical analysis alongside advanced imaging technology—specifically micro-CT scanning—the research team determined that the egg belonged to an early sauropodomorph dinosaur from the Early Jurassic period, dating back approximately 195 million years. Key structural details revealed by the analysis include:

  • An unusually thick inner membrane.
  • A very thin outer layer composed of calcium-based minerals.
  • A flexible overall structure that remained soft despite beginning the process of mineralization.

Why It Matters

This discovery sheds light on the transition from soft prehistoric eggs to rigid eggshells like those laid by modern birds. Scientists believe this distinct structure captures one of the earliest phases of eggshell mineralization. As a result, the fossil helps bridge an evolutionary gap separating completely soft eggs lacking mineralized shells from those that developed stronger, partially mineralized outer coverings.
