Scientists identify heme in dinosaur fossil in world-first discovery

Published on November 06, 2026

Researchers analyzing a fossilized dinosaur bone have uncovered compelling evidence that components of red blood cells can endure the fossilization process over millions of years. This landmark study marks the first time scientists have detected heme directly inside a dinosaur fossil in place.

The Discovery

The research focused on a 115-million-year-old fossilized hind limb belonging to Aratasaurus museunacionali, a small predatory theropod uncovered in northeastern Brazil in 2008. Led by Prof. Kliti Grice from Curtin University, the team published their findings in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.

What Scientists Found

Using chemical and microscopic techniques, the team identified oval-shaped structures within the microchannels of the bone that originally transported blood. Chemical analysis confirmed the presence of heme, the oxygen-carrying iron compound found in hemoglobin, within these shapes.

Researchers believe two primary factors enabled this rare preservation:

  • Rapid burial within an oxygen-deprived environment.
  • The influence of hausmannite, a manganese oxide mineral.

Why It Matters

The finding challenges long-standing scientific assumptions that complex biomolecules like hemoglobin cannot survive across deep time. Scientists suggest that applying this analytical approach to museum collections around the world could unlock previously unexamined molecular information. However, the study authors emphasized that the survival of heme does not mean DNA has survived.

Expert Reactions

Not all experts are fully convinced by the findings. Prof. Mary Schweitzer of North Carolina State University pointed out that non-mammalian red blood cells normally retain nuclei and exhibit a distinctive bulge, whereas the shapes detected in the fossil were concave. Nevertheless, Schweitzer praised the research team's use of multiple analytical techniques, describing the study as an exciting advancement for the field.
