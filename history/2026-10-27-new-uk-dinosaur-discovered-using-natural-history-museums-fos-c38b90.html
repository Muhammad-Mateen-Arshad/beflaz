New UK dinosaur discovered using Natural History Museum’s fossil collections named after devoted dad and husband

Published on October 27, 2026

The Discovery

A newly recognized dinosaur relative has been identified using specimens kept in the Natural History Museum’s collections. The remains were originally uncovered in southern England at wienerberger's Ewhurst brick and roof tile works. The exact timeline of the discovery and details about the specimen's age are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

The fossil belongs to a close relative of Iguanodon and has been formally named Paludohadros. The species name commemorates Will Proudlove, who served as the manager at the Ewhurst site and passed away shortly before the excavation occurred. Physical dimensions and complete anatomical features of Paludohadros are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why It Matters

The area where Paludohadros was retrieved is situated close to the place where Baryonyx was first found. Baryonyx was an iconic meat-eater from the Cretaceous period that fed on fish as well as young iguanodontians, offering insight into the prehistoric life of the region.
