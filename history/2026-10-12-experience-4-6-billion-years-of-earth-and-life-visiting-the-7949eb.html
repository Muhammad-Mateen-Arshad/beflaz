Experience 4.6 Billion Years of Earth and Life! Visiting the Earth Museum with Elementary School Children

Published on October 12, 2026

The Discovery

The Kanagawa Prefectural Museum of Natural History, located in Odawara City, presents the history of Earth and life spanning approximately 4.6 billion years. Through its exhibits, visitors can examine the physical records of our planet's past, from its early geological formation to the evolution of ancient living organisms and regional natural history. Specific excavation dates or scientific species names for the specimens on display are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

The museum organizes its scientific displays across several primary thematic sections:

  • Thinking about the Earth: Features giant models of geological strata, rocks collected from across the globe, and vibrant mineral formations including jade.
  • Thinking About Life: Showcases life-sized skeletal specimens of ancient creatures, including a massive long-necked dinosaur skeleton that dwarfs even a Tyrannosaurus, as well as animal taxidermy and butterfly specimens from around the world.
  • Interactive Education: An internal theater system lets visitors participate directly by choosing between five categories—Earth, plants, fish, animals, and humans—using five seat buttons to answer quiz questions that alter the progression of the video.

Why It Matters

In the "Thinking about Coexistence with Nature" area, the museum highlights how human activities actively influence the atmosphere, water quality, and global biological ecosystems. Seeing full-scale fossil skeletons and natural specimens in person offers a sense of proportion and impact that textbooks and picture books cannot convey. These tangible encounters encourage visitors to recognize the ongoing burden placed on the environment and consider personal actions to support sustainable coexistence with nature.
