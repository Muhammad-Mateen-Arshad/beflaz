Columbia Museum to Unveil 18-Inch Dino Eggs in New 2027 Exhibit

Published on October 31, 2026

An upcoming traveling exhibition at the South Carolina State Museum in Columbia will showcase remarkable prehistoric discoveries. Opening on January 23, 2027, the display titled Tiny Titans: Dinosaur Eggs & Babies brings authentic nests and eggs collected from across the globe together with interactive learning activities for visitors of all ages.

The Discovery

Among the headline specimens in the collection are dinosaur eggs measuring nearly 18 inches long, which stand as the longest ever discovered. These extraordinary eggs were laid by a giant species of oviraptor, an ostrich-like dinosaur. The exhibit also features:

  • A bowling-ball-sized sauropod egg from Argentina, laid approximately 75 million years ago by a long-necked, plant-eating titanosaur.
  • A large cluster of eggs laid by a plant-eating, duck-billed dinosaur.

What Scientists Found

According to Dr. Scott Persons, the museum's curator of natural history, the exhibition features North America's largest collection of dinosaur eggs. The specimens highlight how ancient creatures nested, reproduced, grew, and developed. Guests can inspect reconstructed nests, handle real dinosaur bones, dig for fossilized eggs, observe realistic hatchling models, and view animated videos featuring paleontology experts.

Why It Matters

The display demonstrates the impressive variety of dinosaur reproduction, contrasting compact egg clusters with single shells nearly the length of a toddler. This event arrives alongside broader enhancements to the museum's Natural History gallery, expected to be completed by the end of 2026. These updates include cast skeletons of Deinonychus, Dimetrodon, and Stegosaurus, a habitat mural, and authentic South Carolina fossils.

To celebrate the debut on January 23, 2027, the museum will host Fossil Fest: Roar and Explore Day, featuring live birds of prey, natural history experts, and a member morning (exact timing details are not specified in the source; please check the official notice). Exhibition access is included with standard admission or museum membership, costing $13 for adults, $11 for seniors, and $10 for children ages 3 to 12, while children 2 and under enter for free.
