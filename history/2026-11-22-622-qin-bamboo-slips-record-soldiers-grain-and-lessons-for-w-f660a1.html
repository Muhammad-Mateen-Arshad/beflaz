622 Qin Bamboo Slips Record Soldiers, Grain and Lessons for Women

Published on November 22, 2026

The Discovery

Archaeologists excavating at the Laohudun cemetery in Yunmeng County, Hubei Province, uncovered a valuable trove of ancient documents and artifacts. Dating from the late Warring States period to the early Western Han, the site yielded 622 bamboo and wooden slips from three specific tombs, designated M64, M74, and M116. The work was carried out by the Hubei Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology alongside the Yunmeng County Museum, clearing 113 tombs from the Qin and Han eras in total. What Scientists Found

The recovered bamboo and wooden slips cover political, military, medical, and daily topics. Key items discovered among the texts include:

  • Inventory records detailing military defenses, soldier counts, carts, weapons, ditch sizes, and stocks of grain, fodder, and copper for small local offices known as ting under Shaxian County.
  • The full organizational layout of the Chiqiu ting, outlining its governed area, staff, buildings, ditches, and wells.
  • A moral instruction text for women from tomb M74, marking the first text of its kind retrieved from a scientific archaeological excavation in China. The specific wording of this text is Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Collections covering everyday practical subjects, including almanacs, medical recipes, silkworm care, childbirth, healing, physiognomy, housing, and clothing.

Excavators also recovered over 600 lacquerware items. Highlights include a phoenix-shaped ladle featuring a standing figure, a flat flask decorated with auspicious beasts, and a round box bearing an incised inscription reading guang Qin zhe.

Why It Matters

These administrative documents offer rare insight into the lowest level of government offices in the Qin state. While traditional histories usually record major historical figures and large-scale events, these small office slips preserve the fine details of local governance, inventory, and staffing. Furthermore, researchers interpret the lacquer inscription guang Qin zhe as referring to the people who promoted the Qin system and expanded its territory, providing fresh evidence regarding how ancient people viewed their state's reach.
