Climate Brief:‘The world we know is over’

Published on November 12, 2026

The Discovery

At the Cop31 climate summit in Turkey, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan announced the discovery of two new oilfields containing an estimated in-situ potential of 135 million barrels. Specific details regarding physical dinosaur fossil discoveries or prehistoric biological remains were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

Climate research and data presented during the summit highlight critical environmental trends across the globe:

  • UN statistics indicate that even if all current national pledges are fulfilled, global temperatures are on track to rise by 2.6 degrees, while current policies will push temperatures up by 2.8 degrees.
  • The planet is moving past the 1.5C limit while entering one of the strongest El Niño events in recorded history.
  • Climate scientist Bill Hare noted a darkening mood among researchers studying rainforests and the cryosphere, with many voicing concerns that progress is moving backward.
  • Firsthand accounts shared at the summit documented bright, lush coral reefs bleaching completely white within just a few months.

Why It Matters

During the proceedings, UN Secretary General António Guterres made a direct comparison to prehistoric history, declaring that fossil fuels must go the way of the dinosaurs. He noted that unlike ancient extinct species, the modern fossil fuel industry possesses a powerful lobby that distorts science and delays vital climate action.

World leaders from vulnerable island nations emphasized the extreme danger posed by continued climate inaction. Marshall Islands President Hilda Heine warned that rising sea levels and worsening floods threaten to destroy towns, schools, and hospitals, stating that the discovery and extraction of new fossil fuel resources threatens her country's future. Additionally, Barbados Prime Minister Mia Mottley described temperature overshoot as a heat debt borrowed against future generations that must ultimately be paid back.
