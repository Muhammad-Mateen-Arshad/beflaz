Fourth Liangzhu Forum Opens in Hangzhou to Facilitate Global Heritage Dialogue

Published on October 20, 2026

The Discovery

More than 5,000 years ago, the ancient Liangzhu people built a grand city and developed a rich rice-farming society in southeast China's Hangzhou region. The opening of the fourth Liangzhu Forum on October 18, 2026, coincided with the 90th anniversary of the initial discovery of the Liangzhu Site. In 2019, the Archaeological Ruins of Liangzhu City were officially inscribed on the World Heritage List, offering tangible proof of China's 5,000-year-old civilization.

What Scientists Found

The gathering convened scholars and global participants to address cultural heritage protection under the theme "Bridging the Past and Future: Safeguarding Cultural Heritage for Our Times." Academic discussions across the main event and five sub-forums highlighted several key areas:

  • The announcement of critical research findings concerning the origins of the Yangtze-River civilization.
  • Insights into early-civilization archaeology and procedures for World Heritage nominations.
  • Applications of new technologies for heritage protection, including artificial intelligence and virtual reality setups that recreate the ancient city for visitors.
  • Practical solutions for returning lost cultural relics and balancing site conservation with local tourism.

Why It Matters

First established in 2023, the Liangzhu Forum has evolved into a permanent national dialogue mechanism and is now part of China's 15th Five-Year Plan outline. Rather than turning historical areas into isolated zones, the region utilizes the Liangzhu Cultural Corridor to connect archaeological sites with local villages, ancient towns, and creative parks. This model uses historical assets to fuel local industries, boost household incomes, and involve surrounding communities directly in site preservation.

Cultural outreach activities further connect historical heritage to the modern world. Twenty-five artists from 14 countries explored UNESCO sites including the Liangzhu ruins, West Lake, and the Grand Canal to create location-inspired art. Meanwhile, creative projects incorporate ancient jade-cong motifs into modern merchandise, and youth sessions allow students, foreign ambassadors, and experts to share ideas on civilizational learning.
