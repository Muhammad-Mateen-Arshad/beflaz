New insights into prehistoric South India’s culinary culture

Published on November 11, 2026

The Discovery

The history of ancient food practices in North India's Indus Valley Civilisation, dating back to 2500 BCE, is well established through ceramic studies showing a diet of meat, dairy, and plants. However, a recent study published in npj | heritage science uses sophisticated technology to reveal the culinary traditions of prehistoric South India. The specific names of the researchers who conducted the study are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

By deconstructing pottery shards from two key archaeological locations, researchers identified evidence of cow milk consumption and advanced plant processing between 600 and 100 BC. The examined sites include:

  • Keeladi in Tamil Nadu: Dated from the 6th to 2nd century BC, ceramic fragments here contained a complex mix of organic molecules, notably including peanut residues.
  • Gottiprolu in Andhra Pradesh: Dated from the 2nd to 1st century BCE.

Why It Matters

This study provides the first direct evidence that ancient South Indian populations relied primarily on agriculture, cattle rearing, and dairying traditions. Additional information regarding the team's excavation techniques is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
