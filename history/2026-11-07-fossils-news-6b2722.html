Fossils News

Published on November 07, 2026

The Discovery

Recent fossil finds and archaeological reports highlight a wide range of discoveries concerning prehistoric life and human ancestors. Evidence includes 115-million-year-old dinosaur blood, a 700,000-year-old tooth detailing an ancient world, and a 210-million-year-old dinosaur find in Africa. Additionally, researchers identified the first Diplodocus fossil ever located outside North America, alongside a 245-million-year-old fossil that retained its internal organs, including the stomach and intestines.

What Scientists Found

Examinations of fossilized remains and ancient artifacts reveal key details about early species and human lineages:

  • Early human ancestors were bringing fire into caves 1.8 million years ago, and evidence shows Homo erectus existed 200,000 years earlier than previously anticipated.
  • A 324-million-year-old fossil explains how insects adapted to land, while millipedes reached land 80 million years ahead of vertebrates.
  • A 23-foot crocodylian occupied the top of South America's food web, and a 125-million-year-old crocodile exhibited potential camouflage under UV light.
  • In Peru, archaeologists uncovered a 1,200-year-old mummified hairless dog, while Ice Age populations in China manufactured advanced stone tools 146,000 years ago.
  • A child's tooth found with green stones revealed a 5,500-year-old mystery, while ancient bone dice indicate a 12,000-year history of gambling in America.

Why It Matters

These findings reshape current timelines regarding animal evolution, prehistoric predator behaviors, and ancient human origins. Finds such as ancient ape fossils and hidden Neanderthal DNA suggest that the human family tree requires significant revisions.
