Ancient burial site packed with treasures plus 38 bodies discovered after centuries

Published on November 13, 2026

Archaeologists working in Peru have uncovered a nearly untouched 600-year-old burial site containing ancient artifacts and the remains of 38 people. The complex was revealed at Chan Chan, an ancient pre-Incan city located near Trujillo, approximately 350 miles north of Lima. Peru's Ministry of Culture announced that the Chimú civilization site had remained hidden for more than six centuries.

The Discovery

An overhead perspective of the subterranean complex reveals a structured system of walls, stairways, and three distinct chambers. The distribution of the site includes:

  • A central room containing one individual surrounded by items made from metal and shell.
  • A second room filled with weaponry and metal emblems.
  • A third room holding 20 bodies.

What Scientists Found

Researchers recovered a variety of historic grave goods, including ceramic vessels with sculpted human figures, decorated jugs, metal headdresses, necklaces, earrings, carved wooden boxes, and ceremonial items crafted from quartz and seashells. Red pigment was also found on the heads of several individuals, a mark traditionally reserved for high-ranking members of Andean societies.

One skeleton attracted special attention because it was laid out fully extended. Traditional Chimú practices involved placing the deceased in seated or bent postures. This departure from normal customs has led experts to hypothesize that the individual may have been part of a human sacrifice or held a vastly different social standing than the others buried nearby.

Why It Matters

Most previous knowledge regarding elite Chimú tombs came from sites that were looted and damaged starting in colonial times. Archaeologist Jorge Meneses explained that this site gives scientists a rare opportunity to examine artifacts and human remains exactly where they were placed centuries ago. Because coastal humidity and time have left the remains extremely fragile, researchers are capturing hundreds of photographs to construct a detailed 3D model before carefully removing items by hand. The Ministry of Culture plans to publish its complete findings in an upcoming book.
