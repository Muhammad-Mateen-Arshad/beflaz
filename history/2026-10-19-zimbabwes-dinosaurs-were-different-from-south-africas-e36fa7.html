Zimbabwe’s dinosaurs were different from South Africa’s

Published on October 19, 2026

Africa is famous for its rich fossil history, ranging from early human ancestors to diverse dinosaur species. While the continent's first scientific dinosaur discoveries were recorded in South Africa in the 1850s, Zimbabwe has contributed to this fossil record since 1916. Recent fieldwork in Zimbabwe is reshaping what researchers know about the distribution of ancient life on the continent.

The Discovery

In the 1970s, paleontologists highlighted abundant fossils near Bulawayo and the lower Zambezi Valley. Research later slowed until a local safari camp manager spotted productive fossil beds along the shoreline of Lake Kariba. To reach these remote locations in Matusadona National Park, an international research team from South Africa, Zimbabwe, and the United Kingdom conducted expeditions in 2017 and 2018 using a houseboat as a floating laboratory. They collected fossils from red mudstones and sandstones deposited by ancient river systems roughly 210 million years ago during the Late Triassic period.

What Scientists Found

Among the key discoveries is Musango matusadonaensis, a sauropodomorph dinosaur found near the Nyamasango River. Anatomical study shows it moved on two legs, measured about 4.5 meters (15 feet) in length, and weighed 220 kilograms (485 pounds). Bone growth marks indicate it was a subadult when it died. Because its skull was not preserved, its exact diet is unknown, though its close relatives were mostly plant-eaters.

The expeditions and ongoing research revealed several other notable ancient animals:

  • Musankwa, another distinct sauropodomorph described from the same rocks in 2024.
  • Fossil lungfish and aquatic reptiles called phytosaurs, which are rarely found or missing entirely in South African sites.

Why It Matters

Researchers previously assumed Zimbabwean dinosaurs were simply identical to species known from South Africa. However, the recent finds demonstrate that Zimbabwe was home to a distinct group of prehistoric animals. During the Late Triassic, Zimbabwe was situated farther north and closer to the equator than South Africa, creating climatic variations that likely influenced species habitats. Re-examinations of older fossil collections in Bulawayo support this pattern, establishing Zimbabwe's growing role in explaining early dinosaur evolution.
