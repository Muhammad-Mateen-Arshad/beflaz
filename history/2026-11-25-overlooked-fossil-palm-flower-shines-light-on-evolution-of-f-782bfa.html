Overlooked Fossil Palm Flower Shines Light on Evolution of Flowering Plants

Published on November 25, 2026

The Discovery

A fossil flower no larger than a grain of rice lay unnoticed inside a museum specimen for 130 years. Pioneering paleobotanist Leo Lesquereux originally gathered the sample between the 1870s and 1890s from the Dakota Formation in Midwestern North America. It was later stored at the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History in Washington, D.C. The ancient bloom came to light when paleobotanist Steven Manchester borrowed the rock to examine a surface fossil using a micro-CT scanner. Utilizing X-rays to inspect the interior structure, he unexpectedly identified a hidden floral shape completely enclosed within the stone.

What Scientists Found

The fossilized species, named Tomlinsoniella pedicellata, dates back around 100 million years to the mid-Cretaceous, when present-day Kansas lay along the shore of a vast interior seaway. The flower was preserved as a negative cavity formed after clay solidified around it and the plant decayed. Digital 3D reconstructions revealed a structural arrangement closely resembling modern fan palm blossoms. The anatomy displays classic three-fold symmetry divided into five concentric whorls:

  • Three sepals
  • Three petals
  • Two sets of three stamens
  • Three separate carpels

Why It Matters

Described in the American Journal of Botany, the find is the oldest discovered palm flower and the earliest fossil exhibiting the basic structural blueprint of monocots. Monocots account for roughly 20 percent of all known flowering plants, including orchids, grasses, and palms. Although genetic evidence points to an Early Cretaceous origin for monocots, physical fossils from before the late Cretaceous are extremely rare. Identifying this bloom extends the known geological record of the fan palm subfamily by nearly 20 million years, offering valuable physical evidence of how flowering plants evolved during the dinosaur era while demonstrating the power of modern X-ray imaging on historical museum collections.
