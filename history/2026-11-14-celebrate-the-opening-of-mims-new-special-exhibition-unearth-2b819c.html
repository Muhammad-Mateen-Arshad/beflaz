Celebrate the Opening of MIM’s New Special Exhibition, Unearthed Musical Masterpieces from the Southern Kingdoms of Ancient China

Published on November 14, 2026

The Discovery

Archaeologists working in China's Hubei Province have unearthed extraordinary relics revealing the rich musical culture of the ancient world. Most of these items were recovered from archaeological dig sites that are more than two thousand years old. The specific names of the excavation sites are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Through a joint effort by the Musical Instrument Museum and the Hubei Provincial Museum, organized under the guidance of the National Cultural Heritage Administration of China, these historical items are brought together for a special exhibition titled Unearthed Musical Masterpieces from the Southern Kingdoms of Ancient China.

What Scientists Found

The collection brings together more than 70 ancient musical instruments and related objects that document the evolution of music in early Chinese civilization. Key findings include:

  • More than 40 ancient Chinese artifacts displayed in the United States for the first time.
  • Ten Grade One National Cultural Relics, representing the highest designation for national treasures in China.
  • Instruments reflecting ancient sound traditions, including ancient Chinese bianzhong bell sets, qin and guzheng zithers, and xiao and dizi flutes.

Why It Matters

These ancient instruments offer a rare glimpse into how early societies crafted music and expressed cultural heritage. Recognized as a 2026 "Project for the Bridge of Civilization: Premier Overseas Exhibitions of Chinese Cultural Relics," the collection serves as a major connection to the ancient world. Visitors learn about the archaeological significance of these discoveries through curator presentations by Hsu, design insights from senior exhibit designer Shelby Sickler, and live performances showcasing ancient Chinese instruments.
