67-Million-Year-Old T. Rex to Make UK Debut in Portsmouth

Published on November 20, 2026

In February 2027, a 67-million-year-old Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton named Trinity will make her UK public debut at the Portsmouth Historic Dockyard. Standing 4 metres tall and stretching 11.6 metres in length, Trinity will be housed inside Grade I-listed Boathouse 5 following a £1.5 million venue transformation. The interactive exhibition is the result of a partnership between the Portsmouth Historic Quarter, The Phoebus Foundation, and the University of Portsmouth.

The Discovery

Trinity features 293 genuine fossil bones. Rather than coming from a single creature, her skeleton combines remains from three Tyrannosaurus rex specimens discovered in the United States between 1995 and 2013. These specimens shared similar sizes, qualities, and geological origins. Utilizing multiple individuals is common practice when mounting dinosaur skeletons because complete single-animal discoveries are extremely rare. Her name reflects these three individual creatures.

What Scientists Found

In addition to offering a public spectacle, Trinity is the subject of active palaeontological research. A team based at the University of Portsmouth, led by National Geographic Explorer Dr Nizar Ibrahim alongside PhD students Stella Angelika Ludwig and Josie Warner, is examining the skeleton. Their work focuses on studying Trinity's anatomy, estimating her age, and analyzing bone marks that reveal signs of injuries and prehistoric diseases.

The upcoming exhibition highlights several key elements:

  • Exhibiting authentic fossil remains combined with modern interactive interpretation.
  • Investigating real-life animal health, injuries, and growth patterns rather than fictional portrayals.
  • Connecting the display to the Solent region and the Isle of Wight, an area famous for dinosaur fossil discoveries.

Why It Matters

Major dinosaur fossils are increasingly bought by private collectors for tens of millions of dollars—such as a Nanotyrannus skeleton that recently sold in New York for $47.3 million—raising concerns that valuable specimens will be hidden away from researchers. Trinity was purchased at auction in 2023 for over $6 million by The Phoebus Foundation, an art foundation based in Antwerp, Belgium. Instead of keeping the fossil in a private vault, the foundation previously displayed Trinity in Switzerland and Denmark before arranging her UK arrival, ensuring she remains accessible for ongoing scientific research and public viewing.
