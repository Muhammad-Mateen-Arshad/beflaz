Mystery Skeleton Buried in Sandstone Revealed to Be Tiny Jurassic "Dragon" Reptile Sporting Horns

Published on November 21, 2026

In 2022, paleontologists from the Aathal Dinosaur Museum in Switzerland uncovered a sandstone slab at Wyoming's Meilyn Quarry containing the partial remains of a tiny, unidentified creature. Because the skeleton was largely encased within the rock, researchers initially thought it might be an ancient snake.

The Discovery

To examine the specimen without destroying the stone, the team turned to Pavel Trtik, a materials scientist at the Paul Scherrer Institute. Trtik utilized a specialized neutron imaging technique combined with X-rays. Because neutrons interact with the hydrogen present in bone minerals, taking tens of thousands of scan images allowed scientists to clearly separate the skeletal structure from the surrounding sandstone.

What Scientists Found

The high-resolution scans revealed pelvic bones, portions of a hind leg, and a small skull featuring dragon-like horns. Classified as an extinct choristodere reptile named Meilynodraco amonamarthi, the creature measured about a foot and a half long and lived approximately 156 million years ago during the Late Jurassic. The fossil's head was separated from its torso, suggesting it was killed by a larger predator before being buried in fine river sand.

The scientific name translates to "Dragon of Meilyn" and pays homage to the heavy metal band Amon Amarth, which also references the term for Mount Doom in J.R.R. Tolkien's writings.

Why It Matters

Published in Royal Society Open Science, the research offers fresh insights into reptile evolution. Study author Torsten Scheyer of the University of Zurich explained that the specimen's tiny ear bones closely match those seen in living reptiles, indicating that choristoderes may be more closely related to modern reptiles than scientists previously believed.
