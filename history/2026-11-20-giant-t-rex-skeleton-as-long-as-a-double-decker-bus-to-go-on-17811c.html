Giant T. rex skeleton as long as a double-decker bus to go on display in UK for first time

Published on November 20, 2026

The Discovery

For the first time, a massive Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton is set to go on public view in Britain. Known as Trinity, the composite skeleton is constructed from 293 fossilized bones. These remains belong to three individual T. rex specimens that were excavated in the United States between 1995 and 2013. The fossils themselves date back 67 million years to the era of the dinosaurs.

In 2023, Trinity was acquired at auction for more than $6 million (around £4.5 million) by The Phoebus Foundation, an art organisation located in Antwerp, Belgium. Rather than keeping the rare fossils hidden in a private collection, the foundation has enabled the skeleton to be shared with both researchers and the general public.

What Scientists Found

Standing 4.57 metres tall and stretching 11.6 metres long, Trinity is roughly the length of a double-decker bus. The assembly of genuine fossil material gives scientists a valuable opportunity to learn more about the biology of these prehistoric creatures. Researchers plan to examine the bones to study several aspects of ancient life, including:

  • How T. rex specimens grew and moved.
  • Evidence of physical injuries and diseases preserved within the bone structure.
  • Details regarding the animal's survival and day-to-day existence beyond its reputation as a giant predator.

Experts involved with the project, including representatives from the Dinosaur Museum Bavaria and the University of Portsmouth, note that the physical remains offer ongoing opportunities for scientific investigation while the exhibit is taking place.

Why It Matters

The upcoming exhibition places a strong emphasis on public access and scientific authenticity. Trinity will be housed inside Boathouse 5, a Grade II-listed building located at the Portsmouth Historic Dockyard. Portsmouth was chosen in part due to its existing academic ties to dinosaur research conducted on the nearby Isle of Wight.

The University of Portsmouth is working to connect local schools with the display, alongside plans to host educational public talks and events featuring scientific experts. The Trinity exhibition is scheduled to open to visitors on February 12, 2027, with tickets going on sale on November 20.
