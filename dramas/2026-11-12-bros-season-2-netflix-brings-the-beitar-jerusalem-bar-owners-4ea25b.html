Bros Season 2 — Netflix brings the Beitar Jerusalem bar owners back from Krakow

Published on November 12, 2026

About the series

Bros is a Hebrew-language dramedy on Netflix created, written, and directed by Hanan Savyon and Guy Amir, who also star as lead characters Pini and Nisso. The pair portray childhood friends who co-own a neighbourhood bar in Jerusalem and share a passion for the Beitar Jerusalem football club. Prior to this series, Savyon and Amir created the Israeli show Asfur and wrote the 2017 feature film Maktub.

The first season tracked the friends as they faced financial strain at the bar, a health scare for Nisso, and a trip to Poland for a 2008 Champions League qualifying match against Wisła Kraków. Premiering as Netflix's first original Hebrew-language series, season one arrived globally on April 18, 2024, following initial release delays caused by the outbreak of war.

What's new

Netflix is bringing the show back for a second season, which will debut worldwide on Thursday, November 12, 2026. Ahead of its streaming release, the first two episodes will have a world premiere at the 25th South Cinema Festival in Sderot, which runs from November 1 to 5, featuring Savyon and Amir in attendance.

In addition to Savyon and Amir, several season-one cast members are returning:

  • Efrat Boimold
  • Yael Sztulman
  • Yaniv Swissa
  • Shir Abramov
  • Shlomi Avraham

The new season also adds cast members Ohad Knoller, Yoram Toledano, Maor Schwitzer, and Yuval Gonen. Details regarding their character roles are Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What to expect

Specific storylines for the new episodes are Not specified in the source; please check the official notice. However, while the first season built its story around a countdown to a single match in Krakow, season two starts without a specific fixture on the horizon. The upcoming season shifts focus to how Pini and Nisso maintain their Jerusalem bar and navigate their lifelong bond during ordinary weeknights, forcing them to address personal issues without a game to delay their conversations.
