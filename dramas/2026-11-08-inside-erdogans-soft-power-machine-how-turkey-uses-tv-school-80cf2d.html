Inside Erdogan’s soft power machine: How Turkey uses TV, schools and religion to spread its influence

Published on November 08, 2026

Turkey's global television sector continues to expand rapidly as part of a broader push to project cultural and ideological influence across international markets, according to findings highlighted in a 2026 report by the European Commission.

What's new

In February 2026, Culture and Tourism Minister Mehmet Nuri Ersoy announced financial incentives for television series sold abroad. Shows can receive state subsidies of up to $100,000 per episode, provided they fulfill specific government criteria:

  • Broadcast in a minimum of 10 countries across at least three continents.
  • Actively help promote Turkey and spread the Turkish language.
  • Incorporate filming at tourist destinations selected by the state.

Described by the minister as a massive global Turkish-language lesson, these financial incentives aim to strengthen productions exported to key overseas targets, including Africa, Asia, South America, and Europe.

About the series

The scale of Turkish screen entertainment has grown dramatically over two decades. Overseas revenue stood at approximately $10 million annually in the early 2000s, but international export earnings now exceed $1 billion. Ankara claims to house one of the world's three largest television industries, with programming reaching over one billion viewers in 170 countries. Global interest in Turkish content increased by 184% between 2020 and 2023.

The television industry operates along two parallel tracks:

  • Commercial melodramas: Produced by private studios and distributed across Latin America, Eastern Europe, the Balkans, and Africa to build viewer familiarity with Turkish homes, language, and Istanbul.
  • Historical epics: Produced by public broadcaster TRT, titles such as Dirilis: Ertugrul, Kurulus: Osman, and Payitaht: Abdulhamid weave state-promoted historical narratives into major television dramas. Dirilis: Ertugrul was sold to 71 countries, translated into 25 languages, and viewed by over 130 million people on Pakistan's state television during Ramadan in 2020.

What to expect

International audiences can expect continued backing for historical epics that present Turkey's reshaped historical narratives abroad alongside commercial dramas. Specific upcoming show lineups, release schedules, and cast details are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
