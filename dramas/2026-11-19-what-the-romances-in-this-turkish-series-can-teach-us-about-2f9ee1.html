What the romances in this Turkish series can teach us about homeland and patriotism

Published on November 19, 2026

About the series

The historical drama "Wounded Love" (originally titled "Vatanım Sensin" in Turkish) explores romance, political tension, and patriotism during the final years of the Ottoman Empire and the Turkish War of Independence. Holding an 8.3/10 IMDb rating, the show blends dramatized storytelling with real historical events. Its central protagonist, Cevdet, is directly inspired by the real-life Turkish spy Mustafa Mumin Aksoy.

What to expect

The storyline follows key developments in modern Turkish history, spanning from the Balkan Wars in 1912 to the 1922 Liberation of Izmir. Key historical milestones featured in the narrative include the Greek landing in Izmir, the establishment of the Grand National Assembly of Turkey, and the signing of the Treaty of Sevres.

The main plot elements follow a family torn apart by war:

  • Cevdet and Azize: After battlefield betrayal separates Ottoman officer Cevdet from his family in Thessaloniki, his wife Azize relocates their children to Izmir with help from Cevdet's friend, Tevfik. In 1919, Cevdet suddenly resurfaces as a Greek officer on the day Azize is set to wed Tevfik.
  • The Next Generation: Eldest daughter Yildiz seeks a glamorous lifestyle, while younger daughter Hilal writes for an underground nationalist newspaper resisting the Greek occupation. Their adopted brother, Ali Kemal, contends with issues regarding his personal identity and romantic life.

Romance and homeland

Political conflicts constantly test the show's personal relationships. A notable pairing is between Greek lieutenant Leon and Turkish resistance writer Hilal, whose connection challenges their political allegiances and view of the enemy. At the same time, Cevdet and Azize struggle to balance their devotion to family with their duties to their country. The original Turkish title translates directly to "You are my homeland," highlighting how romantic bonds redefine the concept of national identity.
