Netflix users create petition to remove 'blasphemous' series with 5 million streams

Published on November 20, 2026

What's new

A psychological teen drama titled The Body debuted on Netflix on November 11 and quickly generated massive viewership, drawing 5.1 million views in its first week. Landing as the No. 3 TV title on the platform for the week of November 9 through 16, the series earned a 97% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. However, its release has been accompanied by intense backlash from critics seeking to have the title removed.

About the series

Created by Quinn Shephard, the eight-episode coming-of-age story takes place around Halloween in 2004. The plot revolves around a group of badly behaved Catholic schoolgirls whose dance-team initiation goes wrong. Shortly after, the girls experience prophetic visions that spark mass hysteria across their town. The creator described the project as a raunchy teen psychodrama mixed with Catholic horror, aiming to put a playful spin on the nightmares of teenage girlhood.

Petition details and controversy

Prior to the premiere, a campaign was launched on CitizenGo on November 5, addressed to Netflix Chief Content Officer Peter Rice. The petition has gathered over 128,000 signatures, calling on the streamer to cancel the show. Opponents labeled the series blasphemous and anti-Christian, raising concerns over its depictions of dark initiation rituals and the Devil. The petition also cited past Netflix releases, such as Cuties and The First Temptation of Christ, as part of a recurring pattern. Despite the protest, the series launched on schedule and has generally received positive feedback from viewers and critics.

What to expect

Viewers can expect a gritty, campy mystery exploring teenage themes and supernatural visions. Regarding cast details or plans for future seasons, not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
