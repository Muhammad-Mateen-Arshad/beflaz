About the series

The Turkish historical drama "The Magnificent Century" centers on the life of Suleiman the Magnificent, the great-grandson of Mehmed II who famously conquered Constantinople, alongside his favored concubine, Hürrem. Originally a Ukrainian Christian enslaved after Tatars destroyed her village, Hürrem enters the Imperial Harem determined to win the ruler's favor. Her presence sparks immediate rivalry with Mahidevran, the mother of an Ottoman prince, igniting intense conflict inside the palace walls. Though the narrative includes fictionalized elements, the show is grounded in true historical events.

What to expect

Fans of the series can explore rich historical contexts tied directly to the palace setting featured in the narrative:

  • Topkapi Palace and the Harem: The central residence for Ottoman rulers and their families, housing notable locations like the Imperial Hall used for official rituals and ceremonies.
  • Key Historical Figures: The palace history extends to Suleiman's grandson Murad III, noted for having between 50 and 100 children, as well as Ahmed I.
  • The Kafes System: Introduced by Ahmed I to replace the legal practice of fratricide, this system kept royal brothers under luxury house arrest inside the harem to head off succession disputes.
  • Hagia Irene: A Byzantine-built Christian church located on the palace grounds that functioned uniquely as an armory during Ottoman rule.
