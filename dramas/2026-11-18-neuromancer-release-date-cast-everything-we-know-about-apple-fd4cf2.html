'Neuromancer:' Release date, cast & everything we know about Apple TV's new cyberpunk series

Published on November 18, 2026

As cyberpunk storytelling continues to captivate television audiences, major streaming platforms are expanding their sci-fi lineups. Among the upcoming projects hitting Apple TV+ is "Neuromancer," an adaptation of William Gibson's influential 1984 novel. Filmed across global locations including Istanbul, Tokyo, Los Angeles, Canada, and London, the series is scheduled to launch on January 22, 2027.

What's new

Production for the project officially began on July 1, 2025. Promotional teasers began debuting shortly after, starting with a short video featuring the iconic hacker lounge Bar Chatsubo in Chiba City. A second teaser in July 2026 highlighted the book's classic opening sentence on a retro computer screen. By July 25, 2026, Apple TV+ released a full teaser revealing the show's futuristic aesthetic alongside main cast members Callum Turner, Brianna Middleton, and Mark Strong. Further character details or additional cast members: Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

About the series

The 10-episode prestige drama was co-created by showrunner Graham Roland and pilot director J.D. Dillard. The show is produced through a collaboration involving Skydance Television, Anonymous Content, and Drake's production company, DreamCrew Entertainment. The core narrative focuses on Case, a brilliant yet neurologically damaged hacker who gets pulled into high-stakes corporate espionage alongside Molly, a cyber-enhanced mercenary known as a "razor-girl."

What to expect

The plot follows Case, Molly, and their client Armitage—a former black ops operative with a dark history—as they attempt a dangerous heist. Their primary objective involves infiltrating guarded computer systems to access Wintermute, a next-generation super AI. Their mission eventually leads them into contact with Wintermute's AI sibling, Neuromancer. The series will join Apple TV's existing slate of science fiction shows, which includes titles like "Foundation," "Silo," "Dark Matter," and "Invasion."
