Where the Mask Ends premieres on Netflix and Youku with Tian Xiwei as both heiresses

Published on October 20, 2026

What's new

The period drama Where the Mask Ends, known in China as Jia Jin Chai, has launched across global platforms. Premiering on Friday, October 16, the series airs nightly on CCTV-8 in China while streaming locally on Youku and internationally on Netflix. The production, created by Youku and Alibaba Pictures, runs for 30 episodes on streaming platforms, whereas the CCTV-8 television broadcast consists of 33 shorter episodes.

About the series

Directed by Yang Long and scripted by Story of Yanxi Palace writer Zhou Mo, the show loosely adapts a 2021 web novel by Xiao Jiaren. The story focuses on a substitute marriage driven by a true-and-false heiress plot:

  • Tian Xiwei plays a dual role as A Chou, a noble-born survivor who collects night soil to pay her adoptive mother's medical expenses, and Jun Dai (Wei Rao), the spoiled impostor who grew up in her place.
  • Yan An portrays Lu Zhuo, an unconscious heir from a duke's family whose relatives arrange a wedding to bring him good luck.

To avoid the arrangement, the impostor forces A Chou to step up to the altar. When Lu Zhuo awakens on the wedding night, he immediately suspects his new bride, setting off a dangerous series of household rivalries, personal quests, and a slow effort to earn his trust.

What to expect

The series achieved rapid chart success upon release. It ranked first on the Yunhe Data long-form drama chart on October 19, passed 10,000 on Youku's popularity index, and trended on Netflix, following more than 1.4 million pre-launch reservations across platforms.

Critical and viewer reactions have been mixed. While CCTV entertainment coverage praised Tian Xiwei's vocal performance in separating the humble heroine from the cruel heiress, some audience members reported finding the two identical characters difficult to tell apart. Readers of the original web novel have also criticized the show for replacing the book's light contract-marriage setup with intense household conflict, though other viewers defend the addition of immediate drama for television audiences.
