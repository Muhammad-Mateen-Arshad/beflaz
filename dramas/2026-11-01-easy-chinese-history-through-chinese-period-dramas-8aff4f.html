Easy Chinese History Through Chinese Period Dramas

Published on November 01, 2026

What's new

Historical period productions continue to captivate viewers worldwide, even as traditional markets like Western Europe and Japan produce fewer long-running series in the genre. Beyond British period pieces, both Turkey and China have successfully developed historical dramas into established television genres with global reach.

About the series

While Turkey actively exports historical television overseas, its presence in certain Asian markets remains selective. In Japan, the hit series Magnificent Century became famous, but other Turkish period dramas have struggled to find a wide audience. This dynamic stems partly from regional cultural gaps, as Japanese viewers are generally less familiar with Turkish historical settings and Islamic cultural values.

By contrast, Chinese period dramas have secured a dedicated audience through streaming services like U-NEXT and WOWOW, as well as broadcasts such as Nirvana in Fire 2 on BS Nippon TV. These productions benefit from cultural touchstones—including Confucianism, Buddhism, and Taoism—that feel more familiar to East Asian audiences, alongside expansive timelines and vast geographic settings.

What to expect

Viewers exploring period television across different regions will notice distinct creative choices:

  • Turkish Period Dramas: Recognized as a strong, export-heavy genre internationally, though regional adoption varies based on cultural familiarity.
  • Chinese Period Series: Characterized by imaginative storytelling, flexible historical interpretations, fictionalized dynasties, and diverse cultural settings.
  • Japanese Taiga Dramas: Typically confined to specific historical eras such as the Sengoku period, Genpei War, Taiheiki, or the end of the Edo period. Productions like Toyotomi Brothers occasionally draw debate for introducing modern themes or altering historical facts.
