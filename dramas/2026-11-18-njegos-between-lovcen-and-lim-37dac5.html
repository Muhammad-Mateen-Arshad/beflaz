Njegoš between Lovćen and Lim

Published on November 18, 2026

What's new

On the occasion of Njegoš Day, historical and literary discussions focus on how the legacy of Petar II Petrović Njegoš is understood today. The ongoing debate centers on literary value, Bosniak historical memory, and how modern Montenegro navigates its cultural and religious differences.

The source material focuses on Petar II Petrović Njegoš, a 19th-century poet, bishop, ruler, diplomat, and thinker who created prominent literary works, including:

  • Gorski vijenac
  • Luča mikrokozma
  • Šćepan Mali

His writings reflect a period marked by Ottoman-Montenegrin conflicts, tribal divisions, romantic national movements, and philosophical reflections on existence, freedom, good, and evil.

What to expect

Critical readings of these classic works address complex issues of identity and religious representation. In 19th-century Balkan literature, terms referencing the Islamic world often encompassed Slavic Muslims, affecting historical memory for Bosniaks in areas such as Pljevlja, Bijelo Polje, Rožaje, Plav, Gusinje, and Berane. Scholars including Rašid Durić, who analyzed Turkisms in Gorski vijenac, and Fadil Hadžiabdić have examined these themes. The text highlights the importance of separating historical events from poetic interpretations and political usage.
