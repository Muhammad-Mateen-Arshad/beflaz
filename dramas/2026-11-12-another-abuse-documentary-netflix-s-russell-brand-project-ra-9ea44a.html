'Another Abuse Documentary?': Netflix's Russell Brand Project Raises Questions About True-Crime Profits

Published on November 12, 2026

What's new

Netflix has sparked public debate following its announcement of the upcoming documentary Russell Brand: Open Secret. The project comes shortly after comedian Russell Brand received a prison sentence of 10 years and six months on 9 November, handed down by Judge Joel Bennathan. On 5 November, a jury convicted Brand of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women, while acquitting him of two rape charges and one count of attempted indecent assault. The conviction followed a Metropolitan Police investigation triggered by 2023 reporting from The Times, The Sunday Times, and Channel 4.

Critics and social media users have raised concerns over streaming services profiting from sensitive abuse cases so quickly after legal verdicts. Similar ethical questions previously arose regarding Netflix's 2022 production Monster: The Jeffrey Dahmer Story, which faced criticism from victims' relatives, including Rita Isbell and Eric Perry, for depicting traumatic events without consultation.

About the series

The 84-minute documentary is produced by 72 Films, the company behind Jimmy Savile: A British Horror Story, and is directed by Olivia Isaacs. It is scheduled for release on 13 November. Specific earnings or payments to project participants are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

The production features the following elements:

  • An examination of Brand's transition from comedian and Hollywood actor to political commentator.
  • Archival material and interviews analyzing his career and allegations.
  • Contributions from journalists who investigated the misconduct claims.

What to expect

The documentary arrives amidst broader criticism of Netflix's library management, including its decision to retain Brand's 2018 stand-up special, Re:Birth, while removing it from search results and recommendation systems. Viewers can expect Russell Brand: Open Secret to focus on the investigative findings and Brand's career trajectory, while continuing to highlight ongoing discussions surrounding commercial incentives in true-crime programming.
