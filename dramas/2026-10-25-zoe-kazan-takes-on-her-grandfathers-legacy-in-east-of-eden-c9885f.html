Zoe Kazan takes on her grandfather’s legacy in ‘East of Eden’

Published on October 25, 2026

What's new

Creator, writer, and showrunner Zoe Kazan has brought a new screen adaptation of John Steinbeck's 1952 novel East of Eden to Netflix. Premiering on October 1, the seven-episode miniseries represents a major project for Kazan, who also serves as an executive producer. The release arrives 70 years after her grandfather, director Elia Kazan, made history by directing the original 1955 film version.

About the series

The origins of this adaptation trace back to 2019, when a production company obtained the screen rights to Steinbeck's book with a plan to develop a project centered on the character Cathy Ames. Kazan, who had read the novel at age 15 and long held an interest in the work, stepped in to write and lead the project. Unlike the 1955 feature film—which focused strictly on the final third of the book and highlighted the story of Cal Trask—this streaming miniseries covers narrative elements her grandfather left unexplored.

Key highlights of the project include:

  • A seven-episode streaming structure created specifically for contemporary audiences.
  • A narrative focus that shifts toward the novel's female perspective.
  • A focus on the broader philosophical themes and plot balances present in Steinbeck's writing.

What to expect

The series features Florence Pugh in the central role of the complex antiheroine Cathy Ames. Performers Christopher Abbott and Mike Faist complete the core trio alongside Pugh. Rather than concentrating predominantly on masculine themes, Kazan's version offers a deeply considered reimagining that brings female representation to the forefront of the narrative.
