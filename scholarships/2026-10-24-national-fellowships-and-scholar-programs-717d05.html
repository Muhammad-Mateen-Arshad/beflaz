National Fellowships and Scholar Programs

Published on October 24, 2026

The National Fellowships and Scholar Programs provides guidance to assist students applying for competitive awards. Through interactive workshops and information sessions, candidates receive help preparing their application materials. Workshops concentrate on specific awards, whereas information sessions present broader fellowship themes in both virtual and in-person formats.

Scholarship Overview

The program supports candidates interested in several key opportunities, including:

  • Boren Scholarship: Supports language study abroad focused on less-commonly taught languages for up to one year.
  • DAAD RISE Summer Internship Program: Offers application review sessions with advisors and former recipients.
  • Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's: Allows students to complete a master's degree across multiple European countries.
  • Critical Language Scholarship (CLS): Features intensive summer study abroad focused on critical-need languages.
  • PPIA Junior Summer Institute: Provides application guidance and review sessions with fellowship advisors.

Who Can Apply

Undergraduate and graduate students may apply for the Boren Scholarship. Other sessions are tailored to applicants seeking positions in the DAAD RISE, Erasmus Mundus, CLS, or PPIA programs. Detailed eligibility criteria, such as minimum GPA or citizenship status, are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Benefits

Selected scholars benefit from specialized academic and language opportunities. Boren provides funding for abroad study, while Erasmus Mundus enables students to study in two or more European nations. CLS delivers intensive summer language training. Additionally, applicants gain direct feedback on their materials from national fellowship teams, advisors, selection panelists, and past recipients. Specific funding amounts and stipend figures are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

How to Apply

Interested applicants can refine their application materials by attending scheduled review events and information sessions. These gatherings take place in person at locations such as Russell House, Thomas Cooper Library, and DeSaussure, or virtually via Zoom (registration required).
