University of Liverpool PhD Scholarship in Chemistry 2026

Published on November 06, 2026

The University of Liverpool PhD Scholarship in Chemistry 2026 provides 3.5 years of funding for a doctoral research project focused on sustainable electrochemical ammonia production. This award is open to qualified domestic and international students specializing in chemistry, physics, materials science, or related disciplines. The application deadline is not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Scholarship Overview

Offered through the Department of Chemistry at the University of Liverpool, the research project focuses on developing green, decentralised ammonia through lithium-mediated nitrogen reduction and other metal-mediated reactions. Candidates will investigate electrolyte formulations, electrode interfaces, and reaction mechanisms while gaining practical training in electrochemistry and advanced spectroscopic techniques. The project is supervised by Dr. Alex Neale under reference number CCPR182.

Benefits

  • An annual maintenance stipend of £21,805 for the 2026–27 academic year (following the standard UKRI rate) provided for 3.5 years.
  • Tuition fee coverage at the UK rate of £5,238 per year for 2026–27 (whether higher international fees are covered is not specified in the source; please check the official notice).
  • Access to a Research Training Support Grant to assist with laboratory consumables, research expenses, and conference attendance.

Who Can Apply

Applicants must meet the following eligibility requirements:

  • Hold or expect to obtain a Master's degree with at least a UK 2:1 classification (or equivalent) in Chemistry, Physics, Materials Science, or a related field. Exceptional candidates holding a First Class Bachelor's degree or relevant research experience may also be considered.
  • Prior experience in electrochemistry and analytical chemistry is advantageous, though not mandatory.
  • International candidates must fulfill the university's English-language requirements, such as an IELTS Academic overall score of 6.5 (minimum 5.5 per component) or a PTE Academic overall score of 61 (minimum 59 per component).

How to Apply

Interested applicants should review the official project criteria and contact the project supervisor, Dr. Alex Neale, to discuss the research opportunity. Candidates must prepare their CV, academic transcripts, degree certificates, personal statement, passport details (if applicable), and contact details for two referees. Complete applications must be submitted online through the university's postgraduate portal for a PhD in Chemistry, including the full project title and reference code CCPR182.
