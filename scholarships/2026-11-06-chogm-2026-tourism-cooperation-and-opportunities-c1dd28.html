CHOGM 2026 Tourism Cooperation and Opportunities

Published on November 06, 2026

The CHOGM 2026 Tourism Cooperation and Opportunities initiative refers to economic and trade partnerships discussed at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting held from November 1–4, 2026, in Antigua and Barbuda. The event is aimed at leaders and delegates across 56 Commonwealth member countries representing 2.7 billion people to foster sustainable tourism, youth skills, and investment. Application deadlines are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Scholarship Overview

Hosted in St John’s by Prime Minister Gaston Browne, the summit brought together over 3,000 delegates under the theme "Accelerating Partnerships and Investment for a Prosperous Commonwealth." The Commonwealth Secretariat announced over US$500 million in commitments for projects involving youth skills, renewable energy, trade, and economic development. While these commitments do not form a dedicated tourism fund, the discussions aimed to strengthen economic connections between African nations and Caribbean island states through joint training, investment, and cultural exchanges.

Who Can Apply

The initiatives involve 56 Commonwealth member nations across Africa, the Caribbean, and Pacific island states. Specific applicant qualifications or individual eligibility criteria are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Benefits

Key economic commitments and opportunities highlighted include:

  • More than US$500 million allocated toward renewable energy, youth skills, trade, and broader economic development.
  • Potential for joint hospitality investment, professional training, cultural exchange, and business connections.
  • Historical capacity-building programs, including sustainable tourism development training and sport tourism conferences.

How to Apply

Application procedures and submission steps are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. The source notes that practical barriers to inter-regional travel and cooperation include visa requirements, high travel costs, and limited direct flights.
