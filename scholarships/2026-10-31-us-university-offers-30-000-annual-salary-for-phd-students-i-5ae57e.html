US University Offers $30,000 Annual Salary for PhD Students in Aquaculture Research

Published on October 31, 2026

Scholarship Overview

Auburn University in Alabama, United States, is offering a four-year funded PhD research position focused on the molecular reproductive physiology of catfish. Based in the university's School of Fisheries, Aquaculture and Aquatic Sciences (SFAAS), the doctoral student will join Dr. Ian A. E. Butts’ Reproductive Physiology Lab. The research project aims to improve reproductive performance and offspring production in hybrid catfish. Primary research activities will occur at the E.W. Shell Fisheries Center in Auburn, along with collaborative work at partner facilities across the Midwestern United States. The selected applicant is expected to start coursework and research in May 2027.

Benefits

The position offers four years of financial support, which includes:

  • An annual salary of $30,000 ($120,000 total over the four-year term).
  • Fringe benefits provided by the university.
  • A typical tuition fee waiver (candidates should confirm exact tuition coverage details before accepting).

Who Can Apply

Candidates must fulfill the following qualification criteria:

  • Hold a master’s degree in biology, aquaculture, fisheries, or a closely related field, and meet Auburn University doctoral admission standards.
  • Demonstrate prior laboratory experience in molecular biology, physiology, or both.
  • Show strong abilities in communication, teamwork, and problem-solving.
  • Have access to a vehicle and the capacity to obtain a US driver’s licence.
  • Practical background in aquatic organism care, fish husbandry, or bioinformatics is beneficial but not strictly compulsory.

Note: External listings mention a 3.0 GPA and specific English language scores (IELTS 6.5, TOEFL iBT 79, Duolingo 115), but these are not in the primary recruitment notice. Application fees are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

How to Apply

Applicants must apply directly to Dr. Ian A. E. Butts via email. The application package must be compiled into a single PDF containing:

  • A cover letter explaining academic background, research experience, specific interest in molecular reproductive physiology, and career goals.
  • A curriculum vitae including contact details for three referees.
  • Academic transcripts.
  • Relevant GRE information.

Applications close on February 1, 2027, at 11:59 pm Central Standard Time (CST).
