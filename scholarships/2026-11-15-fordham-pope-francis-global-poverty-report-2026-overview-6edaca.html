Fordham Pope Francis Global Poverty Report 2026 Overview

Published on November 15, 2026

The Fordham Pope Francis Global Poverty Report 2026 provides an updated analysis of international deprivation metrics and development assistance. This annual research document evaluates access to fundamental needs across global populations. Scholarship Overview

Published by Fordham University and edited by economics professor Henry Schwalbenberg, the report measures access to seven basic needs: water, food, housing, employment, education, gender equity, and religious freedom. The global poverty gap rose to 25.8% in 2025, up from 25.1% in the prior period, signaling a potential slowdown in post-pandemic recovery.

Benefits and Findings

The report highlights essential global data points regarding basic living standards and international aid assistance:

  • Drinking Water: Approximately 696 million people (8.5% globally) lacked basic access to drinking water in 2024.
  • Food Security: Around 688 million people were undernourished in 2023, with the highest rates reported in Haiti and Somalia.
  • Housing: Roughly 1.3 billion people (16.1% globally) resided in inadequate housing conditions in 2024.
  • Employment: About 711 million workers (18.8% of the global labor force) were unemployed or earned under $4.20 daily in 2025.
  • Education: In 2024, an estimated 753 million adults lacked basic literacy skills.
  • Gender Equity: About 57.1% of women and girls lived in countries with severe gender gaps in 2025.
  • Development Aid: OECD nations contributed $174.3 billion in official development assistance in 2025, led by Germany ($29.1 billion) and the United States ($29 billion).
