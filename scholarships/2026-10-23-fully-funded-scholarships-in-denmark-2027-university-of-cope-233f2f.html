Fully Funded Scholarships in Denmark 2027: University of Copenhagen Offers Scholarships to Students from Rwanda and South Africa

Published on October 23, 2026

Scholarship Overview

The GeoAI for Land Systems Science initiative offers postgraduate opportunities for students to pursue studies at the University of Copenhagen in Denmark. Established as an international partnership between the University of Copenhagen, the University of Rwanda, and the University of KwaZulu-Natal in South Africa, this initiative is funded under the Danida Knowledge & Innovation Programme (2026–2032). It focuses on building research capacity and academic mobility in geospatial artificial intelligence and land systems science.

For the academic session starting in September 2027, the initiative has announced seven full 22-month scholarships. Over its full term through 2030, the project intends to award 51 scholarships in total, which includes 31 full-degree awards and 20 exchange opportunities.

Who Can Apply

Eligibility for this opportunity includes the following requirements:

  • Applicants must be enrolled as students at either the University of Rwanda or the University of KwaZulu-Natal.
  • Candidates must be eligible students from Rwanda or Southern Africa.
  • Relevant fields of study include climate change, computer science, geography and geoinformatics, remote sensing, and artificial intelligence.

Specific academic qualification thresholds are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Benefits

The scholarship supports 22 months of full-degree Master's studies at the University of Copenhagen. Precise financial coverage regarding tuition fees, living allowances, travel expenses, and insurance details are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

How to Apply

Eligible candidates must apply before the deadline on 15 November 2026 to be considered for the September 2027 intake. Applicants must review the official scholarship notice to verify institution-specific award numbers and admission criteria.
