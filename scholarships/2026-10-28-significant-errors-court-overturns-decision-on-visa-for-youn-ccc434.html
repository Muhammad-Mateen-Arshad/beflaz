'Significant errors': Court overturns decision on visa for young Palestinian student

Published on October 28, 2026

Scholarship Overview

A fully-funded educational opportunity exists for Palestinian students to pursue an engineering degree at University College Cork (UCC) in Ireland. This scholarship recently featured in a High Court ruling after a 20-year-old student from Gaza, Shaymaa Abudalu, successfully challenged an Irish visa refusal. High Court Judge Anthony Barr ruled that the decision by Minister for Justice Jim O’Callaghan contained significant errors, ordering the State to reconsider the visa application within 10 working days.

Who Can Apply

The reported case relates to Palestinian students seeking higher education in engineering at University College Cork. Full eligibility requirements, age limits, and selection criteria are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Benefits

Selected recipients receive a fully-funded scholarship to complete an engineering degree at UCC in Ireland. Additional financial details, stipends, or allowance values are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

How to Apply

Applicants must meet both university admission standards and Irish immigration requirements. Key documentation requirements noted in the legal proceedings include:

  • Official certification demonstrating English language proficiency.
  • Required financial records, such as bank statements, mandatory for Irish student visa processing.

Application procedures, official portals, and key deadlines are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Important Administrative Updates

Prospective international students should be aware of Irish visa processing regulations. In 2026, visa handling shifted to the Department of Justice, whereas applications in 2024 and 2025 were managed by Irish embassy staff in Tel Aviv. Strict adherence to required visa documents, including bank statements and language certifications, is required for applicants seeking entry to pursue their studies in Ireland.
