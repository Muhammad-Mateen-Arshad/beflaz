Published on November 05, 2026

The Medieval Arabic Manuscripts Research Study Overview highlights new findings on how historical knowledge was shared, verified, and transmitted across the Islamic world. The research focuses on handwritten margin notes and audition certificates found within historic texts. Scholarship Overview

Conducted by researchers Saleh Muhammad Zeki Mahmood Al-Leheabi and Ahmed Ameen of the University of Sharjah, this study was published in the Journal of Islamic Thought and Civilization. The authors examined 47 manuscripts containing 132 handwritten audition certificates, known as samāʿāt. These margin records document historical sessions where texts were read aloud before audiences in mosques, madrasas, and private residences across major learning centers such as Baghdad, Fustat, Damascus, Aleppo, and Isfahan.

The findings illustrate how oral instruction, written records, and manuscript verification reinforced one another from as early as 919 through the eleventh and twelfth centuries.
