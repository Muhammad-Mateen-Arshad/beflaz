Why Trump wants a $70,000 fee for international students working in the US

Published on October 27, 2026

International students planning to gain post-graduation work experience in the United States may face new financial regulations under a proposed federal rule.

Scholarship Overview

On October 8, 2026, the Department of Homeland Security published a proposed rule targeting Optional Practical Training (OPT). Rather than charging students directly, the rule requires certified institutions under the Student and Exchange Visitor Program to pay a $70,000 fee for a student's initial OPT recommendation. A subsequent recommendation for the same student would trigger an additional $30,000 fee, bringing combined charges up to $100,000. Federal officials state that the fees aim to prevent fraud, address administrative costs, and safeguard employment opportunities for American workers. The measure is currently under review, with public comments accepted until November 9. Existing rules remain active until a final rule is finalized.

Who Can Apply

The rules affect eligible F-1 international students who wish to pursue temporary employment related to their field of study. Specific scholarship application qualifications are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Benefits

OPT provides international graduates with practical career experience in fields matching their degree programs. Key aspects of the program include:

  • Up to 12 months of standard post-completion practical employment authorization.
  • A 24-month extension for graduates holding qualifying science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) degrees.
  • Opportunities to establish relationships with U.S. employers, though the program does not guarantee an H-1B visa or permanent residence.
