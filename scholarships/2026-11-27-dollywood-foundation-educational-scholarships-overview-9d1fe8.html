Dollywood Foundation Educational Scholarships Overview

Published on November 27, 2026

The Dollywood Foundation Educational Scholarships Overview highlights funding opportunities created to assist graduating high school students pursuing post-secondary education at accredited institutions. Established through the charitable efforts of Dolly Parton, these financial awards support young scholars from Sevier County, Tennessee, including those impacted by regional disasters. Application deadlines are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Scholarship Overview

The Dollywood Foundation was created in 1988 to improve educational opportunities and increase high school graduation rates among young people in Sevier County. During the early 1990s, the organization established the Buddy Program for seventh and eighth graders, providing $500 financial rewards to pairs of students who successfully completed high school together. This effort helped reduce the local high school dropout rate from 35% to 6%. The foundation later expanded its scope by providing ongoing annual scholarships to graduating students. Additionally, following the November 2016 wildfires, the foundation's My People Fund raised more than $12 million and created dedicated scholarships for students whose primary homes were destroyed in the disaster.

Who Can Apply

Eligibility for these funding programs includes:

  • Graduating high school students seeking to continue their studies at accredited institutions.
  • Students from Sevier County, Tennessee.
  • Graduating high school students whose primary residences were destroyed in the 2016 Sevier County wildfires (for specialized disaster-relief scholarship funds).

Benefits

Selected recipients receive financial assistance to support their ongoing education:

  • Annual scholarships worth $15,000 for graduating high school students attending accredited higher education institutions.
  • Special academic scholarship support for families recovering from regional disaster losses.
  • Historical program rewards included $500 completion incentives through student partnership programs.
