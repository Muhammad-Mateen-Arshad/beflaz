University of Strathclyde Commonwealth Shared Scholarship 2027

Published on November 16, 2026

The University of Strathclyde Commonwealth Shared Scholarship 2027 is a fully funded postgraduate opportunity for international students from eligible least developed and lower-middle-income Commonwealth countries. Administered in partnership with the Commonwealth Scholarship Commission, this award supports full-time Master's studies in the United Kingdom starting in September or October 2027. Applications open on 10 November 2026 and close on 8 December 2026.

Scholarship Overview

Funded by the UK government's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), the scheme aims to assist high-achieving individuals who lack the financial means to study in the UK independently. The programme aims to empower candidates to address sustainable development challenges in their home nations. For the 2027 academic intake, six scholarship places are available across eight specified Master's programmes at the University of Strathclyde.

Benefits

The scholarship provides full financial coverage for the one-year duration of the Master's degree, which includes:

  • Full coverage of approved tuition fees.
  • A living allowance for accommodation, food, and essential living expenses.
  • Return economy-class flights between the student's home country and the UK.
  • Additional eligible study expenses governed by Commonwealth Scholarship Commission terms.

Eligible Courses

Applicants can choose from eight approved Master's programmes, though only six total awards will be granted:

  • MSc Renewable Energy and Decarbonisation Technologies
  • MSc Applied Economics
  • MSc Diplomacy and International Security
  • MSc Sustainable Engineering: Renewable Energy Systems and the Environment
  • MSc Political Research
  • MSc Sustainable Engineering: Chemical Processing
  • MSc Offshore Wind Energy
  • MSc Environmental Engineering

Who Can Apply

To qualify for this scheme, applicants must fulfill the following criteria:

  • Be a citizen of, hold refugee status in, or be a British Protected Person from an eligible least developed or lower-middle-income Commonwealth country.
  • Maintain permanent residency in an eligible Commonwealth country when applying.
  • Hold an upper second-class (2:1) undergraduate degree, or a lower second-class degree supported by a relevant postgraduate qualification.
  • Be ready to begin their UK Master's programme at the start of the 2027–2028 academic year.
